# Cut-Rock Candy Lettering

## Dimension Synthesis

| Dimension | Observed system and variation | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Palette | The white core and darker ink recur in most commissions. Solid scarlet, segmented citrus colors and nearly transparent outer layers give the same hierarchy different moods. The Kintaro Sohonten example includes a colored face with pale letters. | High for the sample |
| Type | Short block initials, compact words, or two short curved lines work at sample scale. Low-contrast green marks sometimes lose clarity. Letterforms can shift among slices from one batch. | High for the recognition pattern |
| Texture | Cut faces show fine granulation. The outer surface catches longer highlights on the axis of the original cane. A clear band can expose thickness. | High for material contrast |
| Shape | The cut face is round to ovoid. A solid perimeter appears in the burgundy and scarlet samples, and seam-separated fields appear in several striped samples. | High |
| Motion | Qualifying samples are stills. Repeated slices indicate that the construction runs through the cane. The sources do not establish the timing of a cut or an interface transition. | High for the evidence boundary |
| Spatial | A clear inner margin keeps the core motif apart from upper and lower words and the rim. Maker photography pairs straight-on samples and side-facing slices. | High |
| Cultural markers | Wedding and event commissions dominate the Sticky sample. The other makers show pictograms, date lettering, brands and Kintarō-ame imagery. | Moderate for conventions beyond this sample |

## Analysis

The relationship between inside and outside is the main finding. A white center provides a generous field for block letters, whose thick strokes and enlarged counters stay recognizable as the same motif appears in successive cuts. Alternating color bands remain around the perimeter. The side of the cylinder makes them lengthwise stripes. Some commissions use a quiet solid sidewall instead. Small imperfections are visible at the cut edge and do not have to scale to the dimensions of the whole interface.

The corpus is dominated by modern Australian commissioned candy. The Blackpool museum's account documents the British souvenir practice, while the Kintaro Sohonten collaboration illustrates another tradition that can produce similar geometry. The samples establish a shared visual principle with limited evidence for a single history or common terminology. ## Connections

- **Gummy / Jelly Design** uses color and translucence to communicate material. Cut-rock lettering also depends on a legible embedded mark and matched cross-section and sidewall.
- **European Heraldic Design** also uses contained short text and compact emblems. A cut-rock mark repeats through the depth of a physically modeled cylinder.
- **Fairground Carnival Poster Art** shares some souvenir context and expressive lettering. Its graphic grammar is organized at the sheet scale.
