## Analysis

Memphis should be traced to the 1981–1980s Italian postmodern design group, not to the later tech-industry illustration style that borrowed only a thin slice of its surface vocabulary. The source set consistently anchors the movement in anti-functional furniture, pattern collision, and a critique of modernist seriousness.

Representative visual evidence comes from furniture and interiors more than typography alone: laminated surfaces, asymmetrical geometry, squiggles, dots, and color blocking arranged to feel witty and deliberately 'wrong.' That object logic is what keeps a digital translation from collapsing into generic colorful fun.

Ethical and usage note: avoid conflating Memphis with `corporate-memphis`. The latter is a contemporary platform-illustration descendant with very different politics and cultural reception; borrowing the name without the postmodern design context creates research drift and misleading provenance.

## Connections

- `corporate-memphis` — shares simplified geometry and bright color, but Corporate Memphis is flat platform illustration rather than postmodern furniture/object critique.
- `pop-art` — both are loud and commercial-coded, but Pop Art is representational and media-ironic while Memphis is abstract, object-led, and design-theory-driven.
- `italian-radical-design` — radical design supplies the anti-modernist context; Memphis is the more commercial, pattern-heavy postmodern branch.
