# Brazilian Neo-Concretism

Brazilian Neo-Concretism took shape in 1959 through artists and poets seeking an experiential relation between a work, its material support and a participant. The useful contemporary translation is an active relationship among planes, gaps, color and the reader's actions. It is not a generic primary-color modernism preset. The formal 1959–61 exhibition moment, early-1960s practice and later participatory continuations require distinct dates and boundaries.

## Source / Evidence Links

- [Associação Cultural Lygia Clark: first Neo-concrete exhibition](https://portal.lygiaclark.org.br/acervo/1747/47-artistas-premio-leirner-de-arte-contemporanea-1958) dates the Rio exhibition to March 1959 and identifies the seven manifesto signers. The [complete contemporary exhibition catalogue](https://portal.lygiaclark.org.br/acervo/10979/1-exposicao-neoconcreta) was directly inspected through all 22 supplied page scans. Its manifesto and checklists establish painting, print, sculpture, poetry and ballet, not a single sculptural recipe. No source poem is reproduced here.
- [ICAA/MFAH manifesto record 1110328](https://icaa.mfah.org/s/en/item/1110328) supplies synopsis, attribution and archival context. Its original download required login/contract acceptance and was not read through that route. The freely accessible Clark archive catalogue independently supplied direct manifesto inspection. Metadata March 21/21–22 versus the Clark archive's March 22 newspaper publication should not be silently normalized.
- [MoMA's Lygia Clark essay](https://www.moma.org/artists/27445-lygia-clark), [2014 exhibition release](https://www.moma.org/documents/moma_press-release_389356.pdf), and [MAM Rio's Clark account](https://mam.rio/artistas/lygia-clark/) distinguish the organic line, painting-to-object transition, hinged Bichos and later therapeutic practice. The last is outside this interface brief. MoMA uses 1959–66 for the Neo-concrete phase; a shorter institutional group chronology does not make all later practice an original group exhibition.
- [MoMA's Willys de Castro essay](https://www.moma.org/artists/8474-willys-de-castro), Julia Detchon, 2025, relates concrete poetry, color at edges, physical supports and changing viewpoints. The author distinguishes earlier Concrete paintings, Active Objects and 1980s Pluriobjects. This supports a perceptual vocabulary without pretending every geometric design is Neo-concrete.
- [Lygia Pape: Fernando Cocchiarale, 2004](https://lygiapape.com/en/en-artist/), [Reina Sofía's book research](https://www.museoreinasofia.es/coleccion/proyectos-investigacion/lygia-pape/), and [Glenstone's Book of Time exhibition](https://www.glenstone.org/exhibitions/lygia-pape) ground print, unbound books and spatial reading. Book of Time I's 1961 object record is retained separately from broader series dates used elsewhere. MoMA's Book of Creation current record says 16 pages; an older label's 15 is not silently repeated.
- [MAM Rio on reliefs and Nuclei](https://mam.rio/obras-de-arte/relevos-espaciais-1959-1960-nucleos-1960-1966/) distinguishes Oiticica's floating reliefs from larger chromatic environments. [Glenstone on Oiticica](https://www.glenstone.org/artists/helio-oiticica) relates viewing position, color and space. Some Nuclei allow walking between planes; that does not authorize entering every work or establish movement in a still photograph.

Seven shallow square panels distributed irregularly on a white wall: black and vivid red fields, one black/white divided panel, extremely fine straight gaps/lines and a white open rectangle crossing an edge. ## Analysis

Space and support are active content. A cut may become a gap, a fold may expose another plane, and viewing or handling may change what the work offers. Some works require physical participation; others use still painting, printed language or walking around a support. A digital application should make a meaningful user action alter its organization while preserving readable labels and a stable semantic sequence. A useful translation can be an editable workspace with open/closed panels, explicit earlier/later controls and export of the resulting work. This is a contemporary design proposal, not a recovered historic interface.

Contrast with generic modernism: selecting the same yellow/red/blue and adding triangles is insufficient. The actual information-bearing planes and intervening gaps should change relation. Contrast with optical spectacle: do not substitute endless spinning, hover-only parallax or a screen of hypnotic stripes for agency. Printing's deliberate stillness remains valid; motion is not universal across the corpus. A brief, user-triggered geometric transition can be proposed, while reduced motion uses immediate changes. Text, focus and action outcomes must remain clear in both modes.

## Connections

De Stijl and Bauhaus share geometric abstraction and color/space concerns but differ in historical location, theories and programs. Kinetic Art offers a useful adjacent comparison in actual movement, while Neo-concrete practice includes unanimated print and painting and emphasizes embodied experience rather than motion alone. Concrete poetry is a documented connection, not an alias for every work. Later Clark therapeutic proposals, Oiticica's environments and Pape's performances require their own dated contexts; affiliation is not a blanket aesthetic label.
