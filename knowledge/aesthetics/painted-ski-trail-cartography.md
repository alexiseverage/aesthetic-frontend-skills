# Painted Ski Trail Cartography

## Scope and classification

A distinct descriptive cartographic tradition: painted oblique winter relief made readable as a resort, with geographic relationships and a separate label/route/key layer. Not a new historical movement or a universal style of all ski maps. The panorama painting is a source layer, not sufficient by itself for every UI recommendation. Bare plates and promotional posters are distinguished from annotated trail maps. Summer hiking panoramas are retained as boundary examples.

## Source / Evidence Links

- [James Niehues, artist statement](https://jamesniehues.com/pages/about): identifies brush/airbrush and opaque watercolor, and explicitly describes adjusting viewpoints to combine terrain. This supports technique and constructed perspective, not surveyed accuracy or every other painter’s process.
- [Sun Peaks, The Making of a Trail Map](https://www.sunpeaksresort.com/explore/stories-inspiration/the-making-of-a-trail-map): primary client account of its 2019/20 commission, moving from aerial photographs and painting to later digital route/name graphics. The article’s “current” map means that edition; no 2026 operational claim is made.
- [Savoie heritage, Pierre Novat book/collection](https://patrimoines.savoie.fr/web/psp_11205/plans-des-pistes-les-domaines-skiables-de-france-dessines-par-pierre-novat): institutional context for Novat’s mountain-map practice and works entering museum collections. Dealer dates/attributions below remain dealer reports, not independently authenticated object records.
- [Heinrich C. Berann panorama archive](https://www.berann.com/panorama/archive/): artist archive supplies identified complete winter originals. Its 752–800px published files support broad painting/layout and major label observations; tiny text is not used for font or precise route claims.
- [Winfried Kettler in Skimap](https://skimap.org/artists/43): community archive identifies artist and resort editions, with original linked PDFs/JPEGs. Attribution is archival and was not independently confirmed with each resort; no weather, current-open or safety claims are inferred.
- [SkiVintage Novat collection](https://www.skivintage.com/AFFICHES-PLANS-DES-PISTES-PIERRE-NOVAT.htm): commercial-dealer discovery collection. Only three complete useful zoom scans qualify; six 450px catalogue images do not. Commercial watermarks and print wear are excluded from defining traits.

## Analysis

**Observed:** depth is painted, while lift/route/code systems use flatter marks; forests define corridors and miniature settlements establish a base. Snowmass and Alta/Snowbird demonstrate that bare terrain can have little/no typography. Novat’s posters show a separate promotional wordmark zone. Kettler’s keys demonstrate rectangular information rails, pictogram/label pairings and region-specific difficulty colors. A green summer valley with snowy back peaks is not a winter trail map.

**Source-supported:** Niehues’ technique and deliberate sightline adjustment; Sun Peaks’ separation between painting and later digital annotation; Savoie’s institutional account of Novat’s practice. Exact first-invention dates, undocumented painter media and current resort conditions remain unknown. Promotional superlatives are not adopted.

**Sampled:** Breckenridge plate pixel (1000,30) is #00bbeb; Corbier footer (150,1040) #ffffff; Axalp surround (1100,100) #8baddb. These are reproduction samples, not universal tokens. Labels and route colors need separate contrast review.

**Proposed:** original fictional terrain, HTML trail directory and keyed marker buttons, vector route overlay, useful local itinerary, native difficulty filter and readable selected-trail information. Immediate filtering/selection and a responsive stacked key are application choices. No timing is measured from static references. Preserve color plus code/shape/text, visible focus and a complete list alternative.

## Connections

General cartographic design contributes keys and relational information; alpine resort graphics contribute place identity and practical typography. Painted relief, white slope corridors and the base/summit topology distinguish this from Swiss-style resort branding, top-down maps or generic landscape painting. Snow Crystal Photomicrography shares winter subject matter but has isolated microscopic specimens rather than navigable geography. An actual trail-directory/map interaction differentiates the proposed showcase from image-led travel cards.
