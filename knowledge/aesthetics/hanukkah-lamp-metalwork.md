# Hanukkah Lamp Metalwork

The category studies the organization and fabrication of Jewish ritual lamps made for Hanukkah. The cross-collection comparison foregrounds the repeated festival lights and the location of the auxiliary light. This is a catalog description of a group of objects with different origins, purposes, and material practices.

## Analysis

The corpora include articulated wall-mounted backplates, footed and freestanding objects, Iraq and North African examples, European workshop objects, and modernist interpretations. The Jewish Museum constitutes the majority of the sample. The three other collections support the ability to recognize the grammar across curatorial contexts and add important variants, including a curved Hamburg vessel group, a Moroccan multi-panel hanging lamp, and a Smithsonian example whose wick elements are absent from the photographed tray.

The clearest visual distinction is between the series of functional light positions and the architecture supporting them. A late-17th-century Hamburg example and the 1940 Iraqi lamp show that a horizontal straight line is a common solution with documented exceptions. Backplates, arms, supports, and inscriptions carry the maker's choices. The various forms therefore offer usable typographic and compositional relationships without imposing one metal, prescribed silhouette, symbolic motif, or ritual illustration across all cases.

The proposed interface is an exhibition comparison designed around the objects' support and light arrangement. Use original, labeled interpretive diagrams for recurring structures and keep authentic inscription examples in provenance links. Contemporary typography, measurements, and control states are design decisions. Let the user select an object form or detail with immediate feedback. Avoid treating the count, auxiliary light, or inscriptions as a decorative progress system.
