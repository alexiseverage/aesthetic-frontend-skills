# Plant-Based Challenger Graphics

## Scope and classification

A bounded contemporary food-brand cluster documented in Oatly, NotCo, La Vie and Eat Curious: emphatic product naming, conspicuous shelf/ad color, conversational category-challenging copy and appetite-led product imagery or illustration. It spans packs, outdoor campaigns and web layouts; it is not a style shared by every vegan business or every animal-rights organization.

This is a new canonical entry, screened against current catalog slugs, labels, aliases, redirects and subset names. Neubrutalism may share hard contrast but does not provide food-category logic. Corporate Memphis may share rounded people but lacks this specific pack/product hierarchy. Animal-rights campaigns lead with an ethical claim and action; a challenger pack primarily sells a food product, even when it also advocates change.

## Dimension Synthesis

### Palette

High-contrast black/white messaging with product-coded cyan, pink, lime, yellow, red or brown. Oatly includes kraft and muted cartons; vivid green is not the compulsory sign of plant-based food.

### Type

Oversized expressive, compressed or irregular display lettering with calmer product details. Rotated naming, arches and stretching are documented branches. Fiasco identifies Obviously for Eat Curious; that does not identify every other brand font.

### Texture

Food close-ups, crisp cutouts, illustrated packs and smooth saturated photographic backgrounds. Grain appears on some cartons; clean still-life photography is equally grounded.

### Shape

Cartons, boxes and products become large graphic objects; arrows, ribbons, hand-drawn marks and convivial figures may support them. Avoid making every element a sticker or an unstructured collage.

### Motion

Still case images establish composition only. Fiasco describes motion principles, but they were not directly measured here. Choose user-triggered product changes or short feedback; reduced motion should retain all cart and ingredient states without bouncing.

### Spatial

One loud product/headline field plus orderly flavor, ingredient and purchase information. Outdoor copy adapts to tall, wide or repeated placements; the inspected recipe mockup uses large food cards and an ingredient/steps panel.

### Cultural markers

Plant-based category naming, taste-first language, product variety and playful challenge to familiar food conventions. Distinguish a product claim from an ethical or environmental claim that requires independent evidence.

## Analysis

### Claim-to-artifact support

Emphatic product hierarchy: PB02–03, PB12–16, PB19, PB22, PB30. Type variability: PB01–03, PB24. Food material/flat fields: PB17–18, PB21, PB26–28. Web layout: PB32. Oatly muted cartons counter a universal fluorescent palette.

### Confidence and gaps

High confidence in product/type/color hierarchy, moderate in shared cultural meaning across four commercially selected cases. No actual source motion or site keyboard behavior was inspected; motion advice is explicitly proposed.

Core identity is limited to the supported traits in the canonical non-negotiables. Counterexamples have been incorporated into the guidance rather than discarded to make a uniform mood board. No still-derived exact motion timing, measured physical performance or unverified source claim is promoted to fact.

### Application handoff

Connotation mode: **contemporary application**. Artifact concept: **Full of Beans tasting-box builder**. Signature visual unit: product pack. Build a tasting-box product selector with original packs, flavor buttons, quantity controls, a live basket total and ingredient/allergen disclosures. Keep practical details calm and readable beside oversized display type. Stack cards on mobile, preserve clear prices and do not hide allergens behind playful animation.

These are **proposed application tokens**, not sampled historical colors or measured timings. Background `#f5a8cd`, surface `#e4f1a0`, text `#173928`, muted `#3d5945`, accent `#f1c435`, success `#315d45`, danger `#8f252d`. Display `Impact, sans-serif` at `clamp(2rem,5vw,4.5rem)`, weight 700, line-height 1.02, tracking -0.035em; body `Arial, sans-serif` at 1rem/1.5, weight 400, tracking 0; labels 0.8125rem/1.3, weight 700, tracking 0.06em; numeric outputs use tabular figures at 1rem/1.3. Space 1–6: `4,8,12,20,32,48px`; radius `2px` for functional fields and `0` for panel edges (a circular dial/label retains its own geometry). Default state changes are instant; optional feedback `120ms ease-out` is a proposed web value, disabled under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`. No automatic decorative loop is required.

These tokens are a baseline for an original interface. Include the supported subject/identity, its information hierarchy and its operational/reading context. Avoid copied marks/art, generic palette swaps and inaccessible source-era constraints. The source references remain analytical; original procedural graphics or separately created images should carry the application.

### Accessibility and adaptation flags

Palette tokens do not certify interface contrast. Check actual foreground/background states, focus outlines, disabled controls and overlays; target WCAG AA text and non-text requirements. Body text should remain 16px or larger in the application. Responsive priority is content order and control usability, not a tiny scaled facsimile. Keep explicit text equivalents for graphs and decorative SVGs hidden from assistive technology when captions already describe them. Respect user zoom and narrow layouts.

Reduced motion: stillness or immediate state changes are valid. Any proposed motion is optional, interruptible and removed for reduced motion. Dark mode: retain the artifact's deliberate material palette within its scoped frame; adapt surrounding application chrome separately. Fatigue: limit the loudest texture/type to the defining field. Conflict rule: preserve the non-negotiables and task semantics before introducing a neighboring palette, font or transition.
