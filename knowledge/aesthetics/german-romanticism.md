## Dimension Synthesis

Exact hex colors, spacing, font choices and timing are not historical measurements.

**Palette**: Earth browns, deep green, slate, blue-grey and luminous cream often divide a dark near field from pale distance. Rose, gold, red dawn, and bright pastoral green also occur. Winter can use ochre ice beside cold blue air; an exclusively blue palette is optional, not historical law.

**Type**: Paintings usually contain no functional typography. Inscriptions and print captions are secondary. A measured serif with quiet readable interface labels is a proposed editorial translation, not an identified movement-wide typeface. Blackletter is not a default requirement.

**Texture**: Carefully described bark, stone, snow, mist and painted light; preparatory sheets can expose fast brushwork or fine graphite lines. Runge also used cut-paper silhouette and graphic contour. Preserve meaningful material differences instead of covering everything in heavy crackle, sepia, or glossy glass.

**Shape**: Branching trees, angular rocks, low shores, receding ridges, small ships and human silhouettes; windows, ruins and paths create viewing thresholds. Some scenes organize around a central vertical, others around an almost empty horizontal expanse. Ornament belongs to specific symbolic works, not every panel.

**Motion**: Qualifying references are still works; historical interface timing is unknown. Contemplative stillness is an appropriate application choice. Use explicit user-controlled changes, stable framing and immediate reduced-motion behavior. Do not describe CSS fog drift or parallax as historically observed.

**Spatial**: Near/far contrast and atmospheric recession carry emotional meaning. Allow one landscape to dominate, hold generous sky or mist, and place useful controls outside that quiet field. Small figures are common, but the Wanderer is comparatively large and several works omit figures entirely.

**Cultural markers**: Early nineteenth-century German-speaking artistic contexts, Romantic subjectivity, spiritual nature, memory, seasonal/life cycles and the sublime. Crosses, graveyards and ruined abbeys have specific religious/historical meanings. Dresden connections included Norwegian-born painters; avoid nationalist essentialism.

### Claim-to-artifact support

Historical identity and cultural/technical claims come from the context sources above. No animation, browsing behavior, historical font identity or exact pigment/yarn number was observed from still references. Intentional stillness and system-font roles are proposed application choices.

### Confidence and boundaries

Palette, material, shape and composition: high for the inspected branch, not a census. Type: high where poster lettering is present; low as a historical UI/type claim for paintings and garments. Cultural context: source-supported, with provenance caveats below. Motion: unknown historically, no invented measurements.

26 of 30 works are Friedrich, reflecting the winter landscape emphasis and the anniversary project’s complete reproductions. Runge, Carus and Baade test that boundary. This does not represent all German Romantic artists or all media. The corpus supports a landscape-led application, while broader movement claims also require institutional context. Norwegian-born Baade is included as part of Dresden Romantic connections, not assigned a German nationality.
