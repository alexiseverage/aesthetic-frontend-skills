## Historical / Origin Notes

Scandinavian design emerged in the early twentieth century and flourished in the 1950s across the Nordic countries, combining simplicity, minimalism, and functionality with a strong domestic and human-centered orientation.

## Notable practitioners, artifacts, regions, or institutions

- Kaare Klint, Arne Jacobsen, Alvar Aalto, Danish modern furniture, and Nordic housewares are the central anchors.
- UI translation should preserve the movement’s combination of warmth, craftsmanship, and functional restraint rather than reducing it to blank white minimalism.

## Analysis

Scandinavian Modern balances modernist reduction with comfort: natural materials, humane scale, and quiet functionality make simplicity feel lived-in rather than austere.

Non-negotiables for implementation: daylight-oriented light palette; natural texture; ergonomic restraint; warm human-scale spacing.

Cultural sensitivity assessment: Low identity-risk but historically specific. The mode comes from Nordic social and domestic design traditions; avoid using “Scandi” as a flattened lifestyle shorthand detached from craft and welfare-modernist context.

## Connections

`mid-century-modern` overlaps in period and furniture language but is broader and often sharper; `wabi-sabi-slow-living` shares restraint and material sensitivity but values imperfection over polished utility; `shaker-design` shares honesty and simplicity but is more ascetic and religious in origin.
