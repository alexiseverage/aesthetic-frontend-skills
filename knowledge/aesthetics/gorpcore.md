### Visual evidence summary
The Cut's origin piece grounds gorpcore in Patagonia fleeces, Columbia windbreakers, North Face puffers, Arc'teryx parkas, Tevas, Birkenstocks, painter pants, Vans, and Japanese outdoor/streetwear cross-pollination; the through-line is authentic outdoor gear worn intentionally in the city rather than runway fantasy ([1]). Wikipedia usefully compresses the object set into puffer jackets, hiking boots, fleeces, and technical outerwear from brands such as The North Face, Patagonia, and Arc'teryx ([2]). Vogue's later sneaker-focused piece shows the mature commercial form: Salomon XT-6s, grippy soles, elastic laces, trail silhouettes, car coats plus leggings, and city-styled performance shoes that still read as "ready for anything" ([3]).

### Seven-dimension synthesis
- Palette: Canonical palette is terrain-derived and technical: black, slate, olive, stone, moss, rust, safety-color accents, and occasional alpine reds/oranges. Neons can appear as equipment cues, but the aesthetic reads strongest when most of the surface area is muted and weather-ready ([1], [3]).
- Type: Utility-minded sans-serif labeling, brand marks, topo/map-like numerals, and small technical metadata are common. Typography should feel product-spec or expedition-adjacent rather than editorial luxury ([1], [3]).
- Texture: Ripstop nylon, fleece, shell fabric, mesh, quilting, taped seams, webbing, drawcords, and water-resistant matte synthetics are load-bearing. Texture should feel fabricated for weather, not decorative ([1], [2], [3]).
- Shape: Boxy puffers, anoraks, vests, cargo silhouettes, trail-runner forms, and modular layers. Volume is functional rather than sculptural; straps, toggles, and pockets matter ([1], [2]).
- Motion: Brisk, practical, and route-oriented. In interface terms, motion should imply readiness, navigation, and equipment feedback rather than delight; fast linear or eased utility transitions fit better than theatrical animation (inferred from the survival/utility framing in [1] and the performance-footwear emphasis in [3]).
- Spatial: Layered but ordered. Dense clusters of product or control information are acceptable if they preserve scanability; layouts should feel like gear systems, trail maps, or packing logic instead of boutique minimalism ([1], [3]).
- Cultural markers: Patagonia, The North Face, Arc'teryx, Salomon, Teva, Birkenstock, Stone Island, Japanese outdoorswear/streetwear influence, trail snacks/GORP, hiking competence signaling, and urban-outdoor hybridity are the clearest markers ([1], [2], [3]).

### Related-aesthetic boundaries
- Versus `normcore`: gorpcore inherits normcore's anti-flash stance but swaps suburban blankness for a much narrower hiking/outdoor vocabulary; mall basics are not enough ([1]).
- Versus `athleisure`: athleisure is gym/fitness adjacent; gorpcore is trail/camp/weather adjacent, with shells, fleeces, boots, and equipment details rather than leggings-and-sneaker wellness polish ([1], [2]).
- Versus `techwear`: both like function, but techwear usually pushes darker cyber-urban futurism and more tactical severity; gorpcore stays grounded in recognizable outdoor recreation products and mountain-brand authenticity ([1], [3]).
- Versus `scandinavian-modern` / warm minimalism: those aesthetics simplify; gorpcore layers, straps, panels, and weatherproof utility cues.

### Implementation notes for dictionary entry
- Non-negotiables: authentic outdoor-gear object vocabulary + technical textile texture + urban/outdoor hybridity + utility-first silhouette.
- Avoid reducing gorpcore to "chunky sneakers." Sneakers alone without shells/fleece/webbing/pocket logic collapse into generic sporty streetwear ([3]).
- Connotation mode should be "contemporary revival" or "ironic-to-earnest adoption": the original joke/defiant-ugly framing in The Cut softened into a stable fashion language, but authenticity of gear still matters ([1]).
- Strong dictionary aliases: trailcore (adjacent but not identical), outdoorswear streetwear, hikingcore as descriptive phrase only; do not overclaim them as exact synonyms unless downstream research validates them.
