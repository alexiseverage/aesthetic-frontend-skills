### Visual evidence summary
Vogue's history piece ties the trend to practice wear and long arcs of "balletomania," highlighting leotards, tights, leg warmers, wrap layers, tutus/tulle, and off-duty dancer styling from Miu Miu, Sandy Liang, MM6, and others ([1]). The later Vogue piece adds an important corrective from professional dancers: real rehearsal wear is layered, utilitarian, sometimes grungy, with knitwear, baggy warmups, puffer vests, cut-up garments, and mismatched leg warmers, not just dainty pink fantasy ([2]). Who What Wear frames balletcore as an aesthetic with staying power because it fuses high-brow art, girlhood/femininity performance, and high-fashion adoption ([3]).

### Seven-dimension synthesis
- Palette: Blush pink, cream, dove gray, black leotard black, pale peach, soft taupe, satin ivory, and occasional warm nude dominate. Black is important as rehearsal contrast, not as goth intrusion ([1], [2]).
- Type: Delicate, refined, lightly romantic serif or soft sans. Letterforms should feel poised and elongated rather than sugary or childish. Script can appear sparingly for recital/program notes, but too much script turns it coquette ([1], [3]).
- Texture: Tulle, knit rib, jersey, satin, mesh, pointe-ribbon ties, matte tights, wrap-cardigan softness, and lightly worn rehearsal fabric. A key nuance from dancers: balletcore texture is not only pristine satin; it also includes layered warm-up pragmatism ([2]).
- Shape: Long line, wrap closures, Mary Janes/ballet flats, ribbons, leg warmers, bodysuit silhouettes, cropped shrugs, and skirt lengths ranging from short rehearsal wrap skirts to romantic ankle tulle ([1], [2], [3]).
- Motion: Graceful, vertical, floating, controlled, and body-aware. Animations should feel weightless and precise, not bouncy kawaii. Ease curves can be soft but should preserve balance and posture ([1]).
- Spatial: Airy and centered, with room around key elements. However, rehearsal-informed variants can layer items around the body more densely; the interface version should prioritize elegant spacing with one focal axis, like barre/stage orientation ([1], [2]).
- Cultural markers: Ballet flats, leotards, wrap sweaters, tights, tulle, ribbons, pointe references, barre/studio/stage imagery, Miu Miu/Sandy Liang fashion references, and dancer/off-duty mood ([1], [2], [3]).

### Related-aesthetic boundaries
- Versus `coquette`: both can use pink, bows, and femininity, but balletcore is disciplined, body-trained, and dance-derived; coquette is flirtier, more lingerie/girlhood/romance coded.
- Versus `soft girl`: balletcore is more formalized, posture-conscious, and art-institution adjacent.
- Versus `barbiecore`: balletcore is pale, poised, and graceful; barbiecore is louder, pinker, shinier, and more performatively pop.
- Versus `dark-academia`: both can be cultural-performance aesthetics, but balletcore is bodily and ethereal where dark academia is bookish and sepia.

### Implementation notes for dictionary entry
- Non-negotiables: dance-derived silhouette logic + soft rehearsal/stage material vocabulary + poised, weightless motion + feminine restraint rather than sugary excess.
- Include the dancer critique: authentic balletcore should leave room for rehearsal realism, not only consumer pink-tutu fantasy ([2]). This is an important anti-caricature guardrail.
- Connotation mode: contemporary revival with roots in historical balletomania. It is a revived fashion-language loop, not a wholly new internet-born aesthetic ([1]).
- Strong alias caution: "ballet aesthetic" is descriptive; not every ballerina-inspired look is balletcore unless the silhouette/material/motion system is legible.

### Website-showcase guidance
Loading indicator is the assigned website component. Best direction: pirouette rings, ribbon spirals, barre-like progress bars, tulle-layer fades, or orbiting pointes/ribbons. Use soft pink/cream/graphite with generous whitespace. Avoid saccharine glitter overload; the best reference point is rehearsal poise plus runway polish.

### Cultural-sensitivity / caution notes
Do not trivialize ballet labor or use pointe/dancer imagery in ways that ignore training, injury, and discipline. The Vogue dancer interviews make clear that balletcore can become flattening if it only copies the childlike costume stereotype ([2]).
