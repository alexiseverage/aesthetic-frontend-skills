# Shin-hanga

## Scope and Classification

Recommend a distinct canonical entry in `historical-design-movements`. Shin-hanga is an established early twentieth-century Japanese woodblock-print revival, associated with publishers, designers, carvers and printers working together. Its modern print grammar is separately useful from City Pop's later commercial leisure illustration and from wabi-sabi's material aging. It is related to earlier ukiyo-e; neither all ukiyo-e nor all modern Japanese prints are synonyms. Sōsaku-hanga's self-drawn/self-carved/self-printed ideal is an important production boundary, not an interchangeable spelling.

Use the mature revival as the entry's center, while naming earlier shinsaku-hanga/formative publishing as context. Source attribution matters: artist membership alone does not classify every work. Two Meiji-era Koson prints are retained as precursor comparisons rather than counted toward the mature corpus. Foreign-born designers participated, and Watanabe was highly influential without being the only publisher.

## Context Sources

- [Art & History Museum exhibition](https://www.artandhistory.museum/en/activity/shin-hanga): collaborative traditional woodblock revival, modernizing Japan, landscape/bijin/kabuki/bird-and-flower subjects; named prints anchor attribution. The [visitor guide](https://www.artandhistory.museum/sites/default/files/2022-11/SH_booklet_EN_web.pdf) describes formative publishing, variants and named publishers. The guide is text-only; its object list is not an inspected image corpus.
- [Library of Congress: Beyond the Floating World](https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/ukiyo-e/beyond.html): Shin-hanga and Sōsaku-hanga differ in production organization. Its broader modern-print page includes neighboring movements; those works must not all be counted as Shin-hanga. The Goyō Woman after Bath record is the same design as the inspected Smithsonian print.
- [Smithsonian: Dream Worlds](https://asia.si.edu/whats-on/exhibitions/dream-worlds/): curator-supported portraits, actor subjects, birds and seasonal/light imagery, including Raizan's unusual restrained moonlight. Its exhibition also contains earlier ukiyo-e examples; collection membership is insufficient classification.
- LACMA's named object records, particularly [Awazu](https://collections.lacma.org/object/87594), [Eyebrow Pencil](https://collections.lacma.org/object/87618) and [Spring Rain at Yushima Tenjin](https://collections.lacma.org/object/87907), supply dates/media and explicitly cite its Shin-hanga exhibition scholarship.
- [UMMA actor portrait](https://umma.umich.edu/objects/actor-sawamura-gennosuke-iv-as-niki-danjo-hiding-under-the-floor-2008-2-8/) and [Hawk in snow](https://umma.umich.edu/objects/hawk-in-snow-1930-65/) establish actor and bird branches through attributed museum objects. Some record prose contains typographical errors; do not reproduce the incorrect artist lifespan.

## Analysis

### Identity, variants and non-negotiables

The repeatable system is a modern woodblock-print visual language: deliberate color planes, fine contour/mark hierarchy and a purposefully framed subject. Broad gradation and atmosphere are common, especially in landscape work, but the flat red portrait and isolated bird designs rule out mandatory blue/rain/negative-space formulas. Printed surface character is compatible with crisp control; this is not a requirement for distressed paper or simulated imperfection.

Landscape/weather, bijin/domestic portrait, kabuki actor and bird-and-flower are source-supported subject branches, not new canonical slugs. Kasamatsu's densely veiled pagoda contrasts with Hasui's quiet mountain/lake. Shinsui's early Awazu demonstrates that maker specialization is not an absolute subject boundary. Raizan makes near abstraction credible without generalizing every abstract Japanese print into Shin-hanga.

Corpus distribution: Capelari 1, Koson 4, Raizan 1, Goyō 2, Kasamatsu 6, Hasui 7, Shinsui 4, Kotondo 4 and Shunsen 1. Six institutional collections are independently useful. Sampling favors museum-held landscapes and beauty/bird prints, with one actor portrait and only one foreign-born designer. Hiroshi Yoshida's international scenes and a fuller actor/foreign-designer corpus remain useful future enrichment; no global exhaustiveness is claimed.

### Interface translation and connotation

Recommended mode: contemporary revival with attributed historical context. A seasonal print-inspired journal can use original illustrative panels, clear near/middle/far layers, tonal sky/water fields, local fine marks and a calm sheet-like margin. Use plain readable controls and metadata outside the illustration. Do not make a blue palette the sole identity signal or turn paper wear into a universal effect.

Proposed showcase: a fictional **Weather Leaves** observation journal with three original scenes, seasonal filtering, a selected plate with real condition/date/location metadata, and one saved observation with editable personal note. Unlike the prior catalog and concert shortlist, this is an image-led journal with a structured observation and note workflow. The showcase will demonstrate the landscape branch; its scope note should not imply all Shin-hanga is landscape.

Functional UI fonts, dimensions, contrast values, focus rings and responsive layouts are contemporary proposals. There are no measured historical UI breakpoints or timings. No user-generated note should be transmitted or claim persistence beyond the demonstration's local state unless actual storage is implemented.

### Anti-patterns and boundaries

- Treating every Japanese woodblock print, every modern Japanese print, or every work by an associated maker as Shin-hanga.
- Requiring blue rain scenes, a red torii, a full moon or a dark silhouette in every variant.
- Making faux aging, beige rough paper, random kanji or Latin brush lettering the entire identity.
- Confusing depicted rain/dance/flight with measured animated behavior.
- Copying a museum image, seal or artist composition into new original assets without separate rights handling.
- Putting low-contrast sky gradients or heavy texture under functional body copy.

## Connections

- `city-pop`: later music-packaging leisure illustration, more commercial-clean color and modern urban/resort vocabulary. A palm, blue sky or a Japanese caption alone does not establish either identity.
- `wabi-sabi-slow-living`: shares some quiet material presentation, but aging, impermanence and humble irregularity are not Shin-hanga production rules; fine finished print quality may be central.
- `warm-minimalism`: contemporary restrained styling, not the modern woodblock revival or a synonym for Japanese visual culture. Japandi is a further comparison term, not a canonical slug in this catalog.
- Earlier ukiyo-e is a lineage; Sōsaku-hanga is a neighboring production movement. Neither receives an invented related slug absent from the current manifest.
