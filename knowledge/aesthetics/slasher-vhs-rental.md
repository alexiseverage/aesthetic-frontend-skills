## Analysis

The defining combination is genre-specific sleeve hierarchy, recognizable home-video anatomy and a store/circulation layer. A full killer portrait is common, but the giant axe on Absurd, Christmas gift/knife on Don't Open Till Christmas and wet shoe/knife on Sleepaway Camp disprove a universal character-first requirement. These exact protected arrangements remain source-specific. Original invented objects, locations and lettering can convey threat without copying a franchise or requiring a figure. A generic dark streaming card or a scanline overlay does not supply case/inventory logic.

### Application translation (proposed)

A fictional closing-time rental desk can place three selectable horizontal shelf spines above a large case inspector and paper checkout record. Three wholly original object/location covers use a receiver/door, pool ladder/key and closed gate/light, with different lettering and palettes. Front/back/spine/cassette views change actual content. Borrow marks that copy unavailable; return requires an explicit rewind action and restores stock. This is a local demo, not a real rental or preservation procedure. Still state changes are a contemporary functional choice. Proposed screen colors, label sizes and controls are not measured historic standards.

### Cultural and rights review

Historic artwork often objectifies women or codes disability, gender difference or Indigenous identity as a threat. Body Count's Swedish sleeve includes an Indigenous-shaman menace trope; it is documented critically, not reused as visual vocabulary. Do not imitate those narratives or copy protected performers, masks, artwork, studio/retailer marks, classification symbols or titles. A supernatural-slasher sample does not authorize claims about actual people or communities. Sensitivity choices should preserve genre specificity through fictional objects, suspense and material/inventory grammar.
