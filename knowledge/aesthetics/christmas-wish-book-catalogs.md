# Christmas Wish Book Catalogs

## Dimension Synthesis

| Dimension | Observed system and variation | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Palette | Scarlet and cream recur in title and cover fields. Pine and warm photographic values frequently support them. The Canadian 1978 winter cover and several interiors use cool blue. | High for the sampled markets |
| Type | Covers use large distinct letterforms. Interiors use small keyed IDs, descriptions, prices, sizes and ordering details. Some covers put indices and dates in a separate tier. | High for roles, moderate for interior variants |
| Texture | Painted covers, full-color photo print and visibly worn paper coexist. Wear varies by copy and digitizer. | Moderate |
| Shape | Covers alternately crop to a full rectangle, inset a scene behind a rule, hold it in an oval, or arrange several tiles. Interiors use boxed IDs within a grid or cutout collage. | High for the shape repertoire |
| Motion | The original layout is static. Page turning is a physical interaction, while archive paging and magnification are modern access tools. | High for the source boundary |
| Spatial | A broad merchandising image leads to compact keyed descriptions. Ordering terms sit below or beside the image. Coded labels recur even where the columns differ. | High in four sampled interiors, limited outside them |
| Cultural markers | Gift choice, mail and phone ordering, pricing periods and index references connect the imagery to its retail function. The corpus has a pronounced Canadian cover emphasis. | High for the sampled period and market |

## Direct Media Audit

The audit counts 31 unique catalog editions across three repositories. Collectorville provides 27 individually inspected complete covers of Eaton’s and Simpsons–Sears Christmas publications. Three Catalogs & Wishbooks books and one People's Graphic Design Archive entry provide U.S. Sears and JCPenney interior pages. Multiple pages of one catalog edition were grouped together. An Eaton’s interior from another part of a catalog and the 1976 Eaton’s short gift publication were excluded from the edition count. The archive returned loading errors for several additional U.S. editions. The study uses four interior editions and supports a broader comparison of cover composition than product layout.

## Analysis

The cover gives readers a seasonal entry point and can also show where to find the illustrated merchandise. The catalog page reduces the distance between interest and lookup by attaching small identifiers directly to objects or figures. A separate listing repeats each letter or number near its specifications. This holds in the Sears 1975 sweets page, the Sears 1977 clothing samples and two JCPenney examples, despite substantial differences in design density. Boxed identifiers and column text are more reliable evidence of the format than any particular palette or display font.

An original seasonal layout can give the reader the same relationship between an illustrated object and its ordering information while replacing historical illustrations, people, and shopping arguments with project-created material. Neither decorative wear nor a specific dated storefront is necessary to show the format.
