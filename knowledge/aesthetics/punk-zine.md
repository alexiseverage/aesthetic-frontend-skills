## Analysis

Punk zine aesthetics are best grounded in DIY print culture and politically charged anti-design rather than in the abstract idea of roughness. The Jamie Reid and punk-visual-art sources support a process-first reading: cut, photocopy, tape, steal attention, and reject professional polish as a gatekeeping system.

Representative visual evidence is materially obvious: cutout letters, collaged headlines, copier noise, and page assembly that shows its seams. If the composition feels too carefully distressed or too digitally immaculate, the result drifts toward grunge typography or commercial rebellion cosplay instead.

Ethical and usage note: because punk visual language carries explicit anti-authoritarian and subcultural politics, using it for authority-seeking institutions or polished consumer persuasion can read as extraction. Preserve the DIY social logic, not only the ransom-note surface.

## Connections

- `grunge-typography` — punk is materially DIY and political; grunge is designerly deconstruction and commercial-era distress.
- `risograph` — both live in indie print culture, but risograph foregrounds spot-ink craft while punk foregrounds urgency and anti-polish assembly.
- `harm-reduction-zine` — both borrow zine textures, but harm-reduction zine prioritizes care clarity over antagonistic visual aggression.
