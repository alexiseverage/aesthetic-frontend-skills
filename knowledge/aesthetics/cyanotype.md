# Cyanotype — research profile

## Classification and Context

**Decision:** a separately useful material/process aesthetic, not an alias for botanical illustration, photographic blue toning, or a generic blueprint interface. Its visual system connects a light-formed image, a coated surface and paper. This research emphasizes the untoned blue branch; it does not establish a comprehensive account of toned, textile or wet-cyanotype practice.

**Source-supported:** the [V&A process account](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/photographic-processes) dates Herschel's invention to 1842 and describes iron-salt-coated paper, contact exposure and washing. Its collection metadata identifies both photograms and negative-based prints. Chemical identity is supported by catalog records, not diagnosed from a blue JPEG alone.

**Source-supported:** the [Natural History Museum](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/anna-atkins-cyanotypes-the-first-book-of-photographs.html) places Atkins's self-published *Photographs of British Algae* in 1843 onward, describes handwritten photographic captions, and explains her scientific networks and family economic context, including wealth connected to slavery. She applied the process to botanical publishing; she did not invent it.

**Source-supported:** [MoMA's collection](https://www.moma.org/collection/terms/cyanotype) documents botanical, anatomical, experimental and technical works. [SFMOMA's Ceylon record](https://www.sfmoma.org/artwork/2002.50/) independently identifies a botanical cyanotype. The museum attributions distinguish actual medium from appearance alone.

## Analysis

**Observed:** contact records preserve finely cut silhouettes and varied translucency; photographs from negatives preserve continuous modeling. The coating perimeter and image perimeter can differ, and many works leave no broad white border. Botanical plates often employ quiet naming and asymmetric open ground, while technical and experimental prints can be dense or layered.

**Inference for interfaces:** an album is one productive application of the botanical branch. A large original print, readable notes and immediate sheet selection can preserve its material and spatial logic. Modern controls, responsive stacking, type sizes and palette tokens are design proposals rather than historical measurements.

**Boundary:** blue alone is insufficient, botanical subject matter is optional and paper distress is a variant. Risograph reproduces ink passes and registration; this process records light. Ghosted contact traces do not establish supernatural content. A digital process illustration should not masquerade as a real exposure calculator or an authenticated museum artifact.

**Limitations:** the corpus is museum-weighted, largely paper-based and untoned. It gives no reliable historical interaction, timing, easing or responsive evidence. It does not establish a universal font, precise exposure recipe or calibrated blue standard. Further textile, wet-process and toned-print research would be required before prescribing those branches.
