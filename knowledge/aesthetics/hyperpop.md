# Hyperpop

A separately useful but deliberately scoped visual grouping: PC Music-linked and adjacent experimental pop campaigns, tested against later rough photographic, theatrical and fantasy branches. This is not a claim to invent a new musical category, identify all artist affiliations, or assign every fan one visual identity. Connotation mode: contemporary revival.

## Analysis

A specific authored world is more stable than any material effect. The sample supports a glossy product/portrait branch strongly, but its own negative examples disprove mandatory pink, chrome, extreme density, CGI or irony. Firsthand agency and creator credits prevent anonymous mood-board flattening. A musical umbrella does not turn every associated artist's complete career into a single visual category.

Exact historical animation timing, sound/visual synchronization, responsive breakpoints and accessibility were not measured. Proposed UI tokens, finite feedback, contrast and note-synthesis behavior are new design decisions. A mathematical timbre named Glass does not model glass acoustics or emulate SOPHIE. Reference sound recordings are neither reused nor claimed to have been analyzed.

## Connections

Canonical `y2k`, `web-2-gloss`, `organic-digital`, `rave-flyer` and `myspace-chaos` provide useful overlaps and explicit differences. Hyperpop is separately useful because the artist/network/pop-world context and its deliberately contradictory polished/ordinary imagery cannot be captured by chrome nostalgia alone. Digicore needs its own community and visual evidence before promotion; no provisional cross-reference to an absent canonical slug.
