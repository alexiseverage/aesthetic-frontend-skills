## Historical / Origin Notes

Op Art became a major painting movement in the 1960s, using geometric forms and repeated patterns to create optical effects, vibration, or illusory depth.

## Notable practitioners, artifacts, regions, or institutions

- Bridget Riley, Victor Vasarely, exhibition posters, and gallery-based visual experiments are the key anchors.
- UI translation should frame Op Art as a technique-aesthetic useful for hero moments, motion accents, and controlled spectacle rather than a full-site default.

## Analysis

Op Art is a perception engine: repeated geometry and contrast produce movement, depth, and instability directly in the viewer’s eye.

Non-negotiables for implementation: illusion-first geometry; high perceptual contrast; pattern precision; careful restraint for legibility/accessibility.

Cultural sensitivity assessment: Low identity-risk but medium accessibility risk. Strong optical vibration can trigger discomfort, fatigue, or vestibular issues; accessibility review matters more than nostalgia.

## Connections

`suprematism` shares geometric abstraction but aims at pure feeling rather than retinal illusion; `de-stijl` also uses strict geometry but pursues order instead of vibration; `psychedelic` can overlap in visual intensity yet is looser, more figurative, and culturally different.
