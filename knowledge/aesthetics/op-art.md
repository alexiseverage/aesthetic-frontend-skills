## Dimension Synthesis

“Core”, “common” and “variant” below are qualitative judgments about this purposive corpus, not population percentages.

| Dimension | Core | Common | Variant / limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Palette | Adjacent values/hues participate in a visible perceptual relationship. | Black/white alternating fields; saturated adjacent color. | Blue-gray *Nineteen Greys* and pale color ribbons disprove mandatory maximum contrast or complementary pairs. |
| Type | The work's geometry carries meaning without explanatory display lettering. | Small inscriptions outside the field. | No historical interface or font corpus: neutral UI typography is a proposed supporting choice. |
| Texture | Crisp repeated boundaries and changes in density. | Flat painted/printed reproduction. | Graph-paper studies, slotted construction and hanging rods: flatness is not universal. Surface grain cannot be diagnosed precisely from these reproductions. |
| Shape | Repeat plus systematic variation: width, spacing, angle, scale, interruption or hue. | Waves, warped checker fields, disks/ellipses, nested angles and lines. | Vasarely's zebras retain representation. A regular grid is a tool, not an absolute restriction. |
| Motion | Apparent instability can arise from a stationary work. | Curved line fields and compressed seams suggest shifting depth. | No video or live viewpoint sequence inspected. Digital duration/easing, rotation or flashing remain application proposals, never historical requirements. |
| Spatial | Figure-ground ambiguity, compression and expansion build perceptual depth. | Bounded fields with quiet margins. | Soto's penetrable space and Cruz-Diez's relief require actual depth; the corpus is not exclusively flat or centered. |
| Cultural markers | Specific practitioners, works and institutional context matter. | Riley/Vasarely and the 1960s optical-painting frame. | Soto and Cruz-Diez connect Venezuelan and Paris-based optical/kinetic practice; avoid reducing the movement to one European monochrome style. |

## Image Descriptions

The numbered records below preserve the inspection trace. Record IDs match the ledger. Distinctness was checked against titles, accessions and actual compositions. Record 12 remains excluded because its relation to record 09 is unresolved; the isolated Keiho overlay is counted with the complete construction, not as a new work.

- **op-art-01 — Ascending and Descending Hero** (original, qualifying): Alternating black and pale horizontal bands narrow and widen toward central pinches, forming opposing triangular fields. The illusion comes from varying line thickness and slope on a flat rectangular surface, without depicted objects or physical rotation. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/28890).

- **op-art-02 — Print for Chicago 8, from Conspiracy, The Artist as Witness** (original, qualifying): Repeated tall columns each hold adjacent lavender, turquoise and yellow ribbons whose boundaries bow gently. Broad pale margins separate the pattern from a small handwritten lower inscription; optical color interaction is not restricted to black and white. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/198323).

- **op-art-03 — Turquoise and Red Greys, Sequence Study** (original, qualifying): Precisely repeated turquoise/red-gray zigzag bands sit over visible square graph paper. Saturation shifts down the sheet; penciled margin notes and a signature expose this as a color sequence study rather than a purely finished glossy pattern. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/158410).

- **op-art-04 — Nineteen Greys** (original, qualifying): A square blue-gray field contains a regular matrix of small tilted oval marks whose values darken into intersecting diagonal zones. Low overall contrast still creates directional fluctuation; a high-contrast black/white palette is not compulsory. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/93880).

- **op-art-06 — Zebres** (original, qualifying): Broad black/cream wavy stripes interlock into opposing zebra-like profiles, with the image boundary dissolving into a radiating striped field. A representational source is translated through figure-ground ambiguity rather than excluded by a universal abstraction rule. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/59973).

- **op-art-07 — Blue and Green** (original, qualifying): A regular three-column array pairs circles, triangles and squares with adjacent green/blue color fields. Shape stays constant within a module while neighboring hue/value relationships change the apparent prominence of the interior forms. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/29176).

- **op-art-08 — Capella** (original, qualifying): Thin horizontal pale bands interrupt a black field, stepping outward and changing width into a central oval distortion. The lower half reverses much of the figure-ground relation, creating tension without a material gradient. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/23979).

- **op-art-09 — Untitled, from Constellation Suite** (original, qualifying): A two-part print combines an upper circle and lower square whose edges are interrupted by horizontal alternating bands. Thin pale margins around the dark rectangular field isolate the perceptual image from inscriptions. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/109835).

- **op-art-10 — Abstract Landscape** (original, excluded): Three large gray/white/black flat shapes suggest a horizon and landscape; no repeated optical field is present. Retain as a neighboring work by the same artist, not a qualifying optical-pattern example. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/58232).

- **op-art-11 — Composition with Abstract Figure** (original, excluded): Two large pale pink curving geometric planes sit on black and gray framing with apparent shallow relief. A related abstract composition, but insufficient optical repetition or interaction to count in this corpus. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/58231).

- **op-art-12 — Untitled, from Constellation Suite** (unresolved, excluded): Concentric circle and square panels resemble the two-part Constellation configuration in record 09, with a different line/interference state. Cross-record relationship is unresolved; exclude pending identification rather than assert a separate work or a confirmed duplicate. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/29137).

- **op-art-13 — Vega, from Album I** (original, qualifying): A black/white checker field compresses toward one point and swells around a second point. Small detached circles and diamonds interrupt the edges, while a deep black mat isolates the warped rectangle. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/151197).

- **op-art-14 — Markab, from Album I** (original, qualifying): Dense alternating vertical stripes expand into circles, diamonds, crosses and squared interruptions. Figure-ground changes along the height without a tonal gradient, producing multiple local focal points in a precisely bounded field. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/151207).

- **op-art-15 — Altai, from Album I** (original, qualifying): Tall narrow black blades break at staggered heights across a white field, some ending in small angled points. Large black margins and discontinuous edge alignment emphasize rhythm rather than literal object depth. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/151202).

- **op-art-16 — Cassiopée, from Album I** (original, qualifying): A matrix of black disks is altered by diagonal cuts into diamond-shaped voids. Two enlarged motifs above and one below the field show the relation between a module and the changing group pattern. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/151198).

- **op-art-17 — Gotha, from Album I** (original, qualifying): Three solid ovals above a horizontal striped field correspond to bulging and pinching distortions below. Black, gray and pale rectangular ground areas make the same stripe segments read at different depths. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/151199).

- **op-art-18 — Umbriel, from Album I** (original, qualifying): Black circles, squares, semicircles and narrow connectors interlock with white negative forms. Changing module sizes down the print switch figure and ground, with no continuous shading or modeled material. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/151203).

- **op-art-19 — Keiho, from Album I** (original, qualifying): A yellow-green planar construction has repeated short slanted slots with visible pale and dark edges. The slot angles reverse across a rectangular field, while large open squares above and projecting lower planes introduce actual shallow construction alongside optical pattern. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/151421).

- **op-art-20 — Betelgeuse, from Album I** (original, qualifying): A regular matrix of white disks on black narrows individual disks into ellipses along curved paths and into small squares at upper right. The gradual local changes imply surface deformation without shading. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/151205).

- **op-art-21 — Untitled** (original, excluded): Overlapping rotated black and pale squares fill the sheet without a controlled repeat or strong perceptual distortion. A useful earlier geometric-abstraction boundary, not automatically an Op Art example because of its artist. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/196132).

- **op-art-22 — Procion, from Album I** (original, excluded): One large square cut by circular voids sits above nine circular modules with square holes. Useful module studies, but excluded as a low-information boundary for this optical-effect corpus rather than treating all geometry as Op Art. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/151206).

- **op-art-23 — Oeta, from Album I** (original, qualifying): A central lattice of squares bends into sheared parallelograms and rotated clusters, while enlarged shapes repeat above and below. Black, white and gray swap roles across the composition, implying fractured depth. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/151200).

- **op-art-24 — Ixion, from Album I** (original, qualifying): An oblique lattice of black and white cells is interrupted by angular cutouts and dislocated modules. Edges are crisp but the field is deliberately unstable, challenging a rule that all Op Art must use a perfectly regular unbroken grid. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/151201).

- **op-art-25 — Keiho, mylar overlay, from Album I** (duplicate, excluded): The yellow slotted Keiho mylar layer repeats the same underlying square openings and slanted-slot field inspected in record 19, now isolated. Count the work once; retain this view for construction detail. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/151422).

- **op-art-26 — Bora, from Album I** (original, qualifying): Paired thin vertical pale lines step sideways and change width at staggered levels on black. Alternating continuity and breaks create small raised-looking rectangular zones without a smooth modeled gradient. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/151204).

- **op-art-27 — Red, White and Blue, from 1776 USA 1976: Bicentennial Prints** (original, qualifying): Concentric thin blue rectangular lines repeat over a large red field, enclosing a pale rectangular middle zone and a smaller blue center. Saturated adjacent hues intensify the apparent depth while broad margins keep the artwork bounded. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/94098).

- **op-art-28 — Diamond Chroma, from New York Ten** (original, qualifying): Thin green nested diamond lines and blue diagonal fan lines meet at common points over a red-orange field. Spacing compression at the corners creates directional depth; a green perimeter reinforces the color interaction. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/122556).

- **op-art-29 — Pénétrable de Chicago** (original, qualifying): An overhead rectangular frame suspends a dense curtain of vertical translucent rods or cords into a gallery. Visitors are partially occluded within the repeated field. This still documents physical depth and occlusion, not measured motion or a flat printed pattern. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/38094).

- **yale-01 — Movement in Squares** (original, qualifying): Alternating black/white checker rows compress horizontally into a narrow crease right of center, then expand. Square cells become slender rectangles while row heights stay stable; no drawn shadow is needed for the apparent fold. [Source](https://bridget-riley.publications.britishart.yale.edu/catalogue/1/).

- **yale-02 — Horizontal Vibration** (original, qualifying): A wide horizontal strip uses black bands that vary in thickness and gently undulate. Several compressed light/dark seams cross the field at different heights, creating wavering strata in an otherwise flat monochrome rectangle. [Source](https://bridget-riley.publications.britishart.yale.edu/catalogue/2/).

- **yale-03 — Off** (original, qualifying): Nested angular black/white chevrons converge toward a point at the right edge. Their elbows migrate diagonally and the bands narrow toward the point, creating directional compression without a centered radial tunnel. [Source](https://bridget-riley.publications.britishart.yale.edu/catalogue/3/).

- **yale-04 — Uneasy Centre** (original, qualifying): Black and white circular bands have displaced centers and strongly unequal spacing. Their pinched right-hand zone contrasts with broad left crescents; the outer boundary is roughly circular, but the apparent swirl is built into a stationary drawing. [Source](https://bridget-riley.publications.britishart.yale.edu/catalogue/4/).

- **yale-05 — Broken Circle** (original, qualifying): Thin circular outlines are broken at three radial seams and shifted into stepped sectors. Densely grouped lines alternate with wide white bands; the line clusters and discontinuities, rather than solid filled rings, destabilize the circular form. [Source](https://bridget-riley.publications.britishart.yale.edu/catalogue/5/).

- **yale-06 — Climax** (original, qualifying): Dense black/white zigzag lines fill a concave-sided vertical field. Zigzag elbows align into several wavy vertical seams; changing spacing and slope produces a stretched middle and flared ends without gradients. [Source](https://bridget-riley.publications.britishart.yale.edu/catalogue/6/).

- **yale-07 — Shuttle 2** (original, qualifying): Diagonal stripe segments meet in zigzag seams across a broad convex-sided field. Seam spacing varies across the width, and several reverse directions near the edges; the perimeter also participates in the apparent bulging surface. [Source](https://bridget-riley.publications.britishart.yale.edu/catalogue/7/).

- **yale-08 — Suspension** (original, qualifying): A roughly square field has opposing curved upper/lower boundaries and alternating diagonal black/white segments. Multiple zigzag seams pack tightly toward the lower edge, suggesting a suspended bowed surface while remaining flat. [Source](https://bridget-riley.publications.britishart.yale.edu/catalogue/8/).

- **yale-09 — Blaze 4** (original, qualifying): Concentric angular bands form a circular field around an off-center low white opening. Alternating diagonal line directions create many sharp sawtooth seams and an apparent spiral; this differs from rotating symmetric plain rings. [Source](https://bridget-riley.publications.britishart.yale.edu/catalogue/9/).

- **yale-10 — Current** (original, qualifying): Fine black/white vertical waves bend into tightly compressed horizontal zones near the upper middle and lower middle. The repeated lines continue through the bands, producing rippled apparent depth across an unframed rectangular field. [Source](https://bridget-riley.publications.britishart.yale.edu/catalogue/10/).

- **princeton-01 — Physichromie 1772** (original, qualifying): A deep black square frame encloses a dense vertical sequence of colored fins. Teal, orange, red and dark narrow strips combine into broader subtle rectangular color zones; real relief and material spacing are visible. This single frontal photograph establishes construction, not the actual change with walking angle described by the museum. [Source](https://artmuseum.princeton.edu/art/collections/objects/138201).

## Historical / Origin Notes

The conventional movement frame centers the 1960s, but Riley's 1961 works and Vasarely's earlier experiments precede the label. A later Cruz-Diez relief demonstrates continuity beyond that decade, not a claim that every geometric artwork is Op Art. Museum attribution supplies historical dates; visual resemblance alone does not establish origin or influence.

## Notable practitioners, artifacts, regions, or institutions

Riley's *Movement in Squares*, *Current* and *Nineteen Greys* test compression, repetition and tonal variation. Vasarely's *Vega* album and slotted *Keiho* construction test changed modules and real relief. Anuszkiewicz's nested colored lines test hue adjacency; Soto's *Penetrable de Chicago* extends repeated optical fields into a room. Cruz-Diez's *Physichromie 1772* anchors a later material/viewpoint variant.

## Analysis

The reliable core is a designed perceptual effect produced by relationships among repeated forms, rather than geometry alone. Earlier abstract landscapes and unrelated square arrangements were excluded despite artist attribution. The updated entry should preserve precision and figure-ground instability while relaxing unsupported universals about high contrast, pure abstraction, flatness and animation.

### Application translation (proposed)

Use an original manually adjusted line field as a bounded perceptual experiment. Keep readable labels and navigation outside it. Changes in density, bend and palette should affect the construction, rather than rotating a symmetric circle with little perceptible change. A neutral sans and immediate changes are contemporary application choices. Offer a blank resting state and moderate densities; no automatic animation is necessary. These choices do not certify comfort for every viewer.

## Connections

`suprematism` shares abstraction but has a different cultural and expressive program; `de-stijl` shares planar geometry without making retinal instability its defining function. `psychedelic` overlaps in visual intensity but also carries organic lettering and countercultural contexts absent from many inspected optical works. Optical and kinetic practices can intersect without becoming interchangeable labels.
