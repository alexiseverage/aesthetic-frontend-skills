# Pochoir Fashion Plates

A specific illustrated fashion-publication language associated here with the 1920s: elongated couture silhouettes, carefully separated color areas and linework, framed narrative scenes, quiet paper margins, and captioned plate hierarchy. Pochoir is a stencil-coloring process, not a synonym for Art Deco or every illustrated fashion print. The entry names the fashion-plate application rather than claiming all pochoir decoration has one visual identity.

## Source / Evidence Links

- [Smithsonian Libraries: Vibrant Visions introduction](https://sil.si.edu/ondisplay/pochoir/intro.htm) — stencil coloring, watercolor/gouache, skilled hand production and applications beyond fashion. Crisp color and tactile craft are process context, not proof that every photographed print has identical inks or edges.
- [USC Libraries: 1920s fashion images](https://libraries.usc.edu/article/1920s-fashion-images-usc-libraries-special-collections) — Vogel's journal, collaborating illustrators/couture houses and a later collection of 1920–22 plates. Its Art Nouveau/Art Deco connections support overlap, not equivalence.
- [RISD: Gazette du Bon Ton, 1921 number 1](https://digitalcommons.risd.edu/specialcollections_periodicals_gazzettedubonton/1/) — publication ran 1912–15 and 1920–25; an institutional periodical context distinct from current digital mood boards. PDF download timed out; it adds no inspected media.
- Individual [Rijksmuseum](https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/collection/object/Gazette-du-Bon-Ton-Art-Modes-Frivolites-1920-3e-Annee-No-1--d5d535c8e39c993a177c2f05fe742703), NYPL and Smithsonian records below establish titles, dates, collection provenance and source-specific medium/rights notes. Rijksmuseum metadata was read on its institution-donated Commons file pages, with stable institutional handles retained.

## Direct Media Audit

Thirty-three linked images were opened individually. Thirty-one complete legible reproductions include thirty qualifying unique works and one excluded non-fashion Gazette illustration. Two additional USC article images were only 360 pixels wide and are excluded as small discovery reproductions. No unresolved duplicate candidates or duplicate versions are counted. The floor of ten and working target of thirty are achieved. No video or contemporary website is counted; no reference was republished as an application asset.

Qualifying sources: twenty-eight Rijksmuseum works (institution-donated scans accessed through Commons), one NYPL plate, one Smithsonian plate. These are three independently useful collections, but the sample is heavily concentrated in one publication and one collection: mostly Gazette du Bon Ton 1920–21, one 1923 example, and a clearly labeled 1914 Journal des Dames et des Modes precursor. It cannot characterize every artist, fashion periodical, pochoir print or worldwide stencil tradition. One precursor is continuity evidence, not a false 1920s date claim. Duplicated Hésitation, Mirage and Un peu beaucoup impressions were skipped at discovery; Croquis pages were not automatically treated as stencil-colored plates.

Rijksmuseum accessible originals range from 673×978 to over 5000 pixels tall. The small but complete Le jardin de l'infante scan supports broad composition and color, not microscopic stencil-edge analysis. NYPL's largest exposed free standard image is 575×760; Smithsonian's complete annotated presentation is 1000×721. Color descriptions are estimates from displayed reproductions, affected by paper age, lighting and scan differences. Exact pigment values, historical font identities and UI behavior are unknown. Image URLs retain the inspected file path with tracking parameters removed. All records are linked analysis; no verified local reference downloads resulted from the unsuccessful RISD attempt.

## Dimension Synthesis

| Dimension | Observed grammar / claim map | Confidence and limits |
|---|---|---|
| Palette | Paper-like ground, selective opaque-looking accents and line/flat-fill separation: P03, P05–P06, P09, P14, P18, P23, P25–P29. | High for selective color, moderate for material appearance, low for universal hue. | Type | Centered uppercase serif plate titles, smaller garment credit, detached corner issue/date metadata and fine framing: P02–P10, P14–P21, P23–P30. P11/P12/P16 integrate hand-shaped lettering. | High for sampled page hierarchy; no exact historical typeface established. The Smithsonian precursor includes italic display, not mandatory capitals. |
| Texture | Thin contour, hatching, dense woodcut marks, pattern inside garment silhouettes and separated flat areas coexist: P03–P04, P06–P07, P11–P17, P25/P28. | High for visible contrast, moderate for universal stencil diagnosis. Records specify mixed print/pochoir methods for many works; P01 only says printing. Process cannot be assigned from appearance alone. No global yellowing/noise requirement. |
| Shape | Elongated figures, bell/column/cape silhouettes, ribbons, scalloped hems and geometric/organic ornament: P03/P06/P09/P14/P17–P21/P23–P31. | High within the fashion sample. Garment silhouettes vary; no one body proportion, pose or costume is compulsory. |
| Motion | Static staged poses and implied wind/dance in P17/P31. | Historical still plates establish no animation timing, transition or interaction. Intentional stillness is appropriate. Any UI motion is a contemporary proposal. |
| Spatial | Framed scene or isolated figure, broad paper margin, anchored baseline and separate captions: P02–P11/P13–P21/P23–P31. Dense scenes and near-empty grounds both appear. | High for page structure, moderate beyond this publication. P12's large lower title is a useful exception. Responsive breakpoints are proposed. |
| Cultural markers | Couture houses, illustrator signatures, credited clothing, journal issue/plate numbers, hand production and aspirational social scenes. P08/P15/P19 reveal appropriated or colonial naming/staging; P05/P26 complicate cheerful luxury. | High for metadata-backed fashion-publication context; moderate for connotations. Do not generalize Parisian elite taste into a neutral global 1920s culture. |

## Analysis

**Classification:** a separately useful fashion-publication application of a documented print process, related to Art Deco and Art Nouveau but not interchangeable with either. It combines credited couture presentation, selective hand-colored planes and editorial plate hierarchy. A stencil pattern book, contemporary fashion photograph or generic gold geometric luxury site does not automatically qualify.

**Non-negotiable grammar:** intentional contrast between fine line and color shapes; a composed silhouette/ornament relationship; and publication-like separation of scene, title and attribution. Quiet margins are common, not a ban on dense artwork. Paper aging and elite imagery are historical contingencies, not requirements.

**Application proposals:** preserve fine rules, ink-on-paper reading surfaces and detached captions in product configuration, numbered edition lists, swatch controls, editorial features and order summaries. Let a selection change both a substantive product detail and its colored plate treatment. Use plain labels, robust focus outlines, adequate targets and direct validation. Keep display ornament out of form fields and avoid claiming a new web shop is an authentic period interface.

**Unknowns:** exact inks and fonts, physical stencil variations, worker attribution beyond available records, all-page publication rhythm, original saturation, interaction, mobile behavior and motion timing. No statistics here describe the whole movement. Self-review checked duplicate plate titles/impressions, medium claims, chronological scope and cultural boundaries; no independent reviewer is claimed.

## Connections

- `art-deco`: overlaps in couture presentation, elongated geometry and decorative modernity, but pochoir also colors organic/woodcut/softly modeled scenes.
- `art-nouveau`: flowing contour and floral ornament persist without defining every 1920s plate.
- `swiss-international`: a contrast for rational information hierarchy; plate metadata is precise but image-led and ornamental.
