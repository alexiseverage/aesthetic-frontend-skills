# Animal-Rights Campaign Graphics

## Scope and classification

A descriptive advocacy-communication cluster in which an animal subject, an ethical demand or editorial argument, campaign attribution and an action/reading path form a clear hierarchy. The evidence spans photographic posters, typographic quotes, editorial covers and campaign banners. Rights, welfare and conservation are distinct positions; the entry does not flatten every organization into a single movement or prescribe shock imagery.

This is a new canonical entry, screened against current catalog slugs, labels, aliases, redirects and subset names. Punk-zine can lend activist print cues but does not define the whole corpus. Wartime propaganda has a different institutional and historical context. Plant-based challenger graphics organize food commerce; animal-rights graphics organize an ethical claim and response.

## Dimension Synthesis

### Palette

High subject/text contrast rather than one universal palette: blue/white, black/red, colored quote ribbons and calm editorial surfaces all appear. Accent color can isolate the action without covering the subject.

### Type

Large claim headlines, compact campaign attribution and calmer explanation. Condensed sans and strong serif editorial/quote systems both occur. Do not treat black condensed capitals as the only authentic voice.

### Texture

Animal photography, documentary/campaign stills and clean text fields predominate. Editorial illustration and page layouts provide another branch. Gore, distress and distressed print are not requirements.

### Shape

Poster rectangles, strong split panels, portrait crops, colored ribbons and editorial cover grids. The subject should remain readable as an individual rather than a generic paw-print icon.

### Motion

The corpus is still posters, pages and banners; it does not establish animation conventions. Intentional stillness gives the subject and argument time. Disclosures and local form states can change immediately without animated suffering or emotional countdowns.

### Spatial

An encounter with the subject followed by a claim, explanation and route to act or read. Some posters rely on a single large statement; longer campaign pages need room for evidence and accessible action details.

### Cultural markers

Campaign organization, issue, ethical position and a clear action. Speciesism, farming, captivity and marine harm appear as different issues; visual similarity does not make their arguments interchangeable.

## Analysis

### Claim-to-artifact support

Subject/claim/action hierarchy: AR01–06, AR08–21, AR22–24, AR28–31. Editorial branch: AR25–27. Typographic/palette alternatives across Elena Galan and Artful Union prevent a universal red/black condensed rule.

### Confidence and gaps

High confidence in subject/claim hierarchy, moderate in a cross-organization visual cluster. Motion is unobserved. Do not infer that rights, welfare or conservation positions are identical from a campaign image.

Core identity is limited to the supported traits in the canonical non-negotiables. Counterexamples have been incorporated into the guidance rather than discarded to make a uniform mood board. No still-derived exact motion timing, measured physical performance or unverified source claim is promoted to fact.

### Application handoff

Connotation mode: **contemporary advocacy application**. Artifact concept: **For Every Life campaign and interest form**. Signature visual unit: campaign page. Create a campaign editorial page with one original animal portrait, issue selectors, an evidence/explanation accordion and a local volunteer-interest form. Provide specific action choices, labeled fields and clear confirmation without pretending data was sent. On mobile, keep the subject, claim and action in a coherent sequence.

These are **proposed application tokens**, not sampled historical colors or measured timings. Background `#f3eee3`, surface `#ffffff`, text `#211f1a`, muted `#5c594f`, accent `#cb3c26`, success `#355744`, danger `#942c24`. Display `Impact, sans-serif` at `clamp(2rem,5vw,4.5rem)`, weight 700, line-height 1.02, tracking -0.035em; body `Arial, sans-serif` at 1rem/1.5, weight 400, tracking 0; labels 0.8125rem/1.3, weight 700, tracking 0.06em; numeric outputs use tabular figures at 1rem/1.3. Space 1–6: `4,8,12,20,32,48px`; radius `2px` for functional fields and `0` for panel edges (a circular dial/label retains its own geometry). Default state changes are instant; optional feedback `120ms ease-out` is a proposed web value, disabled under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`. No automatic decorative loop is required.

These tokens are a baseline for an original interface. Include the supported subject/identity, its information hierarchy and its operational/reading context. Avoid copied marks/art, generic palette swaps and inaccessible source-era constraints. The source references remain analytical; original procedural graphics or separately created images should carry the application.

### Accessibility and adaptation flags

Palette tokens do not certify interface contrast. Check actual foreground/background states, focus outlines, disabled controls and overlays; target WCAG AA text and non-text requirements. Body text should remain 16px or larger in the application. Responsive priority is content order and control usability, not a tiny scaled facsimile. Keep explicit text equivalents for graphs and decorative SVGs hidden from assistive technology when captions already describe them. Respect user zoom and narrow layouts.

Reduced motion: stillness or immediate state changes are valid. Any proposed motion is optional, interruptible and removed for reduced motion. Dark mode: retain the artifact's deliberate material palette within its scoped frame; adapt surrounding application chrome separately. Fatigue: limit the loudest texture/type to the defining field. Conflict rule: preserve the non-negotiables and task semantics before introducing a neighboring palette, font or transition.
