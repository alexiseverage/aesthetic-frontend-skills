# Mid-century Modern

## Source Summary

This revision inspected 45 complete object photographs or catalogued textile fragments and retained 41 distinct qualifying designs. Three repeated chair/table designs or close table-family variants were grouped; one medical precursor was excluded. One textile download failed. The previous profile's count of three referred to contextual web pages, not a verified visual corpus, and is not added to this count. The [media ledger](mid-century-modern/media-ledger.json) records actual versions, dimensions, accessions, rights and per-artifact observations.

- [Art Institute of Chicago collection](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/61579): 39 qualifying designs spanning furniture, textiles and ceramics. Museum-served 843px-wide full compositions make silhouettes, grain, weave and repeat relationships legible. A fragment is the complete catalogued artifact, not an invented view of its original room or bolt of fabric.
- [Vitra, Butterfly Stool](https://www.vitra.com/en-us/product/butterfly-stool): Sori Yanagi's 1956 design, inspected in a current production photograph. Manufacturer documentation identifies molded plywood and brass fittings. Its contemporary staged setting is not evidence of a 1950s interior.
- [Hato Press, Uno La Mela](https://hatopress.net/products/uno-la-mela-by-enzo-mari-50-x-70): current edition of Enzo Mari's 1963 design for Danese. The reduced everyday natural form supplies a graphic-print variant. It is not treated as a Risograph reference merely because Hato also publishes Riso work.

Collection stopped above the 30-work target after later objects/textiles continued to challenge the warm-wood, starburst-only stereotype. Concentration remains strong: 39 of 41 qualifying designs come from one museum, and US/UK furniture/textile designers dominate. Yanagi and Mari broaden the sample without making it representative of Japanese, Italian, Brazilian or Scandinavian modernisms. Architecture, original room arrangements, advertising and audiovisual behavior require further evidence. Artwork/design and photographic rights remain with their owners; no historical reference image is a cleared application asset.

## Dimension Synthesis

The following are qualitative findings within a purposive sample, not measured movement-wide percentages.

| Dimension | Core | Common | Variant / limit |
|---|---|---|---|

## Image Descriptions

Each description follows a complete individual inspection. Duplicate relations refer to underlying designs, conservatively grouping material variations where appropriate. No extra counts are assigned to secondary objects visible in one product photograph.

- **mid-century-modern-01 — Screen** (original, qualifying): A tall folding wood screen has repeated broad curved panels and pronounced vertical grain. The scalloped floor edge gives a severe rectangular object an organic rhythm; the adjacent chair is contextual and not an additional artifact count. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/61579).

- **mid-century-modern-02 — Lounge Chair and Ottoman** (original, qualifying): Black tufted leather cushions sit inside separate warm-grained bent shells. The reclined chair and matching ottoman use dark five/four-point metal bases; soft upholstery, exposed wood edges and metal coexist rather than imposing a single natural material. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/154489).

- **mid-century-modern-03 — Chaise** (original, qualifying): A very long low chaise uses a thin dark metal frame beneath several separate dark padded sections. One inclined end and two small raised cushions break its mostly horizontal profile; no wood or mustard/orange accent is visible. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/158242).

- **mid-century-modern-04 — Rocking Armchair (model RAR)** (original, qualifying): A pale molded bucket seat with continuous arms rests on a thin triangulated black-wire frame and two warm wood rocking runners. Material contrasts and negative space beneath the seat make the structure visually light. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/51406).

- **mid-century-modern-05 — Dining Chair (model DCW)** (original, qualifying): A molded wood chair separates rounded rectangular back and scooped seat on broad bent legs. Continuous honey-colored grain remains visible, with rounded edges and a slight outward splay rather than ornament. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/34080).

- **mid-century-modern-06 — Leg Splint** (original, excluded): A long molded wooden medical leg splint has large slotted openings and shaped edge cutouts. It helps explain a technical/material precursor, but is excluded from the domestic-aesthetic corpus rather than counted solely because it is by Eames. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/196168).

- **mid-century-modern-07 — Dining Chair (model DCW)** (duplicate, excluded): A second DCW example repeats the separate curved back/seat and bent legs, with changed grain and angle. Count this underlying chair design with record 05; different museum accessions do not create a new design. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/201743).

- **mid-century-modern-08 — Side Chair (model DKR)** (original, qualifying): A dark wire mesh chair shell and triangulated wire base are interrupted by one slim black seat pad. The regular grid and open edges carry industrial lightness; the silhouette stays curved without wood grain. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/51402).

- **mid-century-modern-09 — Lounge Chair (model LCW)** (original, qualifying): The low LCW lounge chair has a broad scooped seat, separate rounded back and bent splayed legs in visible warm wood. It is a separately identified lounge model from the taller dining DCW; the close design relationship is recorded rather than inferred from color. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/35305).

- **mid-century-modern-10 — Table (model LTR)** (original, qualifying): A low almost-square wood slab with rounded corners sits above a thin open metal-wire cross-braced base. The contrast between solid top and skeletal base creates lightness without a tapered wooden leg. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/93949).

- **mid-century-modern-11 — Coconut Chair** (original, qualifying): A bright red upholstered triangular curved shell forms the seat and back as one concave wedge, supported on slender three-point metal legs with diagonal braces. Saturated color is concentrated in the functional body rather than used as arbitrary background decoration. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/105108).

- **mid-century-modern-12 — Action Office Wall-Mounted Desk** (original, qualifying): A wall-mounted desk combines two vertical rails, dark side panels, a shallow upper shelf and a projecting warm wood writing surface. Repeated rails and compact storage make modular organization visible; the object is rectilinear, not kidney-shaped. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/158243).

- **mid-century-modern-13 — Basket Wall Clock (model no. 2215)** (original, qualifying): A large round woven basket-like clock face uses black dot hour markers, a dark center and contrasting pale hands. Visible open weave and concentric construction supply texture, while the time display avoids ornate numerals. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/236441).

- **mid-century-modern-14 — Platform Bench** (original, qualifying): A long pale wood bench uses closely spaced parallel slats crossed by three broad braces. Two black rectangular open-frame supports lift it above the floor; repeated straight lines and strong material contrast dominate. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/52273).

- **mid-century-modern-15 — Table** (original, qualifying): A transparent square glass tabletop with softened corners exposes a polished metal crossing framework below. Thin rods brace broader supports around a central joint; shiny industrial material is a legitimate variant of the warm wood stereotype. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/105106).

- **mid-century-modern-16 — Swag Leg Armchair** (original, qualifying): Two differently colored molded shell sections form an open-backed armchair. Pale seat and charcoal back have curved edges, while a central polished support divides into four bent legs; the open waist keeps the object light. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/103349).

- **mid-century-modern-17 — Settee** (original, qualifying): A deep blue rectangular upholstered settee has a straight long seat, slightly inclined back, one row of small buttons and thin polished legs. Large quiet planes and restrained details coexist with a saturated color field. [Source](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/105107).

- **mid-century-modern-18 — Tulip Armchair** (original, qualifying): A white continuous molded armchair shell narrows into a flared pedestal foot, with one round red patterned cushion. - **mid-century-modern-19 — Womb Chair** (original, qualifying): A broad rounded armchair encloses separate seat/back cushions in brown-and-cream cowhide. Thin dark rod legs support the shell. ## Historical / Origin Notes

The existing entry's postwar 1945–1970 frame remains useful; several designs have earlier roots or later production dates. The ledger distinguishes design from manufacture/reproduction. A newly photographed classic is evidence of that design's geometry, not automatically a surviving period print or interior. Vitra credits Yanagi's 1956 stool and its relation to molded-plywood practice; Hato identifies Mari's 1963 Danese graphic. Avoid treating one national canon or one material as the origin of all postwar modern design.

## Notable practitioners, artifacts, regions, or institutions

Eames molded shells and wire supports show multiple material routes; the medical splint is a technical precursor kept outside the qualifying count. Nelson's wall desk and slatted bench contribute modular/rectilinear structure. Day and Girard textiles contribute botanical abstraction, all-over repeat and graphic color. Zeisel ceramics offer expressive plain white curvature; Nakashima's woven seat and Yanagi's bent-shell stool demonstrate differing craft/industrial relations. Mari's apple print supplies an economical graphic silhouette.

## Analysis

The research supports functional clarity, proportion, visible materials and expressive structural economy. It does not support insisting that all authentic examples feel warm, have wood, use tapered legs or carry starbursts. A convincing digital interpretation should make relationships among surfaces, supports, modules and empty space visible. Material warmth can be one route; polished metal and pale molded surfaces need not become a different aesthetic automatically.

### Application translation (proposed)

A compact modular listening cabinet can translate functional compartments, a low horizontal silhouette, warm grain, woven speaker cloth and restrained graphic color. Real cabinet arrangements and finish choices should alter the object; do not rely on stock interiors. System sans typography, immediate state changes and specific CSS colors are application proposals. The cabinet need not reproduce a named designer's protected object or imply a real product for sale.

## Connections

`scandinavian-modern` overlaps in craft, use and material clarity; calling it simply paler or more democratic is too broad for this corpus. `warm-minimalism` can share restrained surfaces without the postwar structures or graphic motifs. `atomic-age` overlaps in selective orbital/starburst imagery; the platter is one specific example, while most inspected objects need no atomic symbol. `prairie-school` has a different historical and architectural frame.
