# Guilloché Security Printing

## Scope and Sources

Distinct process-derived graphic system in security-printed documents, with institutional use and a useful software translation. It is not all banknote design, all engraved illustration or machined metal guilloché. The narrow slug prevents an unsupported all-media or all-security umbrella.

The [Bank of Canada Museum's process activity](https://www.bankofcanadamuseum.ca/2020/07/the-art-of-guilloche/) identifies intersecting symmetrical designs and historical lathe/plate production. Its [Scenes of Canada account](https://www.bankofcanadamuseum.ca/complete-bank-note-series/1969-1979-scenes-canada-series/) explains the later open twisting geometry and multicolour treatment. [PWPW's technical descriptions](https://pwpw.eu/en/Competencies/Security_features.html) distinguish thin intercrossing guilloché lines from intaglio, microtext and other features. The [EUIPO guide](https://euipo.europa.eu/anti-counterfeiting-and-anti-piracy-technology-guide/marking-technologies/guillocherainbow-printing) places guilloché and rainbow printing in banknotes/documents/certificates; these can coexist without being synonyms. Its broad claims about reproduction resistance are not independently tested here and do not establish security of a web graphic.

The [Bank of England's primary account](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/quarterly-bulletin/2021/2021-q3/our-new-banknotes-the-journey-from-design-to-launch) documents changing production and reduced security value alongside continued visual familiarity. Its published Gooden trial is identified and directly viewed. Museum/central-bank denomination pages and credited National Numismatic Collection photographs supply artwork identity; visible observations are kept separate from production/attribution claims.

## Analysis

The first 1935 batch showed a stable portrait-left/title/counter hierarchy but varied corner and central geometries: softly lobed, octagonal, cross-like and joined fields. Repetition alone did not explain the aesthetic; fine-line density and nested outlines made distinct information zones. The Scenes batch established open/asymmetric fans, crossing colour fields and red/violet alternatives. British examples corroborated pale space and asymmetry, while the Gooden trial distinguished regular mesh from accompanying botanical engraving. The larger American originals resolved actual interlacing and different recto/verso distributions. Successive batches added important variants without contradicting the core definition; target and representative coverage are met within the stated geographic/media limits.

Non-negotiable: regular fine-line interlacing, shaped pattern fields/counters and a deliberate separation between ornament and identity. Common but optional: pale ground, display serif/numeral, formal labels, nested frames and local motif symmetry. Variant: multicolour open ribbons, extensive pale center, largely pictorial recto with patterned reverse, dramatic green numeral geometry or a pure trial rosette. Optional national portraits, legal wording, seals and stock floral ornament are not transferable requirements.
