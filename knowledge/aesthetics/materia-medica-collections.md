# Materia Medica Collections

A descriptive visual category for historical teaching and reference collections of medicinal raw materials: substances preserved in vessels, boxes or divided drawers, connected to names, numbers and provenance records. Its defining relationship is material specimen + deliberate containment + retrievable identification. It is neither a named contemporary movement nor a visual endorsement of past treatments. Botanical, animal and mineral materials can occur; a dried leaf illustration alone is insufficient.

## Sources and Context

- [Kew's Economic Botany Collection](https://www.kew.org/science/collections-and-resources/collections/economic-botany-collection) identifies a substantial historic materia medica holding within a wider economic botany collection. Its collecting history includes colonial trade; accession labels document institutional classification, not complete ownership or knowledge histories.
- [Kew teaching cabinet 73047](https://ecbot.science.kew.org/read_ecbot.php?catno=73047) documents reconstitution in 1976 and replacement of some contents from authenticated spares. Its photograph must not be described as an untouched nineteenth-century cabinet.
- [Queens' College, Vigani's Cabinet](https://history.queens.cam.ac.uk/college/buildings/viganis-cabinet) dates the oak cabinet to 1704 and collection assembly to 1704–1705, describes nearly 700 samples, wooden subdivisions and surviving documentation. Drawer images establish that mineral colors can be intensely bright.
- [Museum of Historical Medical Artifacts](https://mohma.org/instruments/category/pharmacy/materia_medica_chest/) estimates its mahogany teaching chest to 1880 and describes eight divided drawers with 306 samples and paper keys. The number is historical catalog metadata; the chest contributes one visual artifact here.
- [Worcester Medical Museums, Alan Mann's kit](https://medicalmuseum.org.uk/remedykit) describes animal, vegetable and mineral raw materials, numbered glass tubes and a lid contents list used in teaching. This is useful context; the inspected basal vial photograph was insufficiently informative to add another qualifying artifact.
- [Science Museum Group, kola specimen](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co182737/specimen-jar-containing-kola-nuts) connects its collected nuts to West African social practice and trade as well as European commercial exploitation. [Its sassafras record](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co193765/specimen-jar-containing-sassafras-bark) names Nanticoke knowledge and colonial exchange. Their context resists treating collected materials as anonymous exotic decoration.

Source-supported statements concern collection history, recorded materials and documented identification. Photographic observations below describe visible objects. Interface choices are explicitly inferred. Historical taxonomy, geographical wording and ingredient names can be outdated or uncertain; catalog presence does not establish efficacy, safety or current accepted identification.

## Analysis

### Claim map and boundary

Containment and indexing are non-negotiable relationships, rather than a particular vessel shape. MK-73047, MV-1704 and MH-1880 show distinct drawer systems; MK-56321 uses rectangular glass-front boxes; MS-co182737 uses a transparent lidded cylinder. The index can be adjacent paper, a base number or an external drawer key rather than a conspicuous vintage front label. MS-co183174 and MS-co183172 demonstrate that not every source view exposes readable identification; their catalog record supplies the connection.

The specimen remains visibly particular. Fibrous wormwood, flaky bark, granular powder, long pods, translucent gum and one cut block of fat must not become interchangeable generic leaves. The storage unit changes with that morphology. Natural irregularity inside repeated containers distinguishes this grammar from ornamental botanical borders and from the flat pressed-specimen plates of Autumn Field Guide Naturalism.

Colors come from the stored materials and supporting surfaces. MV-1704's minerals and MS-co183172's red shreds show why a blanket antique-brown filter would distort the corpus. Black photographic surrounds in several SMG originals are neither historical shelf paint nor a mandatory dark UI. Clear highlights can suggest glass, but full glossy glassmorphism would erase the label/material relationship. Later conservation wrapping and reconstructed contents should not be romanticized as original manufacture.

Collection metadata has limits. Unknown makers remain unknown. Where named, communities and knowledge holders belong in the record and the surrounding history, rather than being reduced to sourcing trivia. These holdings cannot support an origin claim that materia medica collections began in one country or century. The descriptive category spans a collection practice; no universal medical worldview or therapeutic validity is asserted.

### Frontend translation (inferred)

A museum collection journal can use a wide original specimen illustration beside a restrained accession column, followed by a provenance accordion and related specimen cards. A real category filter and record selection should update the visible material, title, ID and notes together. A saved-reading list or field-note form can provide a purposeful interaction without simulating prescriptions or treatment selection.

Proposed visual roles are warm paper, dark ink, neutral glass highlights, wood rules and one specimen-specific accent. Use readable book serif headings, conventional italic botanical names only when verified, compact sans metadata labels and tabular/mono IDs. These are contemporary design choices, not historical font identifications. Preserve material texture primarily inside original artwork; keep functional text backgrounds clean. A deliberate rectangular grid and thin rules can evoke compartments without rendering every paragraph as a corked jar.

Record headings should expose provenance status and uncertainty in plain language. Historical uses may be summarized as historical claims with sources, never as instructions for preparing or using remedies. Prefer a collection-reading task over a product purchase for substances. Date, donor and location must be sourced; original demonstration artwork must be labeled illustrative, not a digitized museum specimen.

Accordion buttons need programmatic expanded state and logical focus order; selection must not depend on color. Filter result counts should be announced and empty states useful. Notes forms need explicit labels, validation and clear local-save status. Mobile should preserve the specimen/label relationship while stacking metadata. Proposed motion is a short optional opacity or disclosure transition; reduced motion should show state changes immediately. No autoplay drawer movement is justified by the still corpus.

## Connections

- Autumn Field Guide Naturalism shares observation and identification, but organizes seasonal field evidence and illustrated/pressed specimens; this category centers physically contained raw materials and teaching/reference storage.
- Albarello Pharmacy Ceramics foregrounds painted ceramic volumes, glaze and decorative registers. A sealed raw-material reference vial or divided teaching drawer has a different design engine, even when both relate to historical pharmacy.
- Prescription Label Clarity centers current patient instructions and medication safety. Historical accession labels are not dosage labels and should not be adapted into clinical guidance.
- Spooky Science Lab centers classroom experiments and seasonal demonstrations. Materia medica collections document preserved materials; theatrical bubbles, hazardous procedures and glowing potion imagery obscure the distinction.
- Dark Academia can share wood and paper atmosphere, but a collection record needs material identity, containment and provenance rather than a library mood alone.
