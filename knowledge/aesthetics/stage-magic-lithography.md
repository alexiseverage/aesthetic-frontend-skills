# Golden-Age Stage-Magic Lithography

## Source / Evidence Links

- [McCord Stewart Museum, The Golden Age of Magic Posters](https://www.musee-mccord-stewart.ca/en/collections/documentary-art/the-golden-age-of-magic-posters/) — Institutional historical framing, named printers and cataloged works; the collection includes a film-related example excluded from the core corpus.
- [Library of Congress, Magic Poster Collection](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/95861316/) — Collection scope, production firms and print formats. Individual items and original scans are linked below.
- [The Henry Ford, All Out of a Hat](https://www.thehenryford.org/collections/explore/artifact/138670) — Independent object record identifies chromolithography, material and dimensions. Its circa-1928 date differs from McCord's circa-1924 attribution for a related design; no exact shared edition date is assumed.
- [State Library Victoria, Magic and Magicians: Images and Posters](https://guides.slv.vic.gov.au/magic/images) — Independent collection and reproduction-quality context. Some holdings are photographs of prints; collection membership does not establish full-image access or reproduction rights.

## Dimension Synthesis

The following separates direct visual observations from proposed web translation. Color descriptions and relative geometry are estimated from visual inspection; no corpus-wide numerical sampling or measured animation is claimed.

| Dimension | Synthesis / scope | Confidence and limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Palette | Cobalt/green atmospheric fields frequently oppose red, orange, yellow and cream display accents. Black with cream/red, muted olive, pale portrait fields and monochrome illustrations are real variants. Saturation is common; a universal blue/yellow palette is not supported. | High for the inspected visual sample |
| Type | Performer names may rise diagonally, arch, compress into a tall strip or use elaborate script. Outlines, contrasting inline faces and hard extrusions make letters pictorial. Act titles, endorsements and venue/date billing occupy separate tiers. Quiet portrait sheets and unlettered stock art prevent treating enormous lettering as universal to every individual sheet. | High for observed lettering treatment and hierarchy; individual font identities unverified |
| Texture | Continuous-tone lithographic shading, flat spot-like color areas, fine stippled/linear detail and ink-on-paper surfaces coexist. Visible aging belongs to the surviving artifact, not necessarily its original appearance. Use restrained print texture; brown distress is not the defining material. | High for the inspected visual sample |
| Shape | Frames, medallions, theatrical curtains, rising names, open cabinets and floating objects organize the promise of an illusion. A dominant portrait can coexist with small subordinate scenes. Curved vapour and repeated poses can join several actions inside one artwork. | High for the inspected visual sample |
| Motion | Still print implies levitation, appearance, vanishing and impossible transitions. The corpus establishes no historical animation timing. Contemporary disclosures and booking controls can update immediately; do not add compulsory flashing, autoplay or simulated danger. | Unknown historical behavior; still composition directly observed |
| Spatial | A large name or dramatic scene establishes the first read, with smaller act details and a stable billing zone. Some posters are narrow banners, others broad landscape scenes. Overlap and impossible frame crossings can structure the composition while controls remain predictable and legible. | High for the inspected visual sample |
| Cultural markers | Conjuring publicity, named performers, touring programs, lithographic printing firms, stage apparatus and theatrical claims of wonder provide context. Devils, spirits and occult props are advertising devices in many references, not evidence of supernatural events. Exoticizing costumes, racial caricatures and objectified assistants also occur and require critical treatment. | Medium; supporting context and variable examples |

## Direct Media Audit

**35 qualifying unique still artifacts**, individually inspected at accessible original/full-size resolution. Zero qualifying videos or interactive websites; zero generated images count as historical evidence. The 10-artifact floor and 30-artifact working target are met. Exact source, attribution, observed resolution, content hash and dimension observations are in the [linked media ledger](../media-audits/stage-magic-lithography.json). Only analytical links and observations are published, not the reference files.

The broader discovery pool contained 95 image leads, including repeated views, logos, thumbnails and unresolved candidates. These are not 95 analyzed works. 56 records received full-media inspection; 6 were explicitly marked duplicate views, and 74 files were retrieved for analysis. Retrieval itself contributes zero to the qualifying count.

The corpus is concentrated in McCord and Library of Congress holdings, with several printers and performers represented. The Henry Ford and State Library Victoria provide independently useful object/collection context rather than additional unique counted designs. Reprints, rearranged-title editions and color variants were grouped conservatively; a film poster, text-dominant broadsides, narrow scans with inadequate detail, and later spook-show material were excluded. Surviving paper damage and color-copy photography cannot establish original ink values. Still posters establish no performance or UI timing. Historical occult claims, exoticism, racial caricatures and gendered staging are documented as advertising context, not adopted as modern values.
