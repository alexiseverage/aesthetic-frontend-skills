# Shanghai Calendar Posters

A Shanghai-centered commercial print tradition with a 1920s–1930s peak and earlier lineages: modeled narrative/portrait scenes, business lettering, product emblems and carefully framed informational bands. Many surviving examples show actual calendars; some accessible poster reproductions show no date grid. The genre is broader than a glamorous woman in a qipao, and its circulation extends beyond Shanghai. It should not be renamed generic Art Deco, chinoiserie or all Chinese advertising.

## Direct Media Audit

Thirty-two complete-artifact media inspections yielded thirty-one qualifying unique still works and one excluded cosmetic-tin photograph. The minimum of ten and target of thirty are exceeded. Qualifying collections: twenty-eight FAAM works, one HKU work, two V&A works. No video, generated image, contemporary interface, gallery grid, preview or museum logo contributes to evidence. No reference image file was downloaded or reused as a showcase asset.

FAAM discovery covered fifty detail records from a 133-item genre pool; twenty-seven new selected images plus the separate Anker work were individually opened. Repeated brand names are not automatically duplicates: the selected Qidong, tobacco and dye advertisements have different compositions, poses, frames or products. The Anker work on Google Arts & Culture and FAAM record 3850 is one underlying work and was not counted twice. V&A and FAAM DuPont posters differ. HKU's Zhiguang portrait differs from the selected FAAM Xie works. No unresolved duplicate is counted.

FAAM complete accessible reproductions are 1200 px tall, ranging from 409 px-wide narrow sheets to 847 px-wide broad posters in the selected set. These are complete shaped originals in the collection, not enlarged gallery thumbnails; very small date numerals and microscopic ink analysis remain out of scope. Anker's 807×1200 full accessible image replaced a default 344×512 preview. V&A files were viewed at 2000 px tall. HKU's complete photographic reproduction was viewed in its full-screen original viewer (about 436×774 image on a 1512×944 screen), then zoomed for drapery, border and interior detail. Its native source dimensions are unknown and are not invented. The viewer watermark remained visible.

The corpus is dominated by FAAM's selection and often imprecise 1920–30s dates. It includes one 1913–14 precursor, several 1920s works, explicitly dated 1930s works and an HKU work whose individual date is broad. It is not an all-1930s-only sample, a full history of Shanghai, or proof that every genre object originated in one city. Fading, age, photographic color and source compression affect displayed palettes. Some reproductions may not retain an originally attached date element; absence in the available image does not establish absence in every historical edition.

## Analysis

**Classification:** a separately useful regional commercial-print vocabulary, not an alias of Art Deco or guochao. Calendar, image, brand and product relationships make it applicable to contemporary cultural calendars and hospitality/event booking. The broader Shanghai/Hong Kong network and individual date uncertainty remain explicit rather than turning all examples into precisely dated Shanghai-made posters.

**Non-negotiable grammar:** a defensible commercial-calendar reference; modeled narrative/illustrative content or a contemporary original counterpart; composed business/information hierarchy; and a deliberate relationship between image, frame, emblems and date/product modules. Not every example needs every module, but an ordinary card with an old portrait and red border is insufficient.

**Application proposals:** a cultural-event or tea-room calendar can use bordered date rails, a readable grid, a product-like event summary, typed business name and an original light-filled illustration. The calendar controls, form fields, selected dates, closed days and confirmation should share the line weights, cartouche rhythm and typographic hierarchy. Preserve modern semantics, direct bilingual wording and error feedback. Do not invent lunar dates or counterfeit historic brands. No historical website behavior is asserted.

**Review:** self-review checked repeated advertisers, different compositions, museum/studio attribution, medium versus reproduction, chronological uncertainty, absent calendars and the packaging boundary. This is not an independent review. All references are analysis links; access does not grant commercial asset reuse.

## Connections

- `art-deco`: overlaps in geometric framing, lettering and modernity, but the corpus also includes traditional narrative scenes, soft tonal illustration and distinct local commercial histories.
- `guochao`: contemporary heritage/national-style revival has a different era and community; it is not an alias for Republican-period advertising.
- `chinoiserie`: an external decorative construction is not interchangeable with these Chinese commercial networks and their source-specific imagery.
- `pochoir-fashion-plates`: shared illustrated fashion/commercial hierarchy, different process, page conventions and cultural lineage.
- `taisho-roman`: a useful comparison for regional modern/traditional mixtures, not a substitute Japanese identity for Shanghai work.
