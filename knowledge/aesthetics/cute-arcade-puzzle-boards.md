# Cute Arcade Puzzle Boards

## Scope and classification

This is a descriptive catalog category for a repeatable visual system, not the historical name of a movement. Its center is a bounded, cell-organized task surface linked to friendly, exaggerated character/object shapes and immediately readable game state. Printed package illustration is a complementary source layer, not a requirement that every control become a pixel sprite. The resulting system is separately usable from the existing pixel-art entry because native raster construction is not its only defining trait.

The corpus concentrates on Irem, HAL, Sega, Hudson, Atlus and Game Freak-era action puzzles. A Good Snowman supplies one explicitly labeled contemporary related variant: comparable board/actor/object separation without a claim of direct historical descent. Side-view Catrap is a spatial branch, not evidence that all examples are top-down. Flipull and Western Mendel Palace packaging are retained as boundary comparisons rather than used to fill the qualifying total.

## Source / Evidence Links

Context sources read independently of visual analysis:

- [HAL official Meikyu no Fukkatsu archive](https://www.hallab.co.jp/eng/works/detail/002761/): publisher, console, Japanese release date and original package/game media. It identifies this release as 1988-08-09; this is not the date of every Eggerland work.
- [Nintendo's Adventures of Lolo description](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Games/NES/Adventures-of-Lolo-276082.html): trap-filled rooms and puzzle objectives; the page's displayed 2007 date is a rerelease date, not an original NES publication claim.
- [Irem NES instruction booklet](https://www.retrogames.cz/manualy/NES/Kickle-Cubicle_-_NES_-_Manual.pdf): direct control, stage, collection, HUD and password documentation. Its illustrated pages were rendered and inspected as one booklet artifact.
- [AtGames on Plotting/Flipull](https://www.atgames.net/arcades/plotting/): licensed rerelease context and tile-puzzle description; genre membership does not make the inspected abstract Game Boy cover a positive aesthetic example.
- [Junichi Masuda, 2005 official Game Freak blog](https://www.gamefreak.co.jp/blog/dir_english/2005/01/index.html): a first-person account of Quinty's development and interaction-responsive music. This supports historical context, not a measured visual animation specification.
- [A Good Snowman official press kit](https://agoodsnowman.com/press/index.php): named creators, description and original animated demonstration. Its gentle contemporary rendering is distinguished from historical arcade output.

Individual archive metadata can be incomplete or misleading. The Kickle NES image gallery displays a date associated with the arcade era; the printed North American box explicitly carries 1990 copyright. Preserve platform distinctions and do not derive an entire chronology from a gallery heading. Individual illustrators remain unverified unless explicitly attributed by a reliable source; publisher credits do not identify an illustrator.

## Analysis

### The packaging correction

Kickle's North American package is not an icy-blue pixel screen enlarged onto cardboard. It combines a white/black grid with occasional green squares, glossy multicolor tied letters, a red inflated subtitle, and a tilted toy-world scene over warm gold tiles. The Japanese package reuses the same principal illustration with different title/framing; it is one duplicate group for analysis. The arcade flyer has distinct action art and a cream instructional reverse over a blue rounded-square grid. This directly grounds a packaging-led application without falsely universalizing one franchise's marks.

### A readable board inside an expressive world

Several native screens separate a rigid cell board from a compact HUD while allocating different geometry to the avatar, obstacles and objective. This relation survives palette and camera changes. Lolo's brick rooms, Pengo's blue field, Bomberman's obstacle grid, Catrap's side-view chamber and Quinty's decorated floor tiles are not the same game mechanic, but support a similar hierarchy. A component translation should make positions and selected states useful rather than merely drawing tiles behind unrelated content.

The original package layer is more varied than the screen layer: inflated or extruded lettering, airbrushed round bodies, thick contour, large foreground figures, tilted panels and receding floors are repeated options. Sparse labels and clear silhouettes carry the system more reliably than a prescribed font. Use a neutral readable body face and reserve the expressive title for a short identity moment.

### Claim map and implementation limits

- Bounded board + actor/object separation: 07,11,13,16,22,25,27,30,33,36,38–40. High confidence.
- Smooth illustration can coexist with pixel game logic: 01/39,06/07,09/11,12/13,15/16,20/22,24/25,29/30,31/33,35/36. High confidence; no assertion that every pair has the same art director.
- Glossy inflated lettering is a useful branch: 01,20,29. Moderate confidence across three packages, not mandatory for every example.
- White grid with green interruptions is specifically Kickle's North American packaging: 01/02, not a universal arcade convention.
- Feedback stays connected to board state: 38–40. Moderate motion confidence; sampled capture timing cannot certify native FPS or ease curves.

Proposed web values are application choices, not historical measurements: cream paper, dark navy ink, saturated blue/red/green, pale cyan ice and gold highlights; strong cell rules; larger rounded illustration panels; short optional stepped transitions. Controls require visible focus, labels, text/shape selection cues and reduced-motion immediate updates. A game-like booking interface must let the user complete the task without solving a puzzle or beating a timer.

### Rights and cultural handling

Historical materials are analysis-only links. Build new characters, lettering, landscapes, icons and copy. Neither a successful download nor an archive listing grants reuse rights. A publisher press kit supports analysis and identified media access; it does not clear copying its characters into an unrelated public showcase. Exact fonts, illustrator identities, original animation timings and printing methods remain unknown where not established.
