## Historical / Origin Notes

Futurism emerged in Italy in the early twentieth century and aimed to capture the dynamism of the modern world, celebrating speed, technology, the industrial city, and a militant break with the past.

## Notable practitioners, artifacts, regions, or institutions

- Filippo Tommaso Marinetti, Umberto Boccioni, Giacomo Balla, Fortunato Depero, and Futurist manifestos are the central anchors.

## Analysis

Futurism aestheticizes force: speed, machinery, shock, and rupture become the core compositional engine across image, type, and spatial direction.

Non-negotiables for implementation: directional velocity; high-contrast industrial palette; dynamic/fragmented composition; explicit machine-age thrust.

Cultural sensitivity assessment: High context risk. Italian Futurism is entangled with militarism, misogyny, and later fascist associations. Use historical framing carefully and avoid celebratory political flattening.

## Connections

`constructivism` shares revolutionary modern energy but redirects it toward social utility rather than velocity worship; `atomic-age` later commercializes technological optimism for consumer culture; `vorticism` adapts related machine-age dynamism within a British avant-garde context.
