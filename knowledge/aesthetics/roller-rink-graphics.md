# Roller Rink Graphics

Local roller-rink promotion organized around memorable venue lettering, skate or skater emblems and practical session information. Historic die-cut decals are a center of gravity; programmes, membership cards and current flyers are related variants. This is broader than disco styling and narrower than all roller-skating imagery.

## Source / Evidence Links

- [Indiana Historical Society — Rolling Down Memory Lane](https://indianahistory.org/blog/rolling-down-memory-lane/): Collection context and three directly inspected original decal scans.

## Analysis

**Palette**: Small spot-color groups recur in historical decals: blue/red, yellow/blue, green and black. Current posters add saturated pink and violet. Age-yellowed support is not evidence of an original cream ink palette.

**Type**: Venue names dominate in outlined capitals, condensed display faces, scripts or custom skate-shaped lettering. Location, session, price and invitation copy form secondary tiers. No single font or disco face is universal.

**Texture**: Printed paper, halftones, die-cut boundaries and occasional embroidery in the archive; current digital poster surfaces vary. Keep readable type crisp rather than applying indiscriminate distress.

**Shape**: Winged quad skates, skater silhouettes, shields, circles, triangles and irregular die cuts. Not all works use skate emblems; some rely on oversized rink names or building imagery.

**Motion**: Inspected artifacts are stills. Speed marks and angled lettering imply movement without establishing timing. Proposed selection feedback may use 180 ms opacity changes; reduced motion switches immediately.

**Spatial**: A recognizable venue identity plus practical event hierarchy. Decals compress identity into a small badge; flyers use stacked day, session, admission and rental zones. Programme and newsletter layouts are editorial variants.

**Cultural markers**: Local leisure venues, collecting, club membership and social sessions. The corpus is mainly US material. Present-day inclusive wording is an application choice, not a claim that historic venues welcomed everyone.

The sampled language is venue-specific identity plus useful attendance information, not a mandatory disco palette. Flat printed badges differ from raised puffy stickers. Modern posters sometimes use rendered-looking imagery; they are evidence of published graphic composition, not evidence that a depicted scene happened. Alternate colors of the same badge or flyer are not independent exemplars.

## Connections

Atomic-age and pop-art can share energetic lettering and limited color, but rink graphics are anchored in venue identity and session communication. Puffy-sticker-albums share collectible contours while emphasizing raised material and album arrangement; these rink decals are primarily flat printed promotion.
