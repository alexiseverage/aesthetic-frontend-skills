# De Stijl / Neo-Plasticism

## Source Summary

This revision inspected 32 complete legible artifact reproductions and one excluded small preview, retaining 30 distinct qualifying works: 20 Mondrian paintings/study, six Van Doesburg paintings, one Rietveld chair and three journal cover designs. A later Rietveld Zig-Zag Chair is a related boundary, and a recurring journal masthead/template is conservatively counted once. The earlier profile used contextual text only; its count remains historical rather than being added to the new corpus. The [media ledger](de-stijl/media-ledger.json) retains actual image dimensions, hashes, sources, rights and individual observations. Reference images are analytical evidence, not cleared production assets.

- [Art Institute of Chicago, Counter-Composition VIII](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/64962), plus five other individually catalogued objects: primary accession and creator records; five qualify within the movement/Neo-Plasticist continuation, one later chair remains a boundary.
- [Thyssen, Composition XX](https://www.museothyssen.org/en/collection/artists/doesburg-theo-van/composition-xx): the museum dates the work 1920 and identifies the flexible uneven grid and softened primary palette. Its full image was inspected through a Commons reproduction traced to this museum.
- [Thyssen, Mondrian's Composition in Colours](https://www.museothyssen.org/en/collection/artists/mondrian-piet/composition-colours-composition-no-i-red-and-blue): primary 1931 object documentation explains the use of red and blue, intuitive balance and revisions under the surface. Exact color values remain reproduction-dependent.
- [Kunstmuseum, Counter-composition of dissonants XVI](https://www.kunstmuseum.nl/en/collection/counter-composition-dissonants-xvi): object 0332094, dated 27 August–30 November 1925. Its inspected diagonal lattice belongs to Van Doesburg's counter-composition branch.
- [MoMA, Theo van Doesburg and De Stijl](https://www.moma.org/slideshows/43/798): context for the journal's 1917–32 publication and its expansion into an international avant-garde review. Three inspected cover compositions are traced to journal reprints/catalogue metadata, rather than treating every issue number as a new design.
- [Centraal Museum's Rietveld/Schröder archive history](https://www.centraalmuseum.nl/tentoonstellingen/het-rietveld-schroeder-archief) credits close collaboration between Rietveld and commissioning resident Truus Schröder-Schräder. This contextual text is not an additional inspected house photograph or motion reference.

The corpus reaches the 30-work target while covering sparse and dense grids, direct adjoining planes, lozenge supports, counter-compositions, furniture and graphic design. It remains painting-heavy and overwhelmingly Mondrian/Van Doesburg. Other members, stained glass, architectural drawings and complete interiors need expansion. Commons hosts many museum-derived or visitor photographs; original accession/catalogue metadata and supplied photo dates are separated where available, but several records retain only a hosted catalogue attribution. Rights labels describe the host's assertions; museum reproduction policies may differ. No source image is shipped, and no timed motion or historical interactive behavior was inspected.

## Dimension Synthesis

These are qualitative observations within a purposive corpus, not movement-wide prevalence statistics.

| Dimension | Core | Common | Variant / limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Palette | Planar relations of limited colors and neutrals organize space. | Red, blue, yellow, black, white and gray; large pale areas with sparse accents. | A single blue, yellow or red field can suffice. Van Doesburg's softened green-gray/lavender/ochre planes refute a mandatory set of exact bright primaries. |
| Type | Graphic type participates in composition and hierarchy. | Journal covers use sans-serif title blocks, overlapping NB/DE STIJL, rotated labels and unequal scales. | The anniversary cover includes dense serif text; the Aubette title is diagonal. A blanket prohibition on all serif or diagonal type is unsupported. Exact fonts remain unidentified. |
| Texture | Flat painted/printed planes avoid modeled pictorial depth. | Brush direction, canvas grain, paper and print registration remain visible. | Cracks, worn frames and scan stains are object/reproduction history, not required distressed styling. Literal three-dimensional furniture has material depth. |
| Shape | Reduced geometric relations and unequal planar divisions. | Horizontal/vertical rectangles and black bars in Neo-Plasticist painting; open crossing members in the Red-Blue Chair. | Some colored planes meet without black separators. A diamond support can contain upright bars; later Elementarist interior diagonals are a separate branch. |
| Motion | Still artifacts establish no digital timing, easing or interaction grammar. | Intentional stillness is a valid translation. | Immediate manual modular changes are proposed application behavior, not an observed 1920s interface. |
| Spatial | Asymmetrical equilibrium and active intervals, including open space. | Large pale planes, unequal divisions and off-center crossings. | Dense early grids and solid black masses also occur. Architectural spatial movement needs direct architectural media, not inference from paintings. |
| Cultural markers | Specific Dutch interwar abstraction and a program of universal relation. | Mondrian, Van Doesburg, Rietveld, the journal and links among art/design/architecture. | Neo-Plasticism and De Stijl are closely related but not identical in membership, dates or rules; later Mondrian continuations remain relevant after the group's peak. |

## Historical / Origin Notes

The Netherlands, 1917 and the De Stijl journal remain the historical starting frame. The movement connected painting with a broader social/spatial ideal. Van Doesburg's later diagonal experiments produced a real disagreement with Mondrian; they should neither be erased from De Stijl's history nor retroactively imposed on Mondrian's orthogonal Neo-Plasticism. The journal continued through a 1932 memorial issue, and Mondrian's own relevant practice continued into the 1940s. For architecture, credit the commissioning and collaborative role of Truus Schröder-Schräder alongside Rietveld, rather than attributing every decision to one male author.

## Notable practitioners, artifacts, regions, or institutions

Mondrian's single-color accent paintings show that all three primaries are not obligatory. The 1918 Houston grid offers a dense early variant. Van Doesburg's Composition XX and XXII show adjoining softened planes without a universal black lattice, while Counter-composition XVI establishes interior diagonal relations. Rietveld's Red-Blue Chair exposes crossing members and projecting ends. Journal covers translate scale, position and direction into graphic hierarchy rather than merely repeating a framed painting.

## Analysis

Reduced planar relationships, asymmetrical equilibrium and a historically situated search for order remain defining. A generic equal-cell red/yellow/blue grid is insufficient. White space can act as a plane, and a black block can outweigh multiple small accents. Differences among Neo-Plasticist painting, Van Doesburg's Elementarist branch, journal typography and three-dimensional design should stay visible instead of becoming one inflexible checklist.

### Application translation (proposed)

An editorial index can translate unequal planes, black rules, a restrained primary palette and type as spatial material. Accessible disclosure panels should expose useful article content and a reading queue should change visibly. A custom geometric wordmark and abstract relational diagram can be original assets; no historic painting needs to be reproduced. Immediate states, particular CSS colors, type size and responsive reflow are contemporary proposals. Keep the core composition orthogonal rather than using diagonal confetti to suggest the later branch superficially.

## Connections

`bauhaus` shares modernist abstraction but has a different school/workshop history and wider material pedagogy. `suprematism` often floats geometric forms rather than balancing a continuous planar relation. `scandinavian-modern` has a different domestic/material emphasis. Generic primary-color minimalism and later commercial Mondrian styling do not automatically reproduce De Stijl's cultural program.
