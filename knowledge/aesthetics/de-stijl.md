## Historical / Origin Notes

De Stijl was founded in the Netherlands in 1917 and promoted a strict geometry of horizontals and verticals, aiming for a universal abstract order that could extend across painting, architecture, furniture, and graphic design.

## Notable practitioners, artifacts, regions, or institutions

- Piet Mondrian, Theo van Doesburg, Gerrit Rietveld, and the magazine De Stijl are the central anchors.

## Analysis

De Stijl reduces composition to pure relation: orthogonal line, planar color, asymmetrical balance, and a belief that abstraction can model universal order.

Non-negotiables for implementation: orthogonal geometry; pure planar color logic; asymmetrical balance; anti-ornamental abstraction.

Cultural sensitivity assessment: Low identity-risk but historically specific. De Stijl is not just “Mondrian colors”; it is a Dutch modernist philosophical program about universality and abstraction.

## Connections

`bauhaus` shares abstraction and modernist pedagogy but allows more material and typographic variability; `suprematism` also uses pure geometry but favors floating dynamism over grid-bound orthogonality; `scandinavian-modern` differs by reintroducing warmth, tactility, and domestic comfort.
