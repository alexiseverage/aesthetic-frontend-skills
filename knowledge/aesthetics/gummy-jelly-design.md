# Gummy / Jelly Design

Classification: **separately useful canonical visual system**, related to the neighbors below. A descriptive material-centered hybrid: soft-looking colored volumes, translucent or milky internal depth, thick softened edges and concentrated gloss evoke jelly or candy. The source objects include rigid glass and plastics as well as resin; appearance does not establish physical softness. This is not a verified single historical movement or a name shared by all cited makers.

## Analysis

A descriptive material-centered hybrid: soft-looking colored volumes, translucent or milky internal depth, thick softened edges and concentrated gloss evoke jelly or candy. The source objects include rigid glass and plastics as well as resin; appearance does not establish physical softness. This is not a verified single historical movement or a name shared by all cited makers.

Glassmorphism primarily treats translucent planes and background blur; this hybrid emphasizes rounded bodies. Claymorphism is usually opaque modeled volume. Clear conventional glass, matte rough resin and food subject matter alone were excluded. Historical jelly objects are material references, not proof of today’s label.

The non-negotiable formal traits are: Soft-looking rounded volume; colored translucent or milky depth; visible thickness/gloss cues; readable solid task hierarchy alongside material effects. They are qualitative conclusions from the inspected corpus, not a universal frequency claim. Palette descriptions are estimated by visual inspection; exact hex colors below are authored application choices. Physical images cannot establish software timing, responsive breakpoints or interaction states.

Application proposal: Build a flavor sampler with thick jelly quantity controls, selectable flavor segments and a real basket summary. A pressed state can compress briefly, but text remains stable. Tint overlap belongs in borders and decorative lobes; error/help text uses opaque surfaces.

### Sampling limits and contradictory examples

Corpus is weighted toward physical objects from Helle Mardahl and Fish Design; two creator-led graphic projects and one live website support translation, not a universal UI canon. Behance/Dribbble access failures, small product previews, generic transparent objects and unclear AI examples were excluded. Ploy website assets’ production method is unknown; it is an observed published application. No exact deformation timing was measured.

Excluded and variant records remain visible above to constrain the boundary. Their presence is not a failed attempt to force a single palette, level of ornament or content subject. Specific sacred/personal symbols, brand marks, figures and copyrighted compositions are not reusable design assets.
