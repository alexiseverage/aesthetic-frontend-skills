## Historical / Origin Notes

Die Neue Typographie is associated with Jan Tschichold’s late-1920s typographic modernism, which argued for sans-serif, asymmetrical, standardized layouts that treated typography as a rational communication system rather than historical ornament.

## Notable practitioners, artifacts, regions, or institutions

- Jan Tschichold, modernist posters and books, and later corporate/public information systems are the key anchors.

## Analysis

Die Neue Typographie turns typography into modern information engineering: asymmetrical, standardized, and stripped of historical display habits in favor of legibility and hierarchy.

Non-negotiables for implementation: hierarchy-first typography; asymmetrical grid logic; restrained palette; sans-serif modernist discipline.

Cultural sensitivity assessment: Low identity-risk but historically precise. The movement belongs to specific interwar debates about standardization, modern life, and print reform; avoid collapsing it into generic clean UI type.
