# Neo-Geo

## Scope and source context

A historically situated, loosely associated 1980s New York art tendency, not a universal geometric style and not the SNK game system. A usable frontend translation needs a relationship between rigorous organization, conspicuously manufactured color/surface, and the critical treatment of systems, signs or commodities. The geometric painting branch is particularly implementable, but cannot stand for every artist associated with the name.

**Source-supported classification:** [Getty AAT](https://www.getty.edu/vow/AATFullDisplay?find=&logic=AND&note=work&subjectid=300265635) locates the group in mid-1980s New York and explicitly includes both flat bright painting and consumer-product sculpture. Its alternate terminology is an indexing aid, not proof that every form of Neo-Conceptualism is identical to Neo-Geo.

**Source-supported boundary:** the [MIT Press description of Amy Brandt's *Interplay*](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262027533/interplay/) calls the artists loosely associated and explains that geometry characterized only part of their output. This research read the publisher's description, not the full book. Consequently it uses neither a unified manifesto nor one compulsory palette as a movement-wide rule.

**Source-supported artist context:** [Gagosian's Bickerton biography](https://gagosian.com/artists/ashley-bickerton/) documents the 1986 Sonnabend exhibition with Halley, Koons and Vaisman, while noting Bickerton's closer identification with Commodity Art and Neo-Conceptualism. Its descriptions of carrying-case structures agree with G21/G27/G28. His later Bali work is not silently added to the period sample.

**Source-supported meaning:** [MoMA's Red Cell with Conduit label](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/143627) interprets cells/conduits as a diagram of material or information flows and identifies acrylic, Day-Glo acrylic and Roll-a-Tex. [Mudam's 1980s retrospective](https://www.mudam.com/whats-on/exhibitions/conduits-paintings-from-the-1980s) relates Halley's diagrams to urban infrastructure and technological social organization. The [Smithsonian oral-history search record](https://www.aaa.si.edu/collections/interviews/oral-history-interview-peter-halley-22100) corroborates the artist's account of isolation and mediated connections; full-page retrieval failed, so the interview was not counted as fully read or as media.

The [Hirshhorn's Brand New exhibition](https://hirshhorn.si.edu/exhibitions/brand-new-art-commodity-1980s/) supplies the wider advertising/commerce setting. It covers many artists outside Neo-Geo and is contextual evidence, not a membership list. No market prices or claims about universal audience reactions are adopted.

## Direct Media Audit

Thirty qualifying unique complete still artifacts: 20 Halley paintings, four Taaffe paintings, four Bickerton works and two Koons sculptures, across five independently useful source collections. All were individually opened at original/useful full resolution and inspected. No thumbnails, search cards, contact sheets, mirror versions or generated application assets count. Zero downloaded reference files; link-only analysis. The audit has no unresolved duplicate groups or exclusions.

The 20 Halley works establish the painting grammar and quiet-palette exceptions. Four Taaffe works add quotation, optical repetition and curved collage; four Bickerton works add signs, lettering and packaged objects; two Koons works add clinical display and transformed commodities. This meets the 30-item working target while explicitly retaining the artist distribution bias. Additional later-career pages and alternate photographs of these same sculptures were discovery material and are not additional works.

The two similarly named Halley circulating-conduit paintings are different dated canvases with reversed field relationships, not edited versions of one photograph. Bickerton's MoMA self-portrait and the Gagosian #2 differ in ground, logo arrangement, side construction and source identity; they are separately authored works. Alternate Koons angles/details were not counted separately.

## Analysis

### Essentials, branches and confidence

The shared value lies in how a visual order refers beyond pure formal beauty. G01–G20 turn squares and channels into systems with conditions of connection and isolation; source labels support that interpretation. G21/G27/G28 make identity and reassurance look manufactured, boxed and branded. G29/G30 isolate products or their transformed replicas as art. These practices are related historically, but their meanings are not inferred solely from bright pixels.

Within Halley's branch, conduit thickness, quantity, ground and connectivity change. G15 deliberately has no cell; G18 is nearly black; G19/G20 widen and wrap paths. They prevent a frozen rule of two yellow boxes and one red line. Grain matters: broad rough cells versus comparatively smooth grounds appear repeatedly. Typography is strongest in the Bickerton branch and should not be falsely discovered in blank paintings. G23–G26 demonstrate that art-historical quotation may involve twisted stripes or organic cutouts, not only right angles.

### Application handoff — contemporary inference

Build PARALLEL, a fictional exhibition explorer and personal route planner. It uses an assertive program masthead, a three-room rectilinear diagram, one selected original work, museum-style object notes and a numbered personal route. Immediate room selection changes artwork and interpretive content; adding/removing/reordering rooms changes the actual plan. ## Connections

- **Pop Art:** shares commodities, repetition and signs; Neo-Geo here adds a specific 1980s simulation/system critique and dialogue with abstraction. Not every Pop object belongs to this historical tendency.
- **Op Art:** G24 shares optical repetition, but the wider Neo-Geo field includes objects and semiotic critique that do not require visual vibration.
- **Memphis / Post-Modern:** shares historical decade and manufactured color, but its playful furnishings/ornamental scatter differ from the critical partitions, commodity display and deliberate quotation here.
- **Neubrutalism:** web cards with thick borders may borrow a surface resemblance; drop shadows, comic exuberance and generic block grids do not establish Neo-Geo identity.
- Geometric systems, sign/brand objects, quoted abstraction and commodity display are branches in this entry; no separate subset slugs are created. Broad terms Simulationism and Neo-Conceptualism overlap historically but are not absorbed as universal aliases.
