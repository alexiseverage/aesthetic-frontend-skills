# Agar Art

An established art practice using microbial growth on agar as an image-making medium. Its visual grammar combines bounded culture surfaces, irregular colony edges, accumulation and material translucency with authored images or emergent patterns. The catalog uses the practice's existing name; it does not assert a single historical movement or a universal microbial appearance. A digital imitation without living material is an agar-art-inspired illustration, not a culture photograph.

## Sources and Context

- [ASM overview](https://asm.org/events/asm-agar-art-contest/home) defines the medium, notes natural and engineered pigment sources, varied agar shapes and the contest's 2015 launch. The contest is a dissemination channel rather than the origin of all microbial art.
- [ASM 2024 winners](https://asm.org/press-releases/2024/december/asm-announces-2024-agar-art-contest-winners), [2023 gallery](https://asm.org/events/asm-agar-art-contest/previous-winners/2023) and [2025 winners](https://asm.org/events/asm-agar-art-contest/winners) identify individual works and creators. Winner pages are dated collection evidence; their contents can change.
- [Tal Danino project archive](https://www.taldaninoart.com/bacteria-gallery-1), [Coexistence](https://www.taldaninoart.com/soil), [Harmony](https://www.taldaninoart.com/harmony), and [TERRA](https://www.taldaninoart.com/terra) document non-pictorial, installation and preserved-culture variants. TERRA describes stained cultures sealed in resin; color there is not evidence of natural microbial pigment.
- [Simon Park, Creative Collaborations with Invisible Life](https://www.interaliamag.org/articles/simon-park-creative-collaborations-invisible-life/) is a first-person account of microbial image-making, including collaboration with watercolor artist Sarah Roberts. The visual analysis uses the documented media; it does not adopt unrelated health claims or procedural instructions from the essay.

Source-supported claims concern medium, attribution and project context. Direct observations concern appearance. Interface proposals below are inferences. No biological identification, health assessment or clinical efficacy follows from an image. Specific organism labels are attributed to source captions rather than diagnosed visually.

## Analysis

### Claim map and boundary

The necessary link is agar used as a microbial image-making surface, supported by source context and visible bounded material. Irregular colony accumulation and streaks are common (A24-5/A25-7/AP-self), but there is no universal palette or motif. Abstract growth fields (AD-coexistence/AD-terra), representational animals (A24-2/A24-3/A25-6), diagram-like motifs (A24-1) and full narrative ensembles (A24-0/A25-0) share material practice rather than an identical iconography.

Do not conflate fluorescence, bioluminescence, natural pigment, dyes, watercolor and photographic color. A23-1 and AP-cryptoglyph establish different luminous mechanisms through their source accounts. AP-roberts explicitly includes watercolor. AD-terra preserves stained traces in resin. A23-0 includes non-microbial details. Those works remain art using agar cultures, while an image generated solely by software or applied to porcelain is an adjacent translation rather than direct evidence of the living medium.

The sample favors contemporary contest photography and two artist archives. It establishes a useful visual system and its exceptions, not a complete genealogy. Organism identity, viability, risk or clinical meaning cannot be inferred from these photographs. This design profile supplies no culturing protocol or diagnostic interaction.

### Frontend translation (inferred)

An exhibition archive can make the physical boundaries meaningful: preserve entire containers in thumbnails, allow filtering by composition or medium, and open a focused lightbox with title, artist, material and honest image description. Use irregular colony illustrations or explicitly credited licensed photographs; label newly generated assets as original digital studies. Never present synthetic cells as microscopy evidence.

Give editorial controls a stable neutral surface. Use warm white, charcoal or restrained yellow for navigation; retain rich color inside the artwork region. Display round and rectangular studies to avoid reducing the practice to circles. Original digital art can use varied colony size, beaded irregular tracks, translucent material and finite edges rather than generic gradient blobs.

Filters need visible active states and announced results; lightboxes need a labelled dialog, Escape/close, focus containment and restoration. A mobile grid should preserve legible artwork and place captions below it. Use no automatic slideshow or simulated biological incubation timer. Loading should reflect actual image requests. Reduced motion should retain all content and interactions while removing optional transitions. These behaviors are proposed web patterns, not measured historical properties of the source works.

## Connections

- Organic Digital shares rounded forms and gradients, but does not require culture-medium provenance or bounded colony surfaces.
- Polarized Crystal Photomicrography is an optical/material photographic practice with facets, striations and extinction fields; agar art is authored or emergent growth on agar.
- Haeckelian Biological Plates organizes comparative printed morphology and numbered figures; photographed culture surfaces are not scientific engraving.
- Clinical laboratory interfaces can also show petri dishes, but an assay report's purpose and claims differ from an exhibition of authored artworks. Do not borrow diagnostic status or warning codes as decoration.
- Bio-art is the broader parent territory; not every biological installation, GAN artwork, microscopy video or porcelain culture reproduction belongs to agar art itself.
