# CD-ROM Edutainment Worlds

## Scope and source context

A descriptive visual/interface branch of 1990s multimedia learning: illustrated or rendered places, collections and objects organize useful exploration, supported by persistent navigation and readable detail views. It includes museum rooms, cabinets, maps, workshops, storybooks, notebooks and learning apparatus. It is not every educational program, every CD-ROM or all 1990s software. The material/scene metaphor must participate in information hierarchy or controls, rather than serve as a picture behind otherwise unrelated UI.

**Primary institutional context:** [NFSA's Real Wild Child! record](https://www.nfsa.gov.au/collection/item/real-wild-child-exhibition-cd-rom) dates the music-history disc to 1998, names its collaborators and describes seven period locations with themed help/index/menu objects. It distinguishes main-menu scrolling from the other pages. The inspected embedded recording independently shows an animated introduction, period-world selection and a readable biography with material control objects. This is evidence of particular states, not proof that every location has free spatial movement.

**Primary collection context:** [ACMI's Explorapedia Nature record](https://www.acmi.net.au/works/84340--microsoft-explorapedia-the-world-of-nature-pc/) and [Magic School Bus Solar System record](https://www.acmi.net.au/works/84358--the-magic-school-bus-explores-the-solar-system-pc/) document educational software and period hardware requirements. Both lack available media; neither adds a visual artifact. The Strong Museum's [Explorapedia People object record](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/encyclopedia-microsoft-explorapedia-children-s-interactive-encyclopedia-the-world-of-people/kAHJvcd9l9ec5w?hl=en) is contextual object metadata, not an inspected interface.

**Practitioner/preservation context:** Jeff Martin's [Voyager case study for Electronic Arts Intermix](https://www.eai.org/resourceguide/preservation/computer/pdf-docs/voyager_casestudy.pdf) discusses multimedia publishing, HyperCard, production and preservation through practitioner testimony. The opening portion, not all sixteen pages, was read. [Peter Becker's first-person Criterion essay](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/728-voyagers) situates Voyager across music, museums and electronic books. These support multimedia publishing context, not the claim that every product used a room. [The NEXT's Voyager collection](https://the-next.org/collections/2) preserves a broader experimental publishing corpus; its [If Monks Had Macs record](https://the-next.org/works/856/2/0/) connects the library metaphor to particular authors and copies.

**Contemporaneous educational review:** The [College of Lake County review of The Way Things Work](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED402466.pdf) describes an illustrated workshop, object exploration, persistent tools and an index. It supplies independent period behavior context; the inspected German lesson capture establishes visible layout, not the English version's exact labels or timing.

**Secondary image provenance:** [Version Museum's education-product history](https://www.versionmuseum.com/history-of/discontinued-microsoft-education-products), named Macintosh Repository program records, Old-Games galleries and RAWG link complete screenshots. These are valuable preservation sources, with uncertain individual screenshot provenance and occasional release/version conflicts. They do not establish publication rights, exact fonts or universal UI rules.

## Direct Media Audit

**59 recorded complete selected media versions; 30 qualifying unique programs: 27 represented by useful still screenshots and three by sampled video.** Seven additional versions group under four already-counted programs. Twenty-two other records are boundary, packaging or inadequate-detail exclusions; two excluded intro views also belong to a grouped program. Multiple pages, editions, stills and video frames never multiply a program's count. The audit classifies supplied complete copies as full-media records; that does not mean the 300px copies or packaging qualify.

Qualifying provenance distribution: NFSA 1, Version Museum 11, Macintosh Repository 9, The NEXT/Bob Stein 1, Old-Games.com 6, Old-Games.ru 1, RAWG 1. The screenshot mirrors are not seven independent original design institutions. Microsoft-related titles dominate part of the corpus; DK, Voyager, Living Books, Knowledge Adventure and Orly add meaningful authorship/format variation. Original individual UI artists are mostly unresolved. The review did not count a single site gallery grid as a full artifact.

Complete native-sized or useful full-screen copies were opened individually. NFSA video was viewed in its authorized page embed; standalone player privacy denial was respected. The three independent motion works were sampled at recorded times; a low-detail camera-shot curator traversal of the already-counted Monks program adds no new artifact. Video was muted, so sound was not analyzed. No software was installed or executed and no reference image/video was downloaded, used as a generation input or shipped.

Broadened searches covered Microsoft reference/learning titles, DK museums and creative tools, Living Books, classroom adventures and Voyager collections. ACMI has no available media for the cited titles. MobyGames returned forbidden/challenge pages, RAWG's main site returned a connection error, and the University of Luxembourg Voyager article presented a robot check; none was bypassed or counted as read. Exact RAWG image hashes from the available gallery identified accessible original CDN files; full dimensions and visible content were verified. Numerous DK archive records exposed packaging only; these are explicitly excluded. Old-Games' two 300px JumpStart copies were excluded despite useful themes.

The final additional specimens reinforced material task controls, quiet readable detail pages, restrained museum rooms and culturally specific authored settings. Collection stopped at the working target with these boundary variations resolved. Exact assets/credits, source software versions, full program behavior, non-Western coverage and calibrated colors remain limitations rather than invented certainty.

## Analysis

### Classification and boundaries

The term is a descriptive catalog label, not a documented unified contemporary movement. Distinctness rests on a reusable information system: knowledge as a place or collection, objects as paths/actions, material continuity across scene and controls, and productive alternation between exploration and focused reading or making. It is related to skeuomorphism but more specific than material imitation. It differs from DVD menus' remote/safe-area/video-loop hierarchy and browser chrome's document window. Plain encyclopedic windows E02/E03/E15/E16/E17 test that boundary; being on disc alone is insufficient.

E08 is a complete title/world specimen with no demonstrated controls; it supports composition, not behavior. E21/E24/E25/E35 show that subordinate white reading pages belong within a larger object-oriented system. E23 and E32 prevent compulsory primary color/cheerfulness. E30 and E35b most clearly connect scientific content, variable controls and material form. E18's selected recording may be a later edition; E22 title and bedroom versions are conservatively one program.

### Claim and confidence review

Formal palette/type/shape/texture/spatial claims have high confidence across several publishers and subject types. Cultural context has moderate-high confidence from institutions, practitioner writing and period education review, with concentrated commercial/Western sampling. Motion has moderate confidence in local scene changes/animation across three independent sampled works; no exact historical easing, response latency or universal soundtrack is asserted. Still captures do not establish functioning controls. Source counts are descriptive, not prevalence statistics.

Dates remain qualified: Ancient Lands caption 1994 versus filename 1993; Explorapedia People archive 1995 versus museum 1994 object dating; Monks publisher-demo 1994 versus NEXT 1995; Orly archive 1996 versus 1997 release records; Science 2.0 1996/1997 versions. Just Grandma's archive 1991 and uploader 1992 labels do not establish the exact later-looking recorded edition. The NEXT platform field and curator description conflict (Director/HyperCard); the curator explicitly says HyperCard, so no unsupported single platform is canonized.

### Application handoff

**Proposed mode:** nostalgic quotation with a contemporary learning interface. Build an original cabinet of three small lessons. A physical object launches the corresponding lesson; persistent labelled Home, Index and Help remain. Give each lesson substantive text, an annotated diagram, an actual question/check/retry, and a session notebook stamp only after a correct answer. An index must list and open actual lessons. A model can advance by explicit steps, without fake loading, autoplay or obligatory sound.

Materials must continue through heading plaques, object selectors, page tabs, input groups, result slips and navigation. Use clear HTML text and redundant labels. On narrow screens place cabinet choices above the lesson, preserving touch size and reading order. Do not shrink a fixed 640×480 desktop scene to microscopic controls or make hidden hotspots the only route.

Proposed tokens: ink #29261F, paper #FFF9DF, dark wood #59402C, brass #DFC17B, forest #264B40, blue #294B76. These are authored application roles, not historical samples. Georgia/system serif headings and reading, system sans controls, 16px/1.55 body, 13–14px labels, 44px controls, 8/12/20/32px spacing, 2px light/dark bevels and restrained CSS grain are proposals. Calculate contrast for actual text/state pairs. No added font downloads.

A short 180ms object lift or 240ms lesson reveal may be proposed, never claimed as measured. Favor explicit one-step diagrams and immediate state updates. Reduced motion removes transform/transition decoration while the same lesson/state remains available. Avoid automatic moving scenes. New behavior must respect keyboard order, focus restoration for modal help, screen-reader names and status updates. A simple closed learning example is not a scientifically complete simulation.

### Rights and subject limits

Historical software illustrations, logos, characters, recordings and type treatments remain protected. Use original illustrations, marks and educational prose; source access is not a reuse license. Do not extract Indigenous objects from 500 Nations, ancient cultures from Ancient Lands, Jamaican identity from Orly or monastery motifs from Monks as generic world decoration. Period history/medical/science text can be incomplete or outdated; source current educational facts independently. Do not infer creators' identities from pictures.

- **DVD Menu:** disc-era adjacency; pointer-led exploration and productive lesson tools differ from video/remote navigation.
