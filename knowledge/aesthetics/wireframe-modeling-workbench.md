## Dimension Synthesis

The following separates direct visual observations from proposed web translation. Color descriptions and relative geometry are estimated from visual inspection; no corpus-wide numerical sampling or measured animation is claimed.

| Dimension | Synthesis / scope | Confidence and limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Palette | Neutral gray, charcoal, off-white or pale blue-gray working planes make colored geometry readable. Orange, blue, red, green, yellow, pink and white appear as selection, object, vertex, axis or data colors with application-specific meanings. Light cream Windows-style controls and white wireframe canvases are documented alongside dark skins. Model materials and environment photographs are not interface palette constants; neither black backgrounds nor neon green is compulsory. | High for the inspected visual sample |
| Type | Compact mixed-case sans-serif menus, panel titles, object trees and tool labels; numerical XYZ rows, units, counts and status text carry a second precise hierarchy. Monospaced or tabular figures are useful for coordinates and scripts, but the entire interface need not be monospaced. Small source screenshots do not identify exact historical fonts. Keep display lettering subordinate to the working surface and use legible contemporary sizes instead of reproducing tiny labels. | High for observed interface hierarchy/treatment; individual historical font families and exact metrics unverified |
| Texture | Crisp digital lines, fine grids, vertex marks, flat control planes, restrained native bevels and optional viewport gradients. Surface shading can sit beneath edges without becoming a glossy card treatment. Different densities expose topology, not paper grain or distressed patina. Normals, selection handles and guide curves must be distinguishable from the modeled surface. Do not add scanlines, chromatic noise or weathering as automatic signs of technical authenticity. | High for the inspected visual sample |
| Shape | Rectangular viewports, thin dividers, compact toolbar cells, hierarchical rows and aligned numerical fields frame constructed geometry. Triangles, quadrilateral loops, spline cages, curve sections, wire primitives and rotation rings are distinct variants. Points, arrows and axis triads communicate manipulation or direction; they should correspond to something real. A coarse cuboid model and a dense organic bust can share the grammar without sharing their silhouettes. | High for the inspected visual sample |
| Motion | The inspected corpus is still imagery and native document pages, so exact historical timing, easing and navigation responsiveness remain unmeasured. Contemporary controls should update the actual geometry on explicit input. Intentional stillness is appropriate while reading topology or entering numbers. Optional orbiting must be user-controlled, pausable and unnecessary for understanding; reduced motion removes interpolation and automatic rotation while retaining useful state changes. | Unknown historical behavior; still composition directly observed |
| Spatial | A dominant projection area is coupled to tools, scene hierarchy, properties and feedback. Common arrangements include one large view plus side panels, four coordinated projections, UV/model pairs and floating contextual windows. The active view and selected object/component need a clear state. Orthographic and perspective representations describe the same object; they are not independent decorative pictures. Information density comes from aligned controls and named sections rather than arbitrary tiny text. | High for the inspected visual sample |
| Cultural markers | Modeling labor, geometry construction, technical inspection, topology, numerical precision, object hierarchies and production workflows. Familiar filenames, component modes, mesh statistics and view names work when they describe the actual artifact. Software families have different conventions and histories; this entry does not claim one interface invented them. Avoid fabricated professional authority, fake processing statistics, copied commercial logos and meaningless hacker commands. | Medium; supporting context and variable examples |
