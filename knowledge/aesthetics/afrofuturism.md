# Afrofuturism — research profile

## Classification and Context

**Decision:** distinct transdisciplinary Black speculative framework with plural visual branches. Black histories, authorship, agency and imagined possibilities are essential to the classification. A palette, an African object or a Black person against stars alone is insufficient. It is not an alias of Cyberpunk, Solarpunk, Space Age, Collage or a separately articulated Africanfuturism.

**Source-supported:** [NMAAHC](https://www.searchablemuseum.com/afrofuturism/) distinguishes the 1993 name from earlier practices; [Alondra Nelson's Social Text issue](https://www.dukeupress.edu/Afrofuturism) grounds race/technology and diasporic intervention. Dery's essay was first published in 1993 and expanded for the 1994 Flame Wars collection; do not confuse the book date with first coinage. MoMA's term page misspells the surname “Derry”; retain Mark Dery. The framework does not have one inventor who authored every earlier practice.

**Source-supported:** [SFO's exhibition](https://www.sfomuseum.org/exhibitions/women-afrofuturism/gallery) documents women's technological, ecological, genealogical and community practices. Gaskins' museum labels explicitly name generative tools: these are documented authored works, not photographs or new samples made for this project. Peters' Blueprint is labeled 2024 by SFO; a later artist edition listing does not rewrite that label. Wormsley's project begins in 2012, with first billboard in 2017 and this museum version 2025; those views are one project, not three counts.

**Source-supported:** [NMAAHC's performance account](https://www.searchablemuseum.com/parliament-funkadelic-nona-hendryx-and-labelle/) identifies musicians, designers and narrative personas. The physical Mothership in its collection is a mid-1990s replica, separate from the original craft seen in the 1976 film. [Pinder's 2009 Mothership (Capsule)](https://collections.si.edu/search/results.htm?q=edanmdm:nmaahc_2013.234) is a distinct mixed-media sculpture, not another stage-ship photograph. Unprovided costume/cover designers and Jantjes date remain unassigned.

**Source-supported:** [The Met's guide](https://www.metmuseum.org/exhibitions/afrofuturist-period-room/visiting-guide) reconstructs a possible continuing Seneca Village through credited contemporary commissions and historical objects. That curatorial context does not make every historical African object or every furnishing independently Afrofuturist. [Jean-Louis' artist interview](https://journalpanorama.org/article/about-time/interview-with-fabiola-jean-louis/) supports memory/time and material interpretation; Haitian spiritual references are specific, not generic futuristic ornament.

## Analysis

**Observed:** the strong contradiction is visual uniformity itself. White relief, quiet photographic leisure and plain text sit beside intricate AI portraits, cosmic commercial logos, sequined garments and tarnished salvage. Material depth and human domestic space are as relevant as cosmic flat graphics. The three played films use authored human performance, staged reveal and narrative editing; a generic drifting star layer captures none of that specificity.

**Source-supported:** practitioners use time, technology, spiritual systems, music, genealogy and memory differently. Documentation credits named makers and contextualizes the right to author futures. Histories of displacement and exclusion do not exhaust the field's emotional registers. Jean-Louis' spiritual dress, Denenge's Yoruba reference and Sun Ra's constructed cosmic persona should not be collapsed into one pan-African symbol set.

**Application inference:** original future correspondence from a fictional Black community can connect inherited practices to future forms of care. Illustrations, captions and readable letters should carry a concrete narrative; object annotations can relate what is carried forward to what becomes possible. This is a selected domestic/editorial branch, not a representative claim about every work. Colors, fonts, hotspot positions, responsiveness and immediate state changes are new design decisions. Custom generated illustrations remain application artifacts, never added to the corpus.

**Limits and review:** 31 purposively chosen works, museum/artist-mediated records, predominantly US contexts; no physical visits or community-wide claims. Small complete museum reproductions have explicit detail limits. No copied reference pixels, invented designer/date, sound analysis or measured canonical easing. Implementing-agent claim and duplicate review only; no independent reviewer claimed.

## Connections

- Solarpunk overlaps ecological/collective possibilities; Black historical agency is not optional here.
- Cyberpunk overlaps critique of technology/power without owning all speculative Black work.
- Space Age/Raypunk share cosmic motifs in a branch; hardware is insufficient as cultural identity.
- Collage/Psychedelic are techniques/neighbor languages rather than equivalent frameworks.
- Africanfuturism is separately articulated and not a spelling alias assigned by this catalog.
