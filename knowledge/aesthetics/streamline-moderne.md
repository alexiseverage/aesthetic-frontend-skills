## Historical / Origin Notes

Streamline Moderne emerged in the 1930s as a late Art Deco style inspired by aerodynamic design, emphasizing curving forms, long horizontal lines, and nautical or transport references across architecture and industrial design.

## Notable practitioners, artifacts, regions, or institutions

- Ocean liner imagery, rail and bus design, domestic appliances, and commercial architecture are the core references.
- UI translation should preserve the aerodynamic/transport framing instead of treating streamline cues as generic retro decoration.

## Analysis

Streamline Moderne turns modernity into smooth motion: curving shells, horizontal speed cues, and polished machine optimism make function look aerodynamic and approachable.

Non-negotiables for implementation: aerodynamic contour; horizontal speed lines; polished machine surfaces; transport-era optimism.

Cultural sensitivity assessment: Low identity-risk but historically specific. The style belongs to 1930s industrial optimism and consumer modernization, not timeless luxury futurism.

## Connections

`art-deco` is the parent family but often more ornamental and vertical; `atomic-age` inherits streamline transport optimism and pushes it into neon consumer futurism; `high-tech-architecture` later exposes technology more literally rather than wrapping it in sleek shells.

### Analysis and boundaries

Streamline Moderne translates aerodynamic imagery into continuous shells, rounded corners and repeated horizontal bands across transport, buildings, furniture and stationary appliances. The evidence includes colored moulded radios, brick, lacquered wood, upholstery and chrome. It is a historically situated branch of modern design, not merely a shiny retro finish.

Use transport/hospitality history, departure tools, product interpretation and deliberately period-framed interfaces. Distinguish original 1930s work, postwar continuity and later diner revivals. Not every Art Deco tower, neon sign or rounded modern appliance is Streamline.

### Proposed frontend application

Use a departure console, ticket-like summary, route selector or equipment interpretation with real choices. Integrate speed rules into dividers and curved casing into hierarchy; original object drawings can carry the form without people. Mark fictional timetables clearly.

Proposed values: enamel #eee9d6, navy #234b56, sage-metal #8b9e9b, oxide #a44a36. Combine double horizontal rules, restrained metal gradients, asymmetric 20px/55px casing corners and porthole-like framed artwork. Controls remain labelled with clear focus; reduce to a single column on phones.

Pair geometric/system sans titling, spaced small service labels and tabular departure numerals. Condensed or sloped display treatment may signal movement, but preserve readable locations and times.

### Limits and stop decision

31 unique works from 38 inspected still versions, including one grouped wood-toy body variant and six unrelated/weak boundaries. V&A, Cleveland, Cooper Hewitt and documented Commons architecture provide varied media. Later diner photographs and postwar radios are dated continuity/revival comparisons. Some institutional pages/API endpoints failed (403/410) and Commons originals returned429; unavailable files were not counted. Complete museum images ultimately added seven useful works; exact motion and pigment values remain unknown.

The target has been reached after material, palette, layout and boundary variation. Further examples reinforced the same distinctions. Remaining unknowns are stated rather than filled with invented precision; current support is sufficient for the bounded contemporary translation.
