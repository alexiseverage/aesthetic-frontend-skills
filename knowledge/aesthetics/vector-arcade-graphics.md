# Vector Arcade Graphics

## Scope and classification

The spare line-based image grammar of vector-display arcade games: luminous geometric objects, strokes, counters and unusually open dark playfields. The corpus includes monochrome and color games, flat and perspective scenes, and physical illustrated backdrops. This is a display/game graphic system, not a claim that a browser SVG reproduces vector CRT electronics. Preserve line construction and spatial economy instead of adding a neon skyline.

This is a new canonical entry, screened against current catalog slugs, labels, aliases, redirects and subset names. 8-bit pixel builds images from raster tiles. Synthwave often uses scenic grids, sunsets and gradients. Tactical interfaces prioritize many operational readouts; vector arcade graphics use a comparatively sparse game field. Demoscene emphasizes audiovisual computation and performance rather than a single arcade stage.

## Dimension Synthesis

### Palette

Black playfields with white, green or a small set of bright colored strokes. Color can divide objects or play regions; not every example is green monochrome.

### Type

Angular stroke-like capitals and numerical counters, usually short and legible. Place score and status at the perimeter rather than flooding the stage with microtext.

### Texture

Thin luminous lines on a dark field; modest bloom may suggest light emission. Some cabinets use static illustrated overlays or backdrops. Raster scanlines, pixel tiling and heavy VHS damage are not defining evidence.

### Shape

Polygonal ships, outlined obstacles, radial webs, trajectories and wireframe perspective. Quantum supplies a useful filled-object counterexample: not every visible shape is an empty outline.

### Motion

Viewed Tempest, Asteroids and Battlezone video samples show object motion within stable playfield rules. No universal velocity or easing was measured. For web selection interfaces, a short user-triggered trace can reference the display without constant motion or flashing.

### Spatial

Large empty regions make a few line objects and perimeter counters readable. Perspective trenches, radial tunnels and open two-dimensional fields are distinct compositions.

### Cultural markers

Arcade scorekeeping, stage/lives indicators, line-built vehicles and geometric playfields. Identify the actual game/display context; a fake tactical HUD or synthwave horizon communicates a different system.

## Analysis

### Claim-to-artifact support

Sparse line geometry: VA01, VA03, VA12, VA15, VA23, VA26. Physical backdrop exceptions: Warrior, Battlezone and Asteroids Deluxe. Filled-shape exception: the repaired Quantum capture. Actual motion samples and timestamps are recorded for Tempest, Asteroids and Battlezone.

### Confidence and gaps

High confidence in line geometry and negative space, moderate in type/texture at the available original resolutions. Screen captures cannot measure phosphor, luminance or beam behavior.

Core identity is limited to the supported traits in the canonical non-negotiables. Counterexamples have been incorporated into the guidance rather than discarded to make a uniform mood board. No still-derived exact motion timing, measured physical performance or unverified source claim is promoted to fact.

### Application handoff

Connotation mode: **nostalgic quotation**. Artifact concept: **Vector Select arcade session launcher**. Signature visual unit: line stage. Build a stage/session selector with a large original vector illustration, short difficulty controls and a visible start/reset state. Use real text counters outside the illustration. Decorative traces must be optional, bounded and pausable; mobile should keep the illustration legible without shrinking controls.

These are **proposed application tokens**, not sampled historical colors or measured timings. Background `#07120f`, surface `#10231c`, text `#d9ffe6`, muted `#a0ccb5`, accent `#9ef56f`, success `#75dbed`, danger `#edaa76`. Display `Courier New, monospace` at `clamp(2rem,5vw,4.5rem)`, weight 700, line-height 1.02, tracking -0.035em; body `Courier New, monospace` at 1rem/1.5, weight 400, tracking 0; labels 0.8125rem/1.3, weight 700, tracking 0.06em; numeric outputs use tabular figures at 1rem/1.3. Space 1–6: `4,8,12,20,32,48px`; radius `2px` for functional fields and `0` for panel edges (a circular dial/label retains its own geometry). Default state changes are instant; optional feedback `120ms ease-out` is a proposed web value, disabled under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`. No automatic decorative loop is required.

These tokens are a baseline for an original interface. Include the supported subject/identity, its information hierarchy and its operational/reading context. Avoid copied marks/art, generic palette swaps and inaccessible source-era constraints. The source references remain analytical; original procedural graphics or separately created images should carry the application.

### Accessibility and adaptation flags

Palette tokens do not certify interface contrast. Check actual foreground/background states, focus outlines, disabled controls and overlays; target WCAG AA text and non-text requirements. Body text should remain 16px or larger in the application. Responsive priority is content order and control usability, not a tiny scaled facsimile. Keep explicit text equivalents for graphs and decorative SVGs hidden from assistive technology when captions already describe them. Respect user zoom and narrow layouts.

Reduced motion: stillness or immediate state changes are valid. Any proposed motion is optional, interruptible and removed for reduced motion. Dark mode: retain the artifact's deliberate material palette within its scoped frame; adapt surrounding application chrome separately. Fatigue: limit the loudest texture/type to the defining field. Conflict rule: preserve the non-negotiables and task semantics before introducing a neighboring palette, font or transition.
