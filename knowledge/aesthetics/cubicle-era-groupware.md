## Analysis

Cubicle-Era Groupware is a descriptive visual corpus of office collaboration software and closely related business information interfaces, mainly the 1990s and 2000s with earlier precursors. It is not an established self-named design movement. The shared grammar comes from a task environment: records, dates, folders, routing, recipients, form fields, status and a limited window area organized for repeated work. A generic gray desktop without these relationships is incomplete.

The primary Notes sequence shows evolution rather than a single immutable skin. Colored workspace tabs and overlapping document windows lead to tree/list/preview mail, browser-like welcome pages and integrated composite applications. GroupWise offers separate calendar/mail windows, then a multi-pane dashboard and a cross-platform mail client. Outlook captures show shortcut rails, folder trees and preview/empty states. Groove adds presence and shared discussion/tools; QuickPlace and Braun reporting establish browser intranet patterns. Organizer, InfoCentral, ACT!, GoldMine, Approach, TouchBase and Claris Organizer broaden record/tab/form relationships while remaining explicitly adjacent, not falsely labelled network groupware.

**Proposed frontend translation:** an original fictional “Northline Office / People & Operations” application. A deep blue title strip and compact menu/action row sit above a gray work area. Use a small left workspace tree, a tabbed Leave Request/Expense Record/Calendar task plane, labelled inset controls and a lower status strip showing Draft, Validation required or Ready for approval. Submitting a valid local leave request adds an item to a compact approval queue and visibly changes its status. A reviewer-style local Approve control changes that item, while Reset returns the demonstration to a known state. No real employee data, HR decision or external system is involved. The source-backed relationship is a business record moving through explicit states; all fictional rules and interaction logic are modern application design.

Proposed roles: neutral control gray `#C7C8C3`, paper `#FFFFF8`, ink `#1B2228`, title navy `#133F73`, muted teal `#407A7E`, pale note yellow `#FFF2B1` and status green `#285E43`. Keep dark text on pale surfaces and white on the navy title; validate actual contrast in implementation. Use readable system sans for labels/body, slightly smaller tabular numerals in date/amount tables and bold column/section labels. Raised buttons and sunken fields need original CSS edges, not screenshot backgrounds. A small original pixel envelope, folder, calendar and stamp set can provide toolbar cues. Never copy logos, source contact details or proprietary icon assets.

The form should contain real fields, labels, descriptions, a request-type choice, dates, amount/expense rows when appropriate and a meaningful local confirmation. Preserve the command/form/status hierarchy, compact tab labels and table structure. Do not create dozens of inert decorative menu commands merely to imitate density. If a control exists, give it a useful local behavior or clearly mark a disabled reason. A selected record should have text and a visible border/check as well as color. A status bar may summarize the active task, but errors belong beside fields and in a focusable/announced error summary.

Use semantic labels, fieldsets, native inputs/selects and buttons. The apparent pixel scale must not make keyboard focus, text or touch targets tiny. Tabs need keyboard-operable focus and state, or use ordinary buttons that switch well-labelled sections. A read-only calendar can supplement date fields; do not force pointer-only scheduling. Never use a fake operating-system error, sign-in, permission dialog or install prompt. Keep fictional sample identity clear and avoid collecting actual personal or financial information. A local approval example does not authorize real-world HR decisions.

On phones, stack the workspace navigation above the active form and move secondary queue/calendar content below it. Reflow field pairs; keep labels above their controls and allow help/error text to wrap.
