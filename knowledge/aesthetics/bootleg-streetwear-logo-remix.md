## Analysis

Bootleg / parody streetwear logo remix is a typographic/logo-manipulation grammar with a clear cultural stance: brand flip, satire, and bootleg. The practice remixes recognizable brand logos into unofficial, ironic, or subversive designs — flipping, respelling, rewording, or distorting a familiar logotype. Stussy's Chanel parody is a canonical early example, and the practice is framed academically through culture-jamming accounts of subversive signification.

The aesthetic is distinct from `punk-zine` (anti-professional collage) and `corporate-grunge` (art-directed distress) because it is specifically logo-remix satire with its own legal/parody boundary. The signature is the manipulated logotype — the recognizable brand mark twisted into something subversive.

For frontend translation, the useful rules are: recognizable brand colors remixed or inverted, manipulated logotypes (flipped/respelled/reworded), and a centered logo-lockup composition. The IP boundary is critical: no protected logo may become a mandatory reusable motif; the grammar (manipulation, flip, satire) is the reusable system, not any specific brand mark.
