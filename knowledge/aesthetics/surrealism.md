# Surrealism

Surrealism is an international artistic and literary movement with a formal 1924 Parisian manifesto and earlier experiments in automatic writing and image making. Its visual range includes discontinuous signs, precise impossible scenes, photographic traces and materially contradictory objects. A single pastel palette, floating eye or melting-clock motif cannot define this range.

## Source / Evidence Links

- [Met: Surrealism, James Voorhies](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/surrealism) — literary origins, automatism, differences between abstract and illusionistic branches, migration and international continuation. Kahlo and Rivera were exhibited but did not formally join the movement.
- [MoMA: Surrealism](https://www.moma.org/collection/terms/surrealism) — 1924 manifesto, psychological theories, automatic drawing, hyperreal painted scenes and uncanny material combinations; exact works selected by the museum.
- [Thyssen: Surrealism in Art](https://www.museothyssen.org/en/collection/art-movements/surrealism) — chance, paradox, found objects and the distinct approaches of Miró, Ernst, Magritte and Tanguy. Magritte's careful construction cautions against treating unconscious spontaneity as every artist's literal method.
- [MoMA Library: Surrealism](https://www.moma.org/research/library/library-highlights-surrealism) — periodical and book context. This is consulted contextual text, not a claim that its publications were visually inspected as part of the counted corpus.

## Direct Media Audit

Thirty-two complete linked media were individually inspected: thirty qualifying unique still works and two excluded boundary examples. The working target of thirty and absolute floor of ten are achieved. The audit reports no unresolved records or duplicate groups among inspected media. Collection/gallery thumbnails and repeated source-set views were discovery leads only and are not added to the count. No reference files were downloaded; no video, historic website, generated image or new interface contributes to evidence.

Qualifying collection distribution: MoMA ten, Met twelve, Thyssen eight. Several works originate in the 1920s; later 1930s–1940s works test continuation and variation, and Man Ray's metronome is explicitly a 1964 replica of a 1923 original. This is not a 1920s-only survey, a complete world history, or a survey of Surrealist film. European painting is strongly represented; Carrington, Matta and Tanning broaden the later comparison without making this sample globally representative.

MoMA originals were inspected at up to 2000 pixels; Thyssen complete source-set images at 1120 pixels wide. Met's exposed complete images are 600 pixels on their longest side, including a narrow complete five-part Magritte arrangement. These are useful for the described composition and broad contours; tiny marks, pigment chemistry and exact color sampling are not claimed. Color names are estimated from visual inspection. Native size, source identity and observations are retained in the research ledger; copyright and institutional handling conditions remain with the linked originals.

The excluded Breton portrait is historical context, not defining visual evidence. Kahlo's self-portrait is retained as an artist/self-identification boundary, not silently assigned to a movement because it appears on a thematic page. Duchamp, de Chirico and Picasso leads in the Met essay were not counted or promoted merely because their influence is discussed. Same-work source-set files and multi-part frames are never additional works.

## Analysis

### Defining claims and boundaries

The repeating principle is a meaningful disruption of expected relationships, anchored in the documented movement rather than a generalized strange mood. It can be explicit material substitution (S02, S10), collaborative discontinuity (S03), precise narrative contradiction (S05, S07, S20, S25), abstract signs (S06, S15–S16, S28–S29), or textured transformation (S19, S30–S31). This support warrants high confidence in the relational grammar and classification; it does not establish one mandatory visual motif or production method.

The final eight-work Thyssen batch supplied important counterweights: a broken window that retains its image, a grayscale rocky mirror chamber, spare blue/white sign fields and thick red/green textured masses. They reinforce discontinuity while contradicting rules such as always dense, always softly colored, always airbrushed or always dream-landscape-based. The three collections provide useful independent curation; shared famous names still bias the corpus. The sample does not resolve the movement's full geographic, literary or cinematic diversity.

No artist's entire practice is classified by association alone. The retained Kahlo boundary and uncounted predecessor leads make that distinction explicit. Likewise a painted numeral is not evidence for a specific web font, and a metronome photograph cannot measure its tempo. Visual and textual support remain separate.

### Frontend translation

A contemporary editorial surface can use framing gaps, displaced planes, asymmetrical scale and associative reading order while preserving literal labels and predictable controls. An original arrangement of domestic thresholds and impossible weather-bearing objects can support the same contradiction without reproducing a famous work. The functional interface itself should participate through pane boundaries, typography and article relationships; the image is supporting material.

Use an explicit article index, real text disclosure, saved reading list and visible live feedback. Keep reading order, keyboard focus and target placement stable. User-triggered changes are immediate; stillness follows the inspected source medium. Breakpoints, fonts, contrast pairs and all token values are proposed application decisions. They are not reconstructed historical website behavior.
