## Historical / Origin Notes

Suprematism was developed by Kazimir Malevich from 1913 onward as an abstract language of basic geometric forms and limited colors, arguing for the supremacy of pure artistic feeling over depiction of real-world subjects.

## Analysis

Suprematism strips composition to pure relation: minimal color, spare geometry, and white-field tension used to detach form from worldly depiction.

Non-negotiables for implementation: white-field openness; pure geometric reduction; restrained palette; non-objective compositional tension.

Cultural sensitivity assessment: Low identity-risk but historically precise. The movement belongs to a specific Russian avant-garde philosophical project; avoid reducing it to casual geometric minimalism.

## Connections

`de-stijl` also pursues abstraction but organizes it through orthogonal universal order rather than floating dynamism; `constructivism` emerges from the same milieu but redirects abstraction toward social utility; `op-art` later uses geometry for retinal illusion instead of metaphysical feeling.
