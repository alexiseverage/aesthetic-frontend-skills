## Dimension Synthesis

| Dimension | Core | Common | Variant / limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Palette | Purposeful flat color masses and optimistic brand-world contrast. | Cobalt/navy, coral/orange, yellow, mint/turquoise, purple and white ground. | Both natural and abstract skin; saturated, muted, dark and pale roles. No compulsory pastel scheme. |
| Type | Clear product/content hierarchy around the illustration. | Practical sans copy/buttons, bold friendly headings. | Slack and Humaaans use prominent chunky serif headlines. No universal neutral-geometric-only rule or exact font license inferred. |
| Texture | Simplified illustrated surfaces with selective detail. | Flat vector-like masses, thin accents, local stipple/dither and shallow tonal panels. | Alegria/Lee contain conspicuous grain/brush edges; Humaaans is smooth; Atlassian has gradients/shadows. No blanket ban on texture/depth. |
| Shape | Exaggerated gesture/scale, reduced geometry and a coherent modular vocabulary. | Small heads/long limbs, rounded/angled clothing, giant devices/props, leaves and speech shapes. | Many visible expressions/facial details; simplified objects/fauna/flora can share the system. Faceless humans and amorphous blobs are not universal. |
| Motion | Stillness or context-specific state/assembly changes; usability stays primary. | Observed GIFs replace components, demonstrate editor swaps and switch scenery. | Exact easing, source-frame phase and generic bobbing/scroll timing unsupported. Still figures persist while backgrounds change. |
| Spatial | A readable task/story scene supports product communication. | Airy hero/onboarding/empty-state frame; oversized props beside smaller figures; hierarchy/negative space. | Full sheets are denser; mobile compositions adapt/crop rather than merely shrink. Decorative figures should not displace essential UI. |
| Cultural markers | Late2010s scalable corporate/product illustration and its reception as institutional friendliness. | Alegria, Slack2017, Atlassian's new language and modular stock libraries. | Maker-specific systems have distinct goals and influences. Critique of sameness is not proof that every illustration/artist is cynical or that a style automatically achieves inclusion. |

## Analysis

Palette/shape/spatial confidence is high within the named corpus: BUCK20/28/30/34, Alice1/14/16/22/25, Atlassian26/33/34 and Humaaans9/10/18 triangulate clear task worlds and scale relationships. Type confidence is moderate because product mockups, not every isolated illustration, supply readable interface hierarchy; prominent serif variants directly challenge the old prohibition. Texture confidence is high within these four collections, with both clean vector and brush/stipple/tonal alternatives. Motion confidence is moderate for three modular demonstrations and low for universal timing. Cultural confidence is moderate: four maker accounts clarify purposes and distinctions, but public reception and actual inclusion outcomes are not measured here.

A modern object-led application can preserve metaphorical scale, rounded/angled geometry, selective stipple and a friendly task narrative through oversized folders, paper, devices, plants and real UI choices. This is an adaptation constraint, not a claim that historic Corporate Memphis was originally figure-free. Avoid anthropomorphic stand-ins that simply disguise a person, or generic blobs with decorative buttons. A real short onboarding flow can carry the visual grammar in headings, steps, controls, layout and original object scenes while keeping important text in HTML and transitions immediate. Proposed palette/type/spacing are design decisions rather than statistical historical tokens.

## Connections

Flat Design concerns broad interface reduction; Corporate Memphis is primarily a scalable illustration/brand-world language. Material can structure the surrounding product without being the same illustration style. Memphis Group is a namesake analogy, not proof that every maker descended directly from1980s Italian furniture. Alegria is one proprietary system inside the broader informal category, not the author of every friendly corporate illustration.
