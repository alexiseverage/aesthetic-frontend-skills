# Architectural Property Editorial

## Scope and classification

An architecture-led property-publishing system in which photographs, spatial narrative, architect/history credits and practical listing information form one editorial object. This is a descriptive cross-agency cluster rather than a named historical movement. It includes richly furnished period houses as well as spare modern buildings; beige minimal interiors alone do not define it.

This is a new canonical entry, screened against current catalog slugs, labels, aliases, redirects and subset names. Warm minimalism can influence page restraint, but it does not supply the property report, provenance or practical sales grammar. Architectural movements describe buildings, while this entry describes how a property is photographed, narrated and navigated.

## Dimension Synthesis

### Palette

Quiet page surfaces let architectural photography supply material and landscape colors. Cream, peach, slate and white interfaces occur in the inspected agency pages. Do not recolor every building into neutral beige.

### Type

Large editorial property names, restrained utility navigation and specifications. Serif titles/italic commentary appear in UK examples; the Australian example uses a large sans title. Both retain hierarchy between narrative and facts.

### Texture

Daylight, brick, timber, stone, vegetation and glazing in commissioned architectural images. Flat page surfaces and thin rules support them; fake film grain or a gold luxury texture is unnecessary.

### Shape

Large landscape photographs, ordered fact blocks, columned narratives, ruled details and floor-plan geometry. Architecture may be curved, vernacular, highly decorated or modern.

### Motion

The inspected pages support manual scrolling and photo/plan/viewing navigation; no exact animation timing was established. Prefer deliberate manual gallery changes and still imagery, with no essential scroll effects.

### Spatial

Generous picture fields alternate with address, price/status, specification and longer spatial stories. The reading hierarchy must survive a single mobile column rather than becoming a miniature desktop spread.

### Cultural markers

Architect, design history, materials, location and room relationships alongside ordinary sales facts. Attribute photographs and architectural claims. Distinguish appreciation of architecture from unsupported claims of exclusivity or investment return.

### Application handoff

Connotation mode: **contemporary editorial application**. Artifact concept: **Still Houses Courtyard No. 6 report**. Signature visual unit: architectural report. Compose one house report with an expansive photograph, address/name, room facts, a plan toggle, material notes, shortlist control and local viewing preferences. Keep photographs separate from essential text, show a useful fallback on image failure and maintain a readable caption. On mobile, image, title, facts and narrative become sequential.

These are **proposed application tokens**, not sampled historical colors or measured timings. ### Accessibility and adaptation flags

Palette tokens do not certify interface contrast. Check actual foreground/background states, focus outlines, disabled controls and overlays; target WCAG AA text and non-text requirements. Body text should remain 16px or larger in the application. Responsive priority is content order and control usability, not a tiny scaled facsimile. Keep explicit text equivalents for graphs and decorative SVGs hidden from assistive technology when captions already describe them. Respect user zoom and narrow layouts.

Reduced motion: stillness or immediate state changes are valid. Any proposed motion is optional, interruptible and removed for reduced motion. Dark mode: retain the artifact's deliberate material palette within its scoped frame; adapt surrounding application chrome separately. Fatigue: limit the loudest texture/type to the defining field. Conflict rule: preserve the non-negotiables and task semantics before introducing a neighboring palette, font or transition.
