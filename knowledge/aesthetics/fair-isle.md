## Dimension Synthesis

Exact hex colors, spacing, font choices and timing are not historical measurements.

**Palette**: Light/dark yarn pairing must make each local repeat legible. Cream, brown, navy, red, ochre, blue and green are common; pink, turquoise and other modern palettes are documented. Change pairs across rows without implying that the complete garment has only two colors. Winter-blue and red/green Christmas palettes are optional.

**Type**: Lettering is generally absent from the fabric itself. Legible small book or utility type on a paper label/notebook is an application decision. Keep text in plain areas outside the repeat; do not pretend a chosen typeface is a historic Fair Isle font.

**Texture**: Distinct V-shaped knitted loops, wool halo, ribbed cuffs and hems, strands and garment construction. Render stitch geometry with a fine highlight/shadow or original SVG repeat. Use material texture selectively; a flat pixel checkerboard or cross-stitch X grid is a different surface.

**Shape**: Horizontal large/small bands, diamonds, crosses and eight-point rosettes, with repeats constrained by garment shape. Caps can decrease toward radial crowns; shoulder strips, button plackets and ribs interrupt the field. Snowflakes are not a universal master motif.

**Motion**: All qualifying references are still photographs of textiles; timing is unknown. An intentionally still swatch makes row structure and surface readable. Change motifs/pairs directly on user input; avoid pulsing yarn or continuous pattern scrolling. Reduced-motion should retain all editing functions.

**Spatial**: Rhythmic dense repeats with narrow separating rows; alternate motif scales instead of giving every band equal emphasis. Preserve a calm plain area for labels and controls. Sparse mittens show that dense overall coverage is not obligatory.

**Cultural markers**: A living Fair Isle/Shetland craft and makers' knowledge, knitting labor, wool, named patterns, caps, gloves, stockings, jumpers and contemporary adaptations. Community and provenance matter more than generalized northern-European decoration. The Spanish Armada origin story is unproven.

## Analysis

### Claim-to-artifact support

- **Banded geometric hierarchy and two-color row logic**: FI-O108370, FI-O83823, FI-1913, FI-STM-3.
- **Low density and modern palettes are documented variants**: FI-O1123122, FI-O68344, FI-STM-5, FI-katies.
- **Garment zones change repeat geometry**: FI-O1123130, FI-STM-1, FI-STM-4, FI-BUG.

Historical identity and cultural/technical claims come from the context sources above. No animation, browsing behavior, historical font identity or exact pigment/yarn number was observed from still references. Intentional stillness and system-font roles are proposed application choices.

### Confidence and boundaries

Palette, material, shape and composition: high for the inspected branch, not a census. Type: high where poster lettering is present; low as a historical UI/type claim for paintings and garments. Cultural context: source-supported, with provenance caveats below. Motion: unknown historically, no invented measurements.

The corpus combines museum objects with contemporary patterns/garments and fashion reinterpretations. V&A adaptations are not claims of island manufacture. Folded scarves show the complete photographed object arrangement but not an unseen unfolded chart. Cowl and matching gloves count as one underlying design. No chart has been traced or reproduced.
