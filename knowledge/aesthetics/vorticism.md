## Historical / Origin Notes

Vorticism was a London-based modernist movement formed in 1914 around Wyndham Lewis and introduced through BLAST magazine, seeking to express the dynamism of the modern world through hard-edged abstraction.

## Notable practitioners, artifacts, regions, or institutions

- Wyndham Lewis, BLAST magazine, and the London avant-garde circle are the key anchors.
- UI translation should preserve the movement’s short, polemical, prewar British context instead of blending it vaguely into “cubist industrial graphics.”

## Analysis

Vorticism condenses machine-age force into jagged geometric rhetoric: part manifesto, part magazine assault, part hard-edged abstraction.

Non-negotiables for implementation: angular mechanical geometry; manifesto-grade type; high-contrast impact; British avant-garde framing.

Cultural sensitivity assessment: Low identity-risk but historically narrow. Because the movement was brief and polemical, vague reuse easily collapses it into generic aggressive modernism.

## Connections

`futurism` shares modern dynamism but is more Italian, speed-obsessed, and painterly; `constructivism` shares angular propaganda force but is more socially programmatic; `grunge-typography` may echo the loud typographic aggression without the same modernist machine ethos.
