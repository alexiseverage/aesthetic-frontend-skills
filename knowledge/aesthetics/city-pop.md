## Analysis

City Pop’s provenance is split between the original late-1970s/1980s Japanese music culture and the later internet codification of its visual language around artists such as Hiroshi Nagai. The sources support treating the aesthetic not as all-purpose '80s retro' but as a specific leisure-and-optimism visual system tied to album sleeves, resort imagery, and metropolitan aspiration.

Representative visual evidence centers on blue-sky horizon scenes, pools, palms, cars, and flat architectural illustration. Compared with synthwave, city-pop is less nocturnal and less aggressive; the emotional register is a warm summer day rather than a neon chase sequence.

Ethical and usage note: contemporary reuse often abstracts the look away from its Japanese cultural and economic context. Avoid reducing it to decorative kanji plus palms; keep the bubble-era leisure, music packaging, and place-specific optimism legible.

## Connections

- `synthwave` — palette adjacency exists, but synthwave is dark, neon, and speed-driven where city-pop is daylight, resort-like, and relaxed.
- `vaporwave` — vaporwave adds irony, decay, and consumer-simulacrum; city-pop remains earnest and sunlit.
- `konbini-utility` — both are Japan-linked, but Konbini Utility is operational retail clarity rather than aspirational leisure nostalgia.
