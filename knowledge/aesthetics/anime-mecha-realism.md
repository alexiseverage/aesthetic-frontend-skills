## Analysis

Anime Mecha-Realism is application-ready because its signal is distributed across the seven-dimension framework rather than resting on palette alone. The most important implementation guardrail is to preserve the combined palette, material, shape, motion, spatial, and cultural-marker system; if only one cue is present, the result will read as generic speculative technology.

For frontend translation, begin with the spatial and shape grammar, then layer palette, typography, texture, and motion. Keep critical content on solid readable surfaces when glow, grain, scanlines, particle fields, or dense machine texture would reduce accessibility.

Real-robot realism means a fiction's consistent machine, supply and operational world, not a claim that the robot could actually be built. Civil police/industrial language in Patlabor counters a mandatory battlefield reading. Evangelion's biological/psychological machine language is adjacent, not required in every real-robot interface. Still sheets cannot prove that recoil or thrust animation is mandatory. Successive design and manual batches added useful palette, civilian-machine and instruction-graphic counterexamples; collection stopped after the 30-work target and a coherent still-led assembly grammar. Motion remains outside this corpus rather than being invented from stills.

### Application decision

Retain the existing canonical identity, era, family and relationships. Replace boilerplate universal colors/weapons with the observed coherent-machine, part-code, layered-shell and servicing grammar. Proposed people-free UI: an original service dossier with one leg-drive assembly, matching highlighted parts, an optional exploded state, explicit inspection checklist and release state. Its dimensions, part specifications, font stack, transition timing and device values are invented application data, not recovered franchise specifications. No pilot portrait or franchise robot illustration is needed. Keep the task readable in reduced motion and on narrow screens. This is a contemporary fictional quotation; no claim of affiliation with the cited franchises is made.
