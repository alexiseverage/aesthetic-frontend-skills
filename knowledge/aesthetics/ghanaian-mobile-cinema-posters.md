# Ghanaian Mobile-Cinema Posters

## Scope and Source Context

This profile concerns a named Ghanaian painted advertising tradition and its later continuations, not all cinema art made in Africa. [Poster House's curatorial account](https://posterhouse.org/exhibition/ghana/) distinguishes posters made for local audiences from the later international market. [QAGOMA's expanded label](https://collection.qagoma.qld.gov.au/node/47439) connects the practice with VHS circulation, local sign painters, reused cloth and films from several production contexts. [Chapman's collection account](https://blogs.chapman.edu/collections/2015/09/28/hand-painted-movie-posters-from-ghana/) describes the objects' use and material histories. Their historical summaries are contextual testimony, not measurements of the images.

[Harman Projects/Deadly Prey's exhibition](https://www.harmanprojects.com/exhibitions/97-deadly-prey-movie-posters-from-ghana/) provides artist/date/material metadata for recent works. Recent reinterpretations are retained as a continuation, not projected backwards onto local mobile-video advertising. Exact dates and commission audiences remain unknown for several archival objects. Individual religious meanings, language choices and artist intentions are not inferred from appearance.

## Analysis

### Identity and Boundary

The source collections support a coherent painted advertising system independent of existing Polish Poster School, Slasher VHS Rental and Pen & Pixel entries. A designer following this entry would emphasize original modeled or deliberately flat pictorial scenes, expressive differences of scale, semantic short titles and a cloth-derived picture boundary. Following Polish Poster School instead could lead to symbolic compression without this material/commerce context; following VHS Rental would produce cassette/shelf packaging; Pen & Pixel would substitute digital collage and effects.

The practice is heterogeneous. G06 comedy, G16 martial-arts figures and G24 fantasy establish that weapons, monsters or gore cannot define the entire category. G30 is a recent flat/cartoon outlier against the heavily modeled figure tendency; it broadens the continuation without proving that this drawing mode dominated archival local advertising. G29's graphic violence is a contemporary reinterpretation and must not be treated as an inherent characteristic of family-film subject matter.

### Claim-to-Artifact Support

- Painted title/image hierarchy with subordinate operator inscriptions: G01–G08, G13–G24. Later works G09–G12/G25–G30 continue it.
- Large dominant figure with smaller episodes: G02/G04/G07/G13/G17/G19/G20/G22/G23. Single- or two-scene alternatives G10/G11/G18/G24 challenge compulsory crowding.
- Modeled muscles, skin, cloth and creature highlights: G01/G04/G07/G10/G17/G18/G23/G26; G30 is a genuine flat-drawing exception.
- Border/cloth perimeter as physical support: G01/G04/G05/G07/G17/G22/G24 and the clearly bounded contemporary G09–G12. Damage in G03/G06/G21 is object condition, not a design requirement.
- Typography flexibility: serif G07/G16/G19/G27, sans/condensed G02/G03/G15, rounded G09/G22, swash/script G24/G28/G29. No exact font identification was established.
- Historic local versus later international audience: source-supported by Poster House, Chapman and Harman context; appearance alone cannot establish who commissioned an individual undated object.

### Estimated Values and Proposed Web Translation

Colors were estimated from visual inspection, not sampled from calibrated originals. Proposed roles for an accessible contemporary carousel: warm paper #F5E9C7, dark ink #18242B, vermilion #B52F24, yellow #F1C44E and deep blue #173F5F. None is a canonical pigment specification. Title outlines/shadows can evoke painted lettering while the actual title stays selectable HTML.

Use a normal editorial image carousel or event/article card. Give portrait artwork a full uncropped field beside a quiet text panel, metadata and explicit previous/next controls. On mobile place the image above the copy. A title, description and slide number change together. Do not turn the showcase into a simulated video-club operating system. Original fictional artwork is appropriate; copied movie characters, marks or traceable artist compositions are unnecessary.

Stillness is faithful to the inspected medium. Any optional 160ms crossfade is a proposed web behavior, never an inferred historical rhythm. No autoplay; reduced-motion mode changes content immediately. Semantic buttons, visible focus, live announcements only on deliberate changes and preserved reading contrast matter more than copying physical damage.

### Confidence, Limits and Stop Decision

The corpus reaches the working target with three useful underlying collections, multiple artists, varied title placements and both historical/archive and later-market examples. Later batches reinforced the core hierarchy/material/scale traits while revealing meaningful exceptions in density, genre and flat drawing. The defining boundary is sufficiently supported for a concise entry. More images would not justify universal color, font, symbolic meaning or animation rules.

Limits: West African and non-Hollywood film subjects are underrepresented in accessible full media, despite source text documenting them. Four modest Chapman reproductions do not support fine texture measurements. Archival object dates and individual commission audiences are incomplete. Physical viewing could refine material claims; no claim of exhaustive community representation or artist-intent expertise is made.
