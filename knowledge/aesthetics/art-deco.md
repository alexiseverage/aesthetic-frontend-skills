# Art Deco

> Origin: A historical design movement that emerged in Paris before World War I, took public form around the 1925 Exposition internationale des arts décoratifs et industriels modernes, and spread internationally through architecture, interiors, graphics, transport, fashion, and product design. This pass is grounded in text sources only, so the frontend translation below should be treated as conservative synthesis rather than a corpus-backed visual canon.

## Analysis

Wikipedia's overview is enough to anchor the movement's core claims conservatively: Art Deco is geometric, polished, luxurious, and tied to machine-age confidence rather than to handmade nostalgia or purely functional modernism. The article explicitly links the movement to bold geometric forms, glamour, exoticized ornament sources, and premium as well as modern materials such as chrome plating, stainless steel, and plastic. Those details support a frontend interpretation built around symmetry, hard ornament, metallic or lacquered surfaces, and ceremonial hierarchy rather than around generic black-and-gold branding.

For web and app work, the clearest translation is to keep a modern functional core while using Deco as a presentation scaffold: sunburst or stepped hero framing, high-contrast panels, geometric dividers, premium material accents, and carefully rationed display typography. The movement reads strongest when one clear axis or ornamental frame organizes the page. Sprinkling unrelated zigzags or gold lines across otherwise generic UI produces pastiche rather than Deco structure.

Because this pass did not gather a visual corpus, regional specificity and sub-style claims should stay conservative. The cited source is enough to justify geometry, polish, symmetry, and glamour as load-bearing traits; it is not enough to treat every luxury-modern surface as canonically Art Deco. Motion should remain slow and optional, especially when using light sweeps, reveal sequences, or marquee effects.

## Connections

- `streamline-moderne` — a later sleeker descendant that keeps machine-age optimism but smooths Deco's stepped angularity into aerodynamic curves.
- `art-nouveau` — a useful contrast case: Art Nouveau centers whiplash curves and botanical flow, while Art Deco hardens ornament into geometry, polish, and axial glamour.
- `swiss-international` — the opposite pole in many interface contexts: Swiss style reduces ornament in favor of clarity, while Deco treats hierarchy and ceremony as part of the message.
