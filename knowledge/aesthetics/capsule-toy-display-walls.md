# Capsule Toy Display Walls

## Scope and classification

A proposed distinct vernacular combining repeated compact capsule-vending modules with their product-display inserts. The identity resides in the repeatable **placard → turning control/payment → retrieval** hierarchy, plus collectible-series comparison. It is not a name for all Japanese toys, a single manufacturer's brand, or a historical movement claimed by this profile.

This grouping differs from claw-machine cabinets, which stage one large transparent playable chamber above a separate control deck; capsule walls repeat small self-contained vending cells. It differs from digital gacha banner interfaces, whose event navigation, currency and one/ten actions surround a screen-filling campaign. Both relationships inform classification, not an assertion of identical origin or behavior.

## Direct Media Audit

41 complete source media were individually inspected. **31 qualifying unique artifacts**: 17 Takara product inserts, 7 Kitan product inserts, 2 Bandai cabinet presentations, and 5 separately identified Nagoya-area shop layouts. Ten exclusions consist of nine inadequate-detail historical cabinet images and one regional location map. There are no unresolved duplicate candidates, counted thumbnails, generated research images or measured-motion artifacts. Each complete product insert counts once, irrespective of the number of toy variants it depicts. Each shop is one independently identified layout; a second view of the same shop would not add an artifact.

The floor of 10 and target of 30 are met. Visual evidence spans three independent makers/publishers but remains concentrated in contemporary Japanese capsule retail. The shop illustrations for Noritake and APINA are presented as visualizations, not verified photographs. Small cabinet originals support module relationships and silhouette only; the much larger insert originals support text/frame observations. The last inserts added saturated magenta/yellow and less decorative arrangements without changing the title/variants/facts hierarchy. The source concentration and limited historical cabinet detail remain explicit; global prevalence and physical operation are not inferred.

Only source links and analytical descriptions are published. Original scans, licensed characters, photographs and manufacturer marks remain reference material, with no redistribution or derivative asset permission assumed.

## Analysis

Non-negotiable relationships are repeated self-contained vending modules, separation of product placard from control/retrieval hardware, and readable series/variant information. Common traits are white cases, colored circular controls, outlined titles, small price/count fields and an enlarged featured sample. Pastel colors, licensed characters, a particular coin value, faux Japanese copy and physically simulated random rewards are variants or unnecessary.

For websites, use a collection browser whose modules have an upper product/series insert, a circular clearly labeled disclosure action and a lower status or selection tray. Category filters and named variant chips can remain visible across the wall. A usefully accessible interpretation shows all variant choices and a deterministic detail view. It need not charge, randomize, or disguise selection as a transaction.

Controls must carry the geometry: rectangular case seams, circular handles that actually reveal details, separate price/count and technical fields, and consistent repeated spacing. Original small object assets can populate the inserts; a background photograph of a toy shop cannot substitute for this system. Responsive behavior is a proposed design: reduce the number of complete modules per row instead of shrinking their copy below legibility. Preserve keyboard focus, explicit action names, contrast and reduced-motion operation.

## Connections

- **Claw Machine Cabinets**: related physical amusement hardware; a large playable prize chamber and control deck distinguish claw machines from compact repeated vending modules.
- **Gacha Banner Interfaces**: related collection/series presentation but a digital event banner, currency/status strip and quantity actions define that candidate.
- **Kawaii**: overlapping soft palettes and rounded labels occur, but the vivid/dark insert counterexamples show that cuteness is not the defining wall structure.
- **Japanese Retail Pop**: possible adjacent sales signage, subject to current catalog classification; this proposal is bounded by capsule vending architecture and series inserts.
