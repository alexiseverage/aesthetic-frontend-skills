# Roadside Mini Golf

A playful roadside attraction language built from small curbed putting lanes, numbered stops, oversized landmarks and direct invitation signs. Covers geometric carpet courses and landscaped adventure variants. It is not all golf branding, indoor neon entertainment or generic theme-park scenery.

## Source / Evidence Links

- [John Margolies Roadside America collection](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/search/?q=miniature%20golf%20margolies): Original-transparency scans documenting distinct courses, signs and obstacles; selected full JPEGs inspected.
- [Castle Golf project portfolio](https://castlegolf.com/gallery/): Builder photographs of complete outdoor course projects and material details; course views grouped together.
- [Florida Historic Mini Golf Trail scorecard](https://floridahistoricgolftrail.com/media/2252/mini-golf-scorecard.pdf): Directly inspected practical scorecard and state heritage interpretation.

## Analysis

**Palette**: Green or teal playing surfaces with pale concrete paths and red, yellow, blue or brick-colored low borders are common. A bright blue lane counters green-only assumptions. Landmark colors may be saturated or naturalistic. UI colors are estimated from scans.

**Type**: Large GOLF names, clear arrows and compact numbered hole markers support orientation. Sign lettering varies from plain capitals to tilted scripts. Scorecards prioritize writable fields and legible labels; no universal font is claimed.

**Texture**: Coarse artificial turf, painted wood/metal, rough concrete or brick borders, sculpted scenic surfaces and landscaped water. Scan aging is not part of the original design recipe.

**Shape**: Narrow lanes widen into circles, T-shapes or irregular basins, turn around obstacles and pass through small openings. Oversized animals or miniaturized buildings turn each stop into a landmark.

**Motion**: Moving hazards and water exist in course contexts, but still photos cannot prove rotation speed, flashing or interaction timing. Proposed UI uses immediate step selection and optional 180 ms progress transitions, disabled for reduced motion.

**Spatial**: A bounded playing path differs from its surrounding walking route. Numbered stops and dominant landmarks make a compact world navigable. Contemporary courses can use broad landscaped lanes rather than many tiny figurative obstacles.

**Cultural markers**: Road trips, family outings, miniature worlds and local commercial ingenuity. Evidence is predominantly US courses, with historical scans and contemporary builder projects; this is not a universal history of the sport.

The interface translation should preserve the relationship between route, boundary and landmark. A green background with a golf photograph is insufficient. Natural putting and indoor neon variants are neighbors; the selected scope favors outdoor constructed lanes and roadside invitation. Historical scenery sometimes exoticizes cultures or borrows religious motifs; those examples inform boundaries rather than a production motif library. A state scorecard demonstrates an actual information pattern suitable for a useful interface.

## Connections

Atomic-age roadside graphics can overlap in eye-catching scale and invitation signage, while mini-golf lanes and obstacles form a distinct attraction system. Kidcore can overlap in playful color but does not require a navigable curbed course.
