# Analog-Era Test Instruments

## Scope and classification

The functional front-panel language of bench oscilloscopes, signal sources, meters and counters from the analog era. The label deliberately includes period digital readouts: it describes an industrial-design era, not an all-analog circuit specification. Calibrated quantities, units and local control grouping distinguish it from consumer listening equipment and speculative retro technology.

This is a new canonical entry, screened against current catalog slugs, labels, aliases, redirects and subset names. High-performance HMI concerns live process monitoring and alarm discipline, not the same physical instrument grammar. Cassette futurism extrapolates historical electronics into fiction. Silver-face hi-fi prioritizes listening sources, tuning and volume rather than calibrated laboratory measurement.

## Dimension Synthesis

### Palette

Neutral gray, cream or metal enclosures with dark legends, black control faces and selective colored channel/quantity coding. Green or blue-green display areas recur but are not compulsory.

### Type

Compact utilitarian legends close to controls, numerical scales, unit symbols and clear quantity grouping. Do not copy microscopic hardware labeling into a browser.

### Texture

Painted metal, engraved/printed scales, molded knobs, CRT graticules and occasional illuminated counters. Texture should suggest a fabricated measurement device rather than distressed fiction.

### Shape

Bounded instrument chassis, circular dials, selectors, sockets, rectangular meter windows and ordered scale divisions. Each geometry should communicate an operation or reading.

### Motion

The corpus comprises still front-panel photographs, so sweep speed and knob response are not measured historical facts. An interactive web waveform is a proposed explanatory model. Respect hold/stop and reduced motion; values must remain readable without animation.

### Spatial

Dense local clusters grouped by measured quantity or channel, with the display given a distinct reading zone. Alignment is functional and not necessarily a symmetrical consumer faceplate.

### Cultural markers

Voltage, frequency, time-base, channel, calibration and signal-path markings. Include coherent units and ranges; meaningless technical labels undermine the system.

## Analysis

### Claim-to-artifact support

Quantity/control grouping: Tek 465, 453, 547, 576 and HP 651B, 606B, 4260A. Era-digital counterexamples: HP 521C, 5340A and 405AR. Variation in chassis and display proportions is retained rather than normalized.

### Confidence and gaps

High confidence in panel grouping and calibrated geometry; moderate in tiny lettering and finish. Motion is unobserved in this still corpus, and proposed simulation motion remains an application decision.

Core identity is limited to the supported traits in the canonical non-negotiables. Counterexamples have been incorporated into the guidance rather than discarded to make a uniform mood board. No still-derived exact motion timing, measured physical performance or unverified source claim is promoted to fact.

### Application handoff

Connotation mode: **contemporary revival**. Artifact concept: **Bench 04 signal workbench**. Signature visual unit: measurement panel. Use a signal workbench with waveform choice, labeled frequency/amplitude ranges, channel selection, hold and a calibration disclosure. Provide a textual graph description and numerical outputs. On mobile, stack display above control groups while preserving label-control proximity. Simulation values must not imply real instrument accuracy.

These are **proposed application tokens**, not sampled historical colors or measured timings. Background `#d6d8ce`, surface `#bfc5bc`, text `#17251e`, muted `#465648`, accent `#235c46`, success `#d7e6ad`, danger `#8a3228`. Display `Arial, sans-serif` at `clamp(2rem,5vw,4.5rem)`, weight 700, line-height 1.02, tracking -0.035em; body `Arial, sans-serif` at 1rem/1.5, weight 400, tracking 0; labels 0.8125rem/1.3, weight 700, tracking 0.06em; numeric outputs use tabular figures at 1rem/1.3. Space 1–6: `4,8,12,20,32,48px`; radius `2px` for functional fields and `0` for panel edges (a circular dial/label retains its own geometry). Default state changes are instant; optional feedback `120ms ease-out` is a proposed web value, disabled under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`. No automatic decorative loop is required.

These tokens are a baseline for an original interface. Include the supported subject/identity, its information hierarchy and its operational/reading context. Avoid copied marks/art, generic palette swaps and inaccessible source-era constraints. The source references remain analytical; original procedural graphics or separately created images should carry the application.

### Accessibility and adaptation flags

Palette tokens do not certify interface contrast. Check actual foreground/background states, focus outlines, disabled controls and overlays; target WCAG AA text and non-text requirements. Body text should remain 16px or larger in the application. Responsive priority is content order and control usability, not a tiny scaled facsimile. Keep explicit text equivalents for graphs and decorative SVGs hidden from assistive technology when captions already describe them. Respect user zoom and narrow layouts.

Reduced motion: stillness or immediate state changes are valid. Any proposed motion is optional, interruptible and removed for reduced motion. Dark mode: retain the artifact's deliberate material palette within its scoped frame; adapt surrounding application chrome separately. Fatigue: limit the loudest texture/type to the defining field. Conflict rule: preserve the non-negotiables and task semantics before introducing a neighboring palette, font or transition.
