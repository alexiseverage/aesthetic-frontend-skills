# Wiki Encyclopedia Vernacular

## Scope and classification

A descriptive interface vernacular for structured, linked and maintained knowledge. The familiar Wikipedia article is an important anchor, but reference wikis also use dictionary senses, technical tables, travel guides, source-text editions, book chapters, quotation directories and community portals. The boundary is the document/reference and maintenance grammar, not a white-and-blue palette alone. Skin demonstrations count as authored layout systems; their placeholder content is not factual evidence.

This corpus contains 19 materially different skin systems and 11 live authored site/template systems. Shared MediaWiki chrome is explicitly acknowledged. The Wiktionary lexical hierarchy, Wikivoyage destination-banner/breadcrumb directory, Wikisource edition/volume index, Wikibooks book/chapter navigation, and Wikiquote alphabet/quotation-attribution directory are separate authored document macros, not extra counts for random articles in unchanged Vector. Each site’s multiple pages/states count once. A Citizen light/dark composite also counts once. The resulting sample is a design-system comparison, not a prevalence survey of websites or users.

## Source / Evidence Links

- [MediaWiki Manual: Interface](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Manual:Interface): primary documentation distinguishes the surrounding interface from article body and identifies tabs, sidebar and personal links. Skins can alter layout; article templates and surrounding skin are related but different evidence layers.
- [MediaWiki Design: Typography](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Design/Typography): primary design documentation specifies a serif heading stack and generic sans body for its Vector context, including the history of the 2014 font-stack change. It does not establish every present skin’s exact CSS or justify identifying fonts from pixels.
- [Wikimedia Codex typography](https://doc.wikimedia.org/codex/latest/style-guide/typography.html): current primary guidance favors readable neutral system-available faces and relative line-height, including multilingual clipping considerations. The proposed implementation draws on the principle, not an inferred universal historical token set.
- [MediaWiki Help: Links](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Help:Links): primary documentation describes default blue existing-page and red missing-page links, explicitly noting that themes can change colors. Link semantics must remain distinguishable beyond a copied color convention.
- [Commons skin-screenshot collection](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Screenshots_of_MediaWiki_skins) and [skin collection](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:MediaWiki_skins): discovery sources for published complete originals. Gallery thumbnails were not counted. Individual originals and file-page attribution are linked below.
- Eleven live systems were directly inspected: ArchWiki, OpenStreetMap Wiki, PmWiki, Foswiki, Wiktionary, Wikivoyage, Wikisource, Wikibooks, Bulbapedia, Nookipedia and Wikiquote. Live states establish only the actually viewed layout or invoked behavior, not every capability of their engines.

## Analysis

**Directly observed:** A linked reading plane and page-maintenance grammar recur despite radical changes in typography, sidebar position, palette and outer texture. Serif headings/sans body are common but not mandatory. Right infoboxes, numbered Contents, ruled tables, language or edition directories, source attributions and compact edit/history tools explain the aesthetic more reliably than a palette. Sparse Simple/Nostalgia and spacious Citizen/Erudite are retained variants, not averaged into one imaginary stylesheet.

**Source-supported:** MediaWiki documents separate interface/skin and article-body concerns, while its link documentation explains default existing/missing-page colors and theme exceptions. Vector typography documentation provides one real font-stack specification; it is not a claim that every inspected screenshot uses those fonts. Codex supplies a current readability and multilingual-system-font rationale. Screenshot dates, original skin release dates and current live behavior remain distinct.

**Measured samples:** Exact pixels from original screenshots: Modern at (20,10) #003366; Timeless at (20,300) #f3f3f3; Medik at (20,20) #ffbe00; Vector 2022 at (10,10) #ffffff. These are particular pixels, not universal tokens. Other proportions/type categories are visual estimates. No unsupported font recognition or motion timing measurement is claimed.

**Proposed application:** Open Almanac, an encyclopedia of imagined places, with a fictional Windline Observatory record. A neutral sidebar, thin ruled article hierarchy, original architectural elevation/plan diagram, floated infobox, linked Contents/citations, reading/discussion/history tools and a working local edit/preview/revision flow. All fictional records and local edits are labeled honestly. System Georgia/Arial, 16px body with 1.65 line-height, a larger reading option, white #ffffff / ink #202122 (16.13:1), link #3366cc / white (5.37:1), muted #54595d / white (7.09:1) and light wash #f8f9fa / ink (15.30:1). **Unknown or limited:** Exact CSS, fonts, timing, breakpoints and keyboard behavior of screenshot-only systems; all features not explicitly invoked in live views; authorship of every skin graphic; precise provenance of one Refreshed screenshot. PmWiki’s inspected History view had no revision rows, and Foswiki’s full one-sheet documentation body was not loaded. Bulbapedia ads obscured parts of lower content; visible infobox/Contents grammar was inspected, advertising excluded.
