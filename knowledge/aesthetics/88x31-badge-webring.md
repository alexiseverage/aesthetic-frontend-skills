## Analysis

88×31 badge & webring culture is a tightly constrained micro-system: a fixed 88×31-pixel canvas, Silkscreen-style bitmap lettering, a badge grammar (small footer images promoting sites, standards, or affiliations), and webring navigation (a ring of linked sites navigated via "previous/next" buttons). The format was popularized in the 1990s, especially on GeoCities, and "Best viewed in..." buttons emerged during the browser wars.

The system is distinct from `early-internet` (which names 88×31 badges as one marker among many) because it isolates the badge/webring grammar as its own ruleset: the fixed canvas, the bitmap type, the badge composition, and the ring navigation. The archive culture is well documented — Tim Bornholdt's archive alone contains 29,257 unique buttons scraped from GeoCities.

For frontend translation, the useful rules are: a fixed 88×31 canvas, Silkscreen-style bitmap lettering, limited 2–4 color palettes, beveled button effects, and badge-strip/webring navigation composition. The result should read as a 1990s badge, not as a modern button.
