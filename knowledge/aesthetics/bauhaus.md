## Historical / Origin Notes

The Bauhaus operated as a German art school from 1919 to 1933, combining crafts and fine arts while trying to unify individual artistic vision with mass production and functional design.

## Notable practitioners, artifacts, regions, or institutions

- Walter Gropius, Marcel Breuer, László Moholy-Nagy, Paul Klee, Wassily Kandinsky, and the Weimar/Dessau/Bernau sites are the key anchors.
- The movement matters across architecture, furniture, typography, product design, and pedagogy; UI translation should treat it as a systems school, not just a primary-color mood board.

## Analysis

Bauhaus turns workshop modernism into a coherent language of geometric reduction, industrial honesty, and teaching-through-form, balancing abstraction with production discipline.

Non-negotiables for implementation: geometric primary forms; function-led hierarchy; industrial/material honesty; modular composition with pedagogical clarity.

Cultural sensitivity assessment: Low identity-risk but historically specific. Bauhaus belongs to interwar German modernism, migration under political pressure, and mass-production debates. Avoid collapsing it into generic startup minimalism.

## Connections
