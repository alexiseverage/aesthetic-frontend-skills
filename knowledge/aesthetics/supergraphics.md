# Supergraphics

## Scope and Source Context

Oversized graphic interventions that respond to architectural scale, edges, corners, openings or continuous surfaces. Letterforms, stripes, silhouettes and pattern act as part of a spatial composition. This is a useful visual system within environmental design, not a synonym for every sign, large printed poster or colorful interior.

[SFMOMA: Strips of Stripes](https://www.sfmoma.org/exhibition/barbara-stauffacher-solomon-strips-of-stripes/); [SFMOMA: Going Big Together](https://www.sfmoma.org/read/brett-macfadden-supergraphics-going-big-together/); [AIGA SF: Barbara Stauffacher Solomon](https://aigasf.org/barbara-stauffacher-solomon/); [Morag Myerscough](https://moragmyerscough.com/commissions); [Sussman/Prejza](https://sussmanprejza.com/projects). Individual Pentagram creator case studies are linked with the inspected works below.

Barbara Stauffacher Solomon's Sea Ranch work is a foundational reference; SFMOMA's retrospective writing and exhibition dates differ between 1965 and 1966, so c1965–66 is more responsible than an unsupported exact first year. The inspected Moonraker photograph documents a later condition, not unchanged original paint. Her 2018 BAMPFA Art Wall and 2019 LAXART intervention demonstrate later continuities, while the St Moritz WELCOME installation opened in December 2022. Contemporary creator examples extend the grammar into schools, healthcare, museums, transit and public spaces.

Myerscough's case studies connect color/pattern to site, people and collaborative making. Dazzle Dining Rooms includes words developed with young people and poet Lemn Sissay. Sheffield's clinical context does not prove a medical outcome. Sussman/Prejza's LA84 case supplies a large multicolor environmental program; an erroneous repeated client field in the site template was not adopted. Pentagram cases give project-specific authorship and typography: Rockwell at Achievement First, Verlag at Parking54, Zapata at Frank Sinatra School, DIN at Penn and Jets Bold at the training center. None is a universal font rule.

## Analysis

### Claim-to-Artifact Map and Boundary

S01/S02/S06/S22 establish that graphic bands can connect wall, ceiling, stair and entrance edges. S04 uses an optical ceiling as a single dominant plane; S03/S05 demonstrate bounded school/clinical applications. S08/S10/S11/S12 use lettering and tessellation to make social spaces legible. S18's layered gateway joins large graphics with wayfinding without reducing the whole LA84 program to one sign.

S24/S28/S38 show oversized type organized by doors/columns/reveals. S25 and S26 make school identity architectural through wall/soffit and sawtooth relief. S33 integrates dimensional type with a curved desk and theater arches. S35 connects yard lines across planes. S37's slatted wall and S40's translucent glass expand material range; they are not painted murals. S39/S45 reveal a ribbon spanning museum landings. S41's large comic wall shows an image-led branch. S43 uses narrow tall type against exhibition glass; S46 a restrained stencil logo at facade scale.

S15's ordinary branded counter, S16/S42's print and S47's wearable are excluded. A large logo can qualify when it visibly participates in a building-scale composition, as S46; its small print use does not inherit that status. A full-building image too distant for the relevant graphics, as S17, adds no qualifying evidence. Do not count every identity output by a famous designer.

Distinct from Swiss International's editorial grid: a grid can support this system, but architectural scale/cropping is the defining move. Op Art supplies some perceptual pattern but is not required; Memphis supplies playful geometry but not necessarily surface continuity. Bauhaus shares simple geometry and has a different educational/historical program. These are related entries, not aliases or parent subsets.

### Proposed Web Translation

A three-day cultural festival schedule with real accessible tabs and independently saved sessions. A broad original loop/ray graphic is cropped by a dominant header plane; oversized day numerals repeat its scale in the adjoining program rail. Time, place, title and save control remain plain text. This is a contemporary graphic analogy for adjoining screen regions, not a simulation of architectural wayfinding.

Proposed roles: ivory#F5F1E7, ink#18231D, red#D84832, yellow#F4CE45 and blue#2554BF. Exact values are new screen choices, visually estimated from role relationships. Ink/ivory and ivory/blue are reading pairs; saturated decorative color is not automatically safe for small text. System Arial Black/Arial supports bold functional type with no font payload. Use square edges, large continuous curves and a clear quiet reading zone; no generic card shadows, grain or 3D room.

Responsive proposal: a split header above three equal day tabs and one reading panel; mobile scales the header without splitting actual words and stacks save controls below session copy. Keep real titles uncut; crop only decorative forms. Roving tabs use Left/Right/Home/End and click, panel labels stay associated, save toggles expose pressed state and a text count. No autoplay. Immediate color/panel changes are a proposed still interface behavior, not historic timing. Reduced motion remains still.

### Stop Decision, Review and Limits

Target met with30 unique installed programs across paint, letters, glass, pavement, temporary exhibition, clinical, school and transit settings. Final cases added translucent/data-related stripes and restrained facade typography, correcting a bias toward opaque multicolor pattern. Enough variation supports the core scale/surface relationship without treating every environmental design as an example.

Review method: direct full-media inspection, creator/context triangulation, project-level deduplication and repository validators; no independent reviewer claimed. No measured animation, calibrated color, accessibility certification or medical-effect claim. Corpus geography and famous-practice concentration remain explicit limits. Additional views are evidence of a single project, never a way to pad counts.

## Connections

- **swiss-international**: shared hierarchy and legibility; graphic surfaces replace a primarily editorial-grid identity.
- **op-art**: optical repetition is one branch, not a required effect.
- **memphis**: playful shapes overlap; Supergraphics relies on large surface relationships rather than scattered motifs.
- **bauhaus**: shared geometric economy with different historical and spatial priorities.
- Painted geometric interventions, typographic wayfinding, optical ceilings and translucent window sequences remain internal variants, not separate canonical slugs.
