|---|---|---|---|

### Context and limits

The [Cincinnati collection description](https://cdm16998.contentdm.oclc.org/digital/collection/p16998coll31) identifies circus/theater work by Strobridge. The [Henry Ford artifact account](https://www.thehenryford.org/collections/explore/artifact/134668) explains advance publicity and local letterpress date strips. The [NFCA collection](https://sheffield.ac.uk/library/special-collections-heritage-and-archives/national-fairground-and-circus-archive) situates fairs/circuses within travelling showpeople's culture and business. The [Henry Ford curated set](https://www.thehenryford.org/collections/explore/sets/detail/posters-from-the-golden-age-of-the-circus?AssetId=THF38118) also contextualizes harmful colonial displays and animal treatment. These are contextual claims, not deductions from bright lettering. A Library of Congress guide was discovered but its full page returned 403 and contributes no visual count.

Historical advertising includes exaggerated promises, racialized depictions, human exhibition and captive-animal spectacle. Analyze those as historical evidence without copying them into neutral decoration. Living showpeople's craft is broader than those practices. A contemporary revival can carry lettering, framing, local program fields and printed material logic through original non-figurative ornament.

### Application guidance

Propose a fictional travelling fair broadside with a large outlined name, tightly ranked attraction lines, original ornamental wheel and a clearly separate ticket-office strip. Native controls choose a session and quantity; issuing a keepsake changes a ticket stub, while reset remains clear. Exact palette, system-font substitutions, breaks and immediate state changes are proposed values. Keep critical information on opaque paper; do not manufacture historical timing or require flashing bulbs. A print-rooted still composition is coherent.

### Seven-dimension synthesis
- Palette
  - Canonical: hot red, bright yellow, royal/cobalt blue, black, cream/aged paper, occasional emerald or carnival pink.
  - Common: high-contrast complementary pairings and multicolour fields segmented by type blocks.
  - Variant: monochrome or limited two-ink jobs for cheaper/local print runs.
- Type
  - Canonical: stacked display lettering, condensed capitals, slab/deco/showcard forms, aggressive hierarchy, dense listings of acts and attractions.
  - Common: mixed type weights, curved banners, outlined or shadowed lettering, exclamatory punctuation.
  - Variant: hand-painted or overprinted venue/date fields.
- Texture
  - Canonical: paper wear, paste-up seams, ink saturation variance, letterpress or offset roughness, sun-faded color.
  - Common: overprint mismatch, weathering, creases, cheap-paper grain.

- Shape
  - Canonical: vertical broadside rectangles, medallions, starbursts, scrolls, badge shapes, panel dividers.

- Motion
  - Canonical: implied kinetic excitement through type angle, circus gesture, and starburst emphasis rather than actual animation.
  - Common: upward thrust, performer poses, action vignettes.

- Spatial
  - Canonical: maximal information density; headline at top, spectacle image cluster, dense act copy, asymmetrical sidebars, little empty space.
  - Common: sectional stacking with strong poster border logic.
  - Variant: one huge central figure dominating smaller attraction text.
- Cultural markers
  - Canonical: clowns, animal acts, equestrian acts, trapeze/highwire, boxing kangaroo/novelty acts, fair dates, city names, showman branding, overprinted locality data. Source: Fossett poster; NFCA overview.
  - Common: all-ages spectacle promises, “1000 smiles an hour”-style copy, touring-show rhetoric.
  - Variant: world’s-fair / exposition crossover or seaside amusement park adjacencies.

### Related-aesthetic boundaries
- Vs. `wpa-poster-style`: both are poster-led, but WPA is disciplined civic simplification; carnival poster art is louder, denser, more sensational, and less formally restrained.
- Vs. `art-deco`: deco may inform letterforms, but carnival posters are commercial spectacle vernacular rather than luxury-modernist polish.
- Vs. `vintage-circus` generally: this slug should center printed poster language, not circus interiors, costumes, or props alone.
- Vs. `maximalism`: overlap in density, but carnival poster art is structured by show-broadside hierarchy, not eclectic domestic abundance.
