# Otherworld Domestic

An explicitly contemporary, editorial design synthesis derived from *Coraline*: a recognizable home, a seductive counterpart, and the visible construction of objects and rooms. This is a new catalog category, not a claim that the source creators or a historical community used this name.

### Counting and inspection method

Original creator-hosted images were opened individually. Book pages were inspected as full published pages. The art compilation was surveyed at full page size, after which selected complete reproductions were inspected individually; smaller multi-work pages received isolated PDF renders where necessary. Unselected grids, thumbnails, quote pages and title pages are not counted. Inspection used 1432 × 1050 full-page renders and useful individual reproductions, not a claim to have examined the original production masters.

Each artwork is identified separately from its container PDF. Page and `viewrect` selectors retain the actual inspected subresource. The stock URL auditor removes PDF fragments and consequently collapses the corpus to 14 URL-level identities, still above the 10-work floor. An audit preserving actual document subresource selectors verifies 30 artwork identities with the same eligibility and duplicate rules. This distinction is disclosed rather than presenting a PDF URL count as an artwork count.

Motion inspection sampled live playback, not search thumbnails or transcripts alone:

- [Hand-Making Coraline](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jXqqd0ZBEMA): 142–171s and 285–327s; craft handling, garden surfaces and practical animation examples.
- [Armature test](https://player.vimeo.com/video/806925063?h=48441a07d7&loop=1&autoplay=1): repeated playback of the 4.254249s media, sampled at 1.0025s and 1.4608s; distinct walking poses. The duration is media metadata, not a measured cinematic rhythm.
- [Rebuilding the Coraline Puppet](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=exRD3jFktGI): hand/armature detail near 211s and replacement-face/hair discussion at 365–383s. This is a distinct restoration recording; advertisements and reused film inserts are not extra works.

### Limits and stopping decision

The 30-work target and seven-dimension coverage are met. The last selected batch added meaningful room variants and then reinforced the existing material/threshold grammar; further indiscriminate character or landscape collecting would not strengthen this domestic category. Evidence remains concentrated in one literary/film lineage by design. It supports an interpretive system, not a broad independent movement or population-level frequency claims.

No exact historical hex colors, named production fonts, CSS easing curves or responsive breakpoints were measured. The first-edition McKean interiors were not directly inspected at adequate resolution; claims about their expressionist perspective remain source-supported by the scholarly chapter. The Riddell preview is explicitly a later interpretation. These limits do not make film-specific details universal to the book.

Non-negotiable traits are domestic familiarity plus alteration/doubling, material identity, and spatial/scale estrangement. Keys, buttons, violet and elaborate wallpaper are common or optional signals. They are insufficient alone.

## Analysis

### Origin and classification

Classify as a new **emerging-hybrid** entry with a declared interpretive name. Existing catalog identities, aliases, redirects and descriptions were screened against Coraline, domestic uncanny, stop-motion, miniature and dollhouse terms. The closest entries are Storybook Gothic, Whimsigothic, Storybook Spooky Cute, Dreamcore/Weirdcore and Folk Horror Pastoral. None makes a constructed domestic double its organizing rule.

The underlying narrative is older than this catalog synthesis. Attribution belongs to the book's author, its edition-specific illustrators, and the film's collaborative design/animation team. Uesugi, Krall, Klassen, Schuhler, Choi, Breton and Appelhans offer complementary design work; no single artist is made the author of the whole film's style.

### Claim-to-artifact map

| Claim | Evidence and status | Boundary / implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Familiar comfort becomes suspicious through a counterpart | Book premise and Selick interviews, source-supported; `uesugi-room-pair`, directly observed | A cozy room alone is insufficient |
| Thresholds and depth organize attention | `riddell-door`, `uesugi-hall`, `breton-threshold`, directly observed | Layer framing around readable task space |
| Material individuality carries identity | `krall-living`, `schuhler-unpacking`, three recordings, directly observed | Whole-screen noise is a weak substitute |
| Palette is relational, not uniformly purple | `uesugi-rain-house`, `uesugi-room-pair`, `klassen-kitchen-pair`, monochrome room drawings, directly observed | Preserve restrained and saturated variants |
| Expressive display can coexist with readable text | Circus/apartment lettering and book-page hierarchy, directly observed | Exact font stack remains a proposed translation |
| Intentional stutter is not a defining requirement | Museum account of ones/twos, source-supported; armature playback, directly observed | Do not fabricate a universal 12fps recipe |
| Stable navigation is appropriate for application | Contemporary accessibility/interaction decision, inferred design guidance | Source menace must not become coercive UX |

### Interface translation and external pattern reference

[Haptic Lab's Chicago Quilt product page](https://www.hapticlab.com/products/chicago-map-quilt) was inspected at 1280 × 720 in initial product/variant state and with its native CARE disclosure opened. The large material-image grid, plain prices, variant controls and expandable supporting details are useful functional patterns. Its newsletter interruption was observed and dismissed; an inline invitation is the proposed alternative. This site is not classified as Otherworld Domestic and is excluded from aesthetic evidence counts.

The application should be a complete, comprehensible shop or reading surface, not a widget sampler. A room image can supply the threshold; three original products supply household materiality; variant choices and an explicit reversible bag supply useful state. A world switch changes presentation while keeping content and focus stable. These are proposed frontend behaviors, not observed historical web conventions of the novel or film.

### Rights and review

All external media are analytical links. The scholarly volume is open access, but its third-party film imagery retains separate rights. The educational compilation's availability does not grant reuse of its artwork. Original application assets should depict new rooms and objects without source characters, title logos or recognizable franchise replicas.

Review method: source-to-claim, identity/duplicate, dimension and nearest-neighbor self-review; no independent reviewer is claimed. The name remains explicitly synthetic, first-edition illustration access remains limited, and proposed UI values are labeled as such.

## Connections

- **Storybook Gothic:** emblem/folklore branding versus household counterpart and constructed depth.
- **Whimsigothic:** eccentric object salon versus a familiar room made uncannily different.
- **Storybook Spooky Cute:** reassuring gentle suspense versus sustained ambiguity; no automatic child-safety claim.
- **Dreamcore / Weirdcore:** digital memory/context collapse versus legible tactile domestic construction.
- **Folk Horror Pastoral:** communal land/custom versus intimate counterfeit home.
