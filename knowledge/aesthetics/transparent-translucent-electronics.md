## Dimension Synthesis

The following separates direct visual observations from proposed web translation. Color descriptions and relative geometry are estimated from visual inspection; no corpus-wide numerical sampling or measured animation is claimed.

| Dimension | Synthesis / scope | Confidence and limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Palette | Colorless clear, milky white, smoke gray and saturated translucent blue, purple, green, red and orange are all supported. Green boards, silver mechanisms, cream supports, black controls and small bright signals form an inner palette. A shell tints or softens what lies beneath; the same nominal color looks different at an edge, over a board and against an empty gap. No single candy palette is compulsory. | High for the inspected visual sample |
| Type | Outer shells often carry small functional legends, model identifiers, molded labels and restrained brand marks. Bold italic identities, colored button symbols, conventional keyboard legends and minimal technical labeling coexist. The lettering should clarify the control surface above the busy interior. The corpus does not establish one historical font family or a universal dot-matrix system; package and website type must not be mistaken for the device's lettering. | Medium for observed device labeling; font identities and any universal historic type system unverified |
| Texture | Clear molded edges catch narrow highlights; thick rims, ribs, screw bosses and overlapping parts create darker seams. Diffuse translucent plastic blurs the assembly into silhouettes. Other variants use flat laser-cut acrylic with visible tabs/bolts or a thin flexible welded envelope. Separate polished, matte, textured, metallic and rubber surfaces. A generic backdrop blur without a specific shell/interior relationship loses the construction vocabulary. | High for the inspected visual sample |
| Shape | Rounded molded handhelds, domes, tubes, circular drivers, rectangular sleeves and planar assembled cases all occur. Internal geometry should correspond to the enclosure: fasteners meet supports, controls sit above actuators, ports interrupt edges, and ribs follow the shell. A few coherent parts communicate more than arbitrary circuit squiggles or decorative screws scattered across a flat card. | High for the inspected visual sample |
| Motion | The research uses still photographs and static technical pages. It does not establish a common animation rhythm, LED pulse, operating sound or device-response timing. Intentional stillness supports inspection of layers. A contemporary timer or progress indicator may show real state changes; proposed reveal transitions must become immediate under reduced motion, and a status light must never be the only state label. | Unknown historical behavior; still composition directly observed |
| Spatial | The key hierarchy is outer shell, inner assembly and readable functional plane. Air gaps, clear edges, overlapping ribs, contrasting boards and raised controls establish depth. The screen or input surface may remain opaque while the surrounding housing is clear. Make functional hierarchy stable even when a viewer changes tint or inspects the interior; transparency is not a reason to put essential copy directly over every trace and component. | High for the inspected visual sample |
| Cultural markers | Demonstration, curiosity about mechanisms, hobbyist assembly, tactile consumer technology and selective nostalgia are useful references. Late-1990s clear hardware is one prominent context, not the origin of the entire practice. Institutions also used visibility for inspection and security, with different meanings from consumer playfulness. Attribute those contexts without romanticizing confinement or suggesting that a clear casing universally satisfies a present institution's rules. | Medium; supporting context and variable examples |
