# Greek Black-Figure

## Scope and Sources

A distinct material/process tradition suitable for a canonical entry, not a claim that all ancient Greek art shares one style. The [Met's technique essay](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/athenian-vase-painting-black-and-red-figure-techniques) establishes the difference between dark slip figures with incised/added detail and reserved red figures with painted detail. The [Museum of Cycladic Art](https://cycladic.gr/en/essay/i-aggeioplastiki-kai-aggeiografia-tiw-archaikis-periodou/) identifies Corinthian origins, Attic adoption, regional continuations and subjects beyond mythology. [J. D. Beazley's revised study](https://publishing.cdlib.org/ucpressebooks/public/book/development-of-the-attic-black-figure-revised-edition.html) provides a scholarly technical boundary and differentiated workshops/periods. Its historical terminology and attribution judgments should be read as scholarly claims, not a current archaeological census.

Museum collection records supply titles, dates and qualified maker attributions. Direct pixels were inspected separately. The [Getty kyathos](https://www.getty.edu/art/collection/object/103W2J) explicitly documents black-figure on white slip and an unusual myth configuration, reinforcing that both orange-only and one-story rules would be too narrow. The Fitzwilliam context page was found through search but direct retrieval returned503; its collection viewer was already unavailable during discovery. It adds no qualifying image or essential uncorroborated claim.

## Analysis

The stable defining system is dark filled figuration with contrasting incised interior detail, bounded figure/ground organization and a limited material color vocabulary. Clay ground, black mass and ornament act as composition rather than a generic antique overlay. The first Cleveland batch introduced white ground and late Paestan color variation; the middle batch established animal bands, quiet dark zones, early scale ornament and large eye fields; the final batch showed frontal horses and dense multi-register scenes. Met additions corroborated everyday work, isolated figure scale, athletic overlap and a flat plaque; Getty native detail independently confirmed incision on white slip. Later additions refined variants rather than overturning that grammar. Target and representative coverage are met.

Non-negotiable for a recognizable contemporary quotation: articulated dark silhouettes, strong figure/ground contrast and deliberate narrative/register hierarchy. Common but optional: orange ground, myth subject matter, profile figures, palmettes and added red/white. Variant: white slip, simple regional birds, early animal/scale patterns, unusually isolated figures and late southern Italian color emphasis. A glossy black body with ornament alone is insufficient, hence the excluded face. Red-figure is a neighboring technique rather than a palette variation; mythology alone has no classificatory force.
