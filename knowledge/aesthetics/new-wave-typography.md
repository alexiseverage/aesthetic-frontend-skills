# New Wave Typography / Swiss Punk

> Origin: A late-20th-century typographic approach that deliberately breaks strict Swiss grid orthodoxy through irregular spacing, angle shifts, weight collisions, and expressive composition. Provenance is strongest for the anti-grid typographic definition itself; interface translation should therefore stay careful about where expressive disruption helps and where it destroys usability.

## Analysis

The defining evidence for New Wave typography is formal rather than thematic: the cited overview explicitly identifies grid defiance, inconsistent spacing, weight variation within words, and angled typesetting. That supports a frontend translation in which type itself becomes the visual engine — especially in editorial heroes, poster-like landing pages, event graphics, and arts-culture sites.

Representative interface patterns are oversized typographic hero blocks, off-axis pull quotes, angled labels, abrupt cropping, and navigation systems where type clusters define mood before icons or illustrations do. The style is strongest in expressive editorial or campaign contexts where visual friction is part of the message.

Accessibility constraints are sharp. Irregular spacing, angle changes, and overlapping type can all undermine readability if extended beyond display contexts. Body copy, forms, and core navigation should usually remain more disciplined than the hero treatment, and motion should not add further instability to already-tense compositions.

Anti-patterns for implementation: using New Wave distortion for all text, rotating dense paragraphs, mistaking random misalignment for typographic intelligence, and importing the label without the post-Swiss tension between discipline and rebellion. The evidence supports a targeted expressive typographic system, not generalized chaos.

### Source-supported context

- [LACMA's Weingart curatorial note](https://collections.lacma.org/object/207627) grounds the experiments in typesetting craft, layered phototypesetting/offset screens and visible halftone/moiré rather than in arbitrary digital chaos. It identifies a precursor to digital design, not a movement invented by desktop software.
- [Cooper Hewitt's Dan Friedman Collection](https://www.cooperhewitt.org/2015/05/20/the-dan-friedman-collection/) documents Hofmann/Friedman/Weingart/Greiman connections and a continuing interest in structures alongside expressive diagonals. The collection includes admired predecessors as well as practitioners' own work, requiring classification per object.
- [MoMA's 2025 Greiman installation](https://www.moma.org/calendar/exhibitions/5827) describes hybrid handmade/mechanically reproduced imagery, Swiss and West Coast influences, and her first Macintosh in 1984. The corpus includes pre-computer work and later digital-print experimentation; digital pixelation is a strand, not the origin of all New Wave typography.
- [MoMA's Greiman interview](https://www.moma.org/magazine/articles/1347) provides a creator-led follow-up context. No uninspected interview media is counted. HMCT's collection finding aid was discovered, but its full page returned 403 during this pass and adds no qualifying artifact.

### Dated analysis / promotion corrections

New Wave is not one recipe of a sans-serif headline rotated over CMYK blocks. It reconsiders how type is constructed, reproduced, spaced and encountered, retaining visible systems even when reading becomes plural. The predecessor and quiet practitioner records prevent the claim that either famous authorship or a diagonal alone establishes the identity. McCoy's concept-pair construction brings a semantic strand beyond purely formal letterplay.

The initial profile's mandatory restrained palette, exclusively sans-serif requirement, exclusively geometric shape rule and default directional animation are corrected. The corpus contains saturated multicolor hybrids, serif/script contrasts, organic masks and intentionally still print. Clear critical information may coexist with experimental display; essential labels and paragraphs should remain readable in a digital translation. Historic print's deliberate ambiguity does not excuse inaccessible controls.

Do not collapse this into punk-zine roughness, generic Memphis confetti, 1990s distressed typography or contemporary kinetic-type spectacle.
