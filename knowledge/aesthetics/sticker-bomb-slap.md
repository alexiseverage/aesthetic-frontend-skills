## Analysis

Sticker-bomb / slap materiality is defined by its physical medium and its composition rule, not by any single subject. The medium is adhesive vinyl (or paper postal labels) with die-cut edges, layered and weathered. The composition rule is dense overlap — covering a surface with many stickers until there is no negative space, each sticker from a different hand or source.

The culture is graffiti writing: individual stickers are "slaps," placing them is "getting up," and the goal is visibility, repetition, and name recognition. There is a documented exchange/trading culture, with some collections exceeding 10,000 stickers. The spectrum runs from hand-drawn tags on blank postal labels (graffiti culture) to mass-printed vinyl (design/brand background), with the interesting work in between.

For frontend translation, the useful rules are: many competing saturated colors, varied lettering from many sources, die-cut silhouettes with irregular overlap, and dense no-negative-space layering with weathering. The result should read as accumulated street materiality, not as a clean collage.
