# Early Video-Sharing Channels

## Scope and classification

A separately useful descriptive interface vernacular, adjacent to MySpace Chaos and skeuomorphic controls. The defining combination is an authored page skin with persistent channel identity, landscape video and visibly boxed archive/social modules. It is not a historical self-named art movement. The inspected core is specifically YouTube, mainly museum-labeled 2007–2010; the broad catalog label must not imply uniform design across Vimeo, Dailymotion or all video sites.

Three source collections yield 30 distinct channel skins: 26 Web Design Museum pages, one creator-published Flickr capture and three retrospective screenshots from Websins. Repeated platform templates are acknowledged, but the channel publishers and authored colors/backgrounds/mastheads differ. Different posts, repeated screenshots or crops of the same skin were not counted separately. This is a purposive design sample, not a frequency survey of all channels.

## Source / Evidence Links

- [YouTube, December 16 2008: New, wider layout for your channel](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/new-wider-layout-for-your-channel/): a primary announcement specifies a move to 960px standard channels and asks creators to adapt backgrounds. It establishes a dated width change, not modern responsive behavior.
- [Brian Glick / YouTube, July 3 2009, updated September 16: Update on New Channels](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/update-on-new-channels_1788/): primary update discusses transparency, grid-default and upload/playlist ordering work. It records planned September 30 user and October 14 partner transitions. A plan is not independent proof of every account’s actual switch date.
- [YouTube Creator Blog, December 8 2011: ’Tis the season to decorate](https://youtube-creators.googleblog.com/2011/12/tis-season-to-decorate-make-your.html): primary notice distinguishes basic-channel transition from partners’ optional adoption and describes templates. It supports a transition boundary rather than one immutable early layout.
- [Web Design Museum: YouTube channels exhibition](https://www.webdesignmuseum.org/exhibitions/youtube-channels): useful preserved full-height originals and archive links. Exact loaded asset dates can differ from exhibit labels. Individual page links accompany each inspected artifact below.
- [Thagirion’s creator-published screenshot](https://www.flickr.com/photos/thagirion/6965753985): uploader identifies the old personal channel and records a June 25 2009 capture; Flickr upload is March 2012. Dates are self-reported, not independently authenticated.
- [Websins retrospective, February 2017](https://websins.wordpress.com/2017/02/18/the-evolution-de-evolution-of-youtube-channel-design/): original-size screenshots provide additional creator skins and before/after boundaries. Exact dates are retrospective attributions. The author’s judgments and unrelated offensive wording are not adopted.

## Analysis

**Directly observed:** Channel-specific backgrounds and module color schemes coexist with recognizable shared utility controls. Earlier profile-left and later player/list compositions differ materially. Serif text, plain-color pages and missing custom banners are valid variants, so neither an all-sans font rule nor mandatory maximalism is defensible. Later group shells may have subtle rounding; internally the composition remains boxed and dense.

**Source-supported:** Primary 2008–2011 announcements establish design changes, background customization, transparency and differing rollout treatment. They do not authenticate every museum date. The RHCP capture labeled 2007 displays an I’m With You campaign, and the PlayStation capture labeled 2008 displays Uncharted 2; these are specifically flagged as mixed-date archive states. Their visible modules remain useful evidence, but neither campaign is asserted to have run in the label year.

**Measured analytical samples:** Exact RGB pixels from retained full originals, not universal palette swatches: Hilary (10,700) #4e1d3b; Sesame Street (250,280) #0fa23a; Boxxy (20,700) #5f1718; RCA (600,400) #01458e. Texture pixels and compression can vary nearby. Layout widths/proportions elsewhere are visual estimates, not extracted CSS. No source fonts or timing values have been identified from screenshots.

**Proposed translation:** A fictional handmade-video channel with its own repeating paper-boat tile, bold masthead, colored square module strips, profile column, actual landscape clips, finite upload browser, personal favorites/ratings and a local guestbook. Use opaque readable panels rather than reproducing low contrast. System Arial and a chunky masthead category are application choices. Proposed ink #19363b / paper #f0f5e8 gives 11.60:1; white / #2e5b58 bars 7.63:1; link #164c85 / paper 7.87:1; ink / yellow #ffe286 10.09:1. These pair calculations are not a whole-interface accessibility certification.

**Unknown:** Historical autoplay defaults, exact DOM order, browser-specific behavior, transition easing, mobile layouts, font identities, authorship of individual brand skin graphics and exact dates of some loaded assets. Static spinners do not establish motion style. Generated application clips contribute zero historical artifacts. Still chrome with user-initiated media is a deliberate contemporary adaptation; the visual identity does not depend on decorative page animation.

**Boundaries:** The complete 803×747 Kaizenamazen screenshot is an earlier unskinned biography/latest-video profile; the 1440×760 Machinima screenshot is a later gray-gradient channel shell with Featured/Feed/Videos tabs and pale about column. Both were inspected and excluded from the 30 core count. MySpace pages lack this stable video archive/player hierarchy, while contemporary short-video feeds use a dominant changing portrait plane and overlay rail. Generic branded video cards alone are insufficient.
