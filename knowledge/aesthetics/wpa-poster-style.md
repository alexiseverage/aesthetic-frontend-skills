## Historical / Origin Notes

WPA poster style emerged through the New Deal’s Works Progress Administration and the Federal Art Project between 1935 and 1943, employing artists to create posters and other public-facing visual material as both relief work and civic communication.

## Notable practitioners, artifacts, regions, or institutions

- National park posters, public-health and theatre notices, and Federal Art Project production are the core references.
- UI translation should preserve the style’s public-service and labor-relief context instead of flattening it into generic rustic nostalgia.

## Analysis

WPA Poster Style turns limited-budget public communication into bold civic clarity: flat color, integrated type, and symbolic illustration scaled for common audiences.

Non-negotiables for implementation: limited flat-print palette; silkscreen/poster texture; declarative centered hierarchy; explicit public-service framing.

Cultural sensitivity assessment: Medium context risk. The style is deeply tied to Depression-era U.S. state programs, labor relief, and national mythmaking. Avoid using it as contextless “retro outdoors” branding.
