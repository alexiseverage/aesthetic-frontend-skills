### Visual evidence summary
- nordics.info identifies the cluster clearly: murky atmosphere, dark narratives, flawed protagonists, and a dark underside to the welfare-state image. Source: nordics.info.
- Jensen’s abstract is especially useful for implementation: setting, climate, light, and language are not accidental atmosphere but explicit production values for internationalized Danish TV drama. Source: Jensen/DOI page.
- Hedling frames Nordic noir as transnational popular culture built through dark wintry landscapes, co-production networks, and recognizable regional styling. Source: Frames Cinema Journal.
- Nestingen’s summary adds a thematic distinction that affects visuals: humanized criminals rather than monstrous abstractions. Source: UW.
- Vanden Bossche’s broader noir essay helps preserve the low-key / visual-noir basis so the style does not devolve into generic Scandinavian minimalism with crime copy pasted on top. Source: Academic Journals.

### Seven-dimension synthesis
- Palette
  - Canonical: charcoal, slate blue, cold grey, winter white, desaturated teal, sodium amber, black water, dirty concrete.
  - Common: blood-dark red accents, fluorescent institutional green, pale skin against blue-grey environments.
  - Variant: overcast summer daylight or icy silver when works like `Insomnia` push noir into perpetual light rather than darkness.
- Type
  - Canonical: restrained sans-serif, public-institution clarity, subtitles/signage feel, no flamboyant display type.
  - Common: condensed case files, transport signage, broadcast lower-third sobriety.
  - Variant: luxury serif intrusion only for upper-class rot or political establishment contrast.
- Texture
  - Canonical: wet asphalt, wool, fog, sleet, concrete, glass condensation, coarse knit, dim interior matte surfaces.
  - Common: grainy low-light image, reflective puddles, frost, window haze.
  - Variant: hyper-clean modern interiors used to intensify emotional coldness.
- Shape
  - Canonical: utilitarian geometry, harsh horizontals, bridge lines, apartment blocks, institutional interiors, practical outerwear silhouettes.
  - Common: long corridors, docks, shorelines, road lines, police-file rectangles.
  - Variant: rugged natural topography breaking into strict built environments.
- Motion
  - Canonical: deliberate pacing, slow pursuit, windshield glide, drifting weather, minimal gestural excess.
  - Common: procedural movement, surveillance gaze, patient reveal.
  - Variant: sudden bursts of violence puncturing an otherwise controlled tempo.
- Spatial
  - Canonical: emptiness, weather-separated figures, modern interiors with emotional distance, liminal transport/bridge/harbor zones.
  - Common: horizon-heavy exteriors, institutional rooms, underlit domestic spaces.
  - Variant: occasional crowded city cores, but still emotionally sparse.
- Cultural markers
  - Canonical: welfare-state institutions under strain, detectives as flawed professionals, wintry/damp settings, border/bridge spaces, social critique, humanized criminality. Source: nordics.info; Jensen; Hedling; UW.
  - Common: bilingual/transnational spaces, municipal architecture, subdued domestic realism.
  - Variant: daylight-noir inversions and eco/geopolitical extensions.

### Related-aesthetic boundaries
- Vs. `scandinavian-modern`: Scandi Modern is warm-democratic and daylight-functional; Scandi Noir is cold, suspicious, socially bruised, and often wetter/darker.
- Vs. classic `film-noir`: Scandi noir inherits low-key mood but replaces urban American chiaroscuro glamour with welfare-state realism, overcast exteriors, and institutional chill.
- Vs. `techno-noir`: Scandi noir is not neon-futurist or surveillance-interface heavy by default; its power comes from social realism and climate.
- Vs. generic minimalist crime drama: the Nordic signal requires climate/place plus welfare-state critique, not merely sparse typography and dark blue color.
