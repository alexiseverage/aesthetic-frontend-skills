Evidence count: 4 sources ([CS1]-[CS4])

### Source bibliography / URLs

- [CS1] is a key early mainstream explanation of the style as “post-human” / “nu-tribal,” linking it to Grimes, Y2K tribal revival, H.R. Giger, and climate/doom-era fantasy coping.
- [CS2] shows how the term “cybersigilism” stabilized by 2025 and identifies common line qualities plus scenes and practitioners.
- [CS3] is brand-forward and should be treated cautiously, but it is useful for consolidating the style’s formal vocabulary: black ink, mirrored anatomy-hugging forms, occult “sigil” framing, fashion translation.
- [CS4] is not about cybersigilism directly, but it is useful as a negative boundary: older cybergoth is rave/PVC/neon/goggles, not the same thing.

### Visual evidence summary

- The core look is fine-lined, greyscale or black-only, vein-like, spiked, anatomical, and cybernetic, reading somewhere between tribal tattoo, circuitry, alien scars, and biomechanical ornament [CS1][CS2][CS3].
- Designs tend to snake across sternum, arms, shoulders, back, or face-adjacent fashion surfaces, hugging the body as if they are skin circuitry or scar tissue [CS1][CS3].
- Visual influences include Y2K tribal tattoos, H.R. Giger biomechanics, gothic/baroque flourish, black-metal-like aggression, and digitally drafted symmetry [CS1][CS2][CS3].
- Scene context matters: gallery/rave/tattoo subcultures in London, Berlin, Bushwick, Kreuzberg, Seoul, and fashion-adjacent nightlife appear repeatedly in source descriptions [CS2].

### Seven-dimension synthesis

- Palette: overwhelmingly black or greyscale on skin or black fabric; chrome can appear in adjacent styling, but the linework itself is usually monochrome [CS1][CS2][CS3].
- Type: usually minimal or absent; when typography appears in fashion translations, it should feel sharp, coded, sigilic, black-metal-adjacent, or techno-occult rather than rounded cyberpunk UI [CS3].
- Texture: tattoo linework, scarification illusion, polished black embroidery, mirrored spikes, skin-tight printed graphics, biomechanical filigree, and hard/soft contrast between clean vectors and fleshy substrate [CS1][CS2][CS3].
- Shape: barbed curves, mirrored sigils, thorned anatomy paths, rib-like arcs, blade points, tribal hooks, circuit traces, and symmetrical organic aggression [CS1][CS2][CS3].
- Motion: serpentine growth, reveal along body contours, or subtle pulse; too much neon-tech animation risks collapsing into cyberpunk/cybergoth [CS1][CS4].
- Spatial: body-centric and contour-following rather than environmental; the “space” is skin, garment panel, or intimate object surface [CS1][CS3].
- Cultural markers: Grimes/post-human beauty discourse, Y2K tribal revival, H.R. Giger, rave-nightlife edges, tattoo flash culture, occult “sigil” language, and end-of-world escapist self-customization [CS1][CS2][CS3].

### Related-aesthetic boundaries

- Distinct from `cyberpunk`: cyberpunk is neon megacity worldbuilding; cybersigilism is a body ornament/tattoo language with much less environmental narrative and much more monochrome anatomy [CS1][CS4].
- Distinct from `cybergoth`: cybergoth uses goggles, PVC, rave gear, fluorescent tubing, and black-plus-neon contrast; cybersigilism is mostly black, spiked, veinal, and tattoo-derived [CS4].
- Distinct from `tribal` tattoo revival: the style openly inherits tribal line energy, but its contemporary framing is cybernetic, occult-coded, and post-human rather than simply late-1990s club tattoo revival [CS1][CS2].

### Implementation notes for dictionary entries

- Non-negotiables: black linework + anatomy-hugging sigil geometry + post-human / biomechanical connotation.
- Call out that the label is emergent and scene-based rather than academically stabilized; a precise single origin is hard to prove [CS1][CS2].
- Cultural-sensitivity caution: sources themselves warn that the trend rides an uncomfortable line around “tribal” revival and white users distancing themselves from whiteness via decontextualized tribal borrowing; any entry should acknowledge that critique directly [CS1].
- For web implementation, focus on line system and body-ornament logic, not just “cyber = chrome.” Too much chrome collapses the look into generic Y2K cyberpunk.

### Website-showcase guidance

Assigned component: Toast/Notification.

- Build the toast like a sigil etched into glass or skin: razor-thin black lines, mirrored ornament, restrained metallic accents, and contour-hugging framing [CS2][CS3].
- Keep the component monochrome-first; one icy chrome accent is enough.
- Microinteractions should feel like an activation rune or body-scan pulse, not a dashboard status chip [CS1][CS3].
- Avoid rave-neon goggles/PVC language unless explicitly blending with cybergoth, which this entry should not do [CS4].
