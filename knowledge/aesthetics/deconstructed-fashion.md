## Dimension Synthesis

The following separates direct visual observations from proposed web translation. Color descriptions and relative geometry are estimated from visual inspection; no corpus-wide numerical sampling or measured animation is claimed.

| Dimension | Synthesis / scope | Confidence and limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Palette | Black, off-white, gray, beige and muted work-cloth colors recur, often distinguished through fabric rather than hue. White cut-work, red foundation-wear constructions, floral utility pieces and saturated scarf assemblies are important counterexamples. A neutral atelier palette is a usable variant, not a requirement to desaturate every reference. | High for the inspected visual sample |
| Type | Garments may preserve pattern numbers, grain arrows, preparation notes, material lettering or labels. These are functional traces of making and provenance rather than one coherent display-type system. Some works have no visible lettering. Blank labels and particular attachment stitches can be house signatures; do not copy them as generic branding. Historical web typography and exact raster lettering families remain unverified. | Medium for process lettering/labels; no universal font or historic interface type system established |
| Texture | The join matters: a smooth lining beside rough weave, a frayed cut beside a finished cuff, coarse canvas against transparent muslin, dropped knit stitches or visible paper folds. Different surfaces retain their identities. Distress should follow a seam, interruption or material transformation; an indiscriminate dirty overlay conceals the very construction the aesthetic reveals. | High for the inspected visual sample |
| Shape | Recognizable clothing parts are displaced, interrupted or reassigned. Examples include forward armholes, unequal front panels, a trouser waistband used as a neckline, outer-layer openings and pockets or straps incorporated into a dress. Shapes may be sharply planar, softly draped or awkwardly fitted. An irregular outline is useful when it discloses a relationship, not when it merely makes text difficult to read. | High for the inspected visual sample |
| Motion | The inspected evidence is still garment photography. No universal runway rhythm, card-flip timing or garment-adjustment behavior is established. A contemporary interface can reveal an inner layer, isolate a panel or compare construction states immediately. Intentional stillness supports close comparison; reduced motion must retain every view and operation. | Unknown historical behavior; still composition directly observed |
| Spatial | Inner/outer, front/back and complete/partial relationships become visible through gaps, offsets, asymmetric layers and different lengths. Familiar landmarks such as a lapel, button, pocket or cuff keep the altered garment legible. Negative space can expose what would usually be covered. Keep a stable reading order beneath visual displacement. | High for the inspected visual sample |
| Cultural markers | Tailoring, pattern cutting, knit construction, workshop labor, reuse and criticism of polished fashion conventions locate the vocabulary. Some designers question garment obsolescence, standardized bodies or the value assigned to materials and skilled labor. These are documented local concerns, not a promise that every unfinished-looking object is sustainable, inexpensive or politically radical. | Medium; supporting context and variable examples |

Thirty qualifying garment works/comparative artifacts span eight clothing designers and five museum collections: NGV 15, KCI 7, MoMu 6, MAD 1 and V&A 1. Half the sample comes from one NGV exhibition publication, and many works belong to Comme des Garçons-related labels or Margiela. This is not a measure of prevalence or a complete global history. Different views, front/back details and repeated styling are grouped. One 1997 Stockman vest photograph may repeat the draped-bustier construction and remains unresolved/unqualified. Two garments shown together are conservatively one comparative record. Torn exhibition-paper backdrops are not treated as clothing traits. Met HTML requests returned 429; its tested public API object exposed no original image, and limited web representations added no visual count. Galliera requests returned 503. Museo del Traje full-image links show a teddy coat and denim outfit instead of the described objects; an MAD image also conflicts with its accompanying description. All remain unqualified. Some NGV source captions contain date typos, and artist pages exposed no additional legible originals. All qualifying records are stills: no runway timing, fabric movement, real adjustability, interface behavior or breakpoints are measured. Ordinary oversized, sculptural, decorated or neatly finished archetypes remain boundaries where construction change is not established.

### Translation and remaining uncertainty

The defining palette/material, shape and spatial relationships have strong direct support within this bounded sample. Type and motion must retain their stated limitations. Proposed colors, web spacing, breakpoints, animation duration and controls in the canonical entry are design decisions, not measurements recovered from static imagery. No font family is identified without metadata. Cultural narratives beyond the linked source descriptions remain outside the claim scope.

Review method: single-agent comparison of individual originals, repeated-garment and underlying-work grouping, source-caption checks and mechanical ledger audit. This is not independent review or an exhaustive historical survey.

## Connections

Compared with 1990s-minimalism, deconstructivist-architecture and wabi-sabi-slow-living. Clothing retains specific joins, pattern pieces, inner/outer relationships and garment-function reversals rather than generic architectural fragmentation, quiet fashion photography or an undifferentiated imperfection mood. Ordinary sculptural volume, underwear visibility, kimono-derived flat construction and decorated biker jackets are not automatically canonical. The category is a critical aid, not an exclusive claim of designer self-identification or a single origin story.
