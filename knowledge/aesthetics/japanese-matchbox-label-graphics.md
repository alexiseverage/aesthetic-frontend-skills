# Japanese Matchbox Label Graphics

A compact Japanese commercial-print vernacular spanning domestic retail, export and promotional matchbox labels. Small rectangular surfaces integrate an emblem, figure, geometric field, lettering and business identity with striking economy. The commercial context unites diverse styles: ornate export labels and spare modern cafe graphics are branches, not one compulsory national palette. A generic Japanese illustration or decorative pseudo-Japanese text is insufficient.

- [Tokyo Metropolitan Library](https://www.library.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/english/bunkaweek/2022/matchlabel/): Distinguishes domestic retail, export and advertising labels; its Kaga scrapbook context grounds the classification.

- [Kuandachakupt, 2013 design research](https://ir.library.osaka-u.ac.jp/repo/ouka/all/56286/jjsd63_114.pdf): Research abstract identifies export-market adaptation and Japanese/Thai exchange; the abstract is context, not a media count.
- [Jane McDevitt / Maraid original collection](https://maraid.co.uk/blog/2015/01/japanese-matchbox-labels/): Owner-published complete scans. Individual designers/dates are often unknown; collector dating is not an independently verified issue date.
- [Takeshi Yokomizo / BMLBS](https://www.phillumeny.com/2021/08/18/exhibitor-biography-takeshi-yokomizo-japan/): Two independently identified export-label scans; collector identifies market and period.
- [David Freund collection account](https://designobserver.com/david-freunds-collection-of-japanese-matchboxes/): A collector-authored discussion available as text, including the problem of historical racist caricature. The ordinary media fetch presented a security challenge, so no Freund media count is claimed.

## Direct Media Audit

30 unique useful still labels: 26 complete owner-published Maraid scans, two individually enlarged Tokyo Metropolitan Library labels, two complete Yokomizo/BMLBS export scans. The 26/2/2 distribution is deliberately disclosed: cafe/promotional graphics dominate, with retail/export boundary checks. Detailed original JPEG views reveal the lettering, halftone/line structure and picture-field relationships. No exact pixel dimensions were measured. The library scrapbook-sheet JPEGs and their PDF presentations were viewed for discovery only and count zero; enlarging those sheets did not yield additional original detail. Temporary analytical PDFs were cached outside publication and not used to manufacture a higher count. Freund media access failed; museumcollection.tokyo and its archive link were inaccessible through ordinary fetch. The target of 30 is met through useful full-label scans, not by adding every label in a contact sheet. No generated imagery, application screenshots or unviewed Flickr gallery items count. Final batches stabilized both sparse type-led and dense illustrated boundaries; individual attribution and worldwide reception remain limited.

## Analysis

**Palette**: Small, emphatic sets: black/pale with red; turquoise/navy/coral; yellow/black; green/pale; pink/red. Multiple rich colors occur in export scenes. Keep a bounded few-ink impression, not a mandatory beige-red scheme. Visible reproduction hues are estimates, not original ink samples.

**Type**: Handwritten Latin sans, narrow serif titles, loose script and vertical/horizontal Japanese writing coexist. Lettering is integrated into bands, corners, paths or picture openings. A modern English-language artifact can preserve this economy without invented Japanese glyphs. Tiny historical contact copy must become readable modern metadata.

**Texture**: Flat printed masses, halftone/stipple, fine parallel rules and paper/ink irregularity. Some fine hatch-heavy export illustrations differ from the spare cafe branch. Keep texture localized and subordinate to silhouette. Physical print method cannot be assigned universally from a scan.

**Shape**: Rectangular portrait or landscape labels use strong flat silhouettes, asymmetric cuts, diagonals, circle/emblem openings, bounded strips and deliberately interrupted fields. Dense ornate frames are an export branch, not a requirement. Generic rounded app cards lose the compact print structure.

**Motion**: Inspected labels are still. Motion timing and historical interface behavior are unknown. Keep printed fields stationary and use immediate carousel, selection or disclosure changes. Under reduced motion, preserve all endpoints and use no simulated burning, smoke or perpetual movement.

**Spatial**: Image and lettering interlock within a small bounded plane: a figure may cross a field split, a diagonal band may carry a title, or contact text may sit in a narrow foot strip. White space can be dominant. Scale the grammar up for usable controls, not a miniature fixed scene.

**Cultural markers**: Specific cafes, confectioners, entertainment venues, transport directions, manufacturers and export markets. Do not present all labels as an undifferentiated Japanese national essence. Religious export imagery, historical stereotypes and wartime propaganda retain particular contexts and are not decorative defaults.

The useful common system is compact commercial identification through integrated picture/lettering fields, not a single illustration genre. The library retail labels and Yokomizo export examples prevent the cafe-heavy sample being mistaken for the entire history. Neither a universally minimalist rule nor a universally ornate rule survives the corpus. New digital carousel/form mechanics are contemporary proposals; the labels establish graphic composition, not historic web behavior.
