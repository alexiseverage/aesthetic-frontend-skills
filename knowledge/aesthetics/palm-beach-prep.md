# Palm Beach Prep

## Source / Evidence Links

- [Cooper Hewitt’s Zuzek exhibition](https://www.cooperhewitt.org/exhibition/zuzek/) distinguishes Suzie Zuzek’s original drawings from the brighter colors used in screen-printed fabrics for Lilly Pulitzer. It identifies classic sportswear shapes paired with playful eclectic patterns, her work at Key West Hand Print Fabrics and the importance of crediting the textile designer. Archive drawing colors are not automatically finished-garment colorways.

## Analysis

Palm Beach Prep here means the print-led resort-preppy route associated with Palm Beach and the Zuzek/Lilly textile lineage: playful all-over drawing, vivid color relationships and composed casual forms. It is narrower than all Palm Beach architecture/interiors and broader than one pink-and-green floral logo. The corpus does not imply that every bird, flower or safari motif is local Florida wildlife.

Print structure is the strongest evidence. PB01–09/16/17 show large flowers or fruit over fine stripes, hatch, dots and small blooms. PB10–14/18/19/21–24 show animals, marine life, pictorial patches and interleaved grids. PB20 makes looping lettering part of pattern; PB25 contributes a club/sporting route. PB26–30 retain current color and scale variation. The artwork dates and subdued paper colors belong to drawings, while the museum explicitly distinguishes brighter printed fabrics. PB31/33 show simple garment boundaries and contrasting pale trim around dense pattern. PB32 is excluded because a sparse monochrome cropped shift photograph cannot establish the same visual relationships.

**Proposed frontend translation:** a fictional “Coral Court Weekender” planner and RSVP form. Build a bright original botanical/fruit repeat around a large cream writing plane, with a narrow green/pink striped binding, small original interlaced CC monogram and scalloped tab edges. A week selector changes ruled activity rows; an RSVP form offers date, party size and a local confirmation. Selected days can have a solid green printed-label marker and visible text. The pattern, border, tab, form grouping and control states should embody the aesthetic rather than decorating an otherwise generic image card.

Proposed application roles: warm paper `#FFF8E9`, deep palm `#175444`, raspberry `#B92768`, bright pink `#F27CAF`, lime `#B7CB58` and small turquoise `#3DAEA8`. These are design choices, not recovered print standards. Pair an approachable readable serif heading with a plain sans for dates/controls; one small original monogram can be expressive. Do not claim this type or scalloped stationery shape was measured as a universal historical rule. Keep full pattern in borders, tab surrounds or a narrow header; text and fields need opaque quiet planes.

Create an original repeat from independently drawn citrus arcs, leaf fans and loose blossoms; do not trace Zuzek, reuse hidden Lilly signatures, copy a current print or imply label endorsement. Brightness alone is insufficient: retain varied motif scale, an imperfect hand-drawn line, a controlled palette and a calm framed writing area. Printed borders and small stitched-looking rules can define focus and sections, but must not obscure native input outlines or selected state. Brand photographs remain research links and are not frontend assets.

The real controls need labels, visible focus, keyboard operation, text-based selected states and clear field validation. Keep readable targets larger than a small ornamental flower/monogram. On phones, stack the week and RSVP areas, preserve horizontal date order and leave enough space to read the activity. Reduce outer print coverage before reducing text size. Stillness is valid; if tabs or accordions transition, honor reduced motion and avoid animated all-over patterns or compulsory automatic carousels.

Credit the textile designer separately from the fashion label. The original drawings, screen-print adaptations, garment constructions and current recolors have different authorship/provenance questions. A museum exhibition or auction image does not grant reuse rights. Historical resort culture includes unequal access; the interface need not reproduce class/racial exclusion or a claim of inherited social status. Decorative elephant/safari motifs and their names are historical design subjects, not reasons to borrow cultural symbols without context or treat a region as an exotic playground.
