## Historical / Origin Notes

Metabolism was launched by young Japanese architects around the 1960 World Design Conference, proposing cities and buildings that could grow, renew, and replace parts in response to postwar urbanization and technological optimism.

## Analysis

Postwar Japanese megastructure futurism: modular capsules, infrastructural frames, expandable cities, exposed service systems, and urban growth imagined as biological metabolism rather than fixed composition.

Non-negotiables for implementation: modular growth logic; megastructure/infrastructure frame; postwar Japanese urban reconstruction context; capsule or replaceable-unit thinking.

Cultural sensitivity assessment: Japanese Metabolism is culturally specific to postwar Japan, reconstruction, rapid urbanization, and named architects/institutions. Respect its living architectural lineage and avoid exoticizing Japanese urbanism.
