# UFO Sighting Bulletins

## Dimension Synthesis

| Dimension | Observed system and variation | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Palette | Black on light paper dominates the NICAP and most Minnesota issues. International UFO Reporter also uses deep blue with yellow and orange, blue-gray, and a rose-brown monochrome. | High |
| Type | Large recognizable titles, volume and date marks, short article heads and caption styles organize reports. Typewritten wrappers coexist with formal typeset covers. | High |
| Texture | Gray screening, hatch detail, simple ink lines and the uneven density of scanned printing recur. Other pages are completely text-led. | High |
| Shape | Heavy horizontal rules stabilize the masthead. Boxed notices, field-register cells and rectangular halftones divide the story structures. | High |
| Motion | All archived front pages and wrappers in the sample are static. A case index is a digital translation. | High |
| Spatial | Two and three columns, a full-width lead with multiple sections underneath, and a narrow logistics or editorial rail are common. Some early wrappers use a single column. | High |
| Cultural markers | Issue dates and publishing credits establish the context. Notices, published corrections, editorial viewpoints and preliminary reports have distinct design treatments. | High for sampled English-language association publications |

## Direct Media Audit

Thirty-six distinct front pages or mailing wrappers qualify, including 20 NICAP issues, eight International UFO Reporter issues and eight Minnesota MUFON issues. Thirty-four PDF issues were temporarily fetched and their complete first pages were rendered at a readable size. Two other issues were audited from archive cover images. A 1979 double-issue PDF that exceeded the temporary download limit was excluded. Each qualifying row represents one issue. Reprinted illustrations, inside pages and separate views of a single issue contribute no additional count. The showcase includes original schematic drawings.

## Analysis

Column structure did much of the work. NICAP retained the same publishing vocabulary while changing masthead fashions and varying the ratio of text and exhibits. The International UFO Reporter added a mailed yellow wrapper with an independent rhythm, as well as saturated cover blocks that give issue contents and editorial text visibly different weights. Minnesota MUFON put the community's logistical needs next to a long lead report. Issue dates, credits, and distinct inset states let readers recognize separate kinds of statements.
