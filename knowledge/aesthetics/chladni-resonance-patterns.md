# Chladni Resonance Patterns

## Scope and Source Context

A descriptive catalog category for the visual grammar of particulate nodal figures on vibrating plates and their scientific diagrams. This is not a named historical graphic-design movement. Sand or comparable grains collect near nodal lines while motion displaces them from more active regions; boundary, material, mounting and excitation influence the result. Rings, parallel bands, crosses, curved lobes, loops and asymmetric branches all occur. A perfect radial mandala is not required.

[Harvard bowed plates](https://sciencedemonstrations.fas.harvard.edu/presentations/chladni-plates); [Harvard large driven plate](https://sciencedemonstrations.fas.harvard.edu/presentations/big-chladni-plate); [UNSW Music Acoustics](https://www.phys.unsw.edu.au/jw/chladni.html); [University of Toronto Experimental Nonlinear Physics](https://www.physics.utoronto.ca/nonlinear/chladni.html); [Science Museum Group: Chladni](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/people/cp37102/ernst-florens-friedrich-chladni).

Chladni's eighteenth-century demonstrations and1787 publication are a major historical reference; Science Museum Group also credits Hooke's1680 precursor. Do not erase that earlier work or imply modern cymatics began as a single graphic style. Ørsted's1810 engraved tables are distinct scientific representations. Contemporary teaching, acoustic research and instrument-making are continuities rather than revivals of a lost decorative language.

Harvard's bowed examples and driven plate explain mounting/excitation; their listed frequencies apply to their apparatus. UNSW explicitly distinguishes the plate outline, nodal grains, driving magnet and flash reflection. The source warns that white flash patches are not nodes. Toronto identifies material, dimensions and drive for four different specimens. Simplified membrane trigonometric models can suggest a drawing; they do not solve every elastic plate's flexural equation or yield calibrated frequencies. No healing, sacred-frequency, particle-physics or universal pitch-to-image claim follows from this corpus.

## Analysis

### Claim-to-Artifact Map and Boundaries

C05/C06/C09/C14/C22/C24 compare six actual brass boundaries. C07/C08/C10–C12/C15–C21/C23/C25–C29 retain contradictory density and symmetry without increasing the unique count. C30 establishes colored grains on a light field; C32's dark checkered substrate is visually evident but its material is not established by metadata. C33's double ring is a mode of that same object. C34/C35 establish a print-diagram branch whose crisp lines must not be confused with physically perfect grain placement.

C36/C37 and C38–C41 extend the grammar to wooden instrument parts; historical instrument dates are source metadata, not dates when the photographs were made. C42/C43 compare simple homogeneous plate shapes. C44 shows sparse edge arcs; C45–C48 show radically different high-density fields on circle/square/stadium/violin-shaped metal. C49's imperfect ring and C50's two parallel bands prevent compulsory complexity. C53 is modern diagram notation, and C54 confirms granular ring/edge-arc texture at an independent institution.

Bubble-Chamber Track Photography shares scientific observation and fine marks, but records particle trajectories in a photographic chamber rather than standing-wave nodes on a bounded plate. Op Art shares repeated geometry without requiring particulate accumulation or resonance. Orphism organizes color relationships; rainbow disks and musical analogy alone do not establish Chladni identity. Water ripples, ferrofluid, moiré and generic particle clouds are neighboring phenomena, not aliases.

### Stop Decision and Review Limits

Working target30 met after broadening from a heavily duplicated university collection to independent teaching labs, attributed salt experiments, historical tables and instrument studies. Later cases reinforce sparse/ring/parallel-band counterexamples and the same bounded-granular grammar; no remaining defining contradiction requires collection before authoring. The source mix is technical/educational, not evidence of an established frontend movement. Seven UNSW specimens rely on small native originals, offset by detailed independent photographs for texture. Exact physical settling laws, calibrated colors, comprehensive history and source typefaces remain unmeasured/unknown.

Review consists of direct visual inspection, source-context comparison, conservative specimen deduplication and repository validation. No independent reviewer or scientific simulation validation claimed.
