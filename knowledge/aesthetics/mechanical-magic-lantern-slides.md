# Mechanical Magic Lantern Slides

## Dimension Synthesis

| Dimension | Observed system and variation | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Palette | Bright paint seen through glass occupies a confined field. Blue, red, yellow and artist-specific combinations recur. Outside the image, amber wood and dark masks maintain contrast. | High for the material division |
| Type | Labels include identification and maker marks painted or attached to the support. Most pane text is part of a scene, as in the prank slide, or a principal subject, as in the Adieu chromatrope. | Moderate |
| Texture | Wood grain, metallic collars and glass reflections are distinct. Finely lined paint overlaps or crosses at patterned intervals. | High |
| Shape | Rectangular carriers commonly hold circular discs or rectangular panes. Notches, slots, tabs, and crank shafts sit next to the aperture. | High |
| Motion | Museum photos in two positions show discrete masking. The Randall–Salter and Martyn Jolly videos show changes in radial registration. The Rouse Hill recreation shows a localized pivot. The recordings cannot establish a historical tempo. | High for mechanisms, unknown for historical timing |
| Spatial | Controls usually sit outside the lit field, with much surrounding dark space. A focal word, figure or small rosette can remain central while the image around it moves. | High |
| Cultural markers | Some pieces reflect a popular repertoire of comic scenes and abstract effects. Some objects and reconstructive films represent different makers, periods and performance contexts. | Moderate |

## Direct Media Audit

The 30 qualifying objects comprise 24 physical artifacts and six individually identifiable projected works from three independently produced videos. One of the physical records uses a direct still, and 23 use full object views. Different positions, front and back views, and frames from the same projected work were grouped. The 11 ACMI photos were individually opened at 1200 pixels wide. Their source URLs lead to stable object pages because the media URLs expire. Other motion lectures and small comparative mechanism thumbnails were used for context and excluded.

## Analysis

The aperture controls what the audience can see, which gives otherwise intricate activity a stable frame. A slipping slide stages two registered appearances and changes which parts of the paint transmit light. Pivoting levers add a directional relationship. The chromatrope reorganizes overlapping geometry with rotations, and the Adieu and beehive examples show how a center can remain stable. Some photographed glass is relatively subdued under room light, while transmitted light reveals saturated color. Those lighting conditions are distinct.

A workable browser translation pairs an immediate control with an original fixed mask and independently drawn layers. This lets a reader compare states without reproducing an archived slide. The hand-operated model illustrates the direction and limit of movement. The modern playback sources document present-day viewing rather than a measured historical cadence. ## Connections

- **Stage Magic Lithography** advertises spectacle on a printed sheet. The slide holds a relationship between painterly content and projection hardware.
- **Paper Theatre** also partitions an audience view off from layered material supports. The lantern adds light transmission and optical change.
- **Op Art** can have similar radial and interference patterns. The user-operated aperture and painted material give lantern slides a different construction.
