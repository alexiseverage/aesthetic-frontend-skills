# Precisionism

Precisionism is an established American modernist category that makes buildings, infrastructure, machinery and other recognizable subjects legible through controlled geometric structure. The inspected works include factories and urban blocks, rural barns, a church, domestic still life and symbolic lettering. Deep perspective, flattened planes and later layered abstraction coexist. The category is broader than empty, smooth, monochrome factories.

**Evidence status:** thirty useful, deduplicated complete still works meet the ten-work floor and thirty-work target. This profile supports the locally authored canonical entry after maintainer self-review; publication is pending. Historical reference media are linked for analysis, not supplied as production assets. No historical interface, animation or typeface has been reconstructed.

## Direct Media Audit

The ledger contains **40 records**, including **37 complete-media inspections**, one restricted thumbnail, one uninspected inline version subsequently resolved as a duplicate, and one unavailable PDF lead. **Thirty unique still works qualify**. Two duplicate groups consolidate Demuth's Wren inline/full versions and Sheeler's Suspended Power detail/full versions. Six records remain unresolved and excluded. One fully inspected photograph is a resolved adjacent comparison, also excluded. No video, historical website or generated asset contributes to the count. No reference files were downloaded successfully for this corpus; the Rochester PDF request returned 404 and no file was retained.

Qualifying linked source presentations cover fifteen institutional domains: FAMSF, MoMA, Whitney, Dallas, Cleveland, NGA, Terra, Smithsonian American Art, Montclair, Detroit, Met, Carter, SFMOMA, Wichita and O’Keeffe Museum. These are not fifteen disjoint owning collections: FAMSF exhibits borrowed works, and O’Keeffe Museum's barn record points to the Met. The four Met Commons mirrors have documented exact-accession institutional donation provenance; they remain one Met collection, not an additional source collection. Diverse independent curatorial and owning records establish the range without counting mirrors as diversity.

Maker distribution is Sheeler nine, Demuth six, Crawford three, Lozowick three, Driggs two, O’Keeffe two, Spencer two, Schamberg one, Murphy one and Dickinson one. Most examples are interwar paintings and prints; Sheeler's 1953 work is explicitly a later continuation. The sample strongly favors industrial/architectural subjects. It does not constitute a survey of every artist or region.

Exclusions and limits:

- Met's Water was available only as a restricted 150 × 124 thumbnail. It does not qualify.
- FAMSF's Yankee Clipper and Rolling Power detail versions lack confirmed complete-work boundaries; they do not qualify. Suspended Power was resolved against Dallas's complete canvas photograph, including external frame/calibration material that is not an aesthetic motif.
- Dickinson's PAFA Still Life and NBMAA Abstraction, and Ault's Vilcek View from Brooklyn, were fully inspected but exact movement boundaries remain uncertain. They inform comparisons only.
- Bourke-White's Chrysler Tower photograph was fully inspected as a photographic/Art Deco/Constructivist relation. Industrial subject alone does not make it a core Precisionist work.
- The Rochester Whitestone Bridge PDF has cached searchable text but its actual download returned 404; neither that work nor a Ballet Mechanique illustration was visually inspected. Text is not a substitute for pixels.
- Cleveland's Church Street El source text and live rights notice conflict. Linked inspection establishes composition; no reuse permission is asserted.
- Spencer's two works and Lozowick's Cleveland are qualified by primary artist/movement context plus exact formal evidence. Those work-level boundary inferences are moderate confidence, unlike exact object tags or explicitly selected examples.

Collection stopped after reaching thirty useful works with resolved defining structure, substantial variation and deliberate counterexamples. The final batch reinforced repeated factory modules, stillness and spare rural frontage rather than inventing a new universal motif. Fine lettering attribution and photography's broader relationship remain enrichment opportunities; these gaps do not undermine the painting/print entry's defining grammar.

### 1. Morton Livingston Schamberg; FAMSF exhibition reproduction — Painting (formerly Machine), 1916

Complete vertical composition has a teal upright and horizontal arm, large off-center pale disc, dark backing discs, pale blue curved tubing and a yellow partial ring on textured cream. Spare peripheral space and repeated small circular joints establish a machine-like arrangement without realistic factory surroundings. Brushy paper/paint surfaces and softened edges contradict a universal immaculate photorealist reading. FAMSF describes its wire-stitching-machine subject and Cubist/Dada influences in the development of Precisionism; individual artist membership alone was not used for classification. Signature is small and secondary, not a font specification. Static implied mechanics establish no actual motion timing.

### 2. Charles Sheeler — Bucks County Barn, 1932

## Analysis

### Defining structure and claim map

The non-negotiable is a controlled geometric interpretation of recognizable subject matter, anchored in this historical category. Factory silhouettes and modeled cylinders support it in works 3–10, 17, 23–25 and 29; city prints and architecture in 11–15, 18, 21 and 26–28; rural structures in 2, 7 and 30; machines in 1, 5, 16 and 20; and domestic still life in 22. This cross-subject consistency supports high confidence in structure, not a claim that all subjects must be factories.

Spatial structure varies: works 6 and 9 open deep industrial space, 8 looks steeply downward, 17 drives a road toward a vanishing point, 22 tilts domestic planes, 25 layers later abstract overlays, and 30 emphasizes an almost frontal, exceptionally wide barn. A single centered symmetrical template would discard this variation. Proposed frontend layouts should preserve one original composition's massing and scale while keeping labels legible.

Works 2, 6, 19–22, 25–27 and 30 retain wood, paint, paper, leaf or atmospheric surface. This evidence qualifies the often-used “immaculate” shorthand: reduced handling and clear edges are an emphasis, not a requirement for textureless vectors. Restrained original SVG grain is an application proposal, not a recovered historic production method.

Letters become objects in works 14–16, 19, 23 and 27. Big numerals, serif/script commercial signs and mechanical indices support selective display treatment. They do not establish a movement-wide alphabet or justify setting every caption in period lettering. UI font families, sizes and spacing must be described as proposed values.

### Interpretation, people and boundaries

Museum context supports both admiration of technology and unease about its effects. The same orderly scene can have more than one interpretation; confidence should not be assigned from subject matter alone. The small workers in Suspended Power and pedestrians in Seventh Avenue disprove a mandatory depopulation rule. The entry should not turn the workforce into picturesque scale markers while erasing labor or risk.

Artist membership is not sufficient. The fully inspected but excluded Ault and Dickinson candidates remain useful evidence of uncertain borders. Bourke-White's tower photograph supplies an adjacent photographic comparison, not an automatic core count. Storrs' sculpture appears in the Met essay, but no complete useful sculpture was inspected here; a universal sculptural specification is therefore unsupported. The corpus supports a painting/print entry with source-supported photographic influence.

### Interface proposal and limits

A contemporary visual field notebook can organize original architectural studies, inspection notes and selected views through crisp planar imagery. A useful interaction changes a real selected study, note or saved record; it should not simulate a historic industrial control system. Use clear text labels and calm immediate updates. Layout breakpoints, dark-mode decisions, control colors, tokens and timing belong to the application specification.

Industrial blues and chalk/brick/graphite roles are one coherent proposal, not an exhaustive historic palette. Match accessible text contrast separately from artwork. Do not overlay tiny cream copy on blue artwork, require color-only selection, or animate smoke/rails under an unsupported “Precisionist motion” claim. Stillness is a valid translation.

## Connections

- `art-deco`: shared machine-age buildings and geometry; Precisionism emphasizes pictorial structure and varied meanings rather than mandatory luxury ornament, metal polish or axial ceremony.
- `constructivism`: diagonal planes and industrial subject overlap; revolutionary commands and photomontage are not defining Precisionist requirements.
- `futurism`: a documented influence; compressing every subject into speed, collision or militarist spectacle loses Precisionism's frequent stillness.
- `new-objectivity`: sober modern observation overlaps, but the Weimar social-critical lineage is different from this American geometric category and its varied attitudes.
- `shaker-design`: domestic craft can be a historical subject/influence; depicting a craft object is not the same as applying the religious community's design system.
- Purism and Cubism are documented influences, not existing catalog aliases. Machine Age is a broader era; American Scene and Regionalism are not identity synonyms. Schamberg, Driggs, O’Keeffe and Spencer retain their wider practices.
- Industrial/urban, rural/domestic, symbolic lettering, monochrome print and later overlay branches are useful prose variants, not additional canonical slugs.
