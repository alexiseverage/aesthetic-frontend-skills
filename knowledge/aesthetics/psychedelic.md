# Psychedelic

## Source Summary

This revision inspected 33 distinct complete poster/print designs from three museum collections: 26 V&A, three MoMA and four SFMOMA. The old profile was a text-only backfill and contributes no additional image count. The [media ledger](psychedelic/media-ledger.json) records exact inspected URLs, image dimensions, accessions, artist/technique attribution, rights and individual observations. Six Smithsonian image routes returned rejected HTML and remain unavailable/uninspected, rather than being counted from captions. Poster House and Whatcom image routes were inaccessible; their contextual text is separate evidence.

- [V&A collection, The Move at the Marquee](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O129219/): one of 26 complete individually catalogued works spanning Michael English/Hapshash, Martin Sharp, Michael McInnerney, Wes Wilson, Bonnie MacLean, Lee Conklin, Rick Griffin and Victor Moscoso. The public IIIF originals mostly have a native height/width of768px. They are full museum-served compositions, not enlarged search thumbnails: major lettering/line/pattern relations are legible, but exact printing measurements and some fine text are not.
- [MoMA, MacLean's Yardbirds/Doors](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/5475), [Pink Floyd](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/133411) and [The Who](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/133412): full2000px reproductions, allowing close printed-surface and outline inspection. The latter two are small-format lithographs in the catalogue; do not copy their dimensions onto a larger poster edition.
- [SFMOMA, Sopwith Camel NR5](https://www.sfmoma.org/artwork/2024.17/), plus NR2, NR4 and NR10: four complete native850px poster images with artist, date and copyright. Optical ink relationships, letter deformation and contour systems remain clear. No imaginary high-resolution version or animation was inferred.
- [Smithsonian American Art Museum, Wes Wilson](https://americanart.si.edu/artist/wes-wilson-27389): contextual writing connects his freehand lettering, printing practice, psychedelic experience and Viennese Secession reference. His claimed priority is an artist's claim, not proof that one person invented every branch.
- [V&A, history of the poster](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/a-short-history-of-the-poster): distinguishes British Hapshash screenprinting from the lithographic route of major San Francisco poster designers. There is no universal screenprint process for psychedelic graphics.
- [Whatcom Museum, Bonnie MacLean](https://www.whatcommuseum.org/exhibition/not-one-of-the-boys/): primary exhibition context restores her substantial contribution alongside the familiar male “big five.” Its text was read, but its photographs were not available for full inspection here.

Collection stopped above the30-work target after successive examples confirmed the main contour, letterform, optical and collage routes, while challenging compulsory neon, dense-fill and screenprint rules. This remains a1966–68 British/San Francisco music-ephemera sample with strong V&A concentration. Posters are not the entire aesthetic: liquid light shows, experimental film, album design, textiles, underground publications, non-Western scenes and later revivals need their own direct media. All historical imagery remains with its rights holders and is used for analysis only. No reference image is a cleared production asset.

## Dimension Synthesis

These are qualitative findings within a purposive poster corpus, not universal prevalence measurements.

| Dimension | Core | Common | Variant / limit |
|---|---|---|---|

## Historical / Origin Notes

The existing1966–72 counterculture frame remains useful, while this corpus concentrates on1966–68. Concert promoters, artists and printers developed a visually demanding event language. Lettering/ornament reworked Art Nouveau and Secessionist precedents in the context of music, communal experience and altered perception. British screenprint experimentation and San Francisco lithographic practice should be distinguished. MacLean's work is integral, not a footnote to a male “big five.” This evidence does not establish a singular first inventor or trace all psychedelic media to one city.

## Notable practitioners, artifacts, regions, or institutions

Wilson's pale Otis Rush and largely blue Blues Project posters complicate a neon-only rule. Michael English's enormous MOVE word supplies a strong nonfigurative typography route; the5th Dimension maze connects geometric optical pattern and symbolic border. Sharp's monochrome Live Give Love and explosive Hendrix differ from each other and from a generic liquid background. MacLean combines plume-like linework with swollen display blocks. Moscoso's Sopwith Camel NR5 leaves open orange space, while NR10 packs nested contours around photographic imagery.

## Analysis

A convincing expression makes type, contour, pattern and figure/ground act as one perceptual composition, with a traceable counterculture/poster lineage. It cannot be reduced to generic rainbow decoration. Neither maximum saturation, constant motion, complete unreadability nor one print process is universal. The sampled work also demonstrates a conventional fine-information layer beside highly expressive display zones. A digital interpretation can keep dates, access information and controls readable without erasing its identity.

### Application translation (proposed)

An imaginary evening programme can use an original swollen FAR/OUT wordmark, nested organic borders and a radial floral/optical emblem, with three event palettes/compositions selected by actual controls. Keep a readable programme and local itinerary beside the expressive poster. Flat inks and particular outline widths are proposed screen values, not sampled historic recipes. No musician, person, historic logo or protected poster needs to be reproduced. Immediate selection and intentional stillness are contemporary choices; avoid adding unsupported animated pulses merely because the aesthetic is called psychedelic.

## Connections

`pop-art` overlaps in print, popular imagery and bright color; an absolute irony-versus-sincerity split is too simple. `op-art` overlaps in optical vibration and geometry, but counterculture event lettering and organic/collage context give this corpus a different emphasis; calling Op Art universally clinical is misleading. `rave-flyer` shares music-event density and can also be warm, hand-made or organic, with a different historical/technological context. Preserve distinctions without reducing either neighbor to one palette.
