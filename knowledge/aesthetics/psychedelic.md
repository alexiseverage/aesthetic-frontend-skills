## Analysis

The most defensible provenance path for psychedelic design is the late-1960s concert-poster ecosystem, especially the work around Wes Wilson and related San Francisco poster culture. The museum and poster-archive sources make the lettering, optical intensity, and Art Nouveau revival lineages explicit.

Representative visual evidence is type-led but immersive: swollen letterforms, clashing high-saturation inks, and dense ornamental fill that makes reading part of the sensory event. The style fails when converted into tidy retro grooviness with legible headline hierarchy preserved.

Ethical and usage note: psychedelic language carries strong counterculture and drug-culture associations. It should not be treated as neutral decorative colorfulness in contexts where perceptual overload, intoxication coding, or legibility loss create accessibility or cultural mismatch problems.

## Connections

- `pop-art` — Pop Art is flatter, sharper, and media-ironic; psychedelic is immersive, curved, and sincerity-driven.
- `rave-flyer` — both can be overwhelming, but rave is synthetic cold neon and machine type where psychedelic is organic, warm, and hand-lettered.
- `op-art` — both manipulate perception, but Op Art is geometric and clinical while psychedelic is sensual and countercultural.
