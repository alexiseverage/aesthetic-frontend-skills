# Hotel Luggage Labels

## Scope and classification

Compact printed hotel/place identities designed as bounded labels for luggage: distinctive silhouettes, concentrated lettering and a building, emblem, scene or abstract mark. The corpus includes geometric, heraldic, typographic and illustrated branches. A single label must work as an identity object; random sticker accumulation is a different system, and neither Art Deco nor a circle is compulsory.

This is a new canonical entry, screened against current catalog slugs, labels, aliases, redirects and subset names. Art Deco and Plakatstil describe possible influences rather than the entire label tradition. Sticker-bomb/slap describes accumulated placement and subcultural circulation; this entry concerns one composed hospitality identity. Alpine posters are destination advertisements, usually not compact hotel labels.

## Dimension Synthesis

### Palette

Restricted ink palettes and clear color separations on light or dark paper. Red, blue, green, cream and black appear in varied combinations; a faded brown filter is not required.

### Type

Hotel name and location compressed into a memorable hierarchy: custom lettering, serif, sans, script, arcs and diagonals all occur. Match the chosen branch rather than mixing every historical letter style.

### Texture

Printed paper with flat ink, occasional shading and surviving wear. Use restrained paper cues; do not bury the identity beneath generated scratches.

### Shape

Circles, ovals, rectangles, triangles, diamonds, leaves and scalloped silhouettes. Internal borders and lettering should respond to the outer shape.

### Motion

The source artifacts are static. A manual label carousel may change instantly; intentional stillness preserves the small print-object character. Avoid autoplay and unnecessary floating/sticker animations.

### Spatial

A dense bounded object with hotel/place identity readable at a glance. Illustration and name compete within a small field; a modern collector folio can provide generous whitespace around the object.

### Cultural markers

Hotel and destination names, architecture, crests, transport networks and service/hospitality imagery. Period representations often exoticize people, animals and colonial places; document that history without reproducing it uncritically.
