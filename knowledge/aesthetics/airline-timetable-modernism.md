# Airline Timetable Modernism

## Scope and classification

The modernist corporate-information branch of printed airline timetables: strong carrier/date identity, geometric cover organization, route or index navigation and compact schedule/legend systems. The inspected corpus centers on 1960s editions with earlier/later context. It retains script, serif, photographic and illustrated exceptions; it is neither a claim that all timetable covers are Swiss design nor a digital airport departure-board aesthetic.

This is a new canonical entry, screened against current catalog slugs, labels, aliases, redirects and subset names. Swiss International can explain grid discipline, but script/serif and illustration are documented counterexamples. Destination posters foreground a place and promotional scene. Timetables foreground valid services and how to find/read them. Modern flipboards are another medium and should not replace the printed schedule grammar.

## Dimension Synthesis

### Palette

Limited carrier colors against white/cream paper with black or blue schedule text. Cyan, navy, red, yellow and orange recur across carriers; retain contrast and section logic rather than a universal blue identity.

### Type

Clear carrier/date hierarchy, prominent timetable naming and compact aligned service information. Sans modernist covers coexist with serif and script identities. Schedule typography must serve codes and numbers rather than mimic a poster headline everywhere.

### Texture

Ink on folded paper, printed photography, line maps and flat graphic fields. Aging is a condition of a surviving artifact, not a required design feature.

### Shape

Stripes, wedges, globes, arrows, route lines, rectangular bands and ruled numeric matrices. Cover geometry supplies identity while smaller tables carry operational content.

### Motion

Static print establishes no animation rate. For web translation, manual search/filter and expansion should keep rows stable; intentional stillness preserves comparison and reading. No animated aircraft or flipping characters are required.

### Spatial

Cover identity opens into indexes, maps, weekday keys, offices and schedules. Nested row/column alignment and multilingual legends are central information devices. Modern mobile layouts may stack labeled records instead of shrinking original foldouts.

### Cultural markers

Carrier identity, validity dates, local-time/service keys, destinations and routes. Historical networks can encode colonial power and exclusion; do not reproduce exoticizing people/places as decorative shorthand.
