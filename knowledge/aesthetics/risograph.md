## Historical / Origin Notes

Keep the existing 1980s–present technology and contemporary art-publishing frame, while separating the Japanese machine/process from later aesthetic associations. RISO describes perforated masters around ink drums; VCU documents scanned or computer-sent masters. Calling the process exclusively analog is inaccurate. Publisher and studio sources establish current craft practice without proving that cream paper, neon or visible registration error were universal historical features.

## Analysis

The earlier profile described a useful contemporary quotation but mistook several common styling devices for process requirements. The stronger core is controlled ink separation plus evidence of a printed support. Heavy grain, deliberate drift, a warm cream sheet and fluorescent duotone are optional choices. An actual printer can produce precise type, black-line art, screened photography and more than two passes.
