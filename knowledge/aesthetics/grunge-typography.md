## Analysis

Grunge typography is best grounded in the 1990s editorial break represented by David Carson and Ray Gun rather than in a vague notion of roughness. The sources consistently point to deconstruction, anti-grid hierarchy, and deliberate friction between legibility and emotional tone.

Representative visual evidence is typographic before it is chromatic: mismatched fonts, stressed spacing, image bleed, and distressed surfaces that feel made with professional tools but aimed against modernist order. This distinguishes the style from punk zine process-rawness and from early-internet accidental chaos.

Ethical and usage note: because the style intentionally impairs legibility, it should be limited to headers, posters, or atmospheric editorial surfaces. Treating it as a general UI language can create avoidable accessibility failures while preserving only the costume of rebellion.

## Connections

- `punk-zine` — punk is DIY-by-necessity and materially photocopied; grunge is designer-mediated distress and editorial deconstruction.
- `early-internet` — both can feel messy, but early-internet mess is platform-constraint chaos rather than authored typographic abrasion.
- `risograph` — both use print texture, but risograph is spot-ink/process craft while grunge is abrasion and anti-grid rhetoric.
