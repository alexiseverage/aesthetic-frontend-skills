# Origami

A material and structural vocabulary derived from folding paper: creases divide continuous sheets into planes, flaps, pleats, pockets and volumes. This entry covers figurative, modular and tessellated variants; it does not rename all polygonal illustration as origami.

## Analysis

**Palette**: No obligatory hue. O1–O10 and L1–L10 range from white and earthy sheets to saturated purple, blue and red. K3/K4/K6 expose reverse-side contrast. Proposed UI palette: warm ivory, coral and marine blue; values are application choices, not measurements of a universal palette.

**Type**: The modeled objects have no intrinsic type system. Use restrained, legible supporting labels; fold diagrams can use technical notation, but do not invent Japanese lettering as decoration.

**Texture**: Visible paper grain, crease ridges, tucked layers and localized fold shadows. Mostly matte examples coexist with glossy L9 and crumpled K5. Avoid uniform plastic bevels.

**Shape**: Planes connected by creases; triangle flaps and points are common, while bowls, curved loops and pleats are real counterexamples (O7, K2, K6). Modular and single-sheet constructions both occur.

**Motion**: No historical timing inferred from stills. UI reveal may suggest opening a flap, but instantaneous disclosure is the reduced-motion default; no perpetual folding animation is necessary.

**Spatial**: Layered depth, pockets and overlaps; repeated folds produce relief or volume. Keep content on broad stable faces, with creases and folded corners around usable areas.

**Cultural markers**: Origami has Japanese terminology and important Japanese traditions, alongside internationally authored contemporary practice. Credit named designers and folders; do not reduce the art to cranes or claim every model follows a single uncut-square rule.

Observed material continuity and layered edges are more diagnostic than triangles alone. Interface inference: construct a lesson accordion as joined paper panels with contrasting reverse-side folds and a visible crease spine. Disclosure remains semantic and operable; decorated corners never hold text or reduce hit areas. This application does not reproduce any inspected model or its folding instructions.

## Connections

Papercut uses cut silhouettes rather than folds. Low-poly can share faceted planes without paper continuity or tucked edges. Paper Quilling uses rolled strips and upright coils, a distinct construction. These are related material grammars, not aliases.
