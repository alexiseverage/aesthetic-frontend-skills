# Edo Yōkai Woodblock

## Scope and Sources

A separately useful printed-picture genre/system within Edo ukiyo-e and illustrated publishing, not an autonomous art movement. Late-eighteenth-century monster books and nineteenth-century supernatural sheets share contour/mark hierarchy, meaningful carved text-image relationships and extraordinary interactions with ordinary human/object settings. The classification is narrower than all Japanese folklore or all ukiyo-e. It permits culturally specific yūrei and theatrical narrative subjects without claiming every ghost is the same kind of being. The observed scope ends in1867; Edo dates differ slightly between institutions' period conventions.

Context sources: [Japan Foundation](https://www.jpf.go.jp/e/project/culture/exhibit/traveling/yokai_parade.html), [Edo-Tokyo Museum](https://www.edo-tokyo-museum.or.jp/en/s-exhibition/daiyokai/), [Ōta Museum](https://www.ukiyoe-ota-muse.jp/specters-by-tsukioka-yoshitoshi/). The Foundation exhibition uses high-quality replicas; exhibition publicity is contextual discovery, not a collection of inspected original works. Ōta separates the1865 series from1889–1892 Meiji work. [Tokyo Fuji Soma record](https://www.fujibi.or.jp/en/collection/artwork/09164/) identifies a particular literary adaptation. [Met Sekien book](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/78693) establishes1781 publishing provenance. [Mia Oiwa](https://collections.artsmia.org/art/65717/the-ghost-of-oiwa-katsushika-hokusai) and [Kohada](https://collections.artsmia.org/art/65734/the-ghost-of-kohada-koheiji-katsushika-hokusai) explain particular literary/kabuki narratives; these do not establish universal folklore origins. Artist/publisher/date claims below follow object records; visual claims follow actual inspected pixels.

## Analysis

The defining boundary is a combination of narrative print practice, image/text organization and controlled contour/field/detail relationships. Supernatural subject alone is insufficient. Against Shin-hanga, the period, publishing contexts and recurring extraordinary narrative relations matter more than blue atmosphere. Against Haunted Dark Ride, this remains a sheet/book grammar, not attraction staging. Against Expressionist Horror, darkness and distorted anatomy do not erase different cinematic/graphic lineages. This is a useful genre-level catalog entry, not proof of a single stylistically homogeneous movement.

Non-negotiable: grounded Edo print context or explicitly new quotation; controlled contour/fine marks against flat fields; purposeful narrative image/text hierarchy. Common: cartouches, cloth pattern, strong figure/background separation. Variants: monochrome books; blue-black/pale isolated apparition; busy colored warrior scene; orange burst; pale daylight interior; translucent gray spirits. Counterexamples reject obligatory rectangular labels, gigantic scale, nocturnal blue, grunge and literal supernatural ontology. The Three Heroes ghost is a trick; depicted religious objects are not decorative assets.

Software application is an inference: a cultural-event reservation sheet can place a large contemporary illustration next to a quiet form and a compact descriptive register. Native labeled radio choices, quantity, error correction, a clear local confirmation and return-to-edit behavior can be tested independently of the picture. Historical sources do not prove those interactions. This tests visual transfer beyond a folklore-themed wallpaper; avoid using monstrous art where reassurance or dense task throughput is primary.
