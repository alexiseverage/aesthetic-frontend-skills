## Dimension Synthesis

The following separates direct visual observations from proposed web translation. Color descriptions and relative geometry are estimated from visual inspection; no corpus-wide numerical sampling or measured animation is claimed.

| Dimension | Synthesis / scope | Confidence and limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Palette | Strong illustrated identity colors frame comparatively quiet reading wells: yellow/white, cyan/peach, teal/navy and purple/green occur alongside parchment/brown, charcoal/lime and pale custom-profile palettes. Small item and pet illustrations carry many local colors. Category color can distinguish destinations, while text needs stable contrast on its own surface. A yellow sidebar is a recognizable example, not a universal requirement. | High for the inspected visual sample |
| Type | Playful outlined, handwritten, script or image-lettered identities contrast with compact ordinary sans-serif navigation, labels and paragraphs. Serif story/profile treatments and decorative personal headings are supported variants. Bold category headers, underlined links, small stats and item names support dense scanning. Exact historical font families are mostly unverified; source CSS for an authored template is not proof every portal used that family. Avoid making functional copy illegibly small to imitate an old screenshot. | High for observed display/body hierarchy; historical font identities and exact metrics unverified |
| Texture | Flat reading areas coexist with illustrated banners, tiled surrounds, framed object cutouts, comic outlines, restrained bevels, gradients and custom collage. Some personal pages use notebook, album or laboratory metaphors. Repeated texture belongs around content, not behind every word. Pixel sprites, cel-shaded illustrations and painted creatures coexist; this is not exclusively pixel art or glossy Web2.0. | High for the inspected visual sample |
| Shape | A large illustrated identity/banner, compact category rail or strip, inset content well, small object cells, profile portraits and named status modules establish the vocabulary. Left and right rails, two-column stories, narrow personal columns and playful overlapping characters all occur. Pet art can cross a panel boundary while essential text stays contained. Rounded rectangles, outlined square cells and irregular themed labels may mix coherently. | High for the inspected visual sample |
| Motion | The corpus directly establishes still screen composition and publicly rendered page states. Some current landing pages have carousel/fade affordances; a mid-fade capture is not a measured rhythm or evidence of historical behavior. No common cadence, reward timer or original Flash operation is inferred. A contemporary translation can update tabs, item choices, balances and profile information immediately. Prefer manual browsing; reduced motion removes decorative sparkles, auto-slides and panel movement while retaining readable operation results. | Unknown historical behavior; still composition directly observed |
| Spatial | Density is organized into named zones: destination navigation, active pet/profile, main activity or story, item inventory and smaller news/status modules. Character individuality and a tiny-object economy coexist with readable content. Custom pages may replace default chrome with an authored narrative frame. Preserve category hierarchy and consistent object scale; on mobile reflow rails into compact wrapped navigation, then active content, instead of shrinking the entire desktop portal. | High for the inspected visual sample |
| Cultural markers | Named fantasy pets, community-made profiles, guilds, invented places, collections, item shops, transformation logs, trophies and long-form character stories are supported signals. Some sites explicitly address older audiences; not every pet site is for small children. Real community histories, labor and artists should remain visible. Nostalgia does not require advertising, gambling mechanics, punitive care, unmoderated messaging or fabricated player activity. | Medium; supporting context and variable examples |
