# Pinball Backglass

The composed display face of a pinball machine: title lettering, illustrated theme and scoring/progress zones are designed together. This entry emphasizes early through electromechanical and early electronic examples, retaining both illuminated-number ladders and quiet reel/display openings. It does not cover every arcade scoreboard or imply one illustration genre; later licensed translites are related developments.

## Analysis

**Palette**: Strong flat yellow, cyan, red, green, pink, violet and dark outlines occur in many combinations. Pale or dark quiet number windows contrast with richly colored surrounds. Neither neon glow nor a compulsory black background defines the style; older examples include open green/yellow fields.

**Type**: Large independently drawn title letters may be angular, shaded, inline, script, slab or distorted sans. Numerals and compact player/ball labels have a more disciplined hierarchy. Use expressive short identity type while keeping actual values, task labels and instructions steady.

**Texture**: Crisp bounded color, contour lines, stipple/hatch and selective modeled illustration. Glass, clear score openings and mechanical display recesses contribute depth. Wear and modern reproduction watermarks are not required. Scans cannot establish original ink, tactile finish or illumination timing.

**Shape**: A bounded face holds rectangular or scalloped score windows, circular player medallions, numbered ladders, fan/radial routes and a large title. Artwork flows around functional openings. A single starburst or chrome button without score/display integration is insufficient.

**Motion**: Static backglass artwork surrounds changing scores and indicator states. This still-image corpus does not establish attract sequences, reel timing, flashing rates or physical animation. A contemporary web task/status board may update numbers and indicators immediately; keep ornament still and preserve every value under reduced motion.

**Spatial**: Large theme/title zone plus one, two or four prominent quiet displays; secondary counters and labeled progress routes fit the remaining openings. Some early designs use point ladders instead of reel strips. On mobile, preserve the title/readout relation and stack task controls rather than shrinking detailed artwork and labels.

**Cultural markers**: Coin-operated recreation, named games, player numbers, balls-in-play, skill/progress and whimsical illustrated themes. The corpus is mainly United States commercial production and includes period stereotypes; reproduce graphic structure with original inclusive characters/content rather than borrowing historical caricatures or brands.

Score/progress zones are part of the image composition rather than a UI overlaid afterward. Differences among early ladders, reel-window surrounds and early electronic displays matter more than a fixed palette. P1 and U10 prevent a narrative-illustration-only rule; U5 and P7 prevent a black-neon-only rule. Functional digital task checking and score totals are contemporary application proposals, not recreated historic game rules.
