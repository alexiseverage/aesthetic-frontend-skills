# Art Nouveau

> Origin: An international decorative-arts movement popular from roughly 1890 to 1910 that drew heavily on natural forms, sinuous line, asymmetry, and total-design ambition across architecture, interiors, graphics, furniture, jewelry, and applied arts. This pass is grounded in text sources only, so the UI translation below should be read as conservative synthesis rather than as a full corpus-backed style map.

## Analysis

Wikipedia's overview supports the central distinction this repository needs to preserve: Art Nouveau is not just floral decoration but a total-design approach built from natural forms, dynamic asymmetry, and sinuous line. The source explicitly ties the style to plant and flower inspiration, whiplash curves, movement, and the breakdown of boundaries between architecture, interiors, graphics, furniture, jewelry, and other applied arts. Those features justify a frontend translation where line and ornament connect the whole composition rather than acting as isolated stickers on an otherwise generic interface.

In interface work, the style is strongest when used as a connective ornamental system: curved dividers, botanical border logic, stained-glass-like color accents, integrated heading frames, and section transitions that feel fluid rather than gridded. It can support editorial, cultural, hospitality, cosmetics, event, and storytelling surfaces where atmosphere matters. It usually breaks down when dense workflow tools or data-heavy screens force everything into rigid neutral modules.

Because the evidence here is text-only, the profile should remain cautious about regional subvariants or about overcommitting to any one palette. The cited source clearly supports organic line, asymmetry, and modern-material ornamental ambition; it does not justify flattening the movement into generic floral wallpaper. Motion, if used at all, should feel graceful and optional rather than mechanically repetitive.

## Connections

- `art-deco` — a direct contrast case: both are decorative modern movements, but Art Nouveau is curvilinear, botanical, and asymmetrical where Art Deco is geometric, stepped, and axially polished.
- `vienna-secession` — shares reformist decorative ambition and links to fin-de-siècle modernism, but usually reads flatter, more geometric, and more proto-modern than Art Nouveau's flowing naturalism.
- `beaux-arts` — may overlap in ornamental richness, yet Beaux-Arts is classical and academic where Art Nouveau pushes organic line and integrated applied-art modernity.
