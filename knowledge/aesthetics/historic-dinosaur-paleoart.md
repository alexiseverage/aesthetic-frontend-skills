## Dimension Synthesis

The following separates direct visual observations from proposed web translation. Color descriptions and relative geometry are estimated from visual inspection; no corpus-wide numerical sampling or measured animation is claimed.

| Dimension | Synthesis / scope | Confidence and limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Palette | Painted scenes use variable olive, brown, blue-green, ochre, gray and pale sky tones, sometimes with pink sand or violet shadows. Monochrome and warm-gray illustrations are substantial variants. Dark foreground vegetation against a light atmospheric horizon is more consistent than a single mandatory green/brown palette; photographic glare and aging limit original pigment inference. | High for the inspected visual sample |
| Type | Many artworks contain no typography beyond a signature. Printed plate captions, specimen names and short scale notes provide an adjacent scholarly register; later magazine mastheads belong to their editorial wrapper. A readable serif with restrained small capitals is a proposed interface translation, not an artist-wide historical font identification. | Low for a historical type system; proposed UI typography only |
| Texture | Brushwork, stippled skin, crosshatching, scumbled highlights, granular print reproduction and lightly drawn preparation marks coexist. Keep differences visible between painted bodies, foliage, water and page. Continuous paper noise or a brown overlay cannot substitute for this internal material articulation. | High for the inspected visual sample |
| Shape | Large reconstructed bodies form clear silhouettes, with necks, tails, horns and plates organizing the scene. Weighty upright postures and aquatic sauropods recur historically, but active leaping predators also appear. Preserve anatomical specificity when referring to an artwork; do not present a nostalgic reconstruction as a scientifically validated modern model. | High for the inspected visual sample |
| Motion | These are still artifacts. Poses imply drinking, feeding, confrontation or locomotion, without establishing animation timing or universal slow movement. Reading interfaces benefit from intentional stillness. Optional comparison and disclosure states can change immediately, with no mandatory parallax, breathing creatures or autoplay. | Unknown historical behavior; still composition directly observed |
| Spatial | Foreground bodies and plants, middle-distance water or terrain, and pale distant land establish depth. A narrow upright plate, a soft-edged vignette and an exceptionally wide mural are all supported. Small background figures often supply scale. The environmental composition matters; a floating dinosaur sticker on a normal card loses the system. | High for the inspected visual sample |
| Cultural markers | Museum mural commissions, fossil-based reconstruction, illustrated natural-history publications, artist/scientist collaboration and changing scientific interpretations are central. Taxonomic labels and historical dates need careful attribution. These are authored hypotheses and public images, not photographs of prehistoric life. | Medium; supporting context and variable examples |

## Direct Media Audit

**31 qualifying unique still artifacts**, individually inspected at accessible original/full-size resolution. Zero qualifying videos or interactive websites; zero generated images count as historical evidence. The 10-artifact floor and 30-artifact working target are met. Exact source, attribution, observed resolution, content hash and dimension observations are in the [linked media ledger](../media-audits/historic-dinosaur-paleoart.json). Only analytical links and observations are published, not the reference files.

The broader discovery pool contained 350 image leads, including repeated views, logos, thumbnails and unresolved candidates. These are not 350 analyzed works. 42 records received full-media inspection; 3 were explicitly marked duplicate views, and 71 files were retrieved for analysis. Retrieval itself contributes zero to the qualifying count.

The sample spans seven named artists but remains concentrated in British and American museum/publication collections and accessible reproductions. It is not a global history of fossil reconstruction. Paleoanthropology, ordinary wildlife, non-dinosaur-only synapsid scenes and small gallery previews were excluded. A Life cover was grouped with its underlying Zallinger mural after the source confirmed the reversed detail. The full mural photograph supports overall composition, not fine pigment analysis. Burian has one signed drawing through a collector site, not an estate-verified full painting survey; the accessible Moravian museum PDF was only a catalog excerpt and contributed no qualifying dinosaur artwork. A Princeton shell fetch failed, but museum catalog text and attributed complete Commons originals provided usable access. Historical scientific assertions and old names remain bounded to their dates. Still pictures establish no actual movement speed, animation timing or interface behavior.
