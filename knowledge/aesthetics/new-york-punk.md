# New York Club Punk

A scoped related sibling of `punk-zine`, using the Ramones/Dead Boys brief to examine band and club identity rather than assuming the British ransom-note branch defines every punk graphic. This is a descriptive catalog label for a useful visual system, not a newly discovered historical movement name. The generic Punk Design alias remains owned by its existing canonical entry.

### Duplicates, corrections and limits

- Vega's 1975 poster appears in museum/auction/editorial sources; only one work counts. The 1976 debut sleeve and corresponding promotional poster also count once.
- Gruen concert/session variants are grouped by event. July 1975street crowd has only a month and may overlap the July 18 band portrait session; it contributes zero additional count. This conservative grouping avoids using uncertainty to reach the target.
- The Cramps flyer prints Wednesday May 24, conflicting with the source's 1977 caption. The work is analyzed as a period flyer with exact year unresolved; the contradiction does not become a precise chronology claim.
- Punk issue 3's creator index says April 1976, correcting Huck's 1977 caption. Blue autographs on the collected copy are later annotations, not universal graphic features.
- Children artwork is delivered as a static key frame of current platform video art. Only the complete cover composition was viewed; no historic or present animation timing is claimed.
- Marquee Moon and Blank Generation are current reissue deliveries. Hell explicitly describes the 2018 packaging as facsimile; exact original inks and condition are not measured.

### Coverage and stopping decision

Thirty-reference target and ten-reference floor met. The corpus spans club/tour flyers, fan recruitment, programmes, band/photo posters, comics, a magazine interior, albums and social/performance photography. Plain sans, handwriting, comic lettering, colour photography and restrained portraits all contradict a universal ransom-note prescription. Final sleeve additions confirmed image/identity hierarchy while extending palette and density range. Further band-photo frames would mostly repeat known observations and cannot replace print analysis.

This is a purposive scene sample, strongly weighted to Ramones, Dead Boys and one photographer. It does not establish all New York artists' methods, all global punk design, a compulsory dress code or one political meaning. Print-led identity is sufficiently grounded; historical interface motion and exact typeface/ink values remain unknown.

## Analysis

### Classification and separate usefulness

The scoped system is related to, and overlaps with, Punk Zine. It is not an alias expansion: a designer following only the existing entry's required ransom letters, misalignment, copier damage and single-family prohibition would fail to reproduce central Ramones/Vega and many club-print examples. The proposed sibling instead centers specific band/club identity, an image or expressive name, and concise usable press-bill information. This supports a different frontend artifact: a direct, photograph-led club board with searchable shows and clear admission states. The broad Punk Design alias is preserved; a short reciprocal relation explains this narrower choice.

Observed claims are visual hierarchy, type categories, image treatment, print objects and documented variants. Source-supported claims are named creators, scoped networks, dates where verified, and record/reissue history. Proposed claims are the new fictional venue, application tokens, responsive layout, search, a calendar and waitlist/form feedback. Unknown claims include actual 1970s web behaviour, universal original fonts/inks and exact animation timings. No percentage thresholds are invented.

### Cultural and reuse review

Do not erase the Cleveland–New York relationship of Dead Boys or collapse neighbouring art-rock/new-wave/electronic acts into a single sound or uniform. Photographer/designer contributions matter as much as a leather silhouette. Original fictional names and subjects avoid borrowing a real band's seal, logo, picture or implied endorsement. Documentary adult nudity in one historical photograph is context, not a brief requirement. Violence, addiction and poverty do not provide automatic authenticity.

All source media remain linked analysis. MoMA supplies licensing contacts, Gruen marks copyright, and no source image reuse license was obtained. No media was downloaded or supplied as an image-generation input. An original showcase asset contributes zero to the historical count. Review was performed by the authoring agent against the ledger and boundaries; no independent review is claimed.
