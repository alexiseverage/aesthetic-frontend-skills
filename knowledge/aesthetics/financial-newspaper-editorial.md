# Financial Newspaper Editorial

## Scope and classification

The edited reading-and-data grammar of financial/business newspapers: publication/date/section identity, differentiated reporting and analysis, ordered article fields and financial/economic figures. This label avoids claiming that every inspected publication has the physical broadsheet format. Salmon paper, serif headlines and dense columns are strong branches, with white-paper, sans and image-led weekend exceptions retained.

This is a new canonical entry, screened against current catalog slugs, labels, aliases, redirects and subset names. Bloomberg Terminal Monochrome centers operational financial data and continuous machine-oriented navigation. This entry centers authored reporting and editorial reading. Swiss grids can support it but are not specific to finance; generic newspaper styling without economic/data context is too broad.

## Dimension Synthesis

### Palette

Dark ink on white, cream or salmon paper with limited section/data accents. FT, FD and some Spanish/Mexican examples support the salmon branch; many others use white. Gains/losses require signs or text as well as color.

### Type

Strong publication identity, large reporting headlines and a calmer article hierarchy beside numerical tables. Serif display is common, but Chinese and image-led business fronts provide sans counterexamples. Distinguish headlines, decks, bylines, captions and data.

### Texture

Print-like paper planes, rules, editorial photography and information graphics. Stills do not justify universal ink bleed or distress; keep long text and numbers crisp.

### Shape

Columns, horizontal rules, aligned market strips, chart frames and inset briefs. Image-led weekend fronts can relax the grid while retaining publication and section structure.

### Motion

The observed print front pages are static. Reading tools, section switching and table sorting should update deliberately without a live ticker. No market animation cadence is claimed. Intentional stillness supports numerical comparison.

### Spatial

A curated hierarchy of lead story, supporting briefs, analysis and data; density varies by edition and section. On mobile, preserve editorial priority in a single column instead of squeezing newspaper columns into tiny type.

### Cultural markers

Publication/date, authorship, economic reporting, market summaries and the separation of reporting, analysis and advertising. A data label must say what figures represent and when they apply.

## Analysis

### Claim-to-artifact support

Editorial hierarchy/data: FN01–10, FN13, FN15–17, FN19–25, FN28. Salmon branch: FN01, FN18–19, FN23. Sans/image-led exceptions: FN11, FN14, FN17, FN26–27. FT case: FNFT1–4 grouped once, with only the original case composition counted.

### Confidence and gaps

High confidence in publication/headline/data hierarchy; moderate in a global financial-press style from one date window. FN12 and FN26 small complete originals support major composition only; FD lower body text is deliberately blurred. Individual front-page fonts and news facts are not inferred.

Core identity is limited to the supported traits in the canonical non-negotiables. Counterexamples have been incorporated into the guidance rather than discarded to make a uniform mood board. No still-derived exact motion timing, measured physical performance or unverified source claim is promoted to fact.

### Application handoff

Connotation mode: **contemporary editorial application**. Artifact concept: **The Daily Ledger reading desk**. Signature visual unit: financial editorial page. Create a fictional reading desk with a lead analysis, section buttons, saved-story state, an expandable article and a labeled economic chart/table. Supply an explicit illustration label for fixture data. Preserve full chart labels and a readable text equivalent. Stack on mobile, maintain bylines and keep controls distinct from editorial links.

These are **proposed application tokens**, not sampled historical colors or measured timings. Background `#f2dfcd`, surface `#fff5e9`, text `#262725`, muted `#62615a`, accent `#17616a`, success `#355d48`, danger `#9b2e29`. Display `Georgia, serif` at `clamp(2rem,5vw,4.5rem)`, weight 700, line-height 1.02, tracking -0.035em; body `Arial, sans-serif` at 1rem/1.5, weight 400, tracking 0; labels 0.8125rem/1.3, weight 700, tracking 0.06em; numeric outputs use tabular figures at 1rem/1.3. Space 1–6: `4,8,12,20,32,48px`; radius `2px` for functional fields and `0` for panel edges (a circular dial/label retains its own geometry). Default state changes are instant; optional feedback `120ms ease-out` is a proposed web value, disabled under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`. No automatic decorative loop is required.

These tokens are a baseline for an original interface. Include the supported subject/identity, its information hierarchy and its operational/reading context. Avoid copied marks/art, generic palette swaps and inaccessible source-era constraints. The source references remain analytical; original procedural graphics or separately created images should carry the application.

### Accessibility and adaptation flags

Palette tokens do not certify interface contrast. Check actual foreground/background states, focus outlines, disabled controls and overlays; target WCAG AA text and non-text requirements. Body text should remain 16px or larger in the application. Responsive priority is content order and control usability, not a tiny scaled facsimile. Keep explicit text equivalents for graphs and decorative SVGs hidden from assistive technology when captions already describe them. Respect user zoom and narrow layouts.

Reduced motion: stillness or immediate state changes are valid. Any proposed motion is optional, interruptible and removed for reduced motion. Dark mode: retain the artifact's deliberate material palette within its scoped frame; adapt surrounding application chrome separately. Fatigue: limit the loudest texture/type to the defining field. Conflict rule: preserve the non-negotiables and task semantics before introducing a neighboring palette, font or transition.
