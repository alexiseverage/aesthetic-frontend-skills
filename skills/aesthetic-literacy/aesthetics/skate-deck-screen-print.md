# Skate Deck Screen-Print Graphics

## Scope

Skate Deck Screen-Print Graphics is the visual system of spot-color graphics printed onto skateboard decks. Use it when the design should feel like ink separated into a few screens, applied to a shaped wood product, and tied to skate brand and skate-punk visual culture. It is not generic skateboard imagery, a Thrasher logo imitation, or a clean vector illustration with a deck pasted behind it.

The current evidence is limited and text-source based, with no downloaded image corpus. Brand and artist references such as Santa Cruz, Powell Peralta, Jim Phillips, Vernon Courtlandt Johnson, and Thrasher are analytical context only; protected logos or named graphics must not become reusable motifs.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Flat spot colors dominate: usually two to six saturated inks, often primaries, secondaries, black, white underbase, and wood-toned negative space. Halftone fields may fake blends, but continuous photographic color weakens the screen-print signal.

**Type**: Bold custom logotypes, contoured display lettering, hand-drawn names, comic and psychedelic letterforms, and brand-wordmark-as-graphic compositions. Type should feel drawn for the deck rather than set as editorial text.

**Texture**: Screen ink over wood grain, visible halftone dots, slight bleed, minor misregistration, chipped edges, and underbase layers. A perfectly smooth SVG finish needs noise, dot, grain, or edge treatment to read as printed material.

**Shape**: The deck silhouette is the frame: popsicle or shaped-board proportions, nose/tail symmetry, centered emblems, full-bleed figures, skulls, monsters, hot-rod and surf-adjacent forms, and large brand marks.

**Motion**: The artifact is static. Energy comes from diagonal poses, aggressive composition, and implied action, not from animated graphics; motion in UI should quote reveal/print passes only as an optional enhancement with reduced-motion fallback.

**Spatial**: One dominant graphic usually occupies most of the vertical deck, with a logo in the top or bottom third. Avoid multi-column editorial layouts; think single-object display, underside reveal, sticker-like badges, and nose-to-tail alignment.

**Cultural markers**: Skate-brand culture, board graphics, screen-print production, punk/skate-punk iconography, Ed Roth and psychedelic-poster lineage, and 1980s illustration-forward deck history. These markers should be referenced without copying living artists' protected graphics.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: limited spot-color separation; visible print-on-wood materiality; a deck-shaped single-graphic composition; bold hand-drawn or custom logotype energy; skate-product context. Without those, the result becomes generic punk, risograph, or sports branding.

## Connotation

This reads as nostalgic quotation or contemporary revival of skate product graphics. It connotes youth subculture, brand loyalty, physical wear, and collectible object culture. Use the attitude without mocking skate communities or treating protected brand marks as generic clip art.

## Related / Subsets

`punk-zine` shares DIY aggression but is photocopy/editorial collage. `risograph` shares spot-ink craft but is paper/editorial rather than ink-on-wood product graphics. `grunge-typography` shares distressed letterforms but not the deck silhouette or screen-print production constraint.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Use it for campaign heroes, product cards, music/skate event pages, badges, or collectable-card interfaces. Keep core navigation and forms on readable surfaces, then frame feature modules as deck underside panels or print separations. Use fictional marks, generic deck silhouettes, and original illustration rather than real skate logos.

## CSS Translation

Use 2-6 named ink tokens, hard-edged fills, halftone radial gradients, `mix-blend-mode: multiply` on grain layers, rough masks, wood-tone backgrounds, and slight offset duplicate layers for misregistration. A tall rounded-rectangle or custom-path frame can evoke deck proportions without reproducing any brand shape.

## Typography / Fonts

Favor heavy hand-drawn displays, bold rounded grotesks, comic-style outlines, or psychedelic/custom lettering for focal words. Pair with a plain readable sans for body copy, forms, and accessibility-critical labels. Avoid refined editorial serifs as the primary voice.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Skate references are subcultural and often tied to living artists, youth scenes, and brands. Treat them as context, not asset libraries. Do not trace known deck graphics, reproduce protected logos, or imply endorsement by named artists or publications.

## Anti-Patterns

- Using a famous skate logo or mascot as if it were a reusable motif.
- Rendering smooth full-color vector art with no screen, ink, or wood cues.
- Dropping the deck-shaped composition and calling any punk illustration a skate deck.
- Animating the deck graphic itself as though motion were part of the print system.
