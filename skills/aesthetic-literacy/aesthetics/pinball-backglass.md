# Pinball Backglass

## Scope

The composed display face of a pinball machine: title lettering, illustrated theme and scoring/progress zones are designed together. This entry emphasizes early through electromechanical and early electronic examples, retaining both illuminated-number ladders and quiet reel/display openings. It does not cover every arcade scoreboard or imply one illustration genre; later licensed translites are related developments.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Strong flat yellow, cyan, red, green, pink, violet and dark outlines occur in many combinations. Pale or dark quiet number windows contrast with richly colored surrounds. Neither neon glow nor a compulsory black background defines the style; older examples include open green/yellow fields.

**Type**: Large independently drawn title letters may be angular, shaded, inline, script, slab or distorted sans. Numerals and compact player/ball labels have a more disciplined hierarchy. Use expressive short identity type while keeping actual values, task labels and instructions steady.

**Texture**: Crisp bounded color, contour lines, stipple/hatch and selective modeled illustration. Glass, clear score openings and mechanical display recesses contribute depth. Wear and modern reproduction watermarks are not required. Scans cannot establish original ink, tactile finish or illumination timing.

**Shape**: A bounded face holds rectangular or scalloped score windows, circular player medallions, numbered ladders, fan/radial routes and a large title. Artwork flows around functional openings. A single starburst or chrome button without score/display integration is insufficient.

**Motion**: Static backglass artwork surrounds changing scores and indicator states. This still-image corpus does not establish attract sequences, reel timing, flashing rates or physical animation. A contemporary web task/status board may update numbers and indicators immediately; keep ornament still and preserve every value under reduced motion.

**Spatial**: Large theme/title zone plus one, two or four prominent quiet displays; secondary counters and labeled progress routes fit the remaining openings. Some early designs use point ladders instead of reel strips. On mobile, preserve the title/readout relation and stack task controls rather than shrinking detailed artwork and labels.

**Cultural markers**: Coin-operated recreation, named games, player numbers, balls-in-play, skill/progress and whimsical illustrated themes. The corpus is mainly United States commercial production and includes period stereotypes; reproduce graphic structure with original inclusive characters/content rather than borrowing historical caricatures or brands.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: title/artwork and scoring or progress zones composed as one bounded face; expressive short lettering contrasted with disciplined readouts; clearly labeled player/progress relationships; quiet functional openings inside forceful graphic color. Chrome, black ground, space motifs and flashing are optional.

## Connotation

**Mode:** nostalgic quotation.

Friendly arcade exuberance and visible accomplishment. Use for a habit checklist, project milestone board, reading challenge or progress summary whose numbers genuinely reflect local choices. Avoid introducing chance or gambling mechanics to a straightforward task.

## Related / Subsets

`casino` shares recreational score symbolism, but betting tables, chips and wagering are not necessary here. `vector-arcade-graphics` uses a rendered vector screen; this entry integrates printed artwork around physical display openings. `pop-art` shares crisp commercial color and illustration, but it does not require scoring or machine-facing hierarchy. `atomic-age` is a thematic neighbor for particular space designs, not the identity of every backglass. Early score ladders, electromechanical reels and early electronic-window surrounds remain internal branches.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Build an original progress/checklist board whose actual totals occupy pale score-window strips; numbered milestone indicators and task controls belong to the same illustrated enclosure. Checking/unchecking a task must update totals and completion labels. Use large normal-weight tabular numerals, no fake random score. Controls use strong rectangular outlines, short display labels and explicit selected checks. Inputs remain pale/solid with a clearly contrasting focus outline. Navigation/player selectors have pressed-state semantics; a small original medallion can mark the selected scope. Headings are short and strongly shaded, dividers continue score-window frames, links are underlined, badges are numbered disks. Alerts use an opaque caption strip and a written status. A reset confirmation dialog, if present, requires a visible exit and correct focus behavior. Icons/characters are original silhouettes/linework. Reflow the lower task list at narrow widths and preserve 44px targets.

## CSS Translation

Proposed current-web roles, not sampled original inks: pale readout #FFF4CE, dark text #162539, blue field #165E9B, red action #B42E38, yellow accent #F4C94A, turquoise #71D9D7 and muted #4B5360. Use dark text on pale/yellow/turquoise; pale text on dark blue/red; verify states. Use 3px dark outer contours, 1–2px inset rules, 0–8px readout radii, selected circular indicators and limited offset display shadows. Numerals use font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums. Spacing 6/12/18/24/36/48px, display 42–72px/1.05, readouts 32–48px, body 16px/1.5. Still ornament; immediate indicator changes. Reduced motion removes optional numeric transition/celebration.

## Typography / Fonts

A heavy system sans/Impact-like local display fallback can support short title lettering; Georgia may support a serif branch. Use system sans/monospace tabular values for readouts, without tiny faux electronic segments for essential text. These are current UI choices, not identified historic fonts. Avoid overdecorating every task label.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Use an original fictional game/board identity, characters and imagery. Do not trace licensed art, copy manufacturers, reproduce watermarks or import historical stereotypes and sexualized promotional characters as default decoration. A score is a transparent task total, not an implied payout. Reproduction access/physical ownership does not clear publication rights. Preserve ambiguous attributions such as the museum Old Chicago line instead of silently assigning a single artist.

## Anti-Patterns

- A generic dashboard overlaid with a pinball image.
- Numbers disconnected from actual state or unexplained random scores.
- Mandatory black/neon/chrome, copied game titles or movie characters.
- Flashing attract modes, rolling essential copy or motion-only completion cues.
- Unreadable miniature mobile windows, color-only player state or hidden focus.
