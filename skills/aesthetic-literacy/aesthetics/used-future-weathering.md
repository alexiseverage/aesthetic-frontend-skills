# Used-Future / Lived-In Sci-Fi Weathering

## Scope

Used-Future / Lived-In Sci-Fi Weathering is a material doctrine for making speculative technology look repaired, grimy, and used rather than pristine. Use it when a futuristic interface, prop, shell, or environment needs a history of maintenance and wear. It is not a license to copy any franchise armor, spaceship, faction, or symbol.

Evidence is limited and textual, centered on the documented "used future" / "used universe" discourse around production design. Treat named franchises and artists as provenance context only.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Muted earth and metal tones: dirty whites, warm grays, oxidized browns, faded paint, oil-dark blacks, and restrained amber, red, or blue utility accents. Bright clean sci-fi primaries should be rare warning or status marks, not the base palette.

**Type**: Utilitarian stencil lettering, serial numbers, hazard labels, worn service markings, engraved IDs, small industrial sans text, and rubbed-off decals. Polished futuristic display faces undermine the sense of maintained equipment.

**Texture**: Carbon scoring, chipped paint, grime accumulation, worn edges, scratches, dents, rubbed labels, oil stains, repaired seams, and patched surfaces. The weathering should explain use; random dirt is weaker than wear around handles, edges, fasteners, and high-contact controls.

**Shape**: Functional geometric shells, hard-use silhouettes, removable panels, access hatches, rivets, exposed seams, and modular parts. Sleek aerodynamic curves can appear only if visibly aged, repaired, or industrially repurposed.

**Motion**: Materiality is mostly static. In UI, motion should imply booting, diagnostic flicker, field repair, or slow status change; do not claim specific cinematic timing without motion evidence.

**Spatial**: Lived-in clutter, layered detritus, repair benches, inset panels, bundled controls, and components with accumulated history. Empty showroom minimalism breaks the aesthetic.

**Cultural markers**: Production-design language of grubby, human, repaired futures; Ralph McQuarrie and original Star Wars used-universe discourse; model-making, cosplay, and prop-weathering technique cultures.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: muted weathered palette; chipped/dirty/repaired material surfaces; utilitarian industrial marking; functional modular geometry; visible history of use. If everything is new, glossy, or ornamental, it is not used-future weathering.

## Connotation

The mode is usually nostalgic quotation or contemporary genre revival: believable technology that has passed through hands, travel, conflict, repair, and scarcity. It can evoke war machinery and speculative harm, so keep references fictionalized and avoid turning real militarism into decoration.

## Related / Subsets

`space-western` uses weathering within frontier travel staging. `post-apocalyptic-scavenged-tech` is post-collapse salvage and mismatch. `cassette-futurism` may share analog retrofuture interfaces but does not require grime or repair history.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Use weathered panels, diagnostic cards, equipment labels, worn tabs, service logs, and patched modules for branded surfaces or narrative dashboards. Keep reading surfaces solid and contrast-safe; grime should sit behind or around content, not over body text.

## CSS Translation

Layer desaturated color tokens, scratched masks, soft noise, dark edge gradients, border chips, subtle decal opacity loss, and panel seams. Use accent lights sparingly. Provide reduced-motion and no-texture fallbacks for critical content.

## Typography / Fonts

Use industrial grotesks, DIN-like sans, stencil faces, OCR/serial-number treatments, and compact uppercase labels. Pair them with a legible neutral sans for paragraphs and forms.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Avoid protected franchise identifiers, faction insignia, character armor, and copied spaceship details. When war, weapons, or damaged bodies are adjacent to the design, keep the usage clearly fictional and product-appropriate.

## Anti-Patterns

- Copying a famous sci-fi prop or armor silhouette.
- Adding random dirt without functional wear logic.
- Making all text degraded and unreadable.
- Confusing lived-in functional wear with post-apocalyptic scrap chaos.
