# Pochoir Fashion Plates

An illustrated fashion-publication language combining fine contour, selective colored planes, elongated couture silhouettes and a carefully captioned page. Its 1920s reference includes Gazette du Bon Ton, while earlier plates establish continuity. Pochoir names a stencil-coloring process; this entry covers its fashion-plate application, rather than every stencil print or every Art Deco illustration.

## Scope

Suitable for atelier storefronts, numbered editions, fashion/editorial features, illustrated product configurations and small exhibition catalogs. The interface should inherit plate hierarchy, line/color contrast and decorative silhouette, including its controls and information structure. A generic card grid with vintage pictures is insufficient.

The evidence is weighted toward one Parisian publication and one collection. It does not define global 1920s fashion or all pochoir production. The journal includes varied print methods and non-fashion illustrations; publication membership alone cannot identify technique.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Quiet paper ground with selective color areas and dark contours. Teal, violet, indigo, rust, pink, yellow and green occur in different combinations; dense black/tan plates and softly modeled color are important variants. Choose a small role-based set for the application. Scan yellowing and exact pigments are not canonical tokens.

**Type**: Centered serif plate titles, smaller garment attribution, discreet issue/date corners and generous separation from the illustration. Hand-shaped titles and italic display also occur. Use an expressive serif with clear small labels; exact historical fonts have not been established.

**Texture**: Fine contour and hatching contrast with flat stencil-colored-looking areas, garment patterns and sometimes dense woodcut marks. Mixed print/hand-color methods are source-specific. Preserve clean reading/input surfaces; an all-over distressed filter obscures rather than explains the craft.

**Shape**: Long column and bell silhouettes, spreading capes, ribbons, scalloped hems, precise borders and selective floral/geometric ornament. Treat the relationship of outline and color as the grammar. No single body proportion, costume or pose is compulsory.

**Motion**: Historical plates are static. Poses can imply wind or dance without establishing animation timing. Intentional stillness is a strong translation. Contemporary selection feedback can be immediate and clear; any optional transition needs a reduced-motion equivalent.

**Spatial**: Framed tableau or isolated silhouette, generous paper margins, stage-like baseline and detached title/credit. Dense framed scenes coexist with near-empty grounds. Keep that hierarchy at mobile widths by stacking scene, caption and controls in semantic order.

**Cultural markers**: Couture credits, illustrator signatures, issue/plate numbers, hand production and staged social scenes. Preserve attribution and acknowledge skilled print labor. Some plates contain colonial, classed or appropriative imagery; these are historical contexts, not required decorative ingredients.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: fine-line/color-plane contrast; a deliberate silhouette/ornament relationship; plate-like separation of scene, title and attribution; and functional controls that belong to the same composition. Gold geometry, paper texture or a vintage illustration alone does not establish the application.

## Connotation

**Mode:** contemporary revival.

Can suggest an intimate atelier, illustrated couture, considered handwork and a numbered publication. Use original content and assets. Neither elite exclusivity nor a supposedly universal “Roaring Twenties” lifestyle needs to be reproduced.

## Related / Subsets

- `art-deco` overlaps in fashion, decorative geometry and modern luxury; pochoir also serves organic, woodcut-like and softly modeled scenes.
- `art-nouveau` shares flowing contour and floral ornament, without defining the whole 1920s sample.
- `swiss-international` offers a contrast: both can organize information precisely, but a plate-led decorative hierarchy differs from rational typographic grids.
- Sparse contour plates, dense narrative vignettes and softly modeled plates are useful variants here, without separate canonical slugs.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Build a composed product sheet with numbered edition selection, a framed configuration preview, a detached caption, clearly labeled swatches and a substantial order summary. Use slim rules, generous margins, contour-shaped active marks and layered color in the controls themselves. Selection must change real product information; a preview bag should allow review and removal. Use a straightforward label wherever decorative wording could confuse a task.

## CSS Translation

Grid can keep edition rail, illustration/preview and purchase column distinct. Use 1–2 px ink rules, bounded original SVG ornament, restrained inset frames and flat colored control fills. Pattern belongs in selected silhouettes or margins, with solid text backgrounds. Proposed colors may include warm ivory, dark aubergine ink, deep teal and lilac; calculate actual text/state contrast. Reserve dimensions for assets, preserve focus rings and avoid moving hit targets. Mobile controls need readable 16 px input text and comfortable touch targets. No historical breakpoint or exact ink value is implied.

## Typography / Fonts

Georgia/Times with tracked small capitals and italic garment-like subtitles provide a reasonable system-font translation; use normal casing and adequate size for instructions. This is a proposed treatment, not a historical font identification. Keep serif strokes and rules legible on small screens. Added fonts require verified licensing, language coverage and loading behavior.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Credit the illustrator, fashion house and process where known; anonymous print workers should not disappear behind a claim that one artist personally made every impression. Distinguish printing technique from appearance. Do not copy a protected composition or treat colonial fantasy, appropriated dress, fur use, thin bodies or class exclusion as required signs of authenticity. Reference links are research evidence, not asset-reuse permission.

## Anti-Patterns

- Treating all pochoir as Art Deco, or every journal illustration as a verified stencil print.
- A normal ecommerce grid with period pictures and unchanged generic controls.
- Mandatory sepia grain, gold gradients or black/gold luxury branding.
- Decorative handwritten labels replacing clear field names, tiny captions or pale low-contrast text.
- Invented historical animation, font identities, sampled inks or universal body proportions.
