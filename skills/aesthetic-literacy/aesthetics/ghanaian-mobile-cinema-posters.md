# Ghanaian Mobile-Cinema Posters

## Scope

A culturally situated painted advertising practice associated with Ghanaian video clubs and mobile screenings: oversized titles, individually interpreted film scenes and figures, emphatic scale changes, and paint on cloth supports. Later artist commissions continue and alter the form for international audiences. Preserve that distinction; a contemporary export painting does not establish what a 1990s local screening poster looked like.

Use this language for film/editorial carousels, cultural-program cards, event announcements and illustrated article covers. Give the artwork room while keeping actual website navigation and controls readable. It is not an invitation to label arbitrary distressed horror imagery “African.”

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Strong light/dark separation and vivid local fields; red, yellow, blue, green, black and flesh tones recur in varied combinations. Quiet blue/tan, black/gray and spacious pale examples also occur. Bright contour halos and offset title colors often separate figure and ground; a universal red-yellow palette is unsupported.

**Type**: Large painted titles may use block capitals, condensed sans forms, outlined serifs, rounded letters or slanted script. Contrasting star names, video-club inscriptions and signatures remain subordinate. Titles can sit above, below or beside the figure; no identified digital font is historically mandatory.

**Texture**: Painted modeling, opaque color, sharp contour highlights and visible cloth/paint surfaces. Many archival works show folding, abrasion, repaired corners or support loss; these are condition and use-history evidence, not required decorative damage. New work can be crisp and intact.

**Shape**: Dominant expressive figures, enlarged anatomy or creatures, smaller episodic scenes and irregular painted perimeters. Rounded modeled portraits are common, while flatter cartoon drawing is a documented contemporary variant. Preserve pictorial hierarchy rather than substituting generic zigzags.

**Motion**: The inspected evidence is static painting. Historical animation timing is unknown. A modern carousel may change on deliberate previous/next activation with a short optional crossfade; reduced motion changes immediately. Avoid autoplay or animated violence justified by the source tradition.

**Spatial**: A clear title-and-image anchor inside a predominantly vertical support; disproportionate scene scales and overlapping figures communicate spectacle. Some compositions are crowded; others use one or two figures with open ground. Keep explanatory copy and functional controls outside dense art.

**Cultural markers**: Ghanaian artists, local sign-painting skills, video-club/operator inscriptions, recycled sack supports and locally interpreted international cinema. Individual signatures and regional/market differences matter. Religious or supernatural references require source-specific interpretation; they are not interchangeable motifs.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: A painted pictorial advertisement with expressive scale and a strong title hierarchy; coherent figure/ground modeling or an identified flat-drawing branch; a clear distinction between original Ghanaian practice and a contemporary website interpretation. Palette and distressed edges alone are insufficient.

## Connotation

**Mode:** contemporary revival with explicit attribution of the source tradition. The form conveys spectacle, handmade persuasion and individual interpretation. It can be playful, dramatic or unsettling. Neither violence nor an assumption of technical naivety defines every work.

## Related / Subsets

- **Polish Poster School** also interprets film through painting, but its metaphorical compression and institutional history differ from Ghanaian cloth advertisements, modeled spectacle and video-club context.
- **Slasher VHS Rental** concerns video-store packaging and shelf language; a Ghanaian poster may advertise comedy, fantasy or martial arts and need not imitate a cassette case.
- **Pen & Pixel** uses digital collage and commercial image effects; these posters are painted objects with distinct maker/market histories.
- Historical local advertisements and later international commissions are branches within this entry. Dates and commission contexts must remain visible; no independent subset slug is asserted.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Build recognizable web components: an image carousel with previous/next buttons and named slide indicators, an illustrated article card, or an event card with a real action. Compose original artwork and set semantic titles in HTML. A selected slide must update its title, metadata and description together. Preserve keyboard focus and a restrained live announcement; do not announce every visual ornament.

On narrow screens stack the full artwork above the description. Preserve portrait proportions instead of cropping faces to force a widescreen hero. Use a plain adjacent reading surface for dates, descriptions and buttons. Keep original research interpretation outside the fictional film copy.

## CSS Translation

Proposed application values, estimated from visual inspection rather than sampled historical pigments: warm paper `#F5E9C7`, ink `#18242B`, vermilion `#B52F24`, yellow `#F1C44E`, deep blue `#173F5F`. Use paper/ink for reading, with vivid color reserved for art and emphasis.

Prefer squared panels, 2px rules and a restrained offset title shadow. Keep roughness on artwork edges; text and focus outlines remain sharp. Use `aspect-ratio: 2 / 3`, `object-fit: contain`, `min-width: 0` and a responsive two-column layout. Optional transitions: 160ms ease-out, disabled under `prefers-reduced-motion`. These are contemporary UI choices, not measured source behavior.

## Typography / Fonts

Use a heavy display category with deliberate outline/offset treatment for short titles and an ordinary legible sans for descriptions. A local Impact/Arial Narrow fallback can suggest weight and compression without pretending to reproduce painted lettering. Custom original title lettering may be SVG; do not rasterize necessary UI text or copy an artist's recognizable wordmark. Source examples do not establish one mandatory font family.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Name Ghana and individual makers when discussing references. Do not present generated illustrations as authentic Ghanaian commissions, invent endorsements, trace protected characters or repeat generic “primitive/naive African art” framing. Archive availability is not permission to reproduce paintings, film characters or logos. If new assets use peaceful fiction, retain the pictorial advertising grammar without pretending all historical posters were welcoming or nonviolent.

## Anti-Patterns

- An aged red/yellow background with no original painted image or title hierarchy.
- Treating gore, guns, religious symbols or misspelled words as compulsory comic decoration.
- Describing a 2025 international commission as a historic village-screening poster.
- Replacing the web component with a simulated cinema-management app.
- Tiny illegible image text, automatic slide rotation, hidden keyboard controls, or color-only selection.
