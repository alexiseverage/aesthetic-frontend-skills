# Biedermeier

## Scope

Biedermeier is a documented Central European period and design/art vocabulary, especially associated with Austria and German-speaking regions in the first half of the nineteenth century. Its furniture/interior branch combines useful domestic forms, clear proportions, figured veneers, comfortable upholstery and bounded ornament. Painting includes personal portraits, family/genre scenes, flowers and local landscapes. The [research profile](../../../knowledge/aesthetics/biedermeier.md) documents thirty-one complete works across three owning collections, including non-neutral and ornate variants.

Choose a coherent branch when applying it. An original correspondence cabinet can translate furniture's material planes and compartment hierarchy; a cultural publication can use carefully modeled personal, floral or local-view imagery. Generic brown furniture, beige luxury or every nineteenth-century domestic painting is insufficient. Conventional 1815–1848 dating does not replace source classification: an inspected circa-1810 secretary is explicitly tagged Biedermeier, while later works need contextual distinction.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Warm figured wood and darker outlining are useful furniture signals, with colored upholstery rather than obligatory beige. Red/green sofa, peach/blue armchair, black/gold glass, pale opaque glass and blue/green flask broaden the range. Painting can use modeled greens, blue skies, pale skin/fabric and rich flower colors. Select a material-coherent palette; exact UI hex values are contemporary proposals.

**Type**: Commemorative cursive, botanical numerals and small labels occur on particular glasses or inside painted scenes. They are subordinate object details, not a standardized movement font. Use a readable contemporary serif for correspondence/editorial content and plain labels for controls. Do not enlarge inventory stamps, signatures or tiny inscriptions into a supposedly authentic alphabet.

**Texture**: Figured veneer on broad planes, polished wood, contrasting strips, comfortable fabric, cut/painted/gilded glass and finely modeled painting. Material can carry ornament without applied noise. Source species/process and restoration metadata matter; current upholstery and chips are not automatic original-style evidence. Smooth finish and ornate inlay both belong to documented branches.

**Shape**: Useful rectangular or oval planes, tapered/pedestal supports, open fan or lyre-like chair backs, columns, small knobs and clearly framed compartments. Glass uses flared lips, substantial bases and bounded picture/lettering fields. Carved fans, scrolls, gilded mounts and inlaid shaped cabinets show that no-ornament minimalism is too narrow. Preserve proportion and focal hierarchy rather than attaching arbitrary classical motifs everywhere.

**Motion**: Historical stills establish no animation or UI timing. Open/closed cabinet photographs show functional states, not measured motion. Intentional stillness with immediate compartment selection, reading or filing feedback is a suitable modern translation; reduced-motion users retain every action. Do not claim a digital correspondence workflow reconstructs period behavior.

**Spatial**: Domestic-scale objects and useful compartment hierarchies balance large material surfaces with smaller details. Painting organizes modeled figures, possessions or landscape depth around a clear subject. Density varies: the affluent family interior is richly furnished; the alpine landscape and a plain table are spacious; an ornate secretary is tightly articulated. One universal sparse-room grid would misrepresent the corpus.

**Cultural markers**: Central European material culture, named workshops/artists, private/family and royal-salon settings, local views, flowers and specifically interpreted commemorative or Christian emblems. Domestic ideals exist alongside aristocratic antecedents, social differences and political tensions. Preserve regional attribution rather than treating every maker as anonymous or every household as a uniform bourgeois idyll.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**:

- Preserve the period/context and a coherent furniture, domestic-object or representational-art branch.
- Let material-bearing surfaces, useful proportions and clear focal/compartment hierarchy carry the composition.
- Bound ornament and personal detail without erasing documented color or ornate variants.
- Treat the result as a contemporary interpretation unless actual historical object provenance is supplied.

## Connotation

**Mode:** contemporary revival for a domestic/editorial artifact.

The reference can convey personal attention, comfort, material refinement and a carefully arranged private world. Its class and political history are not neutral: royal commissions, consumer differentiation, repression and social tensions belong alongside domestic ideals. Avoid presenting comfortable interiors as evidence of universal prosperity or political innocence. A fictional correspondence desk is a quotation of material/spatial principles, not a period reenactment.

## Related / Subsets

`shaker-design` shares useful furniture but comes from communal religious restraint, rather than this corpus's private/royal contexts, figured veneer and colored ornamental variants. `arts-and-crafts` has later reform, medieval/vernacular and patterned-book histories. `warm-minimalism` is a contemporary lifestyle language whose edited neutrals exclude many Biedermeier examples.

Empire/Neoclassicism and Romanticism are important neighbors with overlapping forms and subjects, not automatic aliases. Conventional political dates and museum exhibition membership alone cannot classify every object. Furniture, portrait/domestic scene, flowers, local-view glass and landscape remain prose branches; no new subset slugs are defined.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Suitable uses include attributed furniture collections, cultural publications and personal correspondence/organization artifacts. An original writing-cabinet interface can reveal useful compartments and present letters on quiet paper, with legible selected/read/filed states. Give this a clear modern purpose instead of simulated royal provenance or fake antique email delivery.

Separate rich wood/illustration from essential text and hit areas. Use explicit labels and semantic buttons rather than hidden decorative drawer handles. Preserve useful structure on narrow screens; collapse physical metaphor into a readable stacked order when needed. Real selection, empty and reset states should remain clear without relying on color alone.

## CSS Translation

- Choose role colors from one coherent branch: cream paper, warm cherry/walnut planes, dark inlay-like dividers and a bounded textile or floral accent. Red/blue/green accents are valid; beige is not compulsory.
- Build original SVG grain/bookmatched planes or restrained CSS wood marks. Do not reuse photographed veneer, trace a museum cabinet or imply exact species from a procedural texture.
- Use broad rectangular panels with purposeful curves on the outer cabinet/artwork. Thin dark rules and small round pulls can articulate hierarchy; essential buttons remain plainly labeled.
- Keep paper reading panels unpatterned, body text around 16–18px, usable targets at least 44px and focus outlines visibly distinct from grain.
- Use `minmax(0, 1fr)`, wrapping labels, intrinsic artwork proportions and a single-column reading order on small screens. Suggested breakpoints are contemporary choices.
- Prefer zero-duration state changes for a still translation. Disable any nonessential animation under `prefers-reduced-motion` and preserve focus through filing/filtering.

## Typography / Fonts

A readable serif such as a system Georgia fallback can suit correspondence, paired with system sans for explicit actions and metadata. This is a contemporary category choice, not a historical font identification. Restrict any handwriting treatment to short verified content; retain readable body text. New regional-language content requires appropriate shaping, licensed fonts and verified wording.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Biedermeier is not merely tasteful beige or anonymous rural craft. Keep aristocratic and private household contexts, named artists/workshops and regional identities visible in the rationale. Historical gendered furniture labels describe catalog history, not restrictions on who may use the interface.

Do not copy religious emblems, royal seals, signatures or meaningful inscriptions as generic branding. Original correspondence content should have its own purpose and must not fabricate historical letters, identities or dates. Museum photographs and artworks have separate reuse conditions; research access does not authorize production reuse. Contemporary examples should not claim antique manufacture or authenticated heritage.

## Anti-Patterns

- A brown wood texture on generic cards with no useful proportion or compartment hierarchy.
- Beige-only, wood-only, no-gold or no-ornament rules that erase documented variants.
- Treating every Empire/Neoclassical object or every domestic painting as Biedermeier by date alone.
- Calling replacement upholstery, museum wall colors, chips or age damage essential historical features.
- Fake royal provenance, borrowed inscriptions, gendered UI restrictions or a uniformly cheerful politically innocent idyll.
- Decorative drawer handles that conceal actions, tiny text on busy grain, or animation presented as measured period behavior.
