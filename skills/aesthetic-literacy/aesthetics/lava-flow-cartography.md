# Lava Flow Cartography

## Scope

Volcano maps show the relationship between terrain, vent or fissure geometry, and the observed or modeled coverage of lava. This entry focuses on cartographic design in archived and educational settings. [Research profile](../../../knowledge/aesthetics/lava-flow-cartography.md)

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Give flow layers clear values and put them on softer land, water or relief. Warm reds and yellows are common for observed extents. Explicit model keys also use cyan, lilac and green with a different semantics. Use swatches accompanied by text.

**Type**: Use short vent labels, a longer field or sheet title, and crisp small numerals for scale and observation period. Keep labels clear of critical flow edges. Use contrast and grouping to separate source text from on-map annotation.

**Texture**: Thin contour strokes or shaded relief keep slopes legible beneath overlays. Dotted forests, dashed uncertainties and hatching give separate data layers distinct surfaces.

**Shape**: Follow observed landforms with irregular branching polygons. Use separate point or line shapes for vents, fissures and roads. Treat ocean boundaries and insets as orientation features.

**Motion**: The audited maps are static. A reader may switch between dated states or hide a layer. Keep scale and camera position steady to present comparisons at the same spatial reference.

**Spatial**: Use a main view, clear frame, compact legend, and a small regional inset when needed. Put documentation, sheet name, dates and scale outside crowded regions.

**Cultural markers**: Credit the observatory or mapper. Preserve appropriate place-name spelling. Distinguish observations, models, historical periods and illustrative data in the legend and labels.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**:

- Contrasting flow areas on a legible terrain base with consistent spatial reference.
- Legend terms distinguish vent, fissure, mapped extent and modeled coverage where applicable.
- Dates, map unit or schematic status, and traceable provenance.

## Connotation

**Connotation**: Earth observation through colored layers, careful scale and the visible relationship between landform and documented change.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Use an original schematic region with a fixed coastline and controlled flow layer changes. Let the reader compare mapped extent by survey period with dated legend swatches and a version label. Pair visible map changes with text so the data remains available without color or pointer interaction. Historical scans may be linked as sources.

## CSS Translation

Use old map paper `#efe8d4`, ink `#262d32`, grayscale relief `#d7d4c6`, water `#b9d9e0`, earlier flow `#cc7734` and later flow `#a73134`. Add graphic marks for the active area and use high-contrast control labels beside the map.

## Typography / Fonts

Give the sheet title a condensed or compact display sans. Use tabular dates and coordinates in labels. Allow legend rows and note text to wrap comfortably.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Use attributed historic or modeled labels for real cases. Mark fictional geography as a schematic study and present its data as illustrative.

## Anti-Patterns

- Using a glow effect as the sole means of showing where flows run.
- One swatch with multiple meanings for forecast, observation, and terrain.
- Legends placed over high-density data or derived from the palette alone.
- Interpolated motion that implies unobserved intermediate lava positions.
