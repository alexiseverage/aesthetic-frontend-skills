# Biomechanical / Biopunk

Organic and manufactured structures behave as one system: a housing becomes a rib cage, a tube becomes a tendon, or a cavity reads as both chamber and organ. Surface decoration alone is insufficient. This catalog retains its combined label, while distinguishing biomechanical visual grammar from the broader biological and social-fiction scope of biopunk.

## Scope

Use for fictional instruments, organic machines, speculative material systems and immersive art interfaces. Translate shape, connected structure, material and spatial continuity into components; people, portraits, photographs of tattooed bodies and copied creatures are unnecessary. A neutral dashboard with a rib texture at the edge is a light influence rather than a full expression.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Bone, charcoal, gunmetal and brown support the classic sculptural branch. Purple, acid yellow/green, blue, pink and orange occur in practitioner paintings and tattoos. Use contrasting material roles rather than require black, blood red or neon green everywhere.

**Type**: Historical sources in the direct corpus do not establish a single type system. Narrow technical labels, tabular specimen numbers and quiet readable support text are useful proposed UI choices. Gallery website fonts are not evidence of a universal biomechanical font.

**Texture**: Ribbing, routed tubes, cavity depth, polished metal, matte shell and soft membrane-like regions. Make material transitions part of one continuous structure. Wet gloss is optional; rust, dry bone and clean plated surfaces also occur.

**Shape**: Vertebral repetition, coiled conduits, apertures, branching supports, layered plates and asymmetrical enclosing shells. Preserve a coherent relationship between inner channel and outer casing. A human face or explicit body is not required.

**Motion**: Static sculpture and paintings can carry the style fully. A fictional interface may propose tension, a single pulse, contraction or a controlled return to rest after input. Source timing is unknown in the current still-led corpus. Avoid constant twitching; reduced motion preserves the readable state without interpolation.

**Spatial**: Connected cavities, nested chambers and structural repetition across a whole artifact, from an enclosing instrument frame to a room. Labs, vats and operating rooms are possible fictional settings, not required ingredients. Leave stable quiet information zones within the complex shell.

**Cultural markers**: H. R. Giger's machine/anatomy fusion and the biomechanical tattoo/painting practice represented by Guy Aitchison, with individual collaborators credited in source research. Alien is historical franchise context, not a reusable design asset; Ghost in the Shell is adjacent cyborg context rather than proof of one required look.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: - Organic and manufactured structures form a coherent hybrid rather than two unrelated decorations.
- Connected enclosure, segmentation or branching establishes how the artifact is held together.
- Material and depth distinguish shell, recess and inner channel while keeping content readable.
- Components embody the identity without people imagery or a copied franchise creature.

## Connotation

**Mode:** contemporary fictional quotation. Cultivated, invasive, uncanny or symbiotic depending on palette, enclosure and rhythm. Choose the intended tone; gore, sexualized anatomy and horror are not mandatory. Do not present simulated signal values as medical or biological facts.

## Related / Subsets

`organic-digital` shares flowing form but need not expose anatomical/mechanical internals. `cyberpunk` shares speculative bodies and technology while often emphasizing urban/social systems. Biopunk includes genetic and biological speculation beyond biomechanical form; the combined catalog label does not collapse that whole genre into a black ribbed surface. No canonical subsets are currently defined.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Author the shell and inner system together. Use original procedural ribs, an asymmetrical molded control, or a branching SVG membrane that responds to an actual parameter. Give pathways clear names, display the selected state, and make any cycle explicitly user-triggered with a completion state. Keep readable text on stable opaque surfaces. Real tables, labels and focus rings remain useful within the unusual enclosure.

## CSS Translation

- Separate outer shell, rib highlight, deep cavity, membrane, text and active-signal tokens.
- Use layered gradients and inset shadows sparingly to distinguish dry outer material from a recessed soft channel. Do not make every element glossy.
- Build repeated vertebral segments with deterministic CSS/SVG geometry, varying their axis or contour coherently rather than scattering spikes randomly.
- Use asymmetric elliptical corners for housing/control silhouettes; preserve generous target sizes and ordinary keyboard behavior.
- Stack shell, readings and controls on phones. Strong material cues should survive the gallery crop without a backdrop photograph.
- If motion is useful, animate one triggered cycle and return to rest. Under `prefers-reduced-motion`, show the same state and completion text without pulsing.

## Typography / Fonts

A compact system sans with monospaced technical labels is an application option, not a historically definitive face. Use large enough readings, clear unit labels and restrained tracking. Display numerals may be narrow; instructions, focus states and errors need normal legibility.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Credit the particular artists and practices that inform the design. Avoid treating every organic tattoo or all biological fiction as biomechanical. Research can inspect bodies as source material while the delivered web components express the grammar through their own construction. Do not reproduce tattoo photographs, protected creatures, artwork or logos without appropriate rights. [Research audit and limitations](../../../knowledge/aesthetics/biomechanical.md) separates direct observation from invented interface behavior.

## Anti-Patterns

- A person, tattoo photograph, copied monster or franchise still carrying the whole aesthetic.
- Plain rounded dashboard panels with disconnected spikes or a palette-only skin.
- Assuming all examples are black, wet, bloody, laboratory-based or sexually anatomical.
- Every control twitching or breathing forever, especially without a reduced-motion state.
- Random tubes that imply connections the UI does not make, fake live telemetry or invented medical claims.
- Treating the wider biopunk genre and Giger's specific imagery as interchangeable.
