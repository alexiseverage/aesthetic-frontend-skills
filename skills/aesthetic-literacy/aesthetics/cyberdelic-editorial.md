# Cyberdelic Editorial

## Scope

The experimental editorial language associated with late-1980s/1990s cyberculture magazines, especially *Mondo 2000*, with adjacent small-press branches in *FringeWare Review* and *bOING bOING*. It combines surreal image quotation, heterogeneous display type, computer-rendered or pixel imagery, print collage and a curious or critical attitude toward bodies, media and technology. It is a descriptive branch, not a claim that every page or contributor shared one visual style.

Preserve the relation between strange display and useful reading. A cover can be exuberant while an article uses orderly columns, captions and resource lists. Apply this language to the masthead, index, section hierarchy, callouts and controls; a normal page with one psychedelic image is insufficient.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Saturated synthetic green, violet, cyan, warm red/yellow and metallic-looking highlights can collide with photographic skin, black, white or muted archival images. Black/white plus one accent and pale quiet pages are important variants. No universal neon scheme or exact historic hex is established.

**Type**: Multiple deliberate voices: rendered/material letters, angular broken geometry, distorted or rotated display, very narrow capitals, serif reading text, typewriter registers and handwritten/comic accents. One masthead can mix media. Keep essential UI/body copy stable and legible; historical overlaps and tiny captions need accessible adaptation.

**Texture**: Photographic montage, grayscale threshold/halftone, grain, pixel stepping, airbrushed or early-rendered dimensional objects, flat illustration and print fragments. Use bounded texture and purposeful material contrast. Scan creases, ownership stamps, price stickers and glare are not canonical textures.

**Shape**: Inset windows, thin framing rules, slanted labels, oversized punctuation, resource columns, technical diagram overlays, geometric/pixel paths, organic curves and surreal changes of scale. Choose motifs with a relationship to the article or navigation, not a random cyber-symbol collection.

**Motion**: The inspected evidence is still print. Tilt, repeated faces and pixel trees imply energy but prove no animation timing. Immediate article selection and native disclosures suit the reading context. Any brief functional transition is a contemporary proposal; remove nonessential motion, smooth scrolling and animated ornament for reduced motion. No flashing or forced glitch.

**Spatial**: Expressive masthead/hero or article opener paired with structured reading columns, marginal notes, numbered registers, captions and deliberate asymmetry. Dense and spare pages coexist. Preserve a clear mobile reading order; collapse margins into in-flow notes rather than shrinking a desktop spread.

**Cultural markers**: Cyberculture publishing, independent networks, speculative/critical technology, media theory, virtual reality, electronic art and countercultural experimentation. Humor, curiosity and ambivalence coexist with commercialization. It is broader than dystopian worldbuilding and is not a neutral endorsement of every period claim.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: a meaningful analog/digital or real/surreal juxtaposition; heterogeneous but intentional editorial hierarchy; a recognizably cyberculture relationship between content, imagery and technical/print registers; clear reading and navigation adapted for the web. Neither maximal density, a neon palette, a human portrait nor one particular magazine logo is compulsory.

## Connotation

**Mode:** contemporary revival with curious, critical editorial framing.

It can convey playful intellectual experimentation, media skepticism and unstable boundaries between organic life and computation. Avoid treating the period's speculative rhetoric as a fulfilled prediction or using counterculture as a shallow costume. The source publications contain contradictions and varied politics; one style does not prove a shared worldview.

## Related / Subsets

- **Cyberpunk** supplies overlapping authors/topics, while this editorial branch can be playful, pastoral, satirical or utopian rather than a dystopian urban interface.
- **Psychedelic** contributes perceptual/countercultural experimentation; early digital collage, technical references and magazine information systems distinguish this branch.
- **Rave Flyer** shares typography and electronic culture but centers event promotion rather than sustained reading, resource directories and editorial sidebars.
- **Desktop Publishing** shares accessible tools and mixed type. Cyberdelic editorial adds a particular surreal/cybercultural subject and image logic.
- **Early Internet** overlaps with networks, but print artifacts are not evidence for blinking links, browser chrome or authentic web interaction.
- Glossy photographic/rendered montage, threshold/comic neurozine and restrained resource-editorial pages are useful internal branches. They are not separate catalog entries.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Use an independent technology/culture journal: expressive mixed-material masthead, numbered topic index, actual search/filter results, one article reader and in-flow glossary/callout disclosures. Give a surreal original image a bounded role; carry identity through caption bars, rotated short labels, contrast panels, type hierarchy and marginal diagrams.

Search must update real results and provide a clear empty/reset state. Article changes must update title, text, glossary and selected indicators. Native details can expose substantive definitions and source/context notes. Reading controls need clear labels, visible keyboard focus, complete semantic reading order and live result counts. Anchor links must reach actual sections. Do not make visual disorder into a keyboard trap or an unreadable text layer.

## CSS Translation

Proposed roles: paper `#F6F1DD`, ink `#191722`, acid `#CCF249`, violet `#7042A4`, cyan `#84D7DD`, rust `#B9402F`. These are application values estimated from observed hue families, not sampled historic inks. Keep ink/paper and ink/acid for essential reading; verify colored labels/states separately.

Use square reading panels, 1px rules, a few hard offset accents, bounded halftone/pixel diagrams and one irregular/slanted display label. Mixed display lettering must have a solid readable fallback. Prefer 16px body/1.6 line height, 12–14px working metadata, 44px minimum controls and approximately 55–70-character reading lines. Root content containment supports narrow gallery embedding. Under reduced motion disable nonessential animation/transition and smooth scrolling; content remains complete.

## Typography / Fonts

Pair a readable Georgia/system serif article voice with narrow or tracked Arial/system sans metadata, system-monospace resource labels and one short expressive display treatment. A mixed-letter masthead can use original CSS/SVG treatments, while the actual name remains accessible text. These are proposed categories/fallbacks, not identified magazine fonts. Do not imitate the Mondo or FringeWare logos.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Credit specific artists only when verified: art direction, illustration, photography and lettering are different roles. Keep source reproductions linked for research; access does not grant publication rights. The corpus includes religious quotation, altered bodies, health/drug claims, political parody and commercial ads. Do not extract sacred figures as ornament, reproduce unsafe period advice, treat satire as fact or infer identity from a portrait. Create original text, images and marks. Distinguish a fictional journal from an actual historic publication.

## Anti-Patterns

- Normal components whose sole aesthetic signal is a generated cyberdelic picture.
- Copying a magazine masthead, familiar composite, sacred figure or commercial logo.
- Calling every bright 1990s design cyberdelic or every cyberculture page maximalist.
- Overlapping essential body copy, microscopic labels or illegible rotated controls.
- Mandatory glitch, flashing, autoplay, color-only states or fake loading.
- Guessing fonts, exact production process or animation from a scan.
- Treating outdated resource listings or medical/drug/technology claims as current guidance.
