# Balletcore

Balletcore is a contemporary lifestyle and fashion aesthetic that translates ballet rehearsal, stage, and off-duty dancer codes into a broader visual system. Use it when poise, discipline, textile softness, and body-aware movement are as important as the familiar blush-and-ribbon palette.

## Scope

Use Balletcore for fashion, wellness, editorial commerce, performance programs, community class pages, and lifestyle interfaces where elegance, training, and soft discipline matter. Keep task flows light and readable; apply ballet cues through hero composition, product cards, transition timing, dividers, and textile/detail systems rather than covering every surface in ribbons.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Blush, cream, satin ivory, dove gray, soft taupe, peach, nude, and black leotard contrast.

**Type**: Poised serif or soft sans, elongated proportions, recital/program accents, and airy hierarchy.

**Texture**: Tulle, ribbed knit, jersey, satin, mesh, pointe ribbon, tights, and worn warm-up layers.

**Shape**: Wrap closures, long lines, leotards, ballet flats, ribbons, leg warmers, shrugs, and barre anchors.

**Motion**: Controlled float, pirouette loops, ribbon spirals, tulle fades, and precise vertical lift.

**Spatial**: Airy studio/stage/barre orientation, centered axes, generous breathing room, and rehearsal-layer density.

**Cultural markers**: Ballet flats, pointe references, leotards, tights, wrap sweaters, tulle, ribbons, barre, and off-duty dancer pragmatism.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: dance-derived silhouette + rehearsal/stage material vocabulary + poised body-trained motion + feminine restraint. If these are removed, the result collapses into an adjacent or generic style rather than Balletcore.

## Connotation

Balletcore reads as a source-backed contemporary characterization whose cues should be treated as a multi-dimensional system rather than a palette.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Build around one poised vertical axis, recital-like hierarchy, and material contrast between soft textile surfaces and clean support UI. Use ribbons, barre lines, program cards, and tulle fades as accents; preserve generous whitespace and accessible body copy.

## CSS Translation

- Color roles: `--bg-studio`, `--surface-satin`, `--accent-blush`, `--accent-ribbon`, `--ink-leotard`, `--line-dove`.
- Borders/dividers: hairline barre rules, ribbon separators, recital-program frames, and soft translucent overlays.
- Radius language: elongated pills and softened cards; avoid childish bubble shapes.
- Effects: satin sheen, mesh/tulle translucency, subtle textile grain, and controlled shadow lift.
- Layout: centered hero axes, airy cards, rehearsal/stage zones, and restrained product/editorial grids.
- Motion: slow float, precise vertical lift, ribbon sweeps, and reduced-motion opacity or position changes.

## Typography / Fonts

Pair a poised serif or refined soft sans display with readable support text. Use script or recital-program flourishes sparingly for labels and invitations; too much ornate romance pushes the result toward coquette rather than dance discipline.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Do not trivialize dancer labor, body discipline, injury risk, or professional ballet cultures as childlike costume fantasy. Avoid turning the style into compulsory thinness or class-coded femininity.

## Anti-Patterns

- Generic pale pink fashion with no dance-derived silhouette, textile, or motion logic.
- Coquette lingerie/flirt coding where bows and romance replace posture and rehearsal discipline.
- Childlike ballerina costume fantasy that erases dancer labor or physical training.
- Heavy, cluttered layouts that lose air, axis, and controlled movement.
