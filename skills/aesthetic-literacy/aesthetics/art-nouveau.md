# Art Nouveau

Art Nouveau is a historical decorative-arts and architecture movement built around sinuous organic line, nature-as-structure, integrated ornament, and the dissolution of boundaries between image, type, surface, object, and room. For frontend work, treat it as nature-structured total design rather than a floral motif pack: curves, frames, lettering, and content hierarchy should feel connected by one flowing system.

## Scope

Use Art Nouveau for cultural pages, boutique commerce, floral or botanical brands, jewelry and fragrance, literary/editorial launches, hospitality, museum interpretation, music or event posters, and expressive brand worlds where romantic craft luxury is welcome. It is especially useful when a page can behave like an illustrated poster, framed invitation, or ornamental product story.

Avoid applying it wholesale to dense operational software. Modern navigation, forms, and body copy should stay readable while Nouveau linework, frames, initials, and botanical panels carry the atmospheric layer.

Choose a regional and material emphasis rather than combining every recognizable motif. French/Belgian whiplash work, Glasgow's controlled geometry and Austrian/German linear abstraction share an ambition to integrate form and ornament, but do not have identical silhouettes. The [direct visual profile](../../../knowledge/aesthetics/art-nouveau.md) records thirty inspected museum artifacts, regional exceptions and remaining gaps.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Let the chosen medium set the color logic: amber/green/pink iridescent glass, silver enamel, figured wood, cream/taupe textiles or strongly contrasted red/yellow/purple print. Botanical and jewel tones are common options, not a compulsory universal palette. Quiet near-monochrome variants are supported when material and structural line remain convincing.

**Type**: Sinuous custom lettering, integrated titling, expressive display forms, and letterforms that feel drawn into the composition. Use readable serif or sans support type beneath decorative headers, borders, initials, and poster-style titles.

**Texture**: Flat ornamental line, stained glass, enamel, ceramics, wrought metal, carved wood, wallpaper, embossed print, and jewelry-like polish. Digital texture should emphasize contour, craft, and surface integration rather than photographic grime.

**Shape**: Whiplash curves, elongated stems, flared mouths, branching supports, cellular panels, openwork voids and asymmetrical frames. In the flowing branch, curves shape the usable object and its border. Geometric regional variants may instead organize nature-derived motifs into straight uprights, repeated cells or disciplined axes; integration matters more than making every edge curl.

**Motion**: Historical still artifacts establish eye-leading rhythm and implied growth, not animation timings. Intentional stillness is valid. If adding a slow line draw, light change or unfolding reveal, identify it as a contemporary design proposal, keep task feedback immediate and preserve the complete static structure with reduced motion.

**Spatial**: Decorated borders, total-surface integration, poster framing, interlocked image/type fields, and activated negative space. Whitespace may exist, but it should feel shaped by surrounding ornament rather than neutral or empty.

**Cultural markers**: Belle Époque modernity, anti-historicist reform, applied/fine arts integration, Hector Guimard metro entrances, Victor Horta interiors, Alphonse Mucha posters, Louis Tiffany glass, René Lalique jewelry, peacock feathers, lilies, vines, flowing hair, and local names such as Jugendstil or Modernisme.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**:

- Nature-derived structure, expressed through flowing contour or a deliberately chosen regional geometric variant.
- A coherent material and line system that shapes frames, supports, lettering or repeated fields.
- Integrated ornament where type, frame, illustration, and surface participate in one composition.
- Ornament integrated with the function and silhouette, rather than an unrelated layer of flowers.

## Connotation

**Mode:** contemporary revival.

Art Nouveau reads as romantic, botanical, sensuous, and craft-luxury oriented, with Belle Époque modernity behind it. It can feel elegant and enchanted when restrained, or over-sweet and poster-shop nostalgic when reduced to Mucha-style figures alone. Preserve the movement's systems logic: organic line should organize the design, not just decorate it.

## Related / Subsets

- `arts-and-crafts` shares reform-era craft values and nature interest, but Arts and Crafts tends toward handcraft honesty and patterned order where Art Nouveau emphasizes metropolitan flow and sinuous line.
- `vienna-secession` is a same-era neighbor that disciplines ornament toward flatter geometry and proto-modern restraint.
- `art-deco` is the later contrast: it hardens decorative energy into geometry, polish, and machine-age symmetry.
- `gothic-revival` can overlap in ornament density and luxury craft, but Gothic Revival is medievalist-historical where Art Nouveau presents itself as new and nature-derived.

No canonical subsets are defined here; regional names such as Jugendstil, Stile Liberty, and Modernisme are aliases or context labels, not separate dictionary subsets in this entry.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Use Art Nouveau where the page can support an ornamental frame: illustrated hero panels, editorial headers, product storytelling, menus, invitations, chapter dividers, decorative cards, initial letters, and boutique landing pages. Keep key task flows clear by separating expressive display zones from simpler content modules.

Let one organic system guide the screen. A vine frame that becomes a divider and then a card motif will feel more authentic than unrelated flowers, peacock feathers, and curlicues applied independently.

## CSS Translation

- Color roles: `--bg-cream`, `--surface-parchment`, `--accent-sage`, `--accent-peacock`, `--accent-burgundy`, `--accent-gold`, and `--line-ink`.
- Borders/dividers: vine frames, whiplash rules, floral corner pieces, elongated cartouches, and stained-glass panel outlines.
- Radius language: flowing asymmetric curves, elongated ovals, tendril-like masks, and soft but controlled organic frames.
- Effects: SVG linework, mask-based botanical borders, subtle paper grain, enamel/glass highlights, and embossed or letterpress shadows.
- Layout: poster-like framing, integrated title/image areas, ornamental margins, and content panels shaped by continuous line.
- Motion: vine unfurl, slow line draw, gentle drift, curl reveals, and opacity fades with `prefers-reduced-motion` fallbacks.

## Typography / Fonts

Use expressive display lettering for focal titles, initials, wordmarks, and ornamental labels: sinuous, high-contrast, hand-drawn, or period-inspired forms. Pair it with a restrained serif or readable sans for body copy and UI controls.

Avoid making every label ornate. The style reads best when a few display moments lock into frames and illustrations while support typography keeps contemporary usability intact.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Art Nouveau was an international movement with distinct local branches. Avoid presenting one Mucha-poster shorthand as the whole style, and be cautious when borrowing regional architecture, craft traditions, or symbolic flora without context. For historical or museum use, name the local branch when specificity matters.

The reviewed object sample is weighted toward French, British and American museum collections. It does not establish regional dominance or a complete architectural canon. Name influences accurately; avoid pseudo-cultural lettering and detach modern interface proposals from claims about historical behavior.

## Anti-Patterns

- Floral clip art placed on an otherwise neutral layout; Art Nouveau requires integrated line and composition.
- Hard chevrons, sunbursts, stepped symmetry, chrome, and machine glamour; that drifts into Art Deco.
- Minimal Swiss grids and neutral sans hierarchy as the dominant signal.
- Generic fantasy vines or occult clutter without Belle Époque craft, line, or total-design logic.
- Over-centering a single Mucha-style maiden in contexts where architecture, glass, type, or craft would be more appropriate.
