# Fairground / Carnival Poster Art

A publicity vernacular for fairs, circuses and touring attractions: outsized display lettering, competing act scales, framed illustrations and local place/date information turn a sheet into a promise of spectacle. British amusement handbills and American illustrated circus lithographs are related branches, not one fixed template.

## Scope

Use for entertainment programs, event announcements, ticketing and contemporary print-inspired promotion. Preserve attraction hierarchy and local information, with plainly usable controls. The researched corpus is US/UK and strongly Strobridge-weighted; avoid universal geographic claims.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Red, yellow, blue, cream and black commonly organize mastheads and caption strips around multicolor illustration. Pale, monochrome and type-only variants are documented; aged brown is not mandatory.

**Type**: Strong size contrast; condensed capitals, flared/slab display, outlines, shadows, angled names and packed support lines. Script and simpler sans headings are valid variants. Use category/treatment descriptions unless an exact face is verified.

**Texture**: Printed paper, lithographic or other catalog-identified process, visible local paste/overprint strips and ink surfaces. Wear is the condition of surviving sheets, not an essential original design instruction.

**Shape**: Ribbon mastheads, oval medallions, decorated cartouches, framed vignettes, straight rules and local information panels. Stars, tents and stripes are optional motifs.

**Motion**: Still posters imply action through diagonals, loops and repeated scenes. Actual timing/easing is unknown. Stillness or immediate user-triggered changes are valid interface proposals.

**Spatial**: Brand, attraction, image, claim and place/date form an emphatic hierarchy. Dense panoramas coexist with spare single-act pictures and centered typographic handbills; landscape formats are common in the inspected material.

**Cultural markers**: Touring showpeople, advance publicity, local overprinting, extraordinary-act rhetoric and popular entertainment business. Treat exploitative historical content critically rather than as generic carnival decoration.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**:

- Dramatic display hierarchy tied to entertainment attraction and announcement.
- Framed or banded relationships between headline, spectacle/program and practical local information.
- Convincing printed or sign-lettered material logic, including documented monochrome/sparse variants.

Bright color, distressed paper, dense text and a vertical poster are common choices, not universal requirements.

## Connotation

For a **contemporary revival**, evoke the anticipation and craft of a travelling event through original lettering, ornament and program hierarchy. For historical quotation, name the period and source context. Avoid presenting exaggerated claims or harmful stereotypes as an authentic requirement.

## Related / Subsets

- `wpa-poster-style`: public-information print overlaps in technique but usually has different purposes and simplification.
- `wartime-propaganda`: shared mass-publicity tools, different institutional/political context.
- `board-game-box-art`: illustrated promotion of a boxed play system rather than a local touring event.
- No canonical subsets are defined. Distinguish illustrated circus lithograph and typographic amusement handbill branches in prose.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Lead with one show/event name, follow with ranked attractions, then make place, time and ticket information easy to find. Use original ornament, object imagery and broadside dividers to carry identity. Keep functional labels readable even when display lettering is theatrical. Do not treat every control as another competing headline.

## CSS Translation

- Proposed color roles: cream paper, dark ink, red display, blue frame and yellow claim strip; choose actual values for contrast.
- Build outlines/shadows with CSS or original SVG lettering; avoid squeezing long words until illegible.
- Use nested rules, shaped cartouches, ribbons and a distinct local-information strip.
- Keep paper grain subtle behind opaque text areas. Do not force artificial wear onto every surface.
- Preserve rank when stacking on phone: title, attraction/program, place/time, then action.
- Prefer print-rooted stillness unless proposed motion serves a specific interaction; never infer flashing from still art.

## Typography / Fonts

Mix a limited set of strongly different widths/weights: one expressive display, one condensed attraction face and a quiet readable support face. System serif/sans categories can approximate the hierarchy without claiming an exact historical font. Avoid turning all copy into decorative capitals.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

The historical corpus contains colonial exoticism, dehumanizing exhibition, racial caricature and captive-animal spectacle. Those are evidence to contextualize, not motifs to reproduce casually. Recognize travelling showpeople as communities and makers, not merely a nostalgic costume. Contemporary revival can use original non-figurative ornament and honest event details.

## Anti-Patterns

- Cream paper plus stripes with no attraction hierarchy or local-event logic.
- Mandatory sepia distress, maximal density or vertical proportions despite documented variants.
- Copying caricatures, human exhibitions or exploitative rhetoric as neutral decoration.
- Flashing bulbs or kinetic type justified only by printed action.
- Making session selection, quantities or prices ambiguous for theatrical effect.
