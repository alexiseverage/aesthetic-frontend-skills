# Cut-Rock Candy Lettering

## Scope

A lettering and pictogram system built inside cylindrical hard candy. A short phrase, monogram or silhouette repeats through each cut. Solid and striped sidewalls define the perimeter without competing with the central mark. Useful for specimen browsers, badge systems, confectionery packaging and interfaces that explain how an object is assembled. [Research profile](../../../knowledge/aesthetics/cut-rock-candy-lettering.md)

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Place dark berry, plum or blue text against an opaque cream center. Allow red, amber, blue, green or alternating color blocks in the perimeter. Maker samples also show light marks inside colored interiors.

**Type**: Use short heavyweight capitals and broad counters. Two short curved lines can bracket a central motif. Initials can sit in one row, and small marks or dates can use a compact two-line composition. Keep the UI labels outside the artwork in easily read type.

**Texture**: Give the sidewall a fine lengthwise grain and narrow highlights. Keep the cut face softer and give it slight outline irregularity. Treat transparent sections as an option for the outer layer.

**Shape**: Keep the label physically enclosed by a roughly circular cut face. Axial stripes should end in matching rim segments. A single solid ring is a valid alternate construction.

**Motion**: The main visual evidence is static product photography. For a contemporary comparison, offer an explicit selection between views. A cut may change shape slightly while its internal composition remains recognizable.

**Spatial**: Separate the side view, magnified cross-section, and explanatory copy. Show enough white space between the inset lettering and the edge. Use secondary slices or a side elevation to demonstrate repetition.

**Cultural markers**: Seaside souvenir names, celebrations, maker collections and commissioned marks provide contrasting applications. A similar cross-section logic also appears in Japanese Kintarō-ame. Describe each source tradition by name where relevant.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**:

- The letters or pictogram sit visibly inside a cut face and recur across cuts.
- The side profile and head-on color divisions agree.
- Keep the outer rind and internal mark visually separate.

## Connotation

**Connotation**: Legible souvenir lettering, small-scale handiwork, and the variation among repeated cuts. A specimen display can bring focus to the material and the letterforms.

## Related / Subsets

- Gummy / Jelly Design shares colorful material cues. Cut rock places a repeated graphic inside the cross-section.
- European Heraldic Design also puts short lettering and signs in bounded shapes.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Build a large original cut specimen beside a side elevation. Let readers compare a segmented rim, solid rind, and translucent band. Link actions to the same construction in the cross-section and side view. Use human-readable names for every state. Provide equivalent selected-state cues for keyboard readers and in the presence of custom color settings.

## CSS Translation

Start with opaque cream `#fff2d6`, berry ink `#67162e`, raspberry `#c52d52`, amber `#f3ac36`, and pink paper `#f7d0c1`. Keep UI text in `#3f1621` and give active controls a dark border and visible underline. Build structural circles, coarse lettering and matched bands as a vector illustration. Save soft highlight gradients for the rendered sugar.

## Typography / Fonts

Use a chunky display face for the product heading. Keep field labels and captions in a sturdy sans-serif at 14 px or above in the live detail. Preserve spacing between art letters even if the pattern is shown at small scale.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Credit workshops and collections. Keep identifiable commissioned marks, logos and character motifs in linked research. Use original lettering and silhouettes for the interface.

## Anti-Patterns

- Rounded gelatin forms that hide the cut face or make the object read as gummy candy.
- Rim segments that change order between the cross-section and side views.
- Decorative names that run into the central motif or ring.
- Gloss effects that erase legible labels or selected-state boundaries.
