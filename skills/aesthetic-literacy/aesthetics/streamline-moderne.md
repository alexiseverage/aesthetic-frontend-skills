## Scope

Streamline Moderne is the 1930s–1940s late Art Deco language of aerodynamic curves, transport modernity, polished machine surfaces, ocean liners, bus terminals, diners, appliances, and Depression-era “world of tomorrow” optimism. Use it for retro hospitality, transportation history, vintage product campaigns, museum micro-sites, and period-themed events. It reads as period charm, not current cutting-edge technology.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Cream, chrome silver, black, navy, turquoise, mint, cherry red, and restrained pastel appliance tones. The palette should suggest speed, cleanliness, and modern transport with neutral bases and strategic color pops.

**Type**: Geometric sans-serif, Futura/Kabel/Neutraface-like lettering, chrome-era signage, rounded efficient display type, and horizontal titling integrated into bands or architectural marks.

**Texture**: Polished chrome, lacquered wood, glass block, vitrolite panels, enamel, smooth plaster, glossy painted surfaces, and subtle ribbed metal. Rough craft texture breaks the machine polish.

**Shape**: Horizontal speed lines, teardrop curves, fully rounded corners, porthole circles, aerodynamic shells, stepped curves, transport-body contours, and softened rectangles.

**Motion**: Gliding, sweeping, route-like movement, and implied acceleration through elongation. Motion should be smooth and unhurried, not jagged or chaotic.

**Spatial**: Long horizontal composition, low-profile headers, layered decks, cantilevered elements, porthole focal points, and layouts that feel like vehicles, terminals, or ocean liners.

**Cultural markers**: Chrome diner architecture, Chrysler Airflow, Greyhound terminals, Miami Beach Art Deco district, Normandie ocean liner associations, appliances, rail and bus design, and 1930s industrial optimism.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: aerodynamic contour; strong horizontal speed-line orientation; rounded corners or portholes; polished chrome/cream machine surfaces; transport-era optimism.

## Connotation

**Connotation**: Nostalgic quotation of a future that once promised speed, hygiene, and democratic machine luxury. It feels fond, sleek, and charmingly dated rather than neutral, cynical, or cutting-edge.

## Related / Subsets

- `art-deco` — parent/adjacent style; Streamline strips vertical Deco ornament into horizontal aerodynamic forms.
- `bauhaus` — shares industrial modernity but is orthogonal and intellectual where Streamline is curved and populist.
- `atomic-age` — inherits streamline transport optimism and amplifies it into space-race spectacle.
- Subsets include Style Paquebot, Miami Beach Streamline, and streamline diner architecture.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Use horizontal rhythm, rounded modules, chrome accents, and transport metaphors. Keep components low, wide, and sleek. Porthole avatars, speed-line separators, pill CTAs, and smooth horizontal sections can work when the period framing is explicit. Avoid using it for products that need to seem contemporary and serious unless the retro charm is deliberate.

## CSS Translation

- Palette tokens: `--sm-cream: #f5f0e8; --sm-chrome: #c0c0c0; --sm-red: #cc3333; --sm-turquoise: #40b8b0; --sm-mint: #a8d8b9; --sm-charcoal: #2c2c2c;`.
- Use `border-radius: 40px` for cards and `border-radius: 9999px` for pills, with occasional porthole circles.
- Use horizontal rules, ribbed gradients, and restrained metallic `linear-gradient()` accents.
- Favor wide layouts, horizontal scroll sections, and gentle glide transitions.

## Typography / Fonts

Use Montserrat, Poppins, Outfit, Futura-like, Kabel-like, or Neutraface-like geometric sans. Low x-height, circular forms, and sleek signage proportions support the aerodynamic effect.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Streamline Moderne is historically tied to 1930s industrial optimism and consumer modernization during economic hardship. Avoid treating it as timeless luxury futurism or erasing its transport, labor, and Depression-era context. It is low identity-risk but culturally specific.

## Anti-Patterns

Avoid sharp brutalist mass, dense Art Deco ornament, rustic texture, jagged punk motion, generic retro chrome, or futuristic claims that ignore the style’s period nostalgia. It should feel streamlined, not merely rounded.
