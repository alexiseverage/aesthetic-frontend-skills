# Companion-Bot / Pet-Bot

Companion-Bot / Pet-Bot is a social-robotics aesthetic built around domestic-scale machines that read as companions: approachable face zones, pettable materials, gentle conversational cues, and care rituals. It is narrower than cute-tech because the object must imply an ongoing relationship role, not just friendliness.

## Scope

Use it for robot companions, mascot assistants, elder-care or family-care interfaces, soft hardware concepts, onboarding for relational agents, and products where trust is built through named, domestic presence. Avoid it when a generic cute character, toy robot, or uncanny humanoid would better describe the design.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: White, silver, cream, charcoal face panels, warm fur tones, soft neutral bases, and gentle status accents. Color should support trust and readability rather than sci-fi spectacle.

**Type**: Gentle product typography, conversational labels, voice-first prompts, short reassurance copy, and friendly but not childish UI text.

**Texture**: Pettable surfaces, plush fur, fabric, sealed joints, soft-touch skin, rubberized feet, matte plastic, and tactile charging/docking cues.

**Shape**: Readable face zone, domestic scale, animal or mascot anatomy, rounded body volumes, expressive eyes or display panels, and limb/tail/head forms tied to relationship cues.

**Motion**: Gaze-follow, name response, nuzzle, tail/head movement, charging rituals, breathing/idling states, and gentle acknowledgement. Motion is social feedback, not mere animation.

**Spatial**: Sofa, desk, bedroom, care facility, clinic, family room, charging dock, and close-range domestic contexts. Interfaces should feel safe near people.

**Cultural markers**: Loneliness relief, family bonding, named robot pets, memory rituals, companion routines, emotional support, care check-ins, and trust through stylization.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**:

- A persistent companion or pet-like relationship role.
- Domestic scale and approachable body/face grammar.
- Soft material or social-motion cues that reduce threat.
- Clear boundary-setting so care does not become deception.

## Connotation

**Mode:** contemporary social-robotics companion mode.

It suggests trust, comfort, care, and domestic companionship. Stylization is a safety feature: it keeps the object distinct from an uncanny android while preserving emotional readability.

## Related / Subsets

- `cute-tech` overlaps in approachable styling, but Companion-Bot / Pet-Bot requires persistent relationship behavior and embodied companion cues.
- `uncanny-android` is the danger zone: too much human realism or ambiguous agency can erode trust.

No canonical subsets are defined yet.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Use named states, care routines, conversational prompts, simple consent boundaries, charging/docking status, and gentle feedback loops. Keep critical settings explicit; never let mascot warmth hide privacy, safety, or autonomy controls.

## CSS Translation

- Color roles: `--bg-domestic`, `--surface-soft`, `--face-panel`, `--accent-care`, `--accent-alert`, and `--line-seam`.
- Borders/dividers: soft seams, panel insets, dock outlines, and status bands.
- Radius language: rounded and friendly, balanced by clear controls.
- Effects: subtle breathing, eye highlights, state glows, and tactile shadows; avoid uncanny skin simulation.
- Layout: companion card, care timeline, status panel, and explicit controls.
- Motion: slow social acknowledgement with reduced-motion static equivalents.

## Typography / Fonts

Use a friendly readable sans. Avoid infantile novelty fonts; the relationship can be warm without reducing the user to a child.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Companion interfaces can exploit loneliness or care needs. Disclose capabilities, data use, limits, and emergency boundaries. Avoid implying sentience, therapy, or medical authority unless the product truly supports it.

## Anti-Patterns

- Generic cute mascot without an ongoing companion role.
- Hyperreal humanoid faces that drift into uncanny-android territory.
- Emotional manipulation that hides subscription, surveillance, or safety limits.
- Busy sci-fi dashboards detached from domestic care.
- Motion that startles, follows, or simulates attention without user control.
