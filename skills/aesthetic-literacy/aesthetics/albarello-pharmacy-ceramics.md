# Albarello Pharmacy Ceramics

A bounded ceramic vessel family with articulated cylindrical or waisted volume and painted surface organization, often associated with historical pharmacy storage.

Evidence basis: [the research profile](../../../knowledge/aesthetics/albarello-pharmacy-ceramics.md) records 30 qualifying unique artifacts, source distribution, claim support and limitations. ## Scope

A bounded ceramic vessel family with articulated cylindrical or waisted volume and painted surface organization, often associated with historical pharmacy storage. It is broader than blue-and-white Italian jars and narrower than all pottery or medicine branding.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Cream/white glaze with cobalt, ochre, green, orange and dark outlines is common. Cobalt/gold stonepaste, copper-brown lustre and blue/white regional examples are important variants; grayscale photographs cannot establish original color.

**Type**: Optional light inscription ribbons with handwritten capitals or blackletter create label hierarchy. Many vessels have no name cartouche. Functional UI requires readable text, not pseudo-Latin or an invented historical typeface.

**Texture**: Glaze highlights, brush variation and clay/body distinction. Maiolica, fritware and gilded stonepaste are not one material process. Chips/crackle are copy condition and not mandatory decoration.

**Shape**: Cylindrical or concave bodies with shoulder, neck and foot. Waist depth varies; covers and one/two handles are documented exceptions. Preserve the whole vessel silhouette and painted registers.

**Motion**: The corpus consists of still object photographs. Automatic product rotation is not intrinsic evidence. Manual glaze/variant selection can change an original illustration immediately; reduce optional transitions when requested.

**Spatial**: Ornament wraps a bounded volume through bands, repeat fields and optional cartouches. Foliage, geometry, portraits and pictorial scenes vary; museum gray backgrounds are photographic presentation.

**Cultural markers**: Regional ceramic workshops, storage histories and pharmacy associations. Avoid flattening distinct Italian, Iberian, Syrian, Iranian or Mexican histories into a single invention story.

## Typography / Fonts

A contemporary high-contrast or old-style serif can carry studio titles, paired with a plain sans for purchase details. Decorative inscription lettering is optional artwork, never the only accessible product name.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Do not copy museum motifs, heraldry or racialized historical imagery into original products. Preserve regional/material attributions in analysis. Sell decorative ceramic objects in a showcase, not historical remedies or medical efficacy.

## Anti-Patterns

- Blue-and-white pattern wallpaper with no vessel articulation.
- Unreadable pseudo-Latin labels and invented apothecary seals.
- Fake cracks as the main signal or a purchase state that ignores selected glaze.
