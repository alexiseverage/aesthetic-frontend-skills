# Hauntology

Hauntology is a retro-media and lost-futures aesthetic of degraded archives, public-information design, educational broadcast residue, and temporal dislocation. It is spectral critique and media memory, not generic spooky vintage.

## Scope

Use Hauntology for archives, music/editorial projects, memory tools, retro-media essays, public-history presentations, and slow campaign storytelling. The design should feel like retrieved media and lost institutional optimism, not a Halloween filter.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Nicotine beige, faded mustard, moss green, smoke gray, washed brown, institutional cream, oxidized teal, and dusk black.

**Type**: Mid-century educational sans, modest serif captions, library-music sleeve type, schoolbook labels, and broadcast-era kerning.

**Texture**: Paper aging, tape hiss as grain, vinyl crackle, photocopy softness, film dust, print fade, halftone, and scanlines.

**Shape**: Sober rectangles, caption boxes, modest circles, diagrams, map markers, institutional frames, and title-card layouts.

**Motion**: Slow drift, loop, dissolve, flicker, projector jitter, tape wobble, and ghosted reveal.

**Spatial**: Archival title cards, isolated ephemera, educational panels, booklet/page spreads, and public-information sequencing.

**Cultural markers**: BBC Radiophonic Workshop, Ghost Box, library music, children’s television idents, public-information films, analog decay, and lost futures.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: degraded archival texture + institutional/educational design language + subdued palette + lost-futures cultural signal. If these are removed, the result collapses into an adjacent or generic style rather than Hauntology.

## Connotation

Hauntology reads as a source-backed aesthetic system whose meaning depends on the cultural frame as much as the surface treatment.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Compose pages as archival title cards, booklet spreads, public-information panels, and isolated ephemera. Use slow fades, tape wobble, grain, and printed degradation as atmosphere while keeping captions and controls legible.

## CSS Translation

- Color roles: `--bg-nicotine`, `--surface-archive`, `--accent-mustard`, `--accent-oxidized-teal`, `--text-smoke`, `--line-faded`.
- Borders/dividers: caption boxes, title-card frames, public-information rules, and booklet panel lines.
- Radius language: sober rectangles and modest circles; avoid glossy futurism.
- Effects: paper aging, tape hiss/grain, photocopy softness, halftone, scanlines, and ghosted overlays.
- Layout: isolated ephemera, archival spreads, educational panels, and quiet negative space.
- Motion: slow dissolve, projector jitter, tape wobble, ghost reveal, and reduced-motion still-card swaps.

## Typography / Fonts

Use mid-century educational sans, modest serifs, public-information captions, library-music sleeve type, and restrained title blocks. Slight awkwardness and archival kerning are welcome; novelty horror lettering is not.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Do not flatten hauntology into spooky retro texture. Preserve the lost-futures and public-media memory critique, and be careful when using real archival material or community-specific nostalgia.

## Anti-Patterns

- Generic spooky vintage, Halloween sepia, or ghost icons without lost-futures critique.
- Vaporwave consumer nostalgia presented as archival public-media residue.
- Overly polished retro design with no degradation, absence, or temporal dislocation.
- Illegible grain and scanlines over important controls or captions.
