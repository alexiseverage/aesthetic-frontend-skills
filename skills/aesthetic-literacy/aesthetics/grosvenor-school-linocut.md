# Grosvenor School Colour Linocut

Reduced forms, repeated cuts, color-block overlaps and active paper gaps turn everyday activity into rhythmic composition. Claude Flight's teaching network connects this interwar relief-print language to Power, Andrews, Tschudi, Spowers, Syme and Black. Speed and collective activity are common subjects; quieter interiors and landscapes belong too.

## Scope

Use for club schedules, cultural programs, event discovery, compact activity planners and progress displays where grouped actions have a clear rhythm. The interface itself should share the cut geometry and positive/negative-space logic. A racing photograph inside generic cards does not establish the aesthetic.

This is a named school/network, not all linocutting, all work by its artists or a synonym for Futurism. The reference frame centers on 1920s origins and 1930s practice, with explicitly identified precursors and later continuations in research.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: A few composed color blocks and their overlaps, often with substantial unprinted paper. Rust, ochre, blue/navy and green recur, alongside pale and dark variants. A first-state blue proof and a monochrome geometric print caution against an absolute multicolor rule. Exact historical ink colors are unknown; application tokens are proposals.

**Type**: Pencil title, signature, edition and proof notes are usually subordinate. Market/bus lettering belongs to specific scenes. A strong condensed uppercase interface face can complement the compressed shapes, but no universal historical school font has been established. Use readable functional labels.

**Texture**: Granular ink, small repeated cut marks, hatch ribs and color overlaps. Keep texture local to graphic surfaces, away from essential text. Paper wrinkles, fading and scan damage are not mandatory effects. Medium metadata, rather than visual resemblance alone, establishes process.

**Shape**: Stretched simplified figures, tapered limbs/vehicles, hooks, wedges, rings and rhythmic cuts. Curved and angular forms can coexist. Repetition needs a directional or grouping purpose; a particular athlete, machine or faceless body is optional.

**Motion**: Still prints imply pace without showing actual animation. Historical timing/easing is unknown. A contemporary functional progress display may move only while its task runs; preserve pause/reset and text state. Under reduced motion, keep information through static updates and remove decorative movement.

**Spatial**: Curving compression, repeated arcs, nested planes, exaggerated perspective and active white gaps. Quiet enclosed scenes are valid variants. Preserve semantic order, stable controls and clear reading zones while the surrounding graphic structure bends.

**Cultural markers**: International teaching connections, hand printing, editions, modern labor/leisure, travel and the aspiration to widen access to modern art. Formal affinities with Futurism do not imply identical politics. Individual artists retain distinct practices.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: deliberate repeated cut forms; coherent positive/negative space; composed relief/color layers; and credible school/process grounding. A speed motif, distressed surface or artist name alone is insufficient.

## Connotation

**Mode:** contemporary revival.

Can suggest shared momentum, craft, collective activity and interwar modernity. Choose the actual purpose: a calm landscape program need not imitate a racing print. Do not claim a historical interface existed.

## Related / Subsets

- `futurism`: speed and dynamic reduction overlap; media, teaching history and political positions are not equivalent.
- `vorticism`: shares angular concentration, while curving color relief and everyday narratives distinguish this school.
- `wpa-poster-style`: limited color/activity scenes can overlap; public institution, geography and production contexts differ.
- `isotype`: repeated figures may resemble pictograms, but artistic crowds are not automatically quantitative symbols.
- Sport, transport, labor, landscape and quieter figure studies remain prose variants, not new catalog slugs.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Organize grouped actions with repeated wedges or curved bands. A schedule's sessions, an activity planner's phases or an event list's active category can become the compositional rhythm. Condensed headings, cut-tick separators, colored state planes and original vector forms should also shape controls. Keep hit targets rectangular/stable even when their decoration tapers. Use text and a persistent active marker alongside color.

## CSS Translation

Use grid for information, bounded SVG/CSS curves for directional structure and layered solid fills for overprint-like relationships. Avoid distorting text to fake perspective. Proposed warm paper, dark navy, rust and ochre roles need actual contrast checks. Reserve graphic dimensions, constrain bleed and stack semantic groups on mobile. If progress moves, define a small explicit duration/easing vocabulary; disable nonessential transforms/transitions under reduced motion. Never infer timing from printed speed lines.

## Typography / Fonts

An existing licensed condensed sans, or a carefully treated system sans fallback, can support bold compressed headings. Pair with clear normal-width labels and body text. This is a contemporary choice, not a font identification. Small historical pencil inscriptions are context; they do not justify tiny UI instructions or images of text.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Attribute artists, process and institutional sources accurately. An aspiration to democratic access does not mean all historical editions were inexpensive. Keep international participants visible and avoid treating every artist as a single national voice. Do not copy protected prints, flatten individual politics or turn industrial labor into an unexamined decorative fantasy. Reference access does not grant commercial image reuse.

## Anti-Patterns

- A filter over racing photos, generic cards or unrelated woodcut artwork.
- Constantly moving targets, literal blur or rapid loops justified by printed dynamism.
- Misregistered essential text, all-over grain or fake historical type identification.
- Assigning every painting/print by a participating artist to the school.
- Repeating figures as quantities without an actual data relationship.
