# Ligne Claire

## Scope

A narrative drawing language centered on clean deliberate contours, readable forms and settings, and predominantly flat local color. Closely associated with Hergé and later practitioners, but neither a single character franchise nor a synonym for every outlined cartoon. Broader membership has debated edges; this entry prioritizes visual clarity over an imaginary strict purity certificate.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Predominantly flat local color bounded by dark contours: muted mint/yellow/violet and vivid red/cyan both occur. Inked originals supply no final printed palette. Local blush or pale snow tints exist in supplied Hergé resources; avoid an unsupported absolute no-tonal-variation claim.

**Type**: Clear hand lettering in bounded balloons and captions, with expressive sound words and distinct cover lettering. No one display font defines the movement. Readable live text and controlled balloon tails are proposed interface translations.

**Texture**: Clean economical contour is central. Dense material lines can describe pages, stonework or clothes, but dramatic cross-hatched modeling is usually restrained. Paper grain, age and reproduced print texture are not essential traits.

**Shape**: Simplified expressive faces coexist with precisely described furniture, vehicles and buildings. Similar contour attention across figure and setting makes the environment readable. Speech rectangles, rounded balloons and action marks support narrative.

**Motion**: Drawn speed curls, sweat ticks and repeated poses indicate action in still images. They do not establish animation timing. A static UI can be fully faithful; any transition duration is a proposed product choice.

**Spatial**: Panel sequences use deliberate reading order, consistent gutters and controlled shifts of shot scale. Covers can use isometric rooms, large quiet fields or isolated jokes. Flat color does not require flat perspective or empty backgrounds.

**Cultural markers**: Hergé is a key reference; Joost Swarte named the term in 1977. Museums document earlier influences and later international reinterpretations. Historical stereotypes, author signatures and copyrighted characters are not transferable style primitives.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: Deliberate clean contour, readable narrative hierarchy, economical modeled detail and coherent local-color areas when colored. Exact uniform stroke, no black fills and no tonal accents are useful tendencies with corpus exceptions, not absolute laws.

## Connotation

**Mode:** Bright, observant and quietly witty.

Everyday scenes feel understandable and slightly surprising. Use original gentle visual humor without borrowing a famous hero.

## Related / Subsets

De Stijl informs some Swarte geometry but does not require narrative contours or comic settings. Pop Art may quote comics through dots and enlargement, while Ligne Claire owns clarity of depicted objects and sequence rather than quotation. Compare generic comic panels cautiously: borders alone do not establish the style.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Build a real article or guided reading flow with save state and next-step navigation. Give panels, buttons, captions and original setting illustration a coherent ink contour. Preserve keyboard order and readable live balloon text; do not force users to interpret drawings to operate controls.

## CSS Translation

Proposed 1.5–2.5 px dark consistent rules, flat cream sky-blue leaf-green and coral regions, clean white gutters and deliberately drawn perspective shapes. Avoid soft card shadows and glossy gradients. Reflow panels into one column; small optional state transitions can be instant under reduced motion.

## Typography / Fonts

Readable humanist sans or carefully chosen comic lettering for brief labels, with normal-case prose for extended reading. Typography choice is an application proposal; do not copy Tintin or magazine mastheads.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Original scenes and fictional people only. Do not trace copyrighted characters, dialogue, signatures or compositions. Acknowledge historical stereotypes without reproducing them as playful decoration. Movement boundaries are debated; avoid claiming every outlined image belongs.

## Anti-Patterns

- A generic card grid with a Tintin image pasted inside.
- Treating every thick-outlined cartoon as Ligne Claire.
- Unrestrained cross-hatching, gradient modeling or blurry backgrounds.
- Tiny all-caps prose that harms readability.
- Declaring a complete album inspected from one excerpt.
