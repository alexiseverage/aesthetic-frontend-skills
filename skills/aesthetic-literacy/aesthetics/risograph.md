# Risograph

Risograph is a print-process aesthetic derived from digital stencil duplicator production, contemporary zine culture, and small-run art publishing. Its useful UI translation makes ink separation, printed support and tonal/overprint behavior visible. Grain, halftone and registration drift are possible cues, not a requirement to make every print look damaged.

## Scope

Use Risograph for arts organizations, zine shops, indie publishers, event programs, editorial features, portfolio sites, and community or cultural calendars. It fits experiences where tactile print culture, limited editions, handmade production, or editorial poster systems support the content.

Avoid it for mission-critical dashboards, dense financial tables, luxury minimal commerce, or contexts where print texture will be read as low fidelity rather than intentional craft.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Deliberate ink separations on pale or colored stock. Two to four colors are useful application constraints, not a process limit: single black, metallic inks, six-ink compositions and photographic reproductions also occur. Layered tints can create extra tones; screen multiply is a simulation rather than a calibrated print proof.

**Type**: Editorial print mix: bold poster serif or chunky grotesk for headlines, sober sans or serif for reading text, and occasional hand-set caption labels. Headlines can be oversized, tightly composed, and slightly offset; body text remains clean enough to survive grain and color drift.

**Texture**: Ink/paper relationships, dot or line screens, grain, density variation, overprint and possible show-through. Precise edges and controlled registration are valid. Ink formulations include soy- and rice-bran-based variants; pixels cannot establish chemistry. Preserve readable utility copy rather than adding universal grit.

**Shape**: Paper and poster geometry. Rectangular sheets, pinned notices, tape-like labels, folded corners, image windows, blocky silhouettes, and cut-paper captions dominate. Shapes should feel assembled from printed matter rather than generated by a component library.

**Motion**: Stationary print objects support intentional stillness. Manual separation/register comparison can explain process. Hover slips, reveals and sheet shifts are optional contemporary proposals; static references establish no exact duration or easing.

**Spatial**: Editorial spread and poster-grid logic. Use generous margins, asymmetric image blocks, page-like modules, margin notes, and stacked poster cards. Density can be high, but hierarchy should read like a designed zine spread or print-fair program rather than a SaaS dashboard.

**Cultural markers**: Fluorescent spot inks, halftone dot fields, visible overprint overlaps, registration drift, cream paper stock, zines and small-run art publishing, indie print-fair schedules, and duplicator drum/process references.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**:

- Deliberate ink-separation logic rather than arbitrary color effects.
- A convincing printed support and ink/tonal relationship, which may be subtle.
- Composition appropriate to a sheet, card, book or other printed artifact.
- Clear functional text. Fluorescence, conspicuous grain, multiple colors and misregistration are optional, not universal requirements.

## Connotation

**Mode:** nostalgic quotation / contemporary revival.

Risograph reads as affection for small-run print culture and as a living craft process. The right tone is intelligent, tactile, and handmade. It can be playful, but it should not become generic grunge, fake distress, or punk abrasion.

## Related / Subsets

- `punk-zine` overlaps through indie publishing, but punk is photocopy rebellion and ransom-note collage while Risograph is spot-ink craft.
- `desktop-publishing` overlaps through page-making, while Risograph concerns digital stencil duplication and its material output. It is not an exclusively analog opposite of computer publishing.
- `grunge-typography` overlaps through texture, but Risograph texture comes from ink and registration rather than digital distress.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Use Risograph where an interface can behave like a zine spread, print-fair program, poster stack, or small-run publication. Strong surfaces include editorial hero pages, event cards, program schedules, artist directories, issue previews, posters, merchandise cards, and margin-note annotations.

Keep functional text on calmer paper fields and reserve heavy halftone or misregistration for images, badges, hero type, dividers, and decorative print layers. The system should feel printed in passes, not skinned with random noise.

## CSS Translation

- Color roles: `--paper-cream`, `--ink-soy`, `--ink-pink`, `--ink-teal`, `--ink-yellow`, `--ink-ultramarine`, and `--ink-tomato`.
- Borders/dividers: printed keyline rules, crop marks, taped labels, sheet edges, poster frames, and halftone separators.
- Radius language: low 0–6px paper rectangles, folded corners, tape edges, and blocky cut-paper shapes.
- Effects: SVG noise, halftone masks, `mix-blend-mode: multiply`, low-opacity paper overlays, 1–3px offset duplicate layers, and visible overprint intersections.
- Layout: editorial spreads, asymmetric poster grids, stacked sheets, margin notes, pinned notices, and print-program modules.
- Motion: immediate manual layer/register controls or stillness; any animated quotation needs an explicit purpose and reduced-motion handling.

## Typography / Fonts

Pair bold poster display type with sober readable support text. Slight offsets, ink-pass duplicates, and tight headline composition can carry the print-process signal, but body copy should remain stable and legible on textured backgrounds.

Avoid distressed type that reads as generic grunge. Typography should look printed in passes, not damaged for effect.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Respect Risograph as a production process and contemporary small-run publishing culture. The aesthetic should communicate spot inks, stencil duplication, and community print practice rather than using “grain” as a generic vintage filter.

The current profile records 37 distinct inspected works, concentrated in two UK printers. Keep Japanese technology, contemporary international practitioners and individual production choices distinct; this sample does not establish global prevalence or universal environmental benefits.

## Anti-Patterns

- Unseparated digital color effects with no ink or printed-support logic. Gradual tints and photographic color are possible within the real process.
- Random grain overlays without overprint, halftone, or registration logic.
- Heavy grunge distress that overwhelms paper and ink behavior.
- Rounded SaaS cards and dashboard density as the main composition.
- Elastic toy motion or slick digital chrome that contradicts the static print source.
