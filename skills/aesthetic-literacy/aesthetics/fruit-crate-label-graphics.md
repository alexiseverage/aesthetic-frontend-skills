# Fruit Crate Label Graphics

## Scope

Fruit-crate label graphics are a commercial print vernacular: a large identifying brand, bounded illustration or emblem, product/grade information and a compact grower or packer line make a complete small advertisement. The scope includes fruit labels with scenic, heraldic, mascot and primarily typographic compositions; it does not claim one uniform lithographic process or palette. A fruit photograph in a generic card is insufficient.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Deep navy, red, yellow, pale blue and green recur, alongside cream/black and purple/gold branches. Use a few forceful background/lettering contrasts. Grade-related colors are documented for particular apple markets, not universal produce standards. Reproductions do not establish original ink colorimetry.

**Type**: A dominant short brand may be slab-serif, condensed sans, faceted block lettering, italic serif or script. Product text and compact distributor lines occupy distinct scales. Outlines and offset shadows give display letters presence; essential interface copy must remain comfortably readable.

**Texture**: Modeled fruit, landscape illustration, printed dots and crisp flat framing coexist. Some labels are nearly image-free. Aged paper and scanning artifacts are visible in references but fake damage is optional. Do not confuse all later offset/halftone labels with stone lithography.

**Shape**: Rectangular outer frames, nested rules, circle/oval emblems, title bands and occasional shaped cartouches. Fruit, wrappers, ribbons or lettering can cross the inner picture boundary. Keep an unmistakable enclosed label silhouette rather than a universal rounded card.

**Motion**: The inspected artifacts are still prints. Historical interface timing is unknown. Keep ornamental illustration and lettering still; selection, quantity and disclosure changes are immediate. Any optional short feedback transition must disappear under reduced motion.

**Spatial**: Large brand first, bounded emblem or scene second, product/grade third, packer metadata last. Wide strips and nearly square labels both occur. Information bands and inset reading areas organize density. On mobile, reflow useful labels and controls rather than miniaturizing an entire crate-end composition.

**Cultural markers**: Grower/packer identities, place of production, varieties, quality claims and distribution networks. The observed set emphasizes United States orchard commerce. Historical heraldry, national imagery, gender stereotypes and commercial mascots belong to their particular originals; they are not required ingredients.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: dominant identifying brand; bounded label composition with purposeful inner frames or bands; separate product and grower/packer information; strong print-like hierarchy. Fruit illustration, navy ground, wood grain and artificial aging are optional.

## Connotation

**Mode:** nostalgic quotation.

Conveys cheerful commercial confidence, regional identity and a memorable named product. Use for a fruit club, specialty food catalog or seasonal shipment chooser. Present fictional claims honestly; decorative quality seals do not establish certification.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Compose a product/subscription chooser as a complete label: a short oversized name, original produce emblem or landscape opening, clearly boxed variety choices, a quantity strip and a legible packing summary. Let selection change the product description, quantity, price and summary. Keep the display treatment in actual headings and controls; adding a vintage image to generic cards does not produce this grammar. Stack the order controls below the central panel on narrow screens.

Buttons use rectangular double rules and emphatic short text; selected options add a check and written state. Input fields have solid pale fill and a visible dark focus outline. Navigation uses compact ruled tabs with an explicit active marker. Hero/display lettering can use outline/offset shadow; prose stays plain. Dividers are paired rules; links remain underlined; badges are original small circles/cartouches. If a confirmation overlay is needed, use a bounded paper panel with semantic dialog behavior and an explicit close action. Inline alerts use text/icons as well as color. Icons are simple fruit, crate or shipping silhouettes, never copied commercial mascots.

## CSS Translation

Proposed current-web roles, not sampled historical inks: navy background `#172653`, cream surface `#FFF3CC`, dark text `#162447`, red primary `#AC2925`, muted text `#5A4938`, gold accent `#F2C64D`, blue secondary `#2B5F88`. Use cream text on navy/red and dark text on cream/gold; validate actual foreground/state pairs. Start with 0–4px radii, 2px rules plus a 5px inset keyline, and restrained offset display shadow. Keep print texture sparse and away from small type.

Spacing proposal: 6/12/18/24/36/48px; body 16px/1.5; metadata 12–14px; display clamp 40–76px/1.05 with short lines. Borders and internal bands supply hierarchy rather than uniform card shadows. Decorative artwork stays still; essential state changes need no transition. Remove nonessential transitions under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`.

## Typography / Fonts

Georgia or another locally available sturdy serif provides a useful display fallback; a system sans handles product labels and forms. A licensed condensed display face can strengthen the brand band, but no single historical font is supported. Keep faceting, outlines and shadow to short headings. Never reproduce the tiny administrative print at historical scale in a working form.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Use original brands, illustration and seals; reference availability does not grant reuse. Avoid copying stereotypes, protected mascots or real certification marks. Do not turn historical grade-color conventions into invented present-day product standards. Retain place/producer context in research while keeping practical ordering copy concise and truthful.

## Anti-Patterns

- Generic product cards with fruit imagery as the only signal.
- Every label forced into navy, an apple and distressed wood.
- Branding so decorative that price, quantity or selection becomes ambiguous.
- Small outlined/shadowed body copy, color-only state or fixed-width mobile labels.
- Animated fruit, perpetual shimmer, false certification or copied historical brands.
