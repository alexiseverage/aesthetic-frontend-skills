# Brazilian Cordel Woodcut

## Scope

The woodcut illustration branch associated with Brazilian literatura de cordel: folheto covers, narrative popular prints and documented adaptations by named printmakers. Strong relief-print figure/ground, incised interior cuts and a practical title/image hierarchy distinguish it from a generic vintage-paper treatment. Cordel is a broader living literary practice with varied cover media; neither all cordel nor all Brazilian woodcut belongs to this visual subset.

The [CNFCP collection](https://www.gov.br/iphan/pt-br/unidades-especiais/centro-nacional-de-folclore-e-cultura-popular/xilogravuras), [Museu Afro Brasil](https://museuafrobrasil.org.br/acervo/encontro-de-2-bebados/) and [Instituto Brincante](https://www.acervoantonionobrega.com.br/sobre_cordeis) situate makers, printing and literary use. Contemporary women printmakers and collective practice are documented in [CNFCP's2024 catalog](https://www.gov.br/iphan/pt-br/unidades-especiais/centro-nacional-de-folclore-e-cultura-popular/catalogo-on-line-mulheres-na-xilogravura-sap-209.pdf). Poetry, printmaking, publishing and recitation are related roles, not interchangeable authorship.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Common black ink/pale paper contrast; red ink and purple, blue or pink stock are observed variants. Colored contemporary work also exists. Preserve distinct ink/ground roles; old paper stains and scan casts are not mandatory hues. Choose accessible proposed colors rather than copying faded impressions.

**Type**: Centered serif or sans title, author and publisher lines often surround an illustration. Narrow decorative capitals and hand-cut lettering are variants; ordinary typesetting remains important. Body verse uses readable serif lines and stanza gaps. No universal cordel font; do not copy an artist's signature or carve every label.

**Texture**: Raised printing areas carry ink; removed cuts reveal the ground in paper impressions. Directional gouge-like hatches, contour cuts, solid silhouettes and local ink breaks provide texture within the image. A carved matrix is not the same orientation/colorway as its impression. Actual woodcut requires a physical wood matrix; digital artwork should be described as an interpretation.

**Shape**: Bold flattened figures, expressive profiles, plants, animals and narrative objects; angular and curved cuts coexist. A clear image block or sparse isolated scene can work. Thin rules, repeated small ornaments and rectangular book proportions are common but not compulsory.

**Motion**: Print evidence is static. Intentional stillness and immediate user-triggered page changes are appropriate interface proposals. Performance associations do not establish animation timing; optional nonessential motion must respect reduced motion.

**Spatial**: Folheto covers separate title, image, attribution and imprint vertically. Interior verse may use narrow left-aligned stanzas with ample gaps. Standalone prints range from dense stacked crowds to small scenes in broad blank margins. Let illustration carry texture while text remains quiet.

**Cultural markers**: Named Brazilian makers and publishers, Northeastern networks and their movement across Brazil, Portuguese literary context and contemporary invention. Religious, comic, political, urban, health, romantic and everyday topics coexist. Cangaço, cacti, poverty, antiquity and masculine authorship are not universal requirements.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**:

- Grounded cordel/printmaker context and clear distinction between literary tradition, printing medium and digital adaptation.
- Deliberate dark/ground separation with expressive negative-cut structure, beyond a distressed-paper palette.
- Practical title/image or stanza hierarchy appropriate to a readable book, article or interface.

## Connotation

**Mode:** contemporary revival.

Convey storytelling, graphic confidence, material care and a welcoming popular reading culture. Original digital application art can quote the grammar without claiming to be a physical print, an inherited cultural artifact or a named maker's work.

## Related / Subsets

- `punk-zine`: shares inexpensive print and author-led publishing, with different collage, type and cultural histories.
- `risograph`: shares ink/paper relationships; stencil duplication, registration effects and color separations are a different process.
- `arts-and-crafts`: shares material attention, with a distinct historical movement and often different ornamental organization.
- Folheto covers, standalone narrative prints and contemporary illustrated publications are branches. Linocut, rubber relief, other Brazilian woodcuts and non-woodcut cordel covers need their own medium/context rather than automatic inclusion.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

A poetry reader, article card, book product or event listing can use one original relief-inspired illustration and a plain readable text zone. Use semantic headings, normal native buttons and real pagination/bookmark/download behavior. State updates should have clear keyboard focus and accessible labels; preserve Portuguese language metadata and diacritics.

## CSS Translation

- Proposed roles: paper `#F4EBD6`, ink `#201E18`, outer ochre `#E3AD48`, accent `#943A28`, muted `#625849`. These are application values, not measured historic ink samples.
- Rectangular panels,0–2px radii,1–3px rules and modest offset shadow; illustration supplies cut structure rather than noisy CSS overlays.
- Separate a cover/illustration zone from a narrow reading measure. Stack around760px; preserve the complete illustration and keep controls at least44px high.
- Use readable serif body text, clear stanza gaps and plain sans labels. Focus outline, underline, pressed state and text should supplement color.
- Keep artwork and page changes still; no forced paper-flip, ticker or typewriter effect.

## Typography / Fonts

Georgia/Times-style serif categories and plain Arial/system sans can translate the inspected typeset hierarchy without added font cost. Heavy or condensed display capitals can be used selectively. These are proposed local substitutes, not identified historical fonts. Preserve accents and selectable text; never rasterize functional copy to imitate a cover.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Credit poets, printmakers, photographers and collections separately. Public viewing is not a license to republish an illustration. Create original application art and verse, preserve documented women's authorship and avoid stereotypes or claims of universally encoded symbols. Do not call a digital filter a physical xilogravura or claim traditional poetic meter without checking it.

## Anti-Patterns

- Every cordel cover labeled woodcut, or every Brazilian relief print labeled cordel.
- Anonymous “primitive” folk imagery, compulsory cangaço props, rural poverty or invented sacred symbolism.
- Brown grunge and random scratches replacing actual cut/ink structure.
- Copied signatures, source compositions or copyrighted verses.
- Display lettering on every line, low-contrast faded text or pattern behind verse.
- Fake downloads/subscriptions, inaccessible page controls or decorative animation inferred from still prints.
