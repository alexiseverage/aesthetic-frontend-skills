# Model Comp Cards

A compact casting introduction that joins a recognizable portrait, selected photographic range, a clear professional name, practical statistics and representation/contact information. Its visual hierarchy makes a person and their work easy to remember and compare.

## Scope

This is a commercial presentation format, not a single fashion movement, universal agency template or synonym for a full portfolio. Printed two-sided cards, complete digital sheets and model-specific show-pack spreads share the portrait/range/data relationship. Agency practices differ: the inspected sample includes two-photo compositions, four-image backs, dense editorial montages, plain studio selections and commercial headshot/lifestyle cards.

A photographer's proof sheet organizes exposures; a comp card introduces a represented professional. A magazine cover may appear inside a card as portfolio evidence, but does not make every editorial page a comp card. Ordinary agency roster thumbnails, headshots without a composed information structure and show-pack cover graphics remain outside the core sample.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: White, black and pale neutral framing commonly let photographs supply color. Entirely monochrome cards, black-backed supporting montages and pale identity bands are substantial variants. Bright studio backgrounds or clothing can dominate individual pictures. A restrained frame is common; desaturating every photograph or imposing a luxury beige wash is unnecessary.

**Type**: The professional name anchors the card, with smaller labeled statistics and representation/contact details. Plain sans-serif names, larger height numbers, widely spaced agency lettering and marker-like names all occur. Some magazine mastheads are embedded in portfolio images rather than used as card headings. A few PDFs expose actual font metadata, but no one typeface defines the format.

**Texture**: Photographic light, grain, skin, fabric and location detail carry most material interest. Quiet flat backing and clean gutters keep those differences legible. Some show-pack identity bands imitate handwriting or translucent overlays; these are variants. Artificial paper distress, cut-film sprockets or UI noise should not compete with the work.

**Shape**: A large portrait or main image commonly opposes several smaller rectangles. Two-picture spreads, asymmetric grids, wide supporting images and narrow information rails offer alternatives. Compact name/stat blocks and agency marks are compositional anchors. Preserve meaningful picture crops; the face, pose or garment should not be accidentally truncated to fill identical rounded cards.

**Motion**: The evidence is still composed cards and PDF pages. Editorial poses imply no card animation. A modern card viewer may switch model, side or selected photograph immediately; intentional stillness supports comparison. An automatic slideshow, spinning card or moving statistics is not inherent to the format.

**Spatial**: Main portrait, supporting range and identity/data form distinct but related tiers. Information can sit in a side rail, lower white band, black montage panel or carefully chosen image space. Large/small photo contrast matters more than a fixed image count. Supporting pictures can repeat a main photograph within one card; they do not become independent cards.

**Cultural markers**: Agency representation, casting, a current professional name, selected work, styling range and practical garment-fit data locate the format. Commercial/lifestyle and fashion-editorial cards differ. Measurements are reported casting information, not a definition of attractiveness, health or human worth. Agency-specific sizing and field conventions should remain explicit.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: recognizable professional identity; a deliberate relationship between the main portrait and selected photographic range; concise practical information; representation/contact or an honest contemporary equivalent; a compact composed introduction rather than an undifferentiated photo feed.

## Connotation

**Mode:** contemporary professional presentation.

Aim for specificity, confidence and useful comparison. A fictional talent desk, photographer selection tool or casting-note workspace can embody the card without pretending to represent real models. Keep the visual emphasis on the work and the person's chosen professional identity, not a game-like attractiveness score.

## Related / Subsets

- `photographic-contact-sheet` arranges exposures and selection marks; a comp card is an edited introduction with identity, statistics and representation.
- `1990s-minimalism` overlaps restrained fashion typography and open framing, but does not require this casting format. Marker-lettered and highly colored cards remain valid counterexamples.
- `pochoir-fashion-plates` presents illustrated garment/fashion work; these cards normally use photography to introduce a model and their range.
- Commercial/lifestyle, fashion-editorial, two-photo show packs, monochrome cards, black-backed montages and practical side-rail layouts are observed variants.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Make the card itself the working interface. Let the main portrait, supporting views, printed-style name, aligned data and representation strip organize a model viewer. Model navigation, side/view choices and shortlisting can live in that print hierarchy. An attached casting-note form can summarize a selection and export a useful brief without claiming a booking was sent.

Use original commissioned/generated photographs of clearly fictional adults, or properly licensed material. Keep name, statistics, image descriptions and selection state in HTML. Give every photograph a meaningful alt description; do not infer personal measurements, ethnicity or other attributes from a picture. Authored fictional statistics must be labeled as such.

On mobile, stack the main image and information, then retain useful supporting crops and readable labels. Keep manual navigation, visible focus, clear selected marks and a persistent way back to the current card. Front/back terminology is optional for digital sheets; avoid hiding essential information behind a hover flip.

## CSS Translation

Proposed application values, not measured industry constants: paper `#fbfaf6`, ink `#171918`, secondary text `#4d514d`, rule `#b9bcb6`, black backing `#151716`. Use square image edges, 6–12px photo gutters, generous outer margins and restrained 1px information rules. Pair a dominant portrait with a smaller arranged range and an aligned compact data tier.

Use approximately 30–52px names and 12–15px practical labels when the component size permits; source microtype is not an accessibility requirement. Reserve accent color for a selection mark or authored image content. Keep sufficient text contrast when a name overlays a photograph; a stable information band is safer than copying a poor historical overlay. Reduced motion retains all views, shortlist actions and export without compulsory movement.

## Typography / Fonts

System sans-serif lettering is a practical option for names and compact information; a restrained serif or original handwritten accent can quote an observed variant. Stage PDFs identify Microsoft Tai Le and Euphemia UCAS Bold; Bella PDFs identify Arial. Those are documented local choices, not universal prescriptions. The NEVS raster pack's marker-like typeface is unverified. Agency wordmarks and magazine mastheads are not reusable font assets.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Use the person's published professional identity and preserve image/agency provenance. Do not republish source photographs or contact information as fictional business content. Do not turn one agency's body measurements or casting niche into a universal ideal, or treat a card as evidence of health or identity traits. Current data and image accuracy matter operationally; a polished layout cannot guarantee representation, availability or employment. The researched agencies provide useful examples, not endorsement of every field or casting practice.

## Anti-Patterns

- A generic profile card or image carousel whose controls ignore the portrait/range/data composition.
- Declaring one fixed size, photo count, font or monochrome treatment mandatory across all agencies.
- Cropping faces or poses for uniformity, shrinking statistics to unreadable microtype, or hiding details behind hover.
- Borrowed agency logos, magazine mastheads, model photographs or personal contact details presented as original content.
- Attractiveness ratings, inferred personal measurements, fabricated credits or fake booking confirmations.
