# Clowncore

Clowncore is a contemporary internet-native aesthetic centered on clown/jester/pierrot self-styling, makeup, costume materiality, toy-party props, and performative absurdity. Its center of gravity is the embodied clown persona, not circus venue nostalgia or vintage show-poster language.

## Scope

Use Clowncore when the product or artifact can support explicit character-play, theatrical self-presentation, and tactile festive materials. It can be joyful, queer/trans-friendly, absurd, nostalgic, or intentionally uncanny, but the clown coding must remain visible.

Do not use this entry as a generic rainbow maximalist preset. If the design is mostly dense poster type and archival fair ephemera, use `fairground-carnival-poster-art`; if it is childhood playroom nostalgia without makeup/persona cues, use `kidcore`; if it is monochrome Pierrot/Harlequin theatre language, use `pierrotcore`.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Primary-color and candy-rainbow intensity anchored by red, yellow, blue, bubblegum pink, confetti brights, and carnival accents. Pastel, neon, dark, or black/white variants are allowed only when clown costume/makeup grammar remains unmistakable.

**Type**: Playful display lettering, party-sign and novelty headline energy, inflated or off-balance display faces for identity moments, paired with calmer condensed grotesk, mono, or readable sans support type. Avoid letting woodtype barker typography dominate, because that drifts into fairground poster art.

**Texture**: Face paint, greasepaint, pom-poms, ruffles, sequins, kandi beads, stickers, confetti, balloons, glossy toy plastic, felt, paper party supplies, striped tights, and costume trim. Dark branches can introduce photocopy grime or punk abrasion, but only as named subset behavior.

**Shape**: Polka dots, harlequin diamonds, rounded pom-poms, oversized collars, bows, big buttons, balloons, clown silhouettes, ruff scallops, painted stars, tear stamps, toy-theatre frames, and asymmetrical prop compartments.

**Motion**: Bounce, pop, confetti burst, rubbery entrance, quick-change reveal, mirror-bulb chase, compact flip, juggling-path arcs, bead swing, ruffle flutter, and prop-trunk opening. Provide reduced-motion states that freeze the trunk/open-stage pose and replace chase loops with static alternating states.

**Spatial**: Playful clutter around a focal performer, makeup trunk, dressing room, party table, funhouse corner, toy theatre, costume inventory, badge board, or prop case. The layout should feel object-first and performative rather than a vertical advertising broadside.

**Cultural markers**: Clown makeup, red noses, painted stars/tears, rainbow hair, overalls, suspenders, ruffs, Peter Pan collars, circus tights, kandi, balloons, confetti, novelty props, clown dolls, clown OCs, clownblr, queer/trans self-fashioning, and reclaiming “clown” language.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**:

- Explicit clown, jester, or Pierrot costume/makeup grammar.
- Festive toy/circus materiality such as ruffles, pom-poms, greasepaint, confetti, beads, balloons, stickers, sequins, or novelty trims.
- A self-performance, character-play, or theatrical persona tone.
- Bright celebratory chaos, unless the implementation is explicitly declared as a dark, punk, or Pierrot-adjacent clown subset.

If these are removed, the result collapses into generic rainbow maximalism, `kidcore`, `decora-kei`, or `fairground-carnival-poster-art` rather than Clowncore.

## Connotation

**Connotation**: Primary mode is contemporary revival plus authentic self-expression. The strongest current evidence frames clowncore as internet-enabled identity performance, queer/trans-friendly experimentation, escapism, and joyful anti-seriousness. Nostalgic quotation appears when it leans into birthday-party supplies, toys, clown dolls, or retro circus kitsch. Ironic pastiche can work in editorial fashion, but should not erase the sincere self-styling/community branch. Horror-clown or uncanny readings are subset behavior, not the default.

## Related / Subsets

Related aesthetics from the dictionary and candidate set:

- `fairground-carnival-poster-art`: adjacent but poster/broadside-led; do not duplicate its dense type, archival print wear, or showman hierarchy as Clowncore’s main signal.
- `kidcore`: shares toys, stickers, party materials, and rainbow optimism; separated by clown persona and makeup grammar.
- `pierrotcore`: sibling clown lineage for monochrome melancholy, masks, diamonds, and stage-role folios.
- `decora-kei`: shares bright accessory abundance, but decora-kei is Harajuku accessory stacking rather than clown performance.
- `barbiecore`, `dreamcore-weirdcore`, and `goblincore`: useful contrast points for glam doll polish, liminal unease, and earthy creaturely anti-polish.

Subset or routing terms to mention without promoting to top-level slugs: `clownpunk`, `dark clowncore`, `pastel clowncore`, `neon clowncore`, and broad `circuscore` imagery. `circuscore` can contribute canopy, ringmaster, or big-top motifs only when clown persona remains central.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Lead with performer identity and prop logic. Strong object models include a quick-change makeup trunk, stage-side vanity, toy-theatre setup, costume inventory, kandi-trade board, or stickered funhouse station. Use the loudest visual language on headers, badges, hero modules, showcase cards, and non-critical decoration; keep forms, navigation, prices, and error states on calmer readable surfaces.

The UI should feel tactile and costume-like: ruffle borders, makeup wells, painted stamps, bead strands, sticker tabs, confetti, squeaker/horn glyphs, and visible prop compartments. It should not feel like a generic carnival advertisement.

## CSS Translation

- Color roles: define candy primary tokens (`clown-red`, `greasepaint-white`, `confetti-yellow`, `balloon-blue`, `bubblegum-pink`) plus a quieter support surface for body text.
- Surface effects: combine radial greasepaint wells, sticker shadows, ruff/scallop borders, SVG confetti, subtle gloss, and bead highlights; place dense texture behind solid reading panels.
- Shape language: use circles, scallops, diamonds, oversized buttons, and asymmetric trunk compartments rather than neutral cards alone.
- Layout: organize a focal performer/prop zone with surrounding trays, cue sheets, swatches, badges, and sticker inventory.
- Motion: use bounce/pop/reveal/chase loops only as enhancement, with `prefers-reduced-motion` fallbacks that hold the theatrical pose.

## Typography / Fonts

Use one loud playful display face for identity moments: inflated, theatrical, slightly wobbly, rounded, or novelty-sign inspired. Pair it with a restrained condensed grotesk, mono, or readable sans for cue sheets and controls. Avoid all-over circus woodtype, because it changes the read from clown persona to fairground poster.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Clowncore has documented queer/trans-friendly and self-expressive community connotations. Treat those as context, not costume. Avoid mocking clown fandoms, presenting “clown” as an insult, or defaulting to horror/shock imagery. If using sideshow, animal-circus, or freakshow references, acknowledge that those histories can be ethically fraught and keep the implementation fictionalized and non-exploitative.

## Anti-Patterns

- Rainbow palette without clown makeup, costume, or prop grammar.
- Vintage circus poster typography and print wear as the main signal; that belongs to `fairground-carnival-poster-art`.
- Horror-clown blood, gore, or menace as the default tone.
- Collapsing into `decora-kei` by stacking cute accessories without clown persona cues.
- Letting every control become decorative so forms, navigation, or body text become unreadable.
