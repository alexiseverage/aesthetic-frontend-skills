# Klecksography / Inkblot Graphics

Graphic work centered on fluid pooling and a fold/transfer relationship, including Kerner’s literary klecksographs and clearly identified later painted variants.

Evidence basis: [the research profile](../../../knowledge/aesthetics/klecksography-inkblot.md) records 30 qualifying unique artifacts, source distribution, claim support and limitations. This is a descriptive catalog category. Exact historical colors, fonts and screen timings are not inferred from appearance; proposed frontend values below are contemporary translations.

## Scope

Graphic work centered on fluid pooling and a fold/transfer relationship, including Kerner’s literary klecksographs and clearly identified later painted variants. It is an artistic material grammar, not a psychological assessment or a synonym for every symmetrical ornament.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Dark pooled masses and gray transitions on pale ground are common. Reversed fields and muted olive/gold paint broaden the range; cream scanned paper is not a mandatory sepia token.

**Type**: Book variants pair images with title writing or blackletter verse; later paintings may have no intrinsic text. A readable editorial face is a modern choice, not a universal process feature.

**Texture**: Irregular pooling, mottled density, pale voids, rough/soft edges, transfer ridges and occasional added pen lines. Chance and intervention coexist; untouched randomness is not required.

**Shape**: Near-bilateral masses with local mismatches, internal openings and branching lobes. Vertical, horizontal or oblique axes and asymmetrical combined scenes are documented.

**Motion**: The source works are still images; folding is source-described rather than timed. Manual axis/color changes and export are proposed digital behaviors. No automatic ink spreading is required; reduced motion should show results immediately.

**Spatial**: Isolated figures with generous page field, separate verse or whole-canvas paired masses. A blot may frame text or sit below it; no fixed centered card is canonical.

**Cultural markers**: Literary image-to-verse play, chance, imaginative interpretation and later artistic engagement with inkblot forms. Kerner’s ghost/skeleton motifs are variants, not universal meanings or diagnostic cues.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**:

- Fluid/material irregularity rather than smooth vector symmetry alone.
- A legible fold/transfer relationship with ambiguous internal openings.
- Artistic context that leaves interpretation unscored.

## Connotation

**Mode:** Contemporary print-workshop interpretation: tactile chance and personal authorship, without a clinical-test fiction.

The implementation should quote the documented material and organizational relationships. Its contemporary controls and readable type must remain honest about source and application limits.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Do not reproduce standardized clinical cards, diagnose viewers or imply a validated assessment. Later paint and earlier ink/print works have different media; no undocumented direct influence is asserted. Generated digital studies are not authentic physical transfer prints.

## Anti-Patterns

- Clinical scoring, personality claims or copied test cards.
- Perfect smooth mirrored icons with no pooling or voids.
- Ghosts and old German lettering as the entire identity.
