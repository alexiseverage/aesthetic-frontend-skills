# Sticker-Bomb / Slap Materiality

## Scope

Sticker-Bomb / Slap Materiality is the accumulated street-surface system of stickers, slaps, labels, tags, and die-cut graphics layered until the support becomes a record of many hands. Use it for dense identity surfaces, street-art-adjacent campaign modules, fan collectives, or customization metaphors. It is not a clean sticker sheet, a corporate swag wall, or graffiti culture reduced to decoration.

Evidence is limited and textual; entries should preserve the cultural-review caveat. The reusable system is material accumulation and composition, not any specific writer's tag, protected character, or real public surface.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Many competing high-contrast colors from different sources: saturated vinyl hues, black-and-white tags, warning reds, fluorescent greens, glossy blues, and postal-label whites. A unified brand palette weakens the found-layer signal.

**Type**: Handstyle tags, cryptic names, crew shouts, marker lettering, printed wordmarks, character captions, and mismatched display type. Lettering should feel multi-author rather than typeset from one system.

**Texture**: Vinyl gloss, matte paper labels, die-cut edges, overlaid adhesive, scratches, peeling corners, weathering, sticker residue, and torn reveals. Flat digital collage needs edge shadows and material seams.

**Shape**: Irregular die cuts, rectangles, circles, postal labels, eggshell fragments, badges, and overlaid silhouettes. Shapes should collide and occlude each other rather than align to a tidy grid.

**Motion**: The composition is static; the implied action is speed, portability, and repeated placement. UI motion can use quick slap-on reveals, but the core aesthetic is accumulated stillness.

**Spatial**: Dense overlap, minimal negative space, layered hierarchy from age and placement, cropped edges, and repeated name/mark visibility. Sparse, evenly spaced stickers become a sticker collection, not sticker-bomb materiality.

**Cultural markers**: Graffiti writing culture, slaps, "getting up," sticker trading, postal-label use, street-art visibility, activism, promotion, and local accumulation over time.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: dense overlapping layers; many visual authors or mark systems; visible sticker edges/materiality; street or exchange-culture context; weathering or accumulation. A single neat branded sticker does not qualify.

## Connotation

This can read as authentic street accumulation, nostalgic quotation, or contemporary campaign pastiche depending on use. It signals visibility, repetition, and informal networks. Treat graffiti/writing culture with care rather than using it as generic rebellion styling.

## Related / Subsets

`punk-zine` shares collage and DIY politics but is paper/photocopy assembly. `grunge-typography` shares distressed marks but is editorial typographic deconstruction. `maximalism` can share density but lacks slap terminology, adhesive materiality, and street placement.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Use sticker layers for headers, profile walls, collectible badges, event posters, and customization surfaces. Keep task content on clear cards above or beside the sticker field. Avoid encouraging illegal placement or reproducing real tags without permission.

## CSS Translation

Use rotated absolute layers, masks, border-radius variation, drop shadows, torn-corner pseudo-elements, blend modes, subtle scratches, and clipped overlaps. Provide an ordered DOM and readable text layer so visual pileups do not break accessibility.

## Typography / Fonts

Combine marker-like display faces, condensed wordmarks, rough sans, and hand-lettered accents only for short labels. Use neutral sans for body text, alt text, form labels, and navigation.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Sticker and graffiti cultures include legal, local, racialized, youth, and class dynamics. Do not appropriate real crews' names, copy living writers' tags, or present illegal action as product instruction. Prefer fictional marks and permissioned assets.

## Anti-Patterns

- Making a tidy evenly spaced sticker sheet.
- Using one brand palette and one font for all stickers.
- Copying real tags, protest stickers, or street photos without permission.
- Letting decorative overlap obscure required UI content.
