# Cassette Futurism

Cassette Futurism is application-ready because its signal is distributed across the seven-dimension framework rather than resting on palette alone. The most important implementation guardrail is to preserve the combined palette, material, shape, motion, spatial, and cultural-marker system; if only one cue is present, the result will read as generic speculative technology.

## Scope

Use when the product, interface, or visual artifact benefits from this exact machine/body/worldbuilding language. Avoid when a neighboring aesthetic would communicate the intent with fewer cultural or accessibility risks.

Preserve the whole system across interface scale: the aesthetic should survive in a hero, a card, an icon set, and a form state without relying on one decorative cue. If the product only needs a lighter reference, borrow one or two tokens while naming the result as an influence rather than the full aesthetic.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Molded off-white, beige, olive and dark recesses with green or amber displays. Red/white wayfinding and ochre upholstery are grounded variants, not exceptions requiring a green tint.

**Type**: Compact monospaced data, uppercase casing legends, stencils and large zone/media identifiers. Printed labels and screen text operate at different scales.

**Texture**: Molded plastic, inset keys, vents, brushed metal, mounted cables and restrained wear. CRT bloom belongs around the display; keep data and labels clear.

**Shape**: Chunky beveled housings, deep bezels, cartridges, physical keys, fasteners and rounded-square pictogram frames. Information sits inside handled equipment.

**Motion**: Coarse progress, relay state changes or media transport are proposed interface behaviors. Stills do not establish authentic scan speed, tape latency or easing. Provide functional pause/reset and remove decorative movement for reduced motion.

**Spatial**: Bounded control banks and display modules mount into consoles or wall bays. Preserve a readable working area and stack whole modules responsively.

**Cultural markers**: Late twentieth-century terminal/aerospace vocabulary projected into a used future: physical media, repairable equipment and industrial service labeling. Real hardware is an antecedent rather than automatically speculative fiction.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: physical equipment and removable-media logic + bounded screen/control modules + differentiated casing legends and data + an extrapolated used-future context.

## Connotation

Cassette Futurism carries a specific robotics/speculative-technology connotation; use the source-backed cues above as a system rather than as isolated moodboard decoration.

Because the entry is intended for practical frontend use, connotation should be calibrated to the audience and product domain rather than applied as costume. Favor legible homage, contemporary revival, or clearly bounded genre quotation over accidental parody.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Start with cockpit/control-panel density with bounded physical controls and chunky boxes, bezels, toggles, rectangular modules, fasteners, then layer nicotine beige, ABS off-white, olive, CRT green, warning amber., monospace, engraved labels, stencils, all-caps panel legends, and molded plastic, ribbed buttons, brushed metal, CRT bloom. Use the aesthetic for heroes, feature cards, empty states, branded dashboards, campaign pages, illustration frames, and high-value CTAs where its cultural markers are relevant. Keep primary navigation, forms, prices, error states, and body copy on stable accessible surfaces.

## CSS Translation

- Color roles: translate nicotine beige, ABS off-white, olive, CRT green, warning amber into named background, surface, accent, and text tokens; reserve common variants (Adjacent aesthetics can borrow this dimension only if the core non-negotiables remain intact.) for depth or emphasis.
- Surface effects: use molded plastic, ribbed buttons, brushed metal, CRT bloom with CSS gradients, borders, masks, shadows, noise, or SVG filters; keep texture behind solid readable panels.
- Shape language: build cards, badges, dividers, icons, and frames from chunky boxes, bezels, toggles, rectangular modules, fasteners rather than neutral rectangles alone.
- Layout: organize pages around cockpit/control-panel density with bounded physical controls with enough whitespace and hierarchy for product tasks.
- Motion: use blink, scan sweep, coarse cursor steps, relay state changes as optional enhancement with `prefers-reduced-motion` fallbacks.

## Typography / Fonts

Use monospace, engraved labels, stencils, all-caps panel legends for identity moments, headings, numerals, labels, or badges. Pair it with a neutral readable sans or serif for body text and controls. Avoid making all microcopy decorative; the aesthetic reads more strongly when focal typography contrasts with quiet support typography.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

The source-backed cultural markers are Alien/Nostromo, repairability, used future, physical media. Use them as context signals, not as extractive decoration. When an implementation references living cultures, subcultures, fandoms, gambling, youth media, war machinery, or speculative harm, keep claims specific, fictionalized where appropriate, and avoid presenting stereotypes or protected symbols as generic ornament.

## Anti-Patterns

- Using only the palette while dropping the shape, texture, spatial, and cultural-marker system; that becomes generic moodboard styling rather than Cassette Futurism.
- Overloading every component with motifs so navigation, forms, pricing, or body copy become hard to scan.
- Borrowing adjacent-aesthetic cues so heavily that the entry loses its own source-backed center of gravity.
- Animating glow, grain, flash, or mechanical effects without reduced-motion and solid-surface fallbacks for critical content.
