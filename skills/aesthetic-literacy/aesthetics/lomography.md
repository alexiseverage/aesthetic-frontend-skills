# Lomography

## Scope

A photographic community/practice whose reusable visual branch combines spontaneous close or unusual viewpoints, analogue tonal/surface character, and sequences or accumulated prints. It has 1990s roots and a strong 2000s analogue/online identity. This is neither every film photograph nor every member upload. Saturation, vignetting, blur and circular lenses are useful variants rather than universal requirements.

When applied to an interface, the photographic relationships and print/sequence metaphor must organize hierarchy and tasks. One vivid picture on an ordinary card is insufficient. The present interface guidance is an authored translation, not a reconstruction of a verified historical UI.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Cyan/blue against orange/red/yellow and deep shadows is common in the inspected works. Muted milky greens, beige, pale flowers and quieter portraits prevent a compulsory neon preset. Keep reading/control planes solid and high contrast. Specific camera chemistry and calibrated colors remain unverified.

**Type**: No single historical photographic house font is established. Condensed short display, straightforward sans prose and small monospaced roll/caption labels are suitable proposed roles. Incidental photographed signs are subjects, not a mandatory type system. Avoid distressing long text.

**Texture**: Visible grain, soft focus, dark corners, exposure variation and occasional physical print borders. Apply texture to image/edge zones, while labels, inputs and focus indicators stay crisp. Do not confuse digital damage, every light leak or a film-border overlay with an actual analogue process.

**Shape**: Close, off-center and foreshortened crops; rectilinear prints/strips and accumulated walls. Four-exposure bands and circular fisheye distortion belong to particular camera branches. Do not make every image a circle or simulate an unverifiable laboratory frame.

**Motion**: Still photographs can suggest motion through blur or a captured sequence; they do not establish animation timing. Intentional stillness suits a journal. Use explicit next/select/reorder controls and immediate state changes; avoid compulsory slideshow, jitter and fake film-development loaders. Reduced motion preserves all editing and reading states.

**Spatial**: A single frame may be casual/asymmetrical while a sequence or wall creates larger structure through repeated edges, tonal grouping and short annotations. A current app can use a numbered roll, a selected editing plane and an ordered story preview. Mobile should reflow those zones rather than shrink them.

**Cultural markers**: Community experimentation, ordinary moments, embodied viewpoints, analogue practice and shared print accumulation. The brand's histories differ on some early dates; do not turn promotional narration into exact uncontested chronology. A commercial/community identity is wider than one filter.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: photographic viewpoint/tonal character beyond a generic preset; a clear relationship between frame, sequence and presentation; image/material treatment carried into useful hierarchy and controls; honest distinction between source practice and a contemporary application. Extreme saturation, instant borders, fisheye and deliberate damage are optional.

## Connotation

**Mode:** contemporary analogue-journal quotation.

Convey curiosity, proximity, experimentation and personally ordered moments. Avoid pretending a generated picture is a captured memory. Quiet, soft and reflective branches belong alongside energetic color. Brand nostalgia is not a universal audience experience.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Build a useful photo-journal or essay-sequencing surface: a numbered roll selects a frame, a material caption slip edits actual text, and a distinct ordered-story area supports add, move and remove. A reading preview reflects the current order and captions. Use a bold irregular display/condensed masthead, static print offsets and dark frame edges across navigation, editor, buttons and feedback. Preserve draft captions while selecting other frames; state storage scope clearly.

Expose selected, current and in-story states with text/symbols as well as color. Use semantic buttons and inputs, accessible names, logical focus restoration after removal and announced feedback. Caption editing needs a clear label and bounded length. Provide labelled move controls rather than drag-only ordering. Keep 44px touch targets and a natural narrow reading flow.

## CSS Translation

Authored roles: paper #FFF3CE, ink #1F241D, dark frame #18231E, orange #C94620, teal #146A69, pale highlight #D8E6A1. These are proposals, not sampled film values. Use square frames, small offset shadows, restrained paper grain, dark image edges and short numbered labels. Keep textured image regions separate from solid text planes.

Proposed rhythm: 4/8/12/20/32/48px; 16px/1.5 body, 12–13px metadata, 48–76px/0.9 short display. Use 44px controls, clear 3px focus outlines and no continuous motion. Optional static 1–2 degree print rotations must not clip focus or alter reading order. Check actual contrast pairs, selected/disabled/error states and small labels.

## Typography / Fonts

Impact/Arial Narrow/system sans can give a compact expressive masthead; system sans and monospace supply clean caption/roll labels. These are contemporary category choices, not identified historical originals. Avoid tiny emulated print labels, faux handwriting for long prose or additional fonts without a useful role.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Link historical photographs for analysis; access is not a reuse license. Create original assets and clearly identify generated output where relevant. Do not claim a film stock, camera exposure, actual memory or processing method without evidence. Avoid copying the brand mark or turning Soviet/cold-war references into costume. Spontaneity never overrides consent, privacy or location restrictions. Do not infer photographed people's identities or circumstances.

## Anti-Patterns

- A conventional component grid whose only signal is a filtered hero picture.
- Treating every film photo or community upload as the same visual system.
- Mandatory neon, scratches, fake film metadata or indiscriminate light leaks.
- Illegible distressed controls, accidental clipping or color-only selection.
- Automatic slideshow, camera jitter, fake development wait or drag-only ordering.
- Crediting generated images as real captured travel or historical evidence.
