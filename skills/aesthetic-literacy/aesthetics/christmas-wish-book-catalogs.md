# Christmas Wish Book Catalogs

## Scope

North American department-store Christmas catalogs tie seasonal cover storytelling to densely keyed product pages. This entry focuses on the mid-1960s through the late 1970s. Use its techniques for wish lists, seasonal catalog readers, object studies and small collections that need clear item lookup. [Research profile](../../../knowledge/aesthetics/christmas-wish-book-catalogs.md)

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Treat scarlet, dark green, deep warm gray and cream as familiar choices. Some winter scenes use cool blue or a warm photographic range. Keep accent contrasts clear on ivory product listings.

**Type**: Pair an oversized season or department heading with compact product specifications. Give item keys, size or option text, and prices distinct roles. Keep cents associated with dollars, even if they are set higher and smaller.

**Texture**: Start with warm paper and use visible print grain sparingly in imagery or borders. Highlights, colored framing fields and photographed surfaces carry much of the variation.

**Shape**: Use narrow photo rules, small boxed IDs, framed hero pictures, and pressed blocks of text. A cover can use a large rectangle or oval as its primary frame.

**Motion**: The source organization is print. Use immediate page changes and stable item anchors. Keep illustrations still while selection updates the corresponding key and entry.

**Spatial**: Place descriptive text beside or below a broad photo panel. Use a short repeated code to link each pictured object with its listing. A smaller order or lookup register can use multiple columns when it remains readable.

**Cultural markers**: Fall ordering deadlines, table-of-contents references, cover merchandise notes and gift departments are part of the historical format. Cite the retailer, market, season, and page.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**:

- A visible transition from cover or department display to product lookup.
- Distinct item identifiers that match between image and listing.
- Document hierarchy and readable specifications inside the print-inspired layout.

## Connotation

**Connotation**: Abundance arranged for browsing, pencil-marked wish lists and practical ordering information.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Build an original catalog spread with numbered object illustrations, prices clearly marked as illustrative, and a working wish list. Make item numbers available as text on both page and listing. Use the same selection state throughout and keep focus visible on any control. When the spread stacks, put the illustration panel before the keyed list.

## CSS Translation

Use ivory paper `#f6e7cc`, near-black pine `#182b23`, oxblood ink `#792b2c` and faded gold `#cf9a54`. Use thin rules, straight edges and a compact number badge. Keep the key controls at a comfortable target size. Limit paper texture to noncritical regions.

## Typography / Fonts

Use an expressive book serif for the title and a compact user-interface sans for product IDs, controls and notes. Use tabular figures for prices and preserve currency and decimal meaning in accessible text.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Original object illustrations and fictitious goods can convey how these catalogs work. Credit each historical issue by retailer and market. Retain third-party trademarks, period photography and protected artwork in linked references.

## Anti-Patterns

- Seasonal illustration without an identifiable merchandise index.
- Tiny body text that reproduces the scale of printed clauses on a phone.
- Item numbers that change when the layout becomes narrow.
- A fabricated store identity presented as a historical retailer.
