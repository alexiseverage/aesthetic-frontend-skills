# Postal Savings Stamp Cards

## Scope

Printed savings cards and booklets made recurring small deposits visible through rows of affixed stamps. This entry includes postal savings cards and related Treasury thrift and war savings albums offered through post offices and other distributors. [Research profile](../../../knowledge/aesthetics/postal-savings-stamp-cards.md)

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Use light stock with one strong ink, especially blue, green, black or orange. Colored paper and limited-color accents occur in some denomination series. A cancellation can visibly cross a set of already attached stamps.

**Type**: Lead with a short institutional or program title and a prominent denomination. A practice-oriented layer uses compact sans or serif directions, a clearly readable numerical progression, and small form codes. Engraved capitals and occasional script supply emphasis.

**Texture**: Fine line engraving, small dotted or wavy patterns, perforated stamp edges, lightly toothed stock and visible registration marks connect hand-applied stamps with the printed base.

**Shape**: Equal rectangular fields make the object repeatable. Stamp, cancellation, and ownership areas have separate borders. Folds separate instructions, the register, and sometimes a seal or certificate application.

**Motion**: The audited examples are static. Digital examples can let a reader add or remove stamps and update progress by an exact denomination. Keep placement instantaneous or include a short optional settle.

**Spatial**: Preserve the card's rectangular proportions and deliberate columns. Put the register in a wide open area. Keep issue and ownership fields on separate tiers, and show an honest relationship between denomination, number of positions, and total.

**Cultural markers**: Identify the issuer and the original savings scheme when using historical specimens. Official seals, slogans, rate schedules and affixed stamps tell different parts of an artifact's history. Use distinct invented marks for a demonstration.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**:

- A regularly ruled stamp grid with legible position or running-value marks.
- Clear denomination and progress information, plus a way to distinguish an empty position from a filled stamp.
- A printed form vocabulary of discrete instructions and traceable program or demonstration attribution.

## Connotation

**Connotation**: Incremental accumulation made visible and official through printed rules and small repeated acts.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Use an original, explicitly illustrative card with a small total and a stable number of numbered positions. Make every position operable by keyboard and give filled cells a distinct border or mark. Separate actions and total from source or attribution notes. Provide a reset for an interactive demonstration.

## CSS Translation

Use paper `#f4e9ce`, ink `#243e59`, stamp blue `#37536b`, and red overprint `#923b34`. Add thin ruled lines, optional narrow repeating line ornament and a contrasting treatment for an empty position. Keep letters and numbers of the stamp or status readable at small widths.

## Typography / Fonts

Pair a serif or engraved-style title and pronounced tabular denomination with a plain sans or mono for numbered cells. Use ample line height for small explanatory text.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Distinguish the original issuer's terms and imagery from the present-day interface. Preserve historical context for wartime and occupation-era cards. Use fictional amounts and marks for the simulator.

## Anti-Patterns

- Free placement that obscures how many stamps are present.
- A decorative seal or value label that implies an official financial service.
- Repeating borders or paper stains that reduce text and state contrast.
