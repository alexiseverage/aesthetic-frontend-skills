# Toile de Jouy

## Scope

The engraved-looking pictorial printed-cotton grammar associated with Jouy-en-Josas: repeating figural, architectural, pastoral or mythological scenes, often rendered with one colored ink on light cloth. The historical factory also made floral, geometric and polychrome textiles; this entry focuses on the widely recognized pictorial toile visual system rather than claiming all factory production looked alike.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Single red, blue, brown or green ink on pale cloth is common. Dense grounds and pale reverse figures occur; polychrome figural variants complicate an absolute one-ink rule. Several supplied AIC photographs are monochrome and cannot establish original textile color. Colors here are visually estimated.

**Type**: Usually absent from the repeat; maker stamps and collector labels are contextual rather than ornamental headlines. A readable bookish serif is a proposed UI companion, not a historically required toile font.

**Texture**: Fine parallel hatch, stipple and engraved contour turn one ink into modeled tone, laid over visible cotton weave. Do not confuse worn cloth or monochrome photography with the original finish.

**Shape**: Scene islands, branching trees, small buildings, figures, animals, medallions, urns and garlands. Scallops, oval frames and lozenges appear in ornamented variants; no one motif is mandatory.

**Motion**: The textiles and supplied photographs establish no animation timing or responsive behavior. Web transitions are proposed; a static repeat is sufficient and preferable to continuous scrolling ornament.

**Spatial**: Repeating scenes can float in open ground or occupy dense lattice/striped arrangements. Mixed scene scales and disconnected horizons are common. Carry that rhythm into repeated sections, framed controls and margin vignettes while keeping text on quiet ground.

**Cultural markers**: Jouy-en-Josas and Oberkampf matter, alongside Indian printed-cotton influences, other French manufactures and later adaptations. Some historic imagery projects European colonial or classical fantasies; the style is not proof of the depicted place or people.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: Pictorial repeat with modeled linework and a coherent printed-cloth relationship. A limited ink relationship is strongly characteristic; blue, pastoral subjects and wide blank ground are common variants rather than universal requirements.

## Connotation

**Mode:** Light, storybook-like and decorative.

Small everyday scenes invite browsing and repeat discovery; a calm journal can preserve this charm without romanticizing every historical subject.

## Related / Subsets

Chinoiserie concerns European imagined Chinese motifs and can overlap particular historical toiles, but toile also owns pastoral, industrial, classical and current urban subjects. Cottagecore shares rural connotations without requiring engraved repeat structure. Arts and Crafts textile repeats often foreground continuous plants; toile more often combines discrete narrative scenes.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Build a real article/reading-list interface with an expandable seasonal note and saved-state control. Repeat original line-drawn scene islands through section boundaries and ornamented controls. Keep prose on quiet ground, all actions labeled and focus visible.

## CSS Translation

Use original SVG fine-line garden vignettes, hatch shading and recurring ground islands in a restrained blue or red ink. Oval and scalloped borders may frame small controls; large prose stays simple. Mobile margins reduce ornament density, not text size. No perpetual pattern motion; reduced motion keeps instant states.

## Typography / Fonts

No defining historical interface font. Proposed restrained serif headings and readable serif/sans body; preserve live text and avoid fake maker stamps.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Do not copy colonial caricatures, imply all motifs are culturally neutral, or claim modern generic toile was manufactured at Jouy. Source designs are linked evidence; new garden scenes must be original. Historical context is concise and factual.

## Anti-Patterns

- Blue floral wallpaper presented as the whole definition.
- Counting different fragments of the same repeat as independent designs.
- Inferring original ink color from a monochrome photograph.
- Tiny patterned text with inadequate contrast.
- Calling all Oberkampf output monochrome pastoral toile.
