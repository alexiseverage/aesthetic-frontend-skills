# Scandi Noir

Scandi Noir is a Nordic crime idiom and visual aesthetic of cold climate, institutional realism, sparse emotional distance, and social-critique noir. It is not simply Scandinavian minimalism in a dark palette.

## Scope

Use Scandi Noir for crime fiction, investigative journalism, civic data stories, serious media launches, and procedural interfaces that benefit from restraint and tension. Do not use it where warmth, play, or celebratory community are the primary goals.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Charcoal, slate blue, cold gray, winter white, desaturated teal, sodium amber, black water, dirty concrete, and blood-dark red.

**Type**: Restrained sans-serif, public-institution clarity, subtitle/signage sobriety, case-file labels, and transport-signage dryness.

**Texture**: Wet asphalt, wool, fog, sleet, concrete, glass condensation, coarse knit, low-light grain, puddles, frost, and matte interiors.

**Shape**: Utilitarian geometry, harsh horizontals, bridges, apartment blocks, institutional interiors, corridors, docks, shorelines, and file rectangles.

**Motion**: Deliberate procedural pacing, slow pursuit, windshield glide, weather drift, surveillance gaze, and patient reveal.

**Spatial**: Weather-separated figures, emotional-distance interiors, sparse exteriors, transport/bridge/harbor liminality, and horizon-heavy emptiness.

**Cultural markers**: Welfare-state tension, flawed detectives, wintry/damp settings, border/bridge spaces, social critique, humanized criminality, and municipal realism.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: cold desaturated climate palette + low-key realism + institutional typography + social-critique noir layer. If these are removed, the result collapses into an adjacent or generic style rather than Scandi Noir.

## Connotation

Scandi Noir reads as a source-backed aesthetic system whose meaning depends on the cultural frame as much as the surface treatment.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Build with sparse layouts, institutional labels, case-file cards, cold photographic space, and slow procedural reveals. The interface should feel observant and restrained, with tension carried by climate, grid, and typography.

## CSS Translation

- Color roles: `--bg-charcoal`, `--surface-concrete`, `--accent-slate-blue`, `--accent-sodium`, `--accent-blood`, `--text-winter`.
- Borders/dividers: case-file rules, municipal signage lines, bridge horizontals, and institutional card seams.
- Radius language: low-radius utilitarian rectangles; avoid decorative warmth.
- Effects: wet asphalt shine, fog, glass condensation, low-light grain, frost, and matte interior shadows.
- Layout: sparse case grids, horizon-heavy heroes, transport/harbor zones, and emotionally distant content blocks.
- Motion: slow pursuit, patient reveal, weather drift, surveillance pan, and reduced-motion cuts.

## Typography / Fonts

Use restrained sans-serif, subtitle-like hierarchy, municipal signage, case-file labels, and dry transport typography. Avoid flamboyant display type; tension should come from spacing, climate, and restraint.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Avoid reducing Nordic cultures to coldness, violence, or bleakness. The aesthetic’s noir layer includes social critique and institutional context, not just blue-gray weather.

## Anti-Patterns

- Scandinavian modern minimalism simply darkened.
- Classic noir clichés without Nordic climate, institutions, or social critique.
- Techno-noir neon/futurism replacing municipal realism and weather.
- Overdramatic thriller typography that breaks the restrained procedural tone.
