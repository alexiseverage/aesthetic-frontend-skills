# Heavy Metal

Heavy-metal sleeve graphics make a band's identity feel like the entrance to a larger world. Memorable lettering and a deliberately staged image carry a record's story: painted fantasy, science fiction, horror, a charged physical object, or a stark photographic/typographic statement. This entry scopes classic/traditional heavy-metal record and campaign graphics, with current delivery/revival variants; it is not a uniform for every metal subgenre.

## Scope

A strong painted branch builds dramatic scale, modeled materials, atmospheric depth and a recurring artist world. A separate emblem/object branch can be sparse, and early photographic or type-only designs are important counterexamples. Preserve a specific band/story relationship, not merely skulls or dark colours.

Use it for artist release sites, illustrated album companions, record features, music archives and visual liner notes. Choose a branch and credit its actual creators. This corpus is concentrated in British/European and US-market classic metal networks; it does not describe all global scenes, all metal fashion or every later extreme-metal graphic system. Thrash/crossover needs its own evidence, rather than inheriting a slow epic-painting recipe automatically.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Deliberate light/dark staging, metallic highlights, saturated blue, red, gold, orange fire and violet frequently organize the painted branch. Pale icy blue, bright cyan industrial imagery, yellow graphic fields and sparse monochrome are real variants. Bright colour can be an explicit artist/band choice. No universal black/red palette or original pigment hex value is established.

**Type**: A highly repeatable band mark may be angular, pointed, extruded, outlined, blackletter-like, ornamental or broadly rounded. Album titles can use fine script, serif, plain sans, stencil or hand lettering. Keep the name/title hierarchy separate from readable track and archive information. Specific logos are authored identities, not reusable genre fonts.

**Texture**: Modeled painting, luminous clouds, glass/metal reflections, stone detail, photographic grain, constructed physical props and occasional worn reproduction. British Steel's creator account supports an actual oversized metal prop and photograph; not every metallic effect was CGI. A wordless original painting cannot establish the sleeve's typography. Blanket grime is unnecessary.

**Shape**: Monumental figures, creatures, machines and architectural thresholds; sharp silhouettes and small details reveal relative scale. Other designs center one emblem, blade, hand, performance silhouette or large text. Spikes and skulls are optional narrative choices. Distinguish a band's recurring mascot from generic decorative anatomy.

**Motion**: The analyzed artifacts are stills, including current platform static previews. Drawn lightning, fire and speed lines do not establish animation timing; no source video was measured. A contemporary companion can stay still while users select image annotations and open song notes. Any brief colour/focus feedback is proposed; reduced motion must retain the complete art and all controls without transitions.

**Spatial**: A commanding image or mark with intentional secondary title/record information. Painted scenes use foreground, middle distance, atmosphere and scale contrasts; empty skies or black fields can reserve room for lettering. Graphic covers can be flat and spare. Keep artwork whole when its spatial story matters, and put web prose on stable surfaces rather than shrinking sleeve lettering into body text.

**Cultural markers**: Record collecting, band worlds, commissioned fantasy/horror/science-fiction illustration, live performance, fan interpretation and artist-specific recurring identity. Derek Riggs, Rodney Matthews, Rosław Szaybo, Mark Wilkinson and collaborators have distinct contributions. Imagery of demons, religious references, war or confinement does not establish a musician's beliefs, politics or a fan's identity.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**:

- A specific band/record identity expressed through a memorable mark and a purposeful image, object or typographic world.
- Deliberate dramatic hierarchy: controlled scale, material, light or graphic contrast rather than undifferentiated darkness.
- A coherent visual story and useful music/record context that can carry from sleeve to campaign without copying another band's symbols.

## Connotation

**Mode:** contemporary revival.

Monumental, theatrical, defiant, uncanny or transportive. The painted branch rewards close looking and makes a record feel like a world worth entering. Serious craftsmanship can coexist with exuberant impossibility. Avoid treating fear, masculinity, violence or exclusion as requirements of participation.

## Related / Subsets

- `biomechanical`: shares hybrid body/machine imagery; heavy-metal graphics add a band/record identity and may contain no biomechanical figure at all.
- `gothic-revival`: architectural or lettering quotations overlap, but metal sleeve graphics are not a historical Gothic architectural movement.
- `new-york-punk`: both can build strong band identity, with different scene histories and a more direct club/press-bill branch there.
- `pop-punk`: collectible identity and illustration overlap; a comic/skate-merch branch differs from this entry's monumental painted worlds.

Epic painted fantasy, mechanical emblem and photographic/type-led graphics are descriptive branches, not separate canonical subsets. Do not treat all thrash, death, black or power-metal graphics as interchangeable.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Build a release companion with original art, a strong HTML artist/album title, clear record metadata and real song/liner-note disclosures. An explorable sleeve can use labeled image landmarks plus an equivalent text list; essential details must remain available without pointing precisely at art. Do not rely on hover, tiny targets or colour alone.

Use native details or accessible buttons, obvious selection, useful next/previous reading controls and honest progress/reset states. Track names and durations are record metadata, not playback controls. Implement real licensed/user-provided audio before offering playback. On mobile, show the complete painting and move annotations into a legible panel below it.

## CSS Translation

- Proposed epic-companion roles: deep navy `#101828`, raised slate `#1b2940`, pale silver `#e6edf3`, muted gold `#d8b866`, ice blue `#9bd4e4`. These are new application values, not sampled historic pigments.
- Use one original rich painting and restrained metallic borders/highlights. Put essential text on solid navy/slate panels, not directly on complex clouds or fire.
- A short heavy outlined/metallic name may carry identity; plain sans or serif text carries metadata and story. Keep decorative gradient stops sufficiently bright for contrast.
- Preserve focal geometry and full-scene scale. Use intrinsic image dimensions and responsive sources; stack annotation panels instead of clipping the story.
- Thin double rules, square panels and controlled 2–4 px relief can suggest a sleeve/booklet. Avoid giving every control an ornate bevel.
- Proposed feedback may use 100–160 ms colour transitions. Reduced motion removes them; no automatic lightning, zoom, smoke or flashing is required.

## Typography / Fonts

Choose a short angular/heavy display category or restrained serif according to the branch; pair it with readable metadata and prose. Impact, Arial Black, Georgia and Arial are possible system implementations, not identifications of historical logos. Do not imitate famous letterforms or turn every track label into blackletter. Exact source faces generally remain unknown.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Credit individual artists, photographers, designers and revised packages separately. A current remaster, CD or animated-delivery preview can differ from the original sleeve. Fantasy references to ancient cultures, religion, mental illness or war need context rather than anonymous ornament. Do not stigmatize people or infer beliefs, orientation or gender from costume/body imagery. Original fictional worlds and writing avoid borrowing famous mascots, lyrics or likenesses.

## Anti-Patterns

- Generic black/red, skull wallpaper or a jagged font without band/story identity.
- Making every metal style identical, or inferring animation from painted energy.
- Copying Eddie, a Priest creature, Dio's mark or another artist's composition.
- Mistaking a wordless painting or platform crop for the complete historical sleeve.
- Low-contrast metallic text, tiny image hotspots, hover-only lore or fake audio controls.
