# Greek Black-Figure

A ceramic painting technique with a separately useful graphic vocabulary: dark filled figures, incised interior detail, restrained added colors and bounded pictorial registers. Mythology is frequent subject matter, not the definition of the aesthetic.

## Scope

Ground this entry in Greek black-figure pottery and its contemporary graphic quotation, particularly Corinthian and Attic works. Regional, white-ground and later South Italian examples broaden the observed range. It is a material/process tradition, not a unified modern design movement or an umbrella for all Greek art.

The [Met's technique account](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/athenian-vase-painting-black-and-red-figure-techniques) distinguishes dark slip figures with incised or added-color details from red-figure's reserved figures and painted details. The [Museum of Cycladic Art](https://cycladic.gr/en/essay/i-aggeioplastiki-kai-aggeiografia-tiw-archaikis-periodou/) places its beginnings in Corinth and documents wider production and narrative subjects. [Beazley's study](https://publishing.cdlib.org/ucpressebooks/public/book/development-of-the-attic-black-figure-revised-edition.html) supports silhouette plus incision as the useful technical boundary. Do not classify by date, orange/black palette or myth subject alone.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Dark brown-black against warm clay orange, buff or a documented pale/white-slip ground. Added russet/purplish red and white can distinguish garments, bodies and ornament. Two colors are sufficient for a contemporary quotation, but a strict two-color or always-orange rule excludes observed variants. Museum photographs do not establish exact historical CSS values.

**Type**: Some works bear painted inscriptions; no single modern font is intrinsic. Use readable editorial serif type with restrained sans metadata as an explicitly contemporary pairing. Avoid pseudo-Greek Latin lettering, invented inscriptions or all-capital body text.

**Texture**: Flat dark masses with fine light cuts describing anatomy, feathers, dress and equipment; polished ceramic gloss can coexist with restrained surface variation. Incised-looking SVG/raster detail translates better than a universal grunge filter. Cracks, losses, restoration and photographic highlights are not required design features.

**Shape**: Profile figures, animals, circular shields, frontal eyes/faces, patterned garments, vessels, branches and occasional frontal groups. Thin cuts articulate filled forms; do not replace them with generic line icons. Palmettes, lotus chains, dots, rays, tongues, keys and squares are alternative framing vocabularies, not a mandatory motif checklist.

**Motion**: The evidence is still ceramic imagery. Depicted running or waving does not establish animation timing. Prefer deliberate stillness with immediate user-controlled reading, filtering and disclosure changes; preserve identical endpoints under reduced motion.

**Spatial**: Bounded panels or continuous narrow friezes, level baselines, ornament registers and larger quiet clay/black fields. Dense overlapping scenes and isolated monumental figures are both supported. Translate register hierarchy into image, title, reading and reference bands; do not distort essential text around a virtual vase.

**Cultural markers**: Named Greek makers/workshops, ceramic vessel uses, mythology, athletics, work, animals and ritual. Attribution is often qualified. White/black convention in figures is historical pictorial treatment and should not become a modern racial coding system. A generated symbolic scene is a new illustration, not an ancient artifact.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**:

- Dark filled figures or animals articulated through fine contrasting interior cuts.
- Clear figure/ground contrast within bounded narrative or ornamental registers.
- Deliberately limited clay/dark/pale/russet roles and a ceramic-painting reference.

An orange panel with a meander alone is too generic; exact vessel silhouettes, mythology and artificial aging are optional.

## Connotation

**Mode:** contemporary quotation.

Reads as graphic storytelling, material craft and attentive looking. It suits essays, mythology learning, museum interpretation, illustrated reading collections and object catalogs. Its strongest software application is content with pictures and clear chapters; a dense operational dashboard would need a quieter supporting layer. Avoid making antiquity imply prestige, authority or timeless cultural uniformity.

## Related / Subsets

- `beaux-arts` uses later academic architectural classicism, axial monumentality and institutional ornament. Shared antiquity references do not make it the parent of a ceramic painting technique.
- `brazilian-cordel-woodcut` also uses strong silhouettes and cut detail, but its relief-print process, Brazilian literary circulation and lettering/composition form a different tradition.
- Red-figure, white-ground painting and Greek Geometric pottery are neighboring technical categories, not interchangeable aliases. Black figures on a white slip can belong here; pale-ground outline painting without the dark/incised figure system does not qualify automatically.

Corinthian, Attic, band-cup, eye-cup and later regional practices remain contextual branches; no separate subset slugs are introduced.

## CSS Translation

Proposed application roles, not sampled ancient pigments: `--clay: #d68b53`, `--ink: #211913`, `--paper: #fff0d6`, `--wine: #6d2d32`. Test actual text and state pairs. Use warm clay for the picture field, pale paper for extended reading and ink for strong separators.

Use thin SVG paths or an inspected raster for incised-looking detail, restrained repeating borders and flat fields. Avoid shadows that turn a flat figure into a raised sticker. Prefer square content panels and generous text line-height; physical vessel curves may remain in illustration. Disable inherited decorative transitions for an intentionally still composition and preserve all controls under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## Typography / Fonts

A readable system serif or an available editorial book face is a modern adaptation, not an identification of ancient letterforms. Pair it with a plain sans for source labels, reading time and controls. Set long text in sentence case; reserve spaced capitals for short publication labels. Do not use Greek characters as substitutes for unrelated Latin letters.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Keep technique, region, period and qualified maker attribution distinct. Explain mythological figures through identified sources rather than relying on costume alone. Avoid reproducing racist caricature, sexual violence or ritual imagery merely as atmospheric UI decoration. Treat museum object provenance and photographic rights separately from a work's ancient date. Original generated or drawn imagery must be labeled contemporary and cannot count as research evidence.

## Anti-Patterns

- A black background with red figures described as black-figure.
- Orange plus meander without articulated silhouettes or register composition.
- Treating every figure as a god or every Greek vessel as black-figure.
- Fake Greek text, compulsory white marble, gold luxury gradients or column-shaped forms.
- Cracks/noise obscuring reading, tiny all-capital labels or ornament-only control states.
- Inferring web animation, responsive breakpoints or exact colors from still vessel photographs.
