# Guochao

Guochao is a contemporary Chinese heritage-branding and China-chic commerce movement translated into frontend systems through premium product clarity, script-aware typography, ceremonial framing, and selective heritage ornament. Existing evidence is limited but source-grounded in marketing, cultural press, and design research that frame Guochao as present-tense cultural confidence rather than generic historic decoration.

## Scope

Use Guochao for premium retail, tea, cosmetics, collectibles, cultural institutions with contemporary branding, festival campaigns, product launches, and heritage-aware commerce. It works when modern product hierarchy can be integrated with Chinese visual identity rather than decorated after the fact.

Avoid it for generic international SaaS, contexts that cannot support script-aware typography, or implementations that reduce Chinese identity to dragons, red/gold floods, and surface ornament.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Premium Chinese heritage palette: lacquer red, vermilion, imperial gold, jade, ink black, porcelain white, and deep indigo. Color should be ceremonial and disciplined, not a generic red/gold flood.

**Type**: Script-aware hierarchy. Use refined serif or premium sans for body and product information, with calligraphic, seal-script, brush-script, or vertical-label accents reserved for titles, stamps, and brand moments. Chinese typographic identity should be structural: vertical labels, seal marks, bilingual balance, and script-sensitive spacing matter more than decorative font swapping.

**Texture**: Premium material references: embossed foil, paper or fabric fibers, ink-wash gradients, lacquer sheen, carved seal impressions, and subtle brocade or cloud-pattern accents. Texture is selective and ceremonial; all-over ornament weakens product focus.

**Shape**: Framed heritage geometry: medallions, seals, fans, arches, layered borders, moon-gate or card frames, centered product shrines, and balanced symmetry. Frames should feel intentional and crafted.

**Motion**: Restrained ceremonial motion: scroll or processional reveals, seal-stamp impacts, foil glints, and slow parallax of layered patterns. Motion should feel luxurious and deliberate, never meme-like or hyperactive.

**Spatial**: Premium commerce and story-panel structure. Center-weighted heroes, generous negative space around products, framed story sections, dense ornamental accent zones, and disciplined product grids keep heritage detail subordinate to scanning.

**Cultural markers**: Contemporary Chinese brand confidence, seals and stamps, calligraphy or brush accents, festival and gift packaging, jade/red/gold symbolism, opera, court, folk, cloud, wave, and paper-cut motifs used with restraint, plus boxed-set product ceremony.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**:

- Integrated contemporary Chinese heritage references.
- Ceremonial premium product framing.
- Typography carrying Chinese identity through script-aware structure.
- Selective, disciplined ornament that supports product clarity.

## Connotation

**Mode:** contemporary revival.

Guochao should read as present-tense cultural confidence filtered through premium retail and culture-tech branding. It should not feel like museum nostalgia, orientalist fantasy, ironic pastiche, or a generic luxury skin.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Use Guochao where the product, institution, or campaign can carry heritage context. Strong surfaces include hero product shrines, festival campaign pages, gift-box builders, premium packaging previews, cultural storytelling sections, collection pages, seals/badges, and framed editorial modules.

Keep shopping, forms, product data, and accessibility behavior modern and direct. Heritage cues should live in hierarchy, framing, typography, material finish, and story rhythm rather than obscuring price, availability, navigation, or labels.

## Typography / Fonts

Use refined support typography for readability, then introduce calligraphic, seal-script, brush, or vertical-label accents only where the identity needs emphasis. Bilingual layouts should respect line length, spacing, reading direction, and script contrast.

Avoid treating Chinese identity as a font swap. If the type system is not structural and script-aware, the aesthetic loses its center.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Use cultural motifs with specificity and restraint. Do not collapse Chinese heritage into a shallow dragon/red/gold shorthand or borrow motifs without context. Guochao is contemporary brand confidence, not a generalized “Asian” visual costume.

Because the current profile has no reviewed image corpus, keep canonical claims tied to the cited marketing/design synthesis and avoid inventing unsupported regional or motif-specific substyles.

## Anti-Patterns

- Red/gold flooding without product hierarchy or script-aware type.
- Orientalist fantasy, tourist symbols, or shallow dragon decoration.
- Generic Art Deco luxury with Chinese motifs pasted on top.
- Hyperactive kawaii, game, or meme motion.
- Dense ornament that buries commerce information and accessibility.
