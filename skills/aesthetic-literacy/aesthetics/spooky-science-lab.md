# Spooky Science Lab

Spooky Science Lab is a classroom STEM system that uses seasonal framing to teach observable phenomena: chemiluminescence, gas production, static charge, temperature, color change, density, and material properties. Beakers, goggles, worksheets, timers, labels, and safety protocols must remain more important than mad-scientist theatrics.

## Scope

Use it for age-appropriate lessons, demonstrations, worksheets, lab stations, or science-event interfaces. State the question, materials, procedure, observation, and safety boundary. Seasonal language must never blur real hazards or imply that household-looking substances are safe to mix.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Use chalkboard charcoal, lab white, cyan glass, safety yellow, indicator magenta, and chemiluminescent green as measured accents. Reserve red for hazards or stop states.

**Type**: Use clear classroom sans, mono measurement/timer data, chalk-like display headings, worksheet labels, and tabular observations. Chemical names, units, and warnings stay conventional and highly legible.

**Texture**: Chalk dust, ruled worksheets, glass reflections, condensation, label tape, rubber stoppers, metal stands, goggle plastic, and spill-tray surfaces establish the station. Avoid cinematic slime covering instructions.

**Shape**: Use beakers, cylinders, droppers, timers, observation grids, molecule/particle diagrams, hazard diamonds only when accurate, striped safety rails, and numbered stations.

**Motion**: Use measured bubbling, timer progress, temperature/glow comparison, data plotting, or observation reveal. Reduced motion replaces bubbles with a static level and displays numeric status.

**Spatial**: Organize as a lab station: question and safety first, apparatus center, observation/data panel adjacent, cleanup last. Keep decorative formulas separate from real procedure.

**Cultural markers**: Markers include goggles, station numbers, hypotheses, variables, units, observation sheets, RAMP-style risk planning, cleanup, teacher supervision, glow sticks, dry ice, pumpkins, and other age-appropriate seasonal demonstrations.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: real learning question + apparatus/measurement + visible safety protocol + observation or conclusion workflow.

## Connotation

**Mode:** contemporary classroom adaptation. Playfulness should increase engagement without trivializing chemical safety or fabricating scientific results.

## Related / Subsets

Unlike generic mad science it uses structured inquiry and safety; unlike Classroom Bulletin Board it centers an experiment station; unlike dark laboratory aesthetics it is educational and child-facing.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Use station cards, material checklist, teacher gate, timer, variable controls, observation log, graph, conclusion prompt, and cleanup confirmation. Disable unsafe combinations and provide text alternatives to animation.

## CSS Translation

- Roles: `--chalkboard`, `--glass-cyan`, `--glow-green`, `--safety-yellow`, `--hazard-red`, `--worksheet`.
- Use ruled grids, glass highlights, label tape, restrained chalk texture, and accurate measurement ticks.
- Apparatus may round naturally; panels and safety labels remain square.
- Use question/safety header, apparatus field, observation rail, and cleanup footer.
- Motion follows data/timing; reduced motion replaces continuous bubbles with static state and values.

## Typography / Fonts

Use a legible humanist sans with mono numbers and a restrained chalk display for short headings. Never use novelty horror lettering for chemicals, hazards, units, or procedures.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Follow current school and chemical-safety guidance, age appropriateness, PPE, teacher supervision, disposal, allergies, and accessibility. Do not provide hazardous real-world procedures merely for visual authenticity.

## Anti-Patterns

- Green bubbling flask with no learning objective.
- Fake hazard symbols or joking warning labels.
- Decorative formulas presented as real chemistry.
- Autoplay bubbling/flashing with no reduced-motion state.
- Procedure text obscured by slime, glow, or chalk texture.
