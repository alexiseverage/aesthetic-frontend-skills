# De Stijl / Neo-Plasticism

## Scope

De Stijl is Dutch modernist abstraction organized by orthogonal line, pure planar color, asymmetrical balance, and the belief that reduced geometric relation can model universal order. Use it for rigorous modular interfaces, art-system references, and layouts where strict grid relation is the identity. It is not any primary-color composition, Bauhaus workshop language, or Mondrian-themed decoration detached from the grid.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Red, blue, yellow, black, white, and gray as unmixed planar absolutes. White often dominates, with primary accents placed as structural events.

**Type**: Simple geometric sans, rectilinear lettering, and horizontal labels treated as blocks within the grid. Type should never become expressive calligraphy.

**Texture**: Flat matte surface, smooth paint, crisp print, and absence of illusionistic material depth. The plane itself is the surface.

**Shape**: Rectangles, orthogonal lines, grid partitions, unequal modules, and strict horizontal/vertical relations. Curves and diagonals generally break the system unless framed as later Elementarist tension.

**Motion**: Minimal orthogonal reveal, sliding panel, or modular reconfiguration. Motion should feel measured and planar, not gestural or speedy.

**Spatial**: Asymmetrical grid, thick black separations, active white space, unequal rectangles, and equilibrium through relation rather than centering. No perspective or soft overlap.

**Cultural markers**: Mondrian’s mature grid paintings, Rietveld’s Red-Blue Chair and Schröder House, Van Doesburg’s De Stijl journal, and the tension around diagonal Elementarism.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: Orthogonal geometry, pure planar color, asymmetrical balance, and anti-ornamental abstraction must remain intact. If curves, texture, or decoration dominate, it is no longer De Stijl.

## Connotation

De Stijl reads as utopian, rigorous, philosophical, and universalizing. Historically it is a specific Dutch modernist project, not merely “Mondrian colors”; the grid expresses a belief in pure relation and social/spatial order.

## Related / Subsets

Related: `bauhaus` shares abstraction and modernist pedagogy but allows more material and typographic variability; `suprematism` uses pure geometry but floats it in white-field tension instead of grid-bound orthogonality; `scandinavian-modern` reintroduces warmth, tactility, and domestic comfort.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Use strict modular cards, orthogonal navigation, black rule systems, white-dominant layouts, and primary-color blocks for hierarchy. Keep component states aligned to the grid. Let empty cells and unequal modules create identity instead of decorative illustration.

## CSS Translation

Use CSS grid with explicit row/column spans, hard borders, flat primary tokens, no gradients, no shadows, and rectangular masks. Interactions can slide panels horizontally or vertically. Avoid border radius unless accessibility requirements demand a minimal softening.

## Typography / Fonts

Use clean geometric sans or neutral sans families with restrained weights. Keep headings and labels rectilinear, horizontal, and grid-aligned. Avoid scripts, serif ornament, expressive distortion, and diagonal type treatments.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Low identity risk, but the movement is historically and philosophically specific. Avoid turning it into a novelty Mondrian skin for unrelated products, and avoid claiming universality in ways that erase context.

## Anti-Patterns

Do not add gradients, paper distress, organic curves, diagonal confetti, rounded blobs, arbitrary primary-color decoration, or centered poster symmetry. Do not use De Stijl as a synonym for all geometric modernism.
