# Mexican Rotulismo

Mexican Rotulismo is a living Mexican hand-painted commercial sign tradition translated into frontend systems for warm neighborhood commerce, service explanation, and pictorial/typographic friendliness. Existing evidence is limited but source-grounded in regional design essays and sign-painter documentation that identify hand-painted storefront communication as the key visual and cultural anchor.

## Scope

Use Mexican Rotulismo for neighborhood food, market, local-service, cultural guide, event, and community-commerce experiences. It fits interfaces that need friendly explanation, local service presence, hand-painted personality, and text/image communication that feels co-made.

Avoid it for sterile enterprise SaaS, quiet luxury, formal institutions, or any implementation that uses cultural markers as costume without storefront, service, or sign-painting logic.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Sunny sign-paint brights anchored by wall neutrals: tomato red, lemon yellow, market green, aqua, cobalt, warm cream, and charcoal outline. Colors are saturated and friendly, often outlined for readability against painted-wall or storefront surfaces.

**Type**: Hand-painted commercial lettering. Use rounded block caps, outlined display letters, shadowed words, casual scripts, and uneven but confident spacing. Pair sign-painter display faces with a simple sans for body and utility text. Lettering must carry the identity.

**Texture**: Painted wall and brush evidence. Subtle stucco or wall-paint grain, brush drag, enamel sign wear, faded edges, and hand-filled color fields create tactility. Use sparing overlays and irregular strokes; avoid dirtying the whole interface into grunge.

**Shape**: Storefront communication shapes: cartouches, arched sign panels, speech-bubble labels, menu-board rectangles, scalloped badges, illustrated CTA blocks, and mascot/service icons. Outlines and drop shadows can mimic hand-painted dimensional lettering.

**Motion**: Friendly signboard movement: short bounce or nudge, marquee flicker, mascot wink, and hand-painted wipe-ins. Motion should feel like a shopkeeper calling attention, not casino animation.

**Spatial**: Dense but hospitable storefront stacking. Hierarchy follows sign logic: main offer or name first, pictorial explanation second, service or menu modules below. Use stacked panels, painted labels, image-plus-text pairing, and modular menu grids.

**Cultural markers**: Neighborhood storefront signs, hand-painted food and service illustrations, mascot characters, price/menu panels, outlined Spanish lettering, humor and friendliness, and pictorial explanations for everyday commerce.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**:

- Hand-painted typographic personality.
- Friendly commercial or service tone.
- Illustration and text feeling co-made.
- Storefront, signboard, or menu-board layout logic.

## Connotation

**Mode:** contemporary revival / living vernacular translation.

Mexican Rotulismo should feel warm, service-oriented, local, and craft-aware. It should preserve the communication logic of sign painting rather than treating rótulo cues as gallery appropriation, tourist kitsch, or generic “Latin” color.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Anchor the design in a clear signboard hierarchy: what is offered, what it looks like, what it costs or does, and how to act. Strong surfaces include menu grids, service cards, event posters, neighborhood guides, illustrated CTAs, mascot help moments, and local-commerce landing pages.

Use hand-painted lettering as the identity carrier, but keep transactional and accessibility-critical copy in a simple support face. Decorative density should make the interface more hospitable, not harder to parse.

## CSS Translation

- Color roles: `--bg-wall`, `--surface-cream`, `--accent-tomato`, `--accent-lemon`, `--accent-market-green`, `--accent-aqua`, `--accent-cobalt`, and `--text-charcoal`.
- Borders/dividers: charcoal outline strokes, painted double rules, sign-panel frames, menu-board separators, scalloped badges, and drop-shadowed lettering blocks.
- Radius language: friendly 8–18px panels, arched signs, cartouches, and irregular hand-painted corners rather than generic app pills.
- Effects: stucco grain, brush-stroke masks, enamel wear, faded edge overlays, hand-filled color fields, and illustrated mascot accents.
- Layout: storefront stacking, menu boards, pictorial service modules, painted labels, and dense but legible offer panels.
- Motion: short nudge, bounce, wink, marquee flicker, or paint-wipe effects with reduced-motion fallbacks that retain sign hierarchy.

## Typography / Fonts

Use sign-painter display lettering for names, offers, labels, and hero moments. Support text should use a plain sans or simple serif so prices, service details, forms, and navigation remain clear.

Avoid generic sans-only layouts, faux-Spanish novelty fonts, and distressed lettering that no longer feels like legible commercial sign painting.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Treat Mexican Rotulismo as a living vernacular craft and local commercial practice. Use Spanish lettering, food/service illustration, humor, and storefront cues in context, not as stereotypes or tourist decoration.

Because the current profile has no reviewed image corpus, keep canonical claims tied to the cited essays and sign-painter documentation. Do not invent regional variants or motif-specific rules beyond the evidence.

## Anti-Patterns

- Bright “Latin” palette without hand-painted lettering or sign logic.
- Tourist-kitsch stereotypes detached from neighborhood commerce.
- All-over grime or grunge that erases warmth and legibility.
- Casino-like motion, flashing, or exaggerated carnival cues.
- Gallery-poster isolation where the design no longer communicates service or offer.
