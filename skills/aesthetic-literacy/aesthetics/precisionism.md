# Precisionism

Precisionism organizes recognizable subjects through controlled geometric structure: factory masses, silos, urban blocks, barns, bridges, machinery, domestic objects and symbolic lettering. Clear planes, deliberate perspective and repeated structural elements carry the image. Industrial subject matter is prominent, while rural, still-life and later abstract branches prevent the category from becoming a single factory template.

## Scope

Use for architectural field notebooks, industrial-history publishing, infrastructure exhibits, original illustrated catalogs and carefully framed visual studies. Give the surface a composed view of a real or fictional subject, with practical controls supporting the image rather than pretending to reproduce a historical interface.

The category developed around loosely associated American modernists; it was not a unified collective with one manifesto. Sheeler, Demuth, Driggs, Crawford and Lozowick offer substantial architectural and industrial branches; selected Schamberg, Murphy, O’Keeffe, Dickinson and Spencer works broaden the grammar. Classify individual works through context and structure, not an artist's name alone. A later continuation keeps its date; an American movement does not imply every depicted site is inside the United States.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Divide surfaces by coherent hue and value roles so planes and volumes remain legible. Chalk/cream, blue sky, brick/rust, ochre and charcoal are useful architectural combinations. Monochrome prints, teal machinery, gold numerals, floral yellow/blue and later pastel overlays are established variants. A fixed red-black palette or metallic luxury finish is not required. Reproduction colors are visual estimates, not historic paint tokens.

**Type**: Signs, numerals and mechanical indices can become subjects or structural anchors. Demuth's numeral portrait and Lancaster signs, Spencer's billboards and Murphy's watch markings support selective expressive lettering. Most inspected works do not have a dominant text field. Use readable support text and one considered display treatment; exact historical font families remain unidentified. Commercial lettering is not uniformly sans serif, and signatures are not a UI alphabet.

**Texture**: Clear contours coexist with material evidence: lithographic grain, thin watercolor/gouache, graphite construction, brush/canvas variation, gold leaf, wood detail, smoke and reflected water. Surface restraint is common, not an absolute texture prohibition. Keep subtle original material cues inside artwork; avoid covering controls with fake aging or making every image a spotless vector.

**Shape**: Interpret a recognizable subject through deliberate geometry. Rectangular facades and windows, pitched roofs, modeled cylinders, silos, ducts, rails, stepped structures, tilted domestic planes and large curved numerals are grounded alternatives. Let the subject determine the vocabulary. Decorative triangles or an arbitrary industrial icon set alone do not establish Precisionism.

**Motion**: Static works can imply directional force, recession or layered movement, and many impose quiet stillness. The corpus establishes no animation timing, easing or historical UI behavior. Intentional stillness and immediate content updates are suitable contemporary proposals. Optional transitions must be nonessential and respect reduced motion; avoid perpetual smoke, spinning gears or accelerated parallax justified by the movement's name.

**Spatial**: Use massing, framing, perspective, overlap and repeated modules to make structure dominant. Deep plant landscapes, bird's-eye streets, low monumental stacks, broad frontal barns, tilted still lifes and later transparent overlays all occur. Preserve one original composition's scale relationships as the layout adapts. Dense engineering detail and wide negative space can coexist; universal empty symmetry is too narrow.

**Cultural markers**: Early twentieth-century American modernity, industrial and urban change, agricultural/domestic subjects, photographic influence and selective Cubist, Purist and Futurist ideas. Interpretations range from technological promise to unease about work, scale and displacement. Small workers and pedestrians occur; depopulation is common in some scenes, not mandatory. The category's meaning exceeds a general “Machine Age” mood.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**:

- A recognizable subject or grounded subject-derived form is interpreted through controlled underlying geometry.
- Massing, planes, value divisions, perspective or repeated structure organize the image rather than incidental decoration.
- A documented Precisionist branch and its historical boundaries survive the contemporary translation.

Factories, empty scenes, monochrome color, complete flatness, smooth surfaces and industrial optimism are common or variant signals rather than universal rules.

## Connotation

**Mode:** contemporary revival.

Suggest attentive observation, structural clarity and a considered relationship with modern environments. An original notebook can feel composed and deliberate without claiming that industry is harmless or that the interface is a period artifact. For industrial-history subjects, acknowledge people and consequences in the copy. Fictional sites and new artwork should be labeled as such.

## Related / Subsets

- `art-deco`: shared machine-age geometry and architecture; Precisionism does not require ceremonial symmetry, luxurious metal ornament or glamour.
- `constructivism`: shared planes and industrial subjects; political command typography, revolutionary palette and photomontage are different organizing principles.
- `futurism`: a historical influence; mandatory velocity, fragmentation and aggressive spectacle erase Precisionism's frequent stillness.
- `new-objectivity`: sober modern observation overlaps, but its Weimar social-critical lineage differs from this American geometric category and varied interpretations.
- `shaker-design`: craft and domestic objects can be depicted or historically valued; representing them does not reproduce the religious community's functional design system.
- Industrial/urban, rural/domestic, symbolic lettering, monochrome print and later overlay branches are prose variants, not separate catalog slugs.
- Purism, Cubism, modernist photography and Art Deco architecture are influences or relations. Machine Age, American Scene and Regionalism are not automatic aliases.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Give the page one original planar scene and a clear relation between the image, its view, notes and supporting facts. An architectural field notebook can let a user select a site and viewpoint, write an observation, and save or update that exact record. Real content changes, visible selection and explicit persistence scope carry the interaction.

Keep reading surfaces calm and primarily rectangular. Use restrained numbering, structural section rules and repeated data rows without turning the design into a factory dashboard. Preserve image proportions and ensure small captions remain legible at narrow widths. Put text outside perspective imagery unless lettering is intentionally part of an original decorative anchor; keep all essential labels as accessible text.

## CSS Translation

- Proposed contemporary roles: chalk background `#EFEADB`, paper surface `#FAF6EB`, graphite text `#252F32`, muted text `#536366`, blue action `#234F60`, clay secondary `#934D37`, wheat accent `#C8A469`. These are application choices, not sampled historical pigments or a movement-wide palette.
- Build original SVG architecture with explicit planes, hard boundaries, subject-derived repeated modules and restrained value modeling. Perspective should serve the composition rather than become a rotating CSS gimmick.
- Keep component radii at `0` or a small practical value; use solid rules and filled selected states. Reserve painted grain, shadows and overlapping facets for artwork, not every button.
- Use an asymmetric image/notes grid, with a proposed narrow-screen stack that retains reading order and stable image proportions. Artwork crops must not hide the defining subject merely to fit a hero slot.
- Prefer immediate selection and note updates, with no ambient animation. If optional effects exist, remove them under `prefers-reduced-motion`. Historical stills cannot validate CSS duration claims.

## Typography / Fonts

Use a restrained geometric or condensed display category for one original title or large number, paired with a readable system sans or editorial serif. A practical system stack and Georgia are contemporary choices, not identified period typefaces. Integrated historic lettering includes serif, script and numeral treatments; do not enforce one industrial stencil font throughout. Use tabular figures only where comparisons or numbering benefit, and keep long reading at normal tracking.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Retain artists, institutions and exact-work context. Do not treat all American modernists, every industrial photograph or every building abstraction as Precisionism. A factory can support admiration and anxiety; avoid universal political conclusions or labor erased for scenic cleanliness. Distinguish dated continuations and source-supported influence from core corpus evidence. Reference images have independent rights conditions; create original artwork and retain provenance. Exact historic fonts, colors, animation and interface behaviors remain unknown unless separately documented. Evaluate text, controls and focus contrast independently of artwork color.

## Anti-Patterns

- Generic factories or geometric icons without controlled pictorial structure and historical grounding.
- Converting the entry into Art Deco luxury ornament, Constructivist command posters or a Futurist speed field.
- Requiring every scene to be empty, monochrome, industrial, symmetrical or textureless.
- Calling every work by an associated artist Precisionist, or counting exhibition neighbors as core evidence.
- Copying a museum composition, commercial inscription or artist signature into supposedly original assets.
- Inventing historical font names, hex colors, CSS timing or industrial-control semantics.
- Tiny labels over patterned images, color-only selected states, unbounded decorative motion or unsupported claims of persistence.
