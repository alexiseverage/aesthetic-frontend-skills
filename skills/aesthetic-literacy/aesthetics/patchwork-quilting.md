# Patchwork Quilting

## Scope

A textile vocabulary in which distinct fabric pieces form a larger surface, often with a separate stitched quilting layer. The entry includes block, medallion, strip, curved and irregular crazy-patchwork variants. Patchwork, quilting, appliqué and printed patchwork imitation are related but different processes.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Wide variation: muted printed scraps, saturated primary blocks, green/pink, red/black and restrained yellow/white. Value contrast organizes units more reliably than a mandatory pastel palette. Proposed UI colors are warm cream, denim blue, tomato and butter yellow, estimated application choices.

**Type**: Most inspected textiles have no primary typography. Some crazy-quilt patches contain embroidery or printed motifs. Use calm readable labels on quiet cloth panels; stitched display lettering is optional, not universal.

**Texture**: Fabric weave, changing print direction, seam joins, puckering and fine quilting lines. Embroidered crazy quilts add stitch ornament and variable sheen. Patch seams and quilting lines are different layers; tied V1322216 is a counterexample to universal dense quilting.

**Shape**: Nine-patch squares, triangles, diamonds, stars, strips, hexagons, curved units and irregular polygons. Whole shapes emerge from joined units, while appliqué may sit over a pieced ground. Do not define all quilts as patchwork.

**Motion**: No historic timing or movement established by still textiles. Proposed task-selection state is immediate; avoid cloth-flapping or simulated sewing animation as essential interaction.

**Spatial**: Blocks and sashing, all-over tessellation or central medallions with nested borders. Busy small prints sit within larger legible geometry. Use broad quiet units for real UI content and seams to explain grouping.

**Cultural markers**: Global textile practices and named makers have distinct histories. This corpus is weighted toward British and US museum holdings. Do not reduce quilting to scarcity, domestic femininity or a single national tradition; preserve object maker/community attribution where known.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: Distinct joined textile units, a larger organized composition, and material/seam cues. If quilting is depicted, distinguish its stitches from piecing joins. No requirement for flowers, pastels or perfect squares.

## Connotation

**Mode:** Cozy, sociable, resourceful and cheerful.

Small different pieces become a useful whole. The playful application can feel personal and welcoming while respecting the artistic and cultural specificity of actual quilts.

## Related / Subsets

Fair Isle organizes knitted colorwork rather than joined cloth. British Postwar Textile Abstraction concerns printed/woven motifs and modern design, not necessarily quilting. Cottagecore can share cozy domestic associations without seams or block grammar. Arts and Crafts can overlap handmade values while having a broader historical scope.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Make a weekly task planner with semantic checkboxes on quiet cloth patches, pieced header blocks and a separate stitched progress band. Changing tasks updates an announced count. Mobile layout stacks readable task blocks; ornament never controls reading order.

## CSS Translation

Combine CSS grid for real layout with original small geometric SVG patch patterns, fine dashed seam rules and subtle offset inner shadows. Use alternating print scale sparingly. Reserve text padding; never rotate actual labels. No essential animation; remove optional transitions in reduced motion.

## Typography / Fonts

No historic quilt font asserted. Readable serif headings and simple sans task labels are application choices; optional stitched-looking ornament must have normal text equivalents.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Keep maker and community credits with museum evidence. Do not copy named contemporary quilts, signatures, culturally specific patterns or sacred symbols as generic decoration. Make original simple patch arrangements and describe this corpus’s geographic bias.

## Anti-Patterns

- A generic card grid with a quilt photo as its only style cue.
- Treating printed patchwork imagery as proof of joined cloth.
- Claiming quilting, patchwork and appliqué are synonyms.
- Dense fabric prints behind small functional labels.
- Inventing maker identities, original colors from monochrome photos or universal symbolic meanings.
