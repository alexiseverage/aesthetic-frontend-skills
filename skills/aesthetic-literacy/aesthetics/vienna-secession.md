## Scope

Vienna Secession is the Viennese around-1900 total-design language that merges refined ornament, square-grid discipline, gilded surfaces, and institutional exhibition culture. Use it for architecture and design studios, cultural institutions, galleries, opera or museum materials, luxury hospitality, jewelry, editorial publishing, and high-end professional identities that need gravitas. It is too formal for casual, playful, or friendly brands.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Cream, black, white, muted olive, deep burgundy, silver, and gold used as refined ornamental emphasis. Soft pastels and jewel accents can appear, but elegant contrast must remain legible.

**Type**: Tall condensed capitals, proto-geometric lettering, decorative wordmarks, regimented spacing, clean serif support, and typography integrated with borders, marks, and ornament.

**Texture**: Gilded surfaces, smooth plaster, bentwood, marble inlay, burnished metal, printed ornament, ceramic or textile finish, and finely crafted decorative surfaces.

**Shape**: Squares, grids, circles, checker ornament, framed panels, medallions, controlled linear decoration, rectilinear massing, and stylized floral motifs held within geometry.

**Motion**: Ceremonial reveal, slow decorative emphasis, pattern unfurling, and subtle gilded shimmer. Motion should feel composed and cultured rather than aggressive.

**Spatial**: Total-design interiors, gallery-like framing, balanced decorative fields, portals, thresholds, modular cards, square-proportion tracks, and strong image-border-type integration.

**Cultural markers**: Gustav Klimt, Koloman Moser, Josef Hoffmann, Otto Wagner, Secession Building, Wiener Werkstätte, Vienna around 1900, Hoffmann square-grid furniture, and Gesamtkunstwerk thinking.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: elegant geometric ornament; square-grid or framed composition; refined metallic/gold contrast; Viennese total-design context; no casual friendliness.

## Connotation

**Connotation**: Institutional, cultured, elite, ceremonial, and intellectually decorative. Contemporary use usually feels like working in a serious modern-design lineage rather than playful nostalgia, but it remains historically and class coded.

## Related / Subsets

- `art-nouveau` — closest family, but more organic and whiplash-curved than Vienna Secession’s geometric discipline.
- `de-stijl` — later strips geometry toward pure abstraction and away from ornament.
- `beaux-arts` — shares ceremonial composition, but is more classical and monumental.
- Subsets include Wiener Werkstätte, Klimt geometric-gold phase, and Hoffmann square-grid interiors.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Use gridded frames, controlled gold rules, compact formal navigation, and generous white/cream fields. Treat ornament as integrated structure, not decoration sprinkled after layout. The screen should feel like an exhibition room, portfolio plate, or crafted identity system where every object has a designated plane.

## CSS Translation

- Palette tokens: `--vs-cream: #f5f0e1; --vs-black: #1a1a1a; --vs-white: #ffffff; --vs-gold: #c5a44e; --vs-olive: #7a8b5e; --vs-burgundy: #5c1a1f;`.
- Use CSS Grid with square-proportion tracks, framed cards, thin gold borders, and `border-radius: 0`.
- Use pattern backgrounds sparingly: checker grids, fine linear borders, and medallion motifs.
- Avoid heavy drop shadows; use flat, matte, and metallic contrasts instead.

## Typography / Fonts

Use DM Sans or other geometric sans for disciplined support, Bodoni Moda or Playfair Display for formal titling, and tall condensed display capitals where readability permits. Letterspacing and integrated logos matter more than decorative novelty.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Vienna Secession is tied to elite fin-de-siècle Austrian exhibition culture, craft institutions, and European luxury. Avoid flattening it into generic “gold Art Nouveau” or vague European prestige, and do not use its authority to make manipulative institutional messaging feel culturally inevitable.

## Anti-Patterns

Avoid organic whiplash overload, friendly rounded SaaS components, rustic roughness, casual illustration, theatrical faux-luxury gold gradients, and grids with no ornament/context. If it reads as generic premium black-and-gold, it is not specific enough.
