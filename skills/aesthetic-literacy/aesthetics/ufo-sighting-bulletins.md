# UFO Sighting Bulletins

## Scope

Independent investigation associations assembled reports of unidentified aerial sightings in dated, recurring print bulletins. This entry covers the publication format, from the monochrome association circular and typewritten wrapper to the later newsletter with an editorial or contents rail. [Research profile](../../../knowledge/aesthetics/ufo-sighting-bulletins.md)

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Start with dense black or near-black ink on pale paper. The International UFO Reporter also used navy with yellow, orange, or slate, and later printed a whole issue in a warm monochrome. Give a single accent a consistent purpose.

**Type**: Use a strong recurring masthead and a compact issue line. Long reports often use dense serif or sans-serif text, while wrappers and case labels use typewriter or wide-tracked lettering. Captions and continuation notes should be smaller, still legible, and visibly distinct.

**Texture**: Use screened photographs, restrained crosshatching, ruled boxes and an occasional imperfect print edge. Keep body contrast consistent. Historical paper tone and uneven scans vary by copy.

**Shape**: Plain rectangular evidence frames, rules, and outlined notification boxes structure the page. A diagram may contain a sparse, attributed contour with leaders or relative positions.

**Motion**: Original issues are static. The reader can select cases within a fixed issue frame, keeping the masthead and report field names present across selections.

**Spatial**: Group the document around its masthead, issue line and two or three strong columns. A lead can span columns. Place a compact case register, captioned exhibit, or note next to the associated account.

**Cultural markers**: Date and number the issue. Preserve publishing or editorial attribution and differentiate a witness description, a reported observation, and an editor's assessment.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**:

- Recurring publication masthead, dated issue line, and a visible report hierarchy.
- An attributed, captioned exhibit or visibly structured account with report metadata.
- Graphic elements share a practical print vocabulary of rules, blocks, and small captions.

## Connotation

**Connotation**: The earnest work of organizing unusual reports into a repeatable community publication, with room for corrections, alternative explanations, and uncertainty.

## Related / Subsets

- Punk Zine shares some of the inexpensive print production vocabulary and prioritizes a different editorial voice.
- Engraved Celestial Atlas uses keyed evidence-like illustrations and a more formal scientific frame.
- Photographic Contact Sheet makes related visual records comparable with systematic captions and sequencing.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Build a periodical reader. Preserve the issue date, editorial masthead, and case register while the reader chooses entries. Pair every reconstruction with a caption, a source or fictional attribution, and a plain-language description of the reported observation. At smaller widths, place the report index and metadata in the same reading order as their desktop equivalents.

## CSS Translation

Use paper `#f1ead7`, ink `#252322`, shaded-rule `#a99f89`, and narrow muted ochre `#8a501e`. Use deliberate rules and high contrast for the selected case. Keep printed effects sparsely localized to the figure frame.

## Typography / Fonts

Pair a condensed display sans for the masthead with readable print serif for reports. Use a mono face with tabular numerals for labels and dated case identifiers. Prefer logical paragraphs to newspaper-length narrow lines on small screens.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Credit reports and images to their publishers or witnesses where documented. Label original demonstration reports as fictional. Give reported details, editorial interpretation, and documentary corroboration separate language.

## Anti-Patterns

- Neon cockpit controls or decorative space effects taking the place of an issue hierarchy.
- Official seals invented for a volunteer publication.
- Historical allegations written as established facts or displayed without attribution.
