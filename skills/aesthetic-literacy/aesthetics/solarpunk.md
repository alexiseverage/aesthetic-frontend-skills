# Solarpunk

Solarpunk is an ecological-futures aesthetic and social-imagination movement where renewable technology, integrated nature, community repair, and anti-doomer optimism reinforce one another. It should feel practical, public, and regenerative rather than merely decorative greenery wrapped around luxury futurism.

## Scope

Use Solarpunk for climate-positive storytelling, community infrastructure concepts, ecological education, repair/reuse products, garden/energy initiatives, and speculative interfaces that need hopeful sustainability without corporate greenwash. Avoid it for premium eco-luxury surfaces, vines-on-skyscrapers spectacle, or sustainability claims that remove people, labor, affordability, and collective action from the frame.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: sunlit yellow, leaf and forest greens, sky and water blues, earthen browns, copper, warm white, floral accents, terracotta, algae/teal, and reclaimed-material neutrals; colors should read hopeful and ecological rather than grim or toxic.

**Type**: approachable humanist typography, cooperative-poster cues, workshop signage, hand-drawn labels, and practical readable sans/serif systems; avoid authoritarian futurist type or hyper-corporate eco branding.

**Texture**: plants, wood, recycled materials, weather-aware surfaces, solar hardware, woven fibers, greenhouse glazing, ceramic/earthen finishes, rainwater systems, and visible repair.

**Shape**: biophilic curves, terraces, canopy forms, greenhouse arcs, layered gardens, walkable platforms, vertical forests, retrofit add-ons, bikes, seed libraries, modular repair objects, and hybrid natural/engineered structures.

**Motion**: growth, circulation, breeze, maintenance, water capture, transit flow, pollinator/helper-bot action, and calm public activity; motion should feel regenerative rather than frantic.

**Spatial**: dense-with-life public space, shared infrastructure, walkability, gardens, rooftops, microgrids, courtyards, cooperative making/repair spaces, and nature integrated into urban or domestic systems.

**Cultural markers**: renewable energy, DIY repair, post-capitalist or countercultural futures, anti-doomer hope, community self-sufficiency, seed libraries, rainwater harvesting, visible mending, and social-equality rhetoric.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: hopeful ecological palette + integrated nature and technology + community/public spatial logic + practical sustainability cues + visible social repair. If the “punk” commitment disappears, the result becomes greenwashed eco-futurism rather than Solarpunk.

## Connotation

Solarpunk was coined online in 2008 and grew through 2010s internet art, fiction, architecture discourse, and climate-future writing. The sources frame it as both speculative and practical: a positive alternative to dystopian sci-fi that also asks for present-tense mutual aid, repair, renewable energy, affordability, and collective flourishing. Contemporary use is optimistic but politically loaded; it promises that sustainable futures are buildable, not just renderable.

## Related / Subsets

- `cyberpunk` is Solarpunk’s explicit dystopian foil: high-tech alienation versus hopeful ecological coexistence.
- `japanese-metabolism` shares megastructural futurist imagination but is concrete/urbanist and historically specific, not community-ecological.
- `post-apocalyptic-scavenged-tech` overlaps in repair/reuse, but its mood is scarcity and survival where Solarpunk insists on regeneration, abundance, and collective flourishing.
- No canonical subset entries are defined yet; use internal branch labels such as community repair, biophilic architecture, or renewable commons when precision is needed.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Build from bright but grounded ecological color, generous public-space composition, visible infrastructure, and accessible typography. Pair plant life with functional systems: solar panels, water capture, seed libraries, repair benches, cooperative diagrams, transit, and community notices. Keep interface flows practical and readable so the optimism feels usable rather than like concept-art wallpaper.

## CSS Translation

- Color roles: `--bg-sunlit`, `--surface-warm-white`, `--accent-solar`, `--accent-leaf`, `--accent-sky`, `--accent-earth`, `--text-compost`.
- Borders/dividers: greenhouse frames, garden-bed modules, solar-panel grids, civic notice rules, and woven or repaired seams.
- Radius language: organic cards, terrace steps, canopy arcs, and rounded civic modules balanced with engineered panel geometry.
- Effects: soft daylight, plant shadows, recycled-paper texture, subtle material grain, and calm growth or maintenance states.
- Layout: public commons, garden terraces, rooftops, courtyards, modular repair stations, walkable paths, and shared-resource dashboards.
- Motion: seedling growth, breeze, water circulation, pollinator/helper-bot loops, maintenance check-ins, and reduced-motion static civic diagrams.

## Typography / Fonts

Use readable humanist sans-serifs, warm slab/serif support, cooperative-poster lettering, hand-drawn labels, and practical wayfinding systems. Avoid sleek luxury futurist type, militarized sci-fi lettering, or type choices that make the movement feel corporate and detached from community use.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

- **Context:** Solarpunk is a social and ecological movement as much as a visual style; decorative greenery without practical sustainability weakens the aesthetic.
- **Use with care:** Avoid luxury eco-renderings that erase affordability, labor, colonial land politics, and unequal access to green futures.
- **Safer translation:** Keep people, power, maintenance, repair, and shared infrastructure visible alongside nature and technology.
- **Avoid:** vines-on-skyscrapers as a complete concept, greenwashed premium branding, empty utopian spectacle, or sustainability claims that cannot be supported.

## Anti-Patterns

- Green towers, leaf icons, or solar panels used as decoration with no community, repair, or infrastructure logic.
- Dystopian scarcity, survival grime, or militarized technology replacing regenerative abundance.
- Sterile corporate eco branding that removes people and politics.
- Pure cottage garden nostalgia with no renewable technology or future-building claim.
