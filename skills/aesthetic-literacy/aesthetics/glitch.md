# Glitch / Datamosh

Glitch is a digital aesthetic that turns compression artifacts, signal failures, channel shifts, broken frames, and corrupted files into expressive visual language. Databending and datamoshing are technique families inside this broader field; the shared grammar is system failure made visible and intentional.

## Scope

Use Glitch for music visuals, cybernetic editorial work, experimental portfolios, game/horror interfaces, error-state illustrations, club/event pages, and short bursts of campaign energy. Avoid it for trust-critical navigation, long-form reading, accessibility-first services, medical/financial flows, or any UI where instability would obscure state, safety, or comprehension.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: black or dark grounds with toxic green, magenta, cyan, red/blue channel offsets, JPEG smears, corrupted color blocks, and unexpected saturation spikes. Palette can also inherit source imagery and break it through compression or displacement.

**Type**: corrupted text, monospace terminals, misregistered channels, clipped letterforms, displaced baselines, scanline labels, and code/error-message references. Text used for actual instructions must remain readable outside the effect.

**Texture**: bit-error noise, JPEG blocks, pixel sorting, data-bend streaks, codec smear, CRT scanlines, chromatic aberration, and digital artifact grain. The texture is computational, not handmade distress.

**Shape**: fragmented rectangles, sliced bands, offset duplicates, tearing, block compression cells, fractured image planes, and channel-shift silhouettes. Clean geometric order should appear interrupted.

**Motion**: stutters, frame skipping, datamosh melt, jitter, flicker, dropped frames, sudden displacement, and looping corruption. Use restrained timing and reduced-motion fallbacks for flashing or vestibular risk.

**Spatial**: planned chaos layered over an underlying system. Layout can remain structured, but the visible surface should appear interrupted by corrupt overlays, misaligned bands, or damaged media planes.

**Cultural markers**: error messages as art, corrupted-file aesthetics, code-as-art practice, datamosh video techniques, databending, vaporwave/VHS-adjacent degradation, cyberpunk instability, and club visuals that aestheticize broken signal.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: visible digital corruption artifacts; channel shift, displacement, or codec-like tearing; and an intentional tension between system structure and failure. Generic grunge, static texture, or cyber color alone is not Glitch.

## Connotation

Glitch connotes instability, critique, speed, system failure, and expressive breakdown. It can feel cybernetic, aggressive, uncanny, or anti-polish, but it should read as digital failure rather than analog dirt.

## Related / Subsets

- Databending is a technique subset: treating one file format as another to create smeared or streaked corruption.
- Datamoshing is a video-specific subset: manipulating motion/compression frames so pixels bleed across time.
- `cyberpunk` shares technological instability but usually adds dense urban infrastructure, rain, surveillance, and social coercion.
- `vaporwave` may use VHS noise and broken media, but its tone is slower and more melancholic.
- `rave-flyer` and `punk-zine` share high-energy disruption; Glitch remains specifically digital/computational.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Apply Glitch as an accent system: hero headings, hover bursts, transition masks, poster graphics, empty/error illustrations, and media overlays. Keep forms, navigation, prices, legal text, and core instructions outside the distortion layer. Provide a stable fallback state and never make flashing or displacement essential to understanding.

## CSS Translation

- Color roles: `--glitch-bg`, `--glitch-cyan`, `--glitch-magenta`, `--glitch-green`, `--glitch-error-red`, and `--glitch-noise`.
- Effects: offset `text-shadow` layers, `clip-path` slices, transform jitter, scanline overlays, pixelated image rendering, SVG turbulence, and blend modes.
- Layout: structured base grid with corrupted overlays, duplicated slices, and controlled displacement zones.
- Motion: short jitter/flicker bursts, datamosh-like masks, reduced-motion static alternatives, and no rapid flashing beyond accessibility thresholds.
- Assets: use generated or clearly licensed corruption textures rather than private screenshots or unsafe source imagery.

## Typography / Fonts

Use monospace, terminal, OCR-like, pixel, or clean grotesk faces for the distorted layer, paired with a stable readable sans for real content. Keep channel-shift and clipped text decorative unless the same text is also available in a clear layer.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Glitch effects can trigger photosensitivity, vestibular discomfort, or mistrust when used in core flows. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`, avoid rapid flash cycles, preserve semantic text, and do not use fake system-error cues in ways that manipulate users into panic clicks.

## Anti-Patterns

- Applying jitter or corruption to all interface text.
- Treating analog grunge, torn paper, or random noise as Glitch without digital artifact logic.
- Using flashing effects without reduced-motion and seizure-risk checks.
- Making payment, security, or health states look broken as a decorative joke.
- Confusing cyberpunk neon with actual compression/channel failure.
