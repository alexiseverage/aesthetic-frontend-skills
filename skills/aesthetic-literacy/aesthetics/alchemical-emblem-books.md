# Alchemical Emblem Books

## Scope

A printed reading system that pairs visual allegories with short mottoes, stage labels, and explanatory text. It suits annotated editions, exhibition readers, research essays, and interfaces that disclose the relationship between image and annotation. The source sample covers early modern European printed examples. [Research profile](../../../knowledge/aesthetics/alchemical-emblem-books.md)

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Use dark ink and light paper with clear changes in shade created by line density. A quiet contemporary accent can identify an active annotation. Paper condition and color in digitized copies vary.

**Type**: Use distinct typographic roles for a folio title, a compact uppercase figure label, longer notes, and source information. Some historical stage names are lettered directly in the plate. Modern captions should remain readable at narrow sizes.

**Texture**: Draw selective crosshatching, ruled borders, engraved rays and packed or open marks. Let line direction carry shadow and material. Heavier woodcut outlines are a documented alternative to fine tonal engraving.

**Shape**: Put a defined rectangle around the plate. Use discrete motif silhouettes such as a vessel, sun rays, stacked chambers or paired forms. A precise diagram can fulfill the same role as a framed setting with foreground and distance.

**Motion**: The references are static print. Use immediate, deliberate paging and annotation changes. Keep the selected object and its prose still so readers can relate detail to explanation.

**Spatial**: Separate the image, short key, and extended text into reading tiers. Preserve generous space around the plate and place small marginal notes near the region they explain. On small screens place the reading key and notes in a predictable order below the plate.

**Cultural markers**: Cite the book and emblem number where available. Describe the original allegory and its interpretive context. Treat inscriptions and symbols as authored choices tied to specific books.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**:

- A bounded image composed of intentional line-work and discernible tonal or spatial relationships.
- A visible relationship among figure, short key, and explanatory note.
- Deliberate progression through identifiable study units, each with contextual attribution.

## Connotation

**Mode:** Contemporary annotated-edition interpretation.

Build the authority of an edition through calibrated type, frames, citations, and repeatable composition. A translation of the visual system can stay restrained in both light and dark contexts.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Make a three-plate reader with Previous and Next, direct plate choices, and labeled annotation controls. Original apparatus or landscape plates carry the aesthetic without relying on people imagery. The selected mode should identify the same region in diagram and prose. Provide readable dark-ink focus outlines and keep all reading order and interaction available without animation.

## CSS Translation

Proposed colors are warm paper `#f1e3ca`, inset surface `#e6d2ad`, ink `#2d2119`, muted ink `#615046`, dark red `#792f29`, and rules `#796049`. Build square-corner frames with 1 px rules, and use spacing increments of 4/8/12/16/24/32 px. Set the plate in a nested double rule and keep hatching in the original vector or raster asset. Prefer state changes at 0 ms.

## Typography / Fonts

Use a book serif such as Georgia or Baskerville for reading and headings. A 28–44 px heading and 16–18 px body give room to the plate and its notes. Use small tracked uppercase labels for short state controls at 12 px or above, with a plain readable fallback. Preserve tabular numbering.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Identify editorial illustrations. Keep historic claims and symbolism attributed to their edition and interpretive source. Use translated terms for meaning and retain original inscriptions only when needed for the reading task. Present apparatus and imaginary transformations as historical interpretation.

## Anti-Patterns

- A grimy page with scattered symbols and no visual hierarchy.
- Fluent pseudo-Latin as an interface label.
- An image gallery without a maintained association between figure and annotations.
- Continuous fog, particle effects, or flicker over reading material.
