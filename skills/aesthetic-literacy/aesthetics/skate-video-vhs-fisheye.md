# Skate Video VHS / Fisheye Title Cards

## Scope

Skate Video VHS / Fisheye Title Cards is the capture-and-title aesthetic of analog and early digital skate videos: fisheye proximity, tape noise, rough title cards, and interstitial graphics around skate footage. Use it when a surface should feel like a skate part, tape dub, or video title sequence. It is not generic VHS horror packaging or any skate graphic with noise added.

Evidence is limited and textual, and motion/timing claims are inferred from still/frame evidence. Do not canonize specific edit pacing unless actual motion artifacts are inspected.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Desaturated VHS or MiniDV casts, slightly warm or green shadows, low contrast, black title-card grounds, blown highlights, and occasional bold title colors. Clean digital color grading breaks the tape/capture signal.

**Type**: Hand-drawn title cards, bold display lettering, graffiti-influenced names, chunky outlines, low-res captions, and DIY credit typography. Type should feel like an interstitial card or video part title, not a polished editorial masthead.

**Texture**: VHS grain, tracking noise, tape softness, scan artifacts, lens vignette, fisheye distortion, and analog capture blur. Use texture as capture evidence, not just a generic overlay.

**Shape**: Barrel-distorted circular framing, curved edges, close fisheye perspective, title-card frames, low-resolution rectangles, and crop marks from video transfer.

**Motion**: Quick cuts, in-your-face fisheye tracking shots, hand-held drift, and title-card transitions are design recommendations inferred from the medium. Provide reduced-motion stills or poster frames.

**Spatial**: Close-up action filling the frame, skater and environment compressed by fisheye, title cards between clips, and footage treated as a sequence rather than a static poster.

**Cultural markers**: VX1000 and Century Optics MK1 fisheye lore, 1990s skate video culture, videographer subculture, tape dubs, named skate videos as historical context, and DIY title sequences.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: fisheye/barrel perspective; analog video grain/noise; skate-video title-card typography; close action framing; skate video context. Without fisheye or video materiality it collapses into generic grunge or VHS nostalgia.

## Connotation

This is nostalgic quotation of skate video craft and camera lore. It signals proximity, risk, peer-made documentation, and subcultural memory rather than polished sports broadcasting.

## Related / Subsets

`slasher-vhs-rental` is horror/video-store packaging. `grunge-typography` is print/editorial deconstruction. `skate-deck-screen-print` is ink-on-wood product graphics rather than capture/title materiality.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Use it for event pages, reels, media cards, artist/skate portfolios, or campaign sections where moving-image context is appropriate. Keep UI controls outside the distorted footage frame and provide stable captions/transcripts for video-like content.

## CSS Translation

Use SVG/CSS fisheye approximations, radial vignettes, grain overlays, tracking-line masks, low-res poster frames, title-card blocks, and cautious glitch transitions. Gate moving noise and fast cuts behind `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## Typography / Fonts

Use hand-drawn display, marker/graffiti-adjacent lettering, rough sans, or chunky outlined type for title cards. Use a neutral sans for captions, controls, and body copy.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Skate footage and names often belong to specific videographers, skaters, and companies. Use licensed or fictional footage and avoid copying recognizable parts, logos, or titles without permission.

## Anti-Patterns

- Applying VHS noise to a flat layout with no fisheye or title-card grammar.
- Treating horror VHS packaging as the same system.
- Making fast flicker mandatory for reading content.
- Reusing real skate video stills or titles without rights.
