# Afrofuturism

Afrofuturism is a transdisciplinary field that connects Black histories, experiences and self-determination with speculative futures, technology, fantasy and alternative understandings of time. Its forms range from Sun Ra and Parliament-Funkadelic's cosmic performance worlds to fiction, film, collage, garments, sculpture, community projects and contemporary design. Mark Dery coined the term in 1993; the creative practices precede the name. It is a cultural and imaginative framework with multiple visual languages, rather than one universal decorative style.

## Scope

Use for a situated project that makes Black agency, memory and possible futures integral to its content and design. A speculative publication, artist archive, community correspondence project or exhibition can select a documented visual branch and explain its particular setting. The narrative, authorship and relation between past and future matter as much as the surface.

The inspected corpus includes restrained white photography, near-white cut paper, tarnished metal, vivid digital portraits, patterned ceramics, plain bold text, comic record sleeves and ecological garments. A Black person against stars, a neon dashboard or an unspecified “African” pattern does not establish this framework. Africanfuturism, as articulated by Nnedi Okorafor, has its own Africa-rooted emphasis; do not silently use it as a spelling alias or rename every African speculative work Afrofuturist.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Deliberately varied. Cosmic black, violet, blue and reflective silver occur in performance and space imagery; warm brown, copper, gold and green connect material, ecological and domestic worlds. White/black with restrained red is equally documented. Use colors to articulate the selected story and material, not a compulsory Pan-African tricolor or neon gradient. Any proposed hex set belongs to an application, not a historical standard.

**Type**: Record sleeves use angular outlined logos, brush script and comic lettering; text art can use a plain bold sans with generous margins. Many sculptures and photographs contain no lettering. Choose an intelligible editorial voice or an original situated display treatment. Preserve creators' names and actual languages. A gallery website's font is not evidence for the artwork's typography.

**Texture**: Collaged photographs, layered printed images, paper relief, modeled bronze, salvaged embossed metal, sequins, painted cloth, glazed ceramic and digital synthesis are all present. Keep the chosen medium legible. Roughness can carry reuse and memory; polished technology can carry another story. Contemporary artists' declared generative techniques should be credited accurately, not mislabeled documentary photography.

**Shape**: Circular vessels, stars, antenna/ray structures, imagined architectural devices, hybrid bodies, organic forms and figurative portraits occur in different branches. Some projects rely on only a sentence or a dressed human body. No motif is sufficient by itself. Create new objects and characters rather than reproducing a musician's persona, an artist's sculpture or a sacred symbol as an anonymous UI icon.

**Motion**: Performance, choreography, edited film, animation and lenticular objects are documented media. Actual film inspection shows staged reveals, expressive human gesture and shot changes; it establishes no universal speed or easing. A correspondence archive or printed-art interpretation can intentionally remain still, with visitor-controlled changes. Moving work needs its own playback research, explicit controls and a reduced-motion equivalent; do not substitute constant star drift for a meaningful narrative.

**Spatial**: Layered or nonlinear time can appear through collage, repurposed objects, juxtaposed historical/contemporary figures, domestic scenes, cosmic stages or speculative rooms. Deep pictorial perspective, shallow fields and broad empty space coexist. Keep complex imagery on its own plane and give reading/navigation a clear stable surface. A decorative map of an anonymous galaxy is insufficient without the human/cultural relationship.

**Cultural markers**: Black imagination and self-determination; African diasporic histories; collective and individual memory; experimentation with technology; the right to inhabit and author possible futures. The particular artist, location, spiritual tradition, gender and political context must remain specific. Community care, pleasure and ordinary domestic life are as relevant as escape, alienation and technological critique.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: Black agency is integral rather than incidental; past/present/future are deliberately reimagined through speculation, technology or fantasy; the selected visual language has a situated cultural/narrative purpose. Preserve individual authorship and plurality. No palette, hairstyle, metal finish or star motif can replace these relationships.

## Connotation

**Mode:** contextualized contemporary speculation, artist-specific study, or an explicitly fictional community world.

Possibility, liberation, estrangement, critique, pleasure, continuity, vulnerability and care can coexist. Avoid claiming one universally optimistic or dystopian attitude. A new work can depict an imagined Black community without pretending to represent all Black experiences or to document a real historical community.

## Related / Subsets

- `solarpunk`: ecological care and collective possibility can overlap, but Afrofuturism's Black histories/agency and temporal arguments remain integral; neither is an alias.
- `cyberpunk`: technological power and exclusion can be shared concerns, but neon corporate dystopia is neither necessary nor sufficient here.
- `atomic-age` and `cassette-futurism`: period hardware, cosmic optimism or physical media overlap selected forms; those objects alone lack this framework.
- `psychedelic`: some musical/graphic worlds share saturated, expansive imagery; a psychedelic effect is not a cultural classification.
- Collage is a useful medium for layered time and memory, not an equivalent identity.
- Cosmic performance, speculative domestic worlds, ecological garments, text-led assertion and memory-based assemblage are application branches, not newly minted canonical slugs. Africanfuturism is a separately articulated concept, not a subset automatically owned by this entry.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Start with the people, place, authorship and story. Let visitors explore an original imagined archive, correspondence, exhibition object or work, with actual readable content and meaningful choices. Pair a substantial image/object plane with clear captions and plain reading areas. A detail annotation can connect an object with what a community carries forward; navigation can distinguish chapters or possible futures without mimicking surveillance instruments.

Use semantic buttons, full labels, visible focus, keyboard access, and selected-state text beyond color. Provide meaningful image descriptions, image-failure reading continuity and coherent mobile ordering. Never place essential copy in generated artwork or pretend a decorative play button has sound. Visitor-controlled stillness and immediate state changes can suit a reflective archive.

## CSS Translation

- Proposed warm correspondence roles: parchment `#F5EAD6`, deep plum `#2B1730`, copper ink `#88442E`, violet `#6950A1`, pale lilac `#DAC9F0`. These are new application choices, not sampled canonical colors. Measure actual reading/focus pairs.
- Give a full original scene an explicit aspect ratio; use contain/full-frame presentation where annotations depend on positions. Keep captions and notes in HTML.
- Combine a quiet paper surface, thin rules, generous editorial title and a small independently authored orbital/postal mark. Do not turn sacred diagrams, textiles or script into generic repeat backgrounds.
- Define stable spacing and at least 44px interaction targets. Reflow into image, detail text and correspondence on narrow screens instead of shrinking a desktop control rail.
- An image can remain still while details or letters change immediately. Any added reveal transition is a proposed interface choice with a reduced-motion endpoint, not a historical measurement.

## Typography / Fonts

A readable serif or humanist text face with a restrained sans for indexing can support correspondence; a plain bold sans supports another documented branch. Exact pairing is project-specific. Use licensed fonts or system fallbacks, preserve diacritics, and avoid faux-tribal lettering or treating all African languages as one script. Type hierarchy should separate authored narrative, object notes and navigation.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Attribute specific creators and communities. Separate fictional futures from archival evidence, and identify generated application images as original illustrations rather than historical photographs. Do not copy recognizable characters, album art, religious garments, Vodou/Yoruba symbols or existing artists' distinctive compositions into a product. Historical images remain subject to their actual rights; linked analysis does not authorize redistribution. Do not make displacement, racism or enslavement into ornamental distress. Leave room for joy, care and ordinary life without flattening material histories.

## Anti-Patterns

- Generic cyberpunk plus a Black stock portrait or random textile pattern.
- Treating every African object, all Black art, all science fiction or every space costume as Afrofuturist.
- Renaming Africanfuturism without regard to its author's definition.
- Declaring metallic gold, neon, maximalism, spacecraft or constant animation compulsory.
- Copying protected personas, recognizable art or sacred iconography for atmosphere.
- Fake historical provenance, invented translations, unnamed “tribal” motifs, or generated portraits presented as archival people.
- Beautiful imagery with no readable story, no real interaction, or inaccessible control text.
