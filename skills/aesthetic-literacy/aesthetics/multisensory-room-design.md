# Multisensory Room Design

Deliberately adjustable sensory environments combining light, projection, tactile surfaces, soft furniture and distinct activity/rest zones around individual preferences.

Evidence basis: [the research profile](../../../knowledge/aesthetics/multisensory-room-design.md) records 30 qualifying unique artifacts, source distribution, claim support and limitations. This is a descriptive catalog category. Exact historical colors, fonts and screen timings are not inferred from appearance; proposed frontend values below are contemporary translations.

## Scope

Deliberately adjustable sensory environments combining light, projection, tactile surfaces, soft furniture and distinct activity/rest zones around individual preferences. Luminous rooms are common, but not a universal calming prescription. Snoezelen is a branded/named tradition rather than a generic identity for every room.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Localized luminous color against matte pale or dark surfaces. Cool violet/blue, warm orange, green, bright cyan, muted nature tones and restrained lavender are real variants; no hue is universally calming.

**Type**: Interior text is usually sparse; readable entrance labels and large-button controls provide hierarchy where visible. No defining font is established. Promotional website typography is not room typography.

**Texture**: Seamed pads, fabric, beanbags and matte surfaces contrast mirrors, translucent tubes, fibres and hard tactile inserts. Soft appearance alone proves neither material specification nor safety performance.

**Shape**: Low rounded forms, vertical tubes, loose fibres, framed panels and large buttons recur. Familiar sofas, ottomans, ordinary lounge chairs and rectangular bedroom padding broaden the vocabulary.

**Motion**: Filmed installations show mutable light states; rendered camera travel and time-lapse edits are separate media effects. No intrinsic light/bubble timing is measured. Any screen movement must be optional, pausable and disabled initially under reduced motion.

**Spatial**: Distinct sensory focal zones, rest surfaces and space between activities. Home corners, narrow rooms and lounge-like suites vary. A photograph cannot establish a complete accessibility or circulation audit.

**Cultural markers**: Personal choice, accessible learning/leisure/health contexts and adjustable conditions. Quiet rooms, sensory gardens and soft play are related but different scopes; promotional outcome claims are not clinical evidence.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**:

- A purposeful relationship between sensory conditions and individual choice.
- Distinct material/light/rest zones rather than a free-floating gradient.
- Controls and readable information remain usable independently of decorative light.

## Connotation

**Mode:** Contemporary inclusive visit-planning interpretation: familiar, adjustable and calm by choice, without a promised therapeutic outcome.

The implementation should quote the documented material and organizational relationships. Its contemporary controls and readable type must remain honest about source and application limits.

## Related / Subsets

- Chromotherapy is a related light/color idea but is not an evidence-backed therapeutic claim or required identity here. Do not invent color-to-diagnosis mappings.
- Soft play and sensory-integration gyms share padding and movement equipment, but an obstacle course alone is outside this scoped multisensory environment core.
- Quiet rooms emphasize low stimulation; a sensory room may be deliberately more stimulating. The Scottish guidance and Wembley comparison make the distinction explicit.
- Frutiger Aero and Y2K imagery can share glow, watery surfaces or translucent forms, but do not supply the user-centered spatial/control relationships of this category.
- Immersive projection and museum interactives share environmental media. They overlap only where sensory adjustment, tactile/rest zones and personal agency are part of the intended room system.

No separate canonical subsets are introduced.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

A visit-preferences form can pair an original room scene with light color, brightness and optional movement settings, then produce a readable local plan. Do not collect medical details or pretend to book a real clinic. Preserve contrast regardless of scene brightness, offer edit/reset and make all preview information available as text.

## CSS Translation

Rounded matte surfaces, restrained luminous accents and a stable opaque control panel. Keep soft glow confined to the scene. No automatic audio or flashing; user-started ambient motion has a persistent stop control and immediate reduced-motion fallback.

## Typography / Fonts

A familiar legible sans, generous labels and clear selected indicators suit the proposed form. No historical typeface is implied. Use ordinary sentence case rather than futuristic clinical telemetry.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Different people need different sensory conditions; do not map colors to diagnoses or promise relief. Original screen illustrations do not recreate physical touch, scent, sound or vibration. Preserve brand/context distinctions and avoid visual diagnoses of source participants.

## Anti-Patterns

- Compulsory twinkling, camera drift or autoplay audio.
- Purple glow as a universal therapy claim.
- A generic wellness gradient with no room/control relationship or fake booking success.
