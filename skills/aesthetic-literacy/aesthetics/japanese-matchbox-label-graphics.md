# Japanese Matchbox Label Graphics

## Scope

A compact Japanese commercial-print vernacular spanning domestic retail, export and promotional matchbox labels. Small rectangular surfaces integrate an emblem, figure, geometric field, lettering and business identity with striking economy. The commercial context unites diverse styles: ornate export labels and spare modern cafe graphics are branches, not one compulsory national palette. A generic Japanese illustration or decorative pseudo-Japanese text is insufficient.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Small, emphatic sets: black/pale with red; turquoise/navy/coral; yellow/black; green/pale; pink/red. Multiple rich colors occur in export scenes. Keep a bounded few-ink impression, not a mandatory beige-red scheme. Visible reproduction hues are estimates, not original ink samples.

**Type**: Handwritten Latin sans, narrow serif titles, loose script and vertical/horizontal Japanese writing coexist. Lettering is integrated into bands, corners, paths or picture openings. A modern English-language artifact can preserve this economy without invented Japanese glyphs. Tiny historical contact copy must become readable modern metadata.

**Texture**: Flat printed masses, halftone/stipple, fine parallel rules and paper/ink irregularity. Some fine hatch-heavy export illustrations differ from the spare cafe branch. Keep texture localized and subordinate to silhouette. Physical print method cannot be assigned universally from a scan.

**Shape**: Rectangular portrait or landscape labels use strong flat silhouettes, asymmetric cuts, diagonals, circle/emblem openings, bounded strips and deliberately interrupted fields. Dense ornate frames are an export branch, not a requirement. Generic rounded app cards lose the compact print structure.

**Motion**: Inspected labels are still. Motion timing and historical interface behavior are unknown. Keep printed fields stationary and use immediate carousel, selection or disclosure changes. Under reduced motion, preserve all endpoints and use no simulated burning, smoke or perpetual movement.

**Spatial**: Image and lettering interlock within a small bounded plane: a figure may cross a field split, a diagonal band may carry a title, or contact text may sit in a narrow foot strip. White space can be dominant. Scale the grammar up for usable controls, not a miniature fixed scene.

**Cultural markers**: Specific cafes, confectioners, entertainment venues, transport directions, manufacturers and export markets. Do not present all labels as an undifferentiated Japanese national essence. Religious export imagery, historical stereotypes and wartime propaganda retain particular contexts and are not decorative defaults.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: compact rectangular print enclosure; integrated lettering and emblem/figure/field relationships; purposeful economy of scale and negative space; recognizable business/product identity. Red ink, portraits, faux aging and Japanese text are optional.

## Connotation

**Mode:** nostalgic quotation.

A lively, witty miniature advertisement, useful for a small cafe menu, reservation chooser, event carousel or independent shop. A playful original emblem and concise identity feel more convincing than a generic vintage-Japan mood board.

## Related / Subsets

`taisho-roman` overlaps in urban modernity and mixed lettering, but this entry is a specific commercial object/format and includes export/retail branches. `plakatstil` shares direct identification; the small plane and integrated business microcopy distinguish match labels. `fruit-crate-label-graphics` shares label commerce, but its brand/product/packer hierarchy and larger scenic fields differ. Cafe-promotional, domestic-retail and export-trade branches remain internal variations.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Build a compact cafe label carousel whose entire panel changes composition with the selected menu/course, with persistent previous/next controls and a plain position label. Integrate the heading, menu choices, price and reservation fields into ruled fields and diagonal title strips. Each item needs substantive description; avoid artwork-only slides. Use rectangular buttons with strong outline/fill inversion, pale labeled inputs, visible focus, underlined links, compact navigation with a written active state, and short display lettering. Dividers may continue the label rule; tags use small strips/circles. Alerts pair a plain-language message with an original emblem. A confirmation dialog, if used, stays opaque, rectangular, closable and keyboard contained; do not hide the close control inside an illustration. Original icons use flat silhouettes and consistent simple strokes.

## CSS Translation

Proposed current-web roles, not sampled inks: stock #FFF4D6, ink #192C37, turquoise #217783, vermilion #B83428, yellow #F5CB58, muted #5A5249. Use dark text on pale/yellow and pale text on dark/vermilion; test rendered states. Use 0–2px radii, 2px keylines, occasional 6px inset rule, and only short display offset shadows. Typography proposal 42–68px display / 1.05, 16px body / 1.5, 12–14px metadata. Spacing 6/12/18/24/36/48px. Keep full label visible on mobile while reflowing form fields vertically; do not rotate input text. Decorative fields stay still; reduced motion removes optional transitions.

## Typography / Fonts

A system sans or locally available geometric sans for business display, Georgia for a short contrasting serif strip, and normal sans for forms are application choices. Hand-lettered original wordmarks may supplement accessible HTML. Japanese copy requires accurate language, appropriate font coverage and competent review; it is not necessary for an English-language quotation.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Use original business names and drawings. Do not copy brands, racist caricatures, religious scenes, propaganda or protected characters. Preserve export-market context in research and avoid claiming every Japanese label shares one movement. The scholar abstract and industry page contextualize exchange/collecting, not rights clearance or exact dating for these individual scans.

## Anti-Patterns

- Generic cafe cards with Japanese pictures as the sole signal.
- Pseudo-Japanese characters, copied historical brands or decontextualized religious/export motifs.
- Treating all labels as minimalist Bauhaus or all as ornate ukiyo-e.
- Upscaled scrapbook thumbnails presented as a rigorous corpus.
- Tiny essential contact text, angled form labels, color-only selection or animated burning effects.
