# Winter Solstice Alignment Diagrams

## Scope

Modern plans, sections, measured views and modeled sky profiles that explain how midwinter sunlight relates to a structure or a local horizon. Keep the physical geometry, projected path and evidence for illumination separately legible. [Research profile](../../../knowledge/aesthetics/winter-solstice-alignment-diagrams.md)

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Set terrain and masonry in muted values. Use a controlled amber, ochre or red line or band for light. A horizon diagram can introduce cooler sky colors.

**Type**: Use short survey labels, orbital or compass notation and legible figure captions. Distinguish units, dates, direction and model era through typography.

**Texture**: Use stone outlines, light section hatch and restrained terrain marks. In a measured photo, preserve the surface that the light actually reaches.

**Shape**: Use cutaways, passage axes, apertures, horizon contours, solar discs and directional arcs. Use distinctly keyed solids and dashed lines where ground truth and projections coexist.

**Motion**: A still figure records one date, time or modeled position. Stepping between sun positions digitally shows changes while the architectural frame holds steady.

**Spatial**: Place an avenue, passage or alignment on a stable axis. Keep orientation, scale and annotations attached to their view. Link a plan, section or horizon only where they explain different geometry.

**Cultural markers**: Identify the monument, observer position, date and contribution of any restoration. Assign interpretation of historic intent to its source.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**:

- Stable structure or horizon geometry with a visible observer or aperture relationship.
- A narrow, explicitly labeled light path, direction or solar position.
- A legible distinction between the observed, modeled and interpreted material.

## Connotation

**Connotation**: Heritage interpretation and field documentation. Use the pair of a specific place and a specific phenomenon to convey attention to orientation and season.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Build an alignment inspector with a cutaway, an optional plan and a horizon strip. Let readers step among below-horizon, aligned and off-axis states. Update notes and ray extents together and keep controls operable by keyboard.

## CSS Translation

Use survey paper `#eee5ce`, ink `#272921`, horizon `#253d46`, modeled beam `#f8bd58` and dark annotation `#875014`. Apply hatch behind labels only where it indicates cut material. Stack the views on narrow screens.

## Typography / Fonts

Use a readable serif for narrative and instrument-style monospace for azimuth, date and panel labels. Use a differentiated size for the model disclaimer and retain readable contrast.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Lead with observation and cite context from site custodians and researchers. Label the model date and assumptions. Treat the purpose of an alignment and reconstruction of a ritual as claims with specific provenance.

## Anti-Patterns

- Golden-hour decoration with no relationship to the surveyed geometry.
- Schematic degrees presented as measurements.
- Collapsing sunrise and sunset at distinct sites into one generic narrative.
