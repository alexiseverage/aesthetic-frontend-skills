# Pichwai

## Scope

Pichwai is a tradition of devotional hangings associated with the Hindu Pushtimarg community and Krishna as Shrinathji. Its recognizable visual system joins a devotional focal relationship with meaningful seasonal or festival motifs, framed textile fields, repeated borders/registers and deliberate scale hierarchy. Painted cotton is prominent; collection documentation also describes embroidered and other textile variants.

An external icon may supply the center, so a hanging need not depict a large Krishna itself. Central icons and attendants, circular dance, repeated narrative rows, cow fields, peacock fields, palace scenes and lotus water are different grounded branches. Nathdwara, Deccan, Kishangarh and source-described Gujarat practice retain their identities. Not every Nathdwara painting or Indian floral textile is Pichwai. The [research profile](../../../knowledge/aesthetics/pichwai.md) documents thirty-one inspected works, source concentration and metadata caveats.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Use color to separate focal image, textile field, nature and border registers. Indigo/gold Deccan grounds, green forests, cream cows, red fields, pink flowers, pale architecture and blue lotus water coexist. Select one coherent branch rather than requiring every bright color at once. Scientific research documents changing and imported pigments; “all natural pigments” is not a valid universal rule. Exact UI hex values are proposed.

**Type**: Small inscriptions can accompany individual panels or a lower strip. They are optional and generally subordinate to the hanging. Use readable contemporary labels and modest serif/sans hierarchy for interpretation. Historical fonts and exact words were not recovered; do not substitute fabricated scripts or a novelty “Indian” font.

**Texture**: Flat painted contours, detailed garment and leaf infill, cloth edges, dyed grounds and applied metallic material support the surface. Source-described embroidery adds stitch and sequin variation. Gold/tin/silver are not present in every record. Preserve a chosen material branch without fake distress: conservation tears, mounts, frame reflections and paint loss are not mandatory features.

**Shape**: Rectangular hanging fields, nested shrine frames, scalloped arches, lotus forms, banana leaves, peacock fans, cows and figurative profiles provide scale and rhythm. Motifs belong to particular devotional narratives or occasions, not an interchangeable decorative pack. The center may be a depicted icon, an external-icon space, a circular scene or an architectural stage.

**Motion**: Dance, procession and weather are depicted narrative; seasonal/festival changes concern display context. The researched corpus establishes no historical animation, timing or easing. Intentional stillness with immediate study selection and readable context is a suitable modern proposal. Optional digital motion must retain reduced-motion parity and should not pretend to reconstruct ritual performance.

**Spatial**: Borders and registers frame a devotional relationship; enlarged figures, smaller shrine panels and landscape episodes establish hierarchy. Density varies from medallion fields to spacious palace or water scenes. Off-center stages and multi-episode layouts belong alongside axial compositions. Retain the whole hanging's proportions and edge structure rather than cropping it into an anonymous floral background.

**Cultural markers**: Pushtimarg devotion, Shrinathji, Braj, source-traced regions and meaningful occasions such as Sharad Purnima, Gopashtami, Annakut and monsoon display. Specific figures, gestures, foods and sacred symbols require source interpretation. Unknown maker names in museum records do not prove universal anonymity or erase living practice.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**:

- Preserve the Pushtimarg devotional setting or explicitly contextualized contemporary quotation.
- Organize ornament around a meaningful focal relationship, frame and scale hierarchy.
- Make motif repetition and borders/registers serve the chosen scene or occasion.
- Keep textile/process character and regional attribution visible without inventing authenticity.

## Connotation

**Mode:** contemporary revival for cultural interpretation; authentic devotional use requires actual community and object context.

The reference can convey devotion, abundance, seasonal care and a carefully framed encounter. It is strongest in an attributed art, craft or cultural setting. A current original study is not automatically a historical object or a ritual installation. Secular decorative adaptations should state their relationship and preserve enough composition/context to remain more than generic lotus luxury.

## Related / Subsets

`mingei` shares material/process vocabulary, but its beauty-of-use and collecting framework differs from this specific devotional backdrop tradition. `maximalism` shares abundance; density alone cannot establish Pichwai. `art-nouveau` shares some botanical or peacock forms, but its whiplash line and Belle Époque total-design history are different. These are visual comparisons, not claims of direct influence.

Nathdwara painting and Mewar painting are broader regional categories, not automatic aliases. All Hindu art, temple architecture and Indian floral textiles are also broader or different. Monsoon/Deccan gilding, autumn full moon, circular dance, cow festival, Annakut, architecture and lotus-water compositions are useful prose branches; no additional canonical subset slugs are defined.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Suitable uses include attributed gallery guides, textile collections, cultural publications and educational interpretation. Present a complete original hanging study with readable contextual text outside dense artwork. A season/occasion accordion can change both the visible study and its explanation; a focused detail panel can explain a motif in that exact study. Preserve the distinction between artwork and controls.

Use plain, labeled controls, explicit selected states and quiet text surfaces. Keep all meaningful borders/registers visible at narrow widths. A cultural guide should explain which aspects are historical and which are contemporary, without turning research/debug metadata into the artifact's main experience. Do not imply museum ownership, authentic manufacture or ritual authority that the product lacks.

## Typography / Fonts

A restrained readable serif can carry cultural/editorial headings, paired with a language-capable sans for labels and dense interpretation. System stacks are valid modern proposals. Decorative script is unnecessary when the source image already carries the strongest identity. Exact historical fonts remain unknown.

If Hindi, Gujarati or other regional text is included, use verified wording, an appropriately licensed script-capable font and correct shaping. Do not use untranslated glyphs, simulated Sanskrit lettering, sacred names or artist signatures as decorative filler. Do not turn a tiny museum inscription into a supposedly standardized movement alphabet.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Pichwai belongs to a living devotional tradition, not an interchangeable “Indian aesthetic.” Preserve Pushtimarg, Shrinathji, Braj and regional context where relevant. Sacred cows, peacocks, lotuses, offerings and gestures have specific relationships; use sources or knowledgeable interpretation rather than inventing a meaning for every shape.

Educational original depictions should retain attribution and purpose. Avoid using the deity as a commercial mascot, inventing festival dates, claiming a generic temple simulation or presenting a contemporary asset as authenticated heritage. Source photographs, scans and practitioner works require separate reuse permission; research access does not make them production assets.

The corpus favors historical museum holdings. Two TAPI accession conflicts, one tentative festival title and one dimensional-label conflict remain in the profile. They do not authorize precise origin, technique or ownership claims. Seek practitioner/community input when a product intends actual devotional use; that broader use is different from a bounded cultural study.

## Anti-Patterns

- A lotus wallpaper or peacock palette with no devotional setting, framing or scale hierarchy.
- Calling every Indian painting Pichwai or reducing all regions to Nathdwara.
- Requiring a painted central Krishna in every hanging, or forcing every scene into perfect symmetry.
- Universal gold, all-natural pigment, anonymous-maker or deliberately distressed authenticity claims.
- Treating repeated figures as data icons or animation frames without supporting context.
- Fake scripts, copied sacred imagery as a logo, unsupported festival dates or tiny controls lost in ornament.
