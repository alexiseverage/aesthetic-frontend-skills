# German Romanticism

## Scope

An established artistic movement, with a particularly useful landscape vocabulary of subjective nature, the sublime, spiritual contemplation, longing, and historical memory. Caspar David Friedrich is a central reference, alongside Philipp Otto Runge, Carl Gustav Carus, and the transnational Dresden circle. Winter landscapes are a documented branch, not the definition of the movement.

Translate relationships between observer, natural form, light, and distance. A snowy photograph, a back-facing figure, or an antique serif alone does not establish this identity. Runge's symbolic floral/allegorical compositions widen the boundary beyond Friedrich's landscape practice. This entry emphasizes the landscape branch while retaining that distinction.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Earth browns, deep green, slate, blue-grey and luminous cream often divide a dark near field from pale distance. Rose, gold, red dawn, and bright pastoral green also occur. Winter can use ochre ice beside cold blue air; an exclusively blue palette is optional, not historical law.

**Type**: Paintings usually contain no functional typography. Inscriptions and print captions are secondary. A measured serif with quiet readable interface labels is a proposed editorial translation, not an identified movement-wide typeface. Blackletter is not a default requirement.

**Texture**: Carefully described bark, stone, snow, mist and painted light; preparatory sheets can expose fast brushwork or fine graphite lines. Runge also used cut-paper silhouette and graphic contour. Preserve meaningful material differences instead of covering everything in heavy crackle, sepia, or glossy glass.

**Shape**: Branching trees, angular rocks, low shores, receding ridges, small ships and human silhouettes; windows, ruins and paths create viewing thresholds. Some scenes organize around a central vertical, others around an almost empty horizontal expanse. Ornament belongs to specific symbolic works, not every panel.

**Motion**: Qualifying references are still works; historical interface timing is unknown. Contemplative stillness is an appropriate application choice. Use explicit user-controlled changes, stable framing and immediate reduced-motion behavior. Do not describe CSS fog drift or parallax as historically observed.

**Spatial**: Near/far contrast and atmospheric recession carry emotional meaning. Allow one landscape to dominate, hold generous sky or mist, and place useful controls outside that quiet field. Small figures are common, but the Wanderer is comparatively large and several works omit figures entirely.

**Cultural markers**: Early nineteenth-century German-speaking artistic contexts, Romantic subjectivity, spiritual nature, memory, seasonal/life cycles and the sublime. Crosses, graveyards and ruined abbeys have specific religious/historical meanings. Dresden connections included Norwegian-born painters; avoid nationalist essentialism.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: Nature or a symbolic natural order must carry the emotional structure; preserve deliberate light/distance relationships; give the viewer a contemplative position rather than filling the scene with product chrome.

## Connotation

**Mode:** contemporary revival. Quiet awe, longing and vulnerability can coexist with hope, renewal and luminous warmth. The result need not feel tragic or sinister. Position original contemporary imagery honestly; do not pass generated landscapes off as historical paintings.

## Related / Subsets

Winter landscape, coastal contemplation, and Runge's allegorical floral work are documented branches without separate catalog slugs. Biedermeier emphasizes intimate domestic order; Gothic Revival emphasizes architectural/formal revival; Shin-hanga has its own Japanese print production and viewing context. Similar weather or stillness does not erase those distinctions.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Use a landscape exhibition, contemplative reader, environmental story or image-led archive. Keep titles, captions, image selection, and save actions in a quiet adjacent band. Make image descriptions available and keep text off variable snow/sky unless a solid backing supplies contrast. On mobile, show the full image before secondary commentary; preserve the observer and horizon when cropping.

## CSS Translation

Proposed winter application roles: `--ground:#17272c`, `--paper:#eee9dc`, `--ink:#203136`, `--muted:#53656a`, `--accent:#9b7d4d`. Use square image mats, fine `1px` borders, restrained frame shadows and `24–48px` spacing. Prefer `object-fit:contain` for artwork. Scope textures to the frame/background and keep user state changes immediate. These CSS values are design decisions, not sampled historical pigments.

## Typography / Fonts

Georgia or another readable book serif can provide the display/caption hierarchy; a restrained system sans can handle controls. Use regular/italic contrast before excessive weights. Keep essential labels at readable sizes, supply fallbacks, and document licensing if adding fonts. No extra font download is required.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

[The Met's exhibition account](https://www.metmuseum.org/exhibitions/caspar-david-friedrich-the-soul-of-nature/inside-the-exhibition) situates Friedrich's constructed landscapes in his artistic and spiritual practice. [The National Gallery's Winter Landscape](https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/paintings/caspar-david-friedrich-winter-landscape) supports a hopeful religious reading alongside wintry isolation. Treat sacred symbols as content with context, rather than arbitrary icons. Public-domain subject matter does not automatically license every modern reproduction; use original assets or verify rights separately.

## Anti-Patterns

- Equating the whole movement with snow, despair, a lone man, or one Friedrich painting.
- Using bright cyan glass panels, procedural snowflakes or fantasy crystal castles as the identity.
- Imposing blackletter, fake German quotations, national insignia or sacred symbols without context.
- Heavy moving fog, image zoom loops, unreadable captions or a card grid that overwhelms the landscape.
