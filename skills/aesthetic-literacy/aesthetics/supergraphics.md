# Supergraphics

## Scope

Oversized lettering, stripes, silhouettes and patterns that respond to architectural scale, surfaces, corners and openings. Graphics become part of a spatial composition. Foundational Sea Ranch work and later civic, school, exhibition and public-space programs provide different branches. This is a visual system within environmental design, not every sign or colorful interior.

Adapt it to event schedules, navigation, tabbed programs, editorial headers and registration steps: screen regions become adjoining graphic planes while functional content remains readable.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Strong figure/ground relationships, often black/white or a few saturated colors. Multicolor geometry, monochrome type and translucent window stripes are all evidenced. Rainbow color is optional; no single historical palette is mandatory.

**Type**: Monumental letterforms, numbers and words interact with walls, openings and thresholds. Sans, slab, stencil and custom lettering vary by project; some works are pattern-led. Use actual readable labels beside decorative crops. Font identification requires project metadata.

**Texture**: Opaque painted planes, built letters, slats/mesh, translucent vinyl and glass. Material and daylight can matter more than simulated grain. Distress, paper noise and soft shadows are not defining.

**Shape**: Large bands, rays, chevrons, diamonds, curved counters and optical repeats. Shapes are cut by edges or continue into adjoining surfaces. Avoid sprinkling small unrelated motifs across a generic interface.

**Motion**: Still source evidence establishes no mandatory animation. Viewpoint can change a physical reading; that does not supply digital timing. Immediate state changes and intentional stillness are valid. Respect reduced motion for any optional transition.

**Spatial**: Architecture-scale figure/ground, continuity, cropping and changes in orientation. Doors, corners, columns and ceiling planes organize the composition. Quiet functional zones coexist with dominant graphic areas; not every surface needs pattern.

**Cultural markers**: Site-specific institutional, civic and community identity. Credit named designers, collaborators and local context. A school motto, team identity or community-authored text is not interchangeable decorative content.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: A dominant graphic element at spatial scale; a meaningful relationship to boundaries or adjoining planes; clear figure/ground; readable functional information. Bright blocks plus large text alone are insufficient if neither responds to the composition.

## Connotation

**Mode:** contemporary revival. Can suggest public presence, orientation, collective energy and confidence. Match the content and context; do not imply the work recreates an original commission or represents a community without participation.

## Related / Subsets

- **Swiss International** shares hierarchy/legibility; its editorial grid is a different center of gravity.
- **Op Art** overlaps in optical pattern, which is one optional branch.
- **Memphis** shares playfulness but does not require architecture-scale continuity.
- **Bauhaus** shares geometric economy with another historical program.
- Painted geometry, typographic environments, optical ceilings and translucent windows remain internal variants.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Use large repeated numerals or a continuous original band to connect a header, navigation and content panel. Keep real headings and labels fully readable; decorative crops should be hidden from assistive technology. Event tabs should expose selected state and panel association, support arrow/Home/End keys and maintain visible focus. Saved cards should expose pressed state and actual content changes.

On mobile, reduce the number of simultaneous planes while preserving one bold continuous gesture. Keep44px targets and generous quiet reading areas. Avoid elaborate fake transit consoles, rooms or decorative status dashboards.

## CSS Translation

Proposed screen roles, estimated rather than sampled historical paint: ivory#F5F1E7, ink#18231D, red#D84832, yellow#F4CE45, blue#2554BF. Verify actual text pairs. Use flat fills, square corners, strong rules and finite original SVG/CSS geometry. Clip only decorative shapes, with overflow isolated from focus rings and functional text.

Align continuous forms deliberately across adjacent regions. Large relative units and responsive grid work better than a photograph of a branded room. No compulsory gradients, grain, drop shadows, parallax or autonomous movement.

## Typography / Fonts

Choose bold sans/slab/stencil treatment to suit the branch. Local Arial Black/Arial is a practical modern pairing with no extra download. Rockwell, Verlag, Zapata, DIN and Jets Bold belong to specific creator case studies, not the entire aesthetic. Do not fake a font attribution or stretch body text for spectacle.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Sea Ranch attribution and dating need qualified historical context; later installation photos are not untouched originals. Collaborators and community authors matter alongside prominent designers. Clinical installations do not prove health effects. Use original graphic assets and text; institutional/team marks, comic images and modern photographs retain distinct rights.

## Anti-Patterns

- Calling all environmental design or oversized logos Supergraphics.
- Counting every photograph of one program as an independent work.
- Colorful shapes floating independently of edges or hierarchy.
- Cropping actual instructions or hiding focus inside clipped decoration.
- Mandatory rainbow, Op Art shimmer or animated bands inferred from stills.
- Importing a community's words or an institution's identity without context.
