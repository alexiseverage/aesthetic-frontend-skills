# Entomological Specimen Drawers

A collection-design grammar of preserved insects, shallow drawers or unit trays, linked identification and comparable series.

Evidence basis: [the research profile](../../../knowledge/aesthetics/entomological-specimen-drawers.md) records 30 qualifying unique artifacts, source distribution, claim support and limitations. This is a descriptive catalog category. Exact historical colors, fonts and screen timings are not inferred from appearance; proposed frontend values below are contemporary translations.

## Scope

A collection-design grammar of preserved insects, shallow drawers or unit trays, linked identification and comparable series. It is useful for collection explorers, comparative catalogs and museum record interfaces. A decorative butterfly silhouette or wall frame alone does not establish the category.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Pale specimen beds, dark identifiers and localized natural color. Metallic green/blue beetles, warm cork, mint taxon strips and multilingual bright cards broaden the range; neither sepia nor dark ground is compulsory.

**Type**: Hierarchical group labels, specimen data and retrievable codes. Historical handwriting/serif slips, compact mono-like names and modern sans/barcodes coexist. Enlarge functional text rather than copying miniature physical labels.

**Texture**: Dry biological surfaces, cards, thin pins, foam or cork and shallow container edges. Preserve differences between glossy shells, fuzzy bodies and translucent wings; aging and glass glare are incidental.

**Shape**: Rectangular shallow containment, nested unequal unit trays, repeated bodies, mount cards and stacked pin labels. Provide clearance for long legs, antennae and wings rather than cropping them into identical icons.

**Motion**: The researched corpus is still photography. Drawer affordances are visible but no handling speed is measured. Keep preserved specimens still; optional filter/selection transitions are contemporary proposals and disappear under reduced motion.

**Spatial**: Comparable groups, rows/columns, gaps and identification relationships. Unequal density, sparse columns and unresolved working arrangements are valid variants; perfect symmetry is not required.

**Cultural markers**: Collection/reference practice, specimen codes and taxonomic organization. A source TYPUS tag carries actual nomenclatural meaning and is not a decorative warning badge.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**:

- Preserved insect forms inside a shallow organizational container.
- Identification remains linked to each specimen or group.
- Repeated morphology and grouping serve comparison rather than generic ornament.

## Connotation

**Mode:** Contemporary museum-catalog quotation: careful observation, material specificity and retrievable knowledge.

The implementation should quote the documented material and organizational relationships. Its contemporary controls and readable type must remain honest about source and application limits.

## Related / Subsets

- Autumn Field Guide Naturalism shares natural-history identification but centers seasonal field observation and plates. This category focuses on dry-preserved insects, mounting, drawers and retrieval through collection records.
- Photographic Contact Sheet shares comparative arrays, but here physical specimens, mounting depth and taxonomic groups determine organization rather than film frames or exposure sequences.
- Materia Medica Collections shares containment and provenance, but organizes raw substances in jars/boxes/drawers rather than pinned insect bodies and comparative morphology.
- English Bestiary Illumination uses manuscript animal imagery and moral narratives; it does not supply modern taxonomic specimen evidence.
- Decorative insect shadow boxes and framed teaching panels are nearest neighbors. They may share bodies and labels, but a wall display without drawer/unit-tray organization is outside the qualifying core used here.

No separate canonical subsets are introduced.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Build a drawer explorer with real category filters, a selected-record inspector and a comparison tray. Preserve relative-form differences and keep record titles with their illustrations. Announce result counts, show empty states and provide add/remove/clear actions by keyboard. On mobile stack inspection and comparison without clipping anatomy.

## CSS Translation

Use a pale specimen field, thin dark rules, restrained wood edge and subtle pin shadows. Unequal CSS grid groups can retain a shared baseline. Keep text on clean paper and use a selected outline plus text, not hue alone.

Proposed application roles, not measured historical samples: background `#e9e6da`, surface `#faf8ef`, text `#242e28`, muted text `#526056`, action `#234f42`, secondary `#d5c5a1`, accent `#d8a947`. Proposed small/medium/large radii: 0/2/4 px. Use a 4/8/12/16/24/32 px spacing scale. Validate actual rendered text, controls and states independently of these illustrative tokens.

## Typography / Fonts

A readable book serif for collection titles, neutral sans for controls and tabular/mono identifiers is a proposed pairing. No universal entomological typeface is established.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Collection history and acquisition provenance can be incomplete. Identify original study illustrations as such; do not invent museum accessions, type status or species determinations. This visual system is not collecting, preservation or wildlife-trade instruction.

## Anti-Patterns

- Uniformly scaled butterfly silhouettes with no record relationships.
- Animating preserved insects as if alive.
- False type labels, ornamental Latin or invented location data.
