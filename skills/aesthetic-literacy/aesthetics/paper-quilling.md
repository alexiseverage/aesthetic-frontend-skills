# Paper Quilling

## Scope

Rolled, curled and shaped narrow paper strips form upright edges, coils and relief compositions. This entry distinguishes paper quilling from cut silhouettes, folded sheets, metal filigree and Indigenous porcupine-quill embroidery. Contemporary edge-paper work often combines several paper techniques.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Contemporary examples often use graded saturated hues, but Y7 is white-on-white and Y8 gold/white. W1 is aged mixed-material gold, red, blue and green. No obligatory rainbow palette; proposed UI uses ivory, teal, coral and golden yellow.

**Type**: Paper-edged display lettering is directly observed in Y6–Y10 and S2–S4. Broad conventional print type accompanies it. Decorative shadow-defined letters are unsuitable for small essential labels; use an ordinary readable text layer.

**Texture**: Thin upright paper edges, tightly rolled disks, loose curls, hairpin fills and local cast shadows. Matte sheets dominate; overlapping cut paper and mixed materials are variants, not evidence that all quilling is made from identical coils.

**Shape**: Spirals, concentric cells, S-curves, teardrops and pinched leaves build silhouettes or letter counters. Repetition can be airy or dense. Broad flat cut ribbons alone are insufficient (excluded S13).

**Motion**: Still artworks do not establish animation. Proposed UI button feedback is immediate with optional brief color change; no constant spinning coils or simulated paper-unrolling required.

**Spatial**: Upright boundaries divide shallow relief cells; nested curls fill some cells and leave others open. Keep readable content on quiet paper faces with ornamental relief around the perimeter.

**Cultural markers**: Paper filigree has documented historical domestic/decorative uses and diverse contemporary practitioners. Named artist practices and mixed-material objects should retain provenance. Paper quilling is distinct from Indigenous quillwork.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: Edge-on strip construction, coiled or curved relationships, shallow paper relief and intentional empty space. A flat spiral wallpaper is insufficient.

## Connotation

**Mode:** Bright, delicate, handmade and celebratory.

Careful small curls accumulate into an inviting whole. White and gold variants can feel quiet; saturated bands can feel exuberant without requiring heavy narrative.

## Related / Subsets

Origami folds sheets into planes and volumes; Papercut cuts broad silhouettes. Metal filigree shares scroll morphology but has a different material and history. Modern quilling can combine folded and cut elements without making those categories aliases.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Use relief edges to shape an editorial signup and topic selector. Plain text labels sit inside quiet fields. A submitted state confirms selected topics; validation is inline and announced. Controls remain at least 44 px and keyboard-operable.

## CSS Translation

Create original SVG spiral paths with layered fine strokes and short offset shadows; pair them with narrow double borders and soft contact shadows on fields. Scale ornament down on phones and reserve padding. No motion is essential; disable optional transitions under prefers-reduced-motion.

## Typography / Fonts

No exact source font identified. Choose readable serif editorial headlines with simple sans form labels; custom coiled lettering is ornamental and needs an accessible text equivalent.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Source artworks, brand marks, portraits and book designs remain linked evidence only. Do not trace signature compositions or imply artist endorsement. Describe technique rather than claiming an unsupported ancient inventor or conflating paper filigree with Indigenous quill traditions.

## Anti-Patterns

- Rainbow swirls without visible strip thickness or relief.
- Low-contrast white paper lettering as functional form labels.
- Putting dense coils behind paragraphs or focus rings.
- Counting a cover’s miniature projects as separate artifacts.
- Inferring physical dimensions, responsive behavior or animation from still photographs.
