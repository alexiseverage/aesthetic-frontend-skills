# Kidcore

Kidcore is an internet aesthetic built from affectionate childhood-memory semiotics: saturated toy colors, classroom and playroom objects, sticker-sheet graphics, plush/cartoon references, school supplies, and 1990s–2000s nostalgia. It is object- and play-infrastructure led rather than performer-led.

## Scope

Use Kidcore when the interface can become a sticker binder, recess supply caddy, toy shelf, reward chart, pencil case, or playroom grid. It should read tactile, youthful, optimistic, and specific to childhood objects rather than simply “bright.”

Do not use Kidcore for clown makeup/persona systems (`clowncore`), Harajuku accessory-stacking fashion (`decora-kei`), hot-pink doll glam (`barbiecore`), or uncanny nostalgic liminality (`dreamcore-weirdcore`).

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Saturated primary and secondary brights, rainbow combinations, crayon-box contrast, toy-package color, cereal-box accents, and Lisa Frank-adjacent intensity. Keep contrast strong and cheerful rather than muted, sleek, gothic, or luxury-coded.

**Type**: Rounded classroom lettering, toy-package display, marker captions, lunchbox labels, sticker-sheet lettering, alphabet-magnet voices, and sturdy rounded sans support type. Handwritten accents can appear as notes or doodles rather than all-over body copy.

**Texture**: Plastic school supplies, stickers, crayon scribble, notebook paper, glitter, plush, molded toy surfaces, arcade/reward tokens, juice-box gloss, binder plastic, rubber erasers, craft foam, tape, and doodled margins.

**Shape**: Stars, hearts, smiley faces, flowers, toy-block geometry, sticker outlines, chunky compartments, tabs, rounded bins, alphabet magnets, reward stamps, snap bracelets, yo-yos, crayons, plush silhouettes, and simple cartoon icons.

**Motion**: Tab slides, sticker peels, magnet-letter bounce, reward-token pops, plastic divider snaps, crayon scribble reveals, stamp thumps, toy-bin shuffle, and arcade-token spins. Reduced-motion treatments should preserve static offsets, shadows, and alternate sticker states.

**Spatial**: Compartment trays, sticker binders, supply caddies, playroom grids, reward charts, classroom desks, toy shelves, pencil cases, lunch trays, scrapbook spreads, and block-grid dashboards. The space should be object-clear, not merely cluttered.

**Cultural markers**: 1990s–2000s childhood nostalgia, school supplies, cartoon motifs, stuffed toys, Polly Pocket, Lisa Frank, Tokidoki, Strawberry Shortcake, smiley faces, rainbows, retro flowers, slinkies, stickers, playground objects, prize/reward systems, and rainbowcore adjacency.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**:

- Explicit childhood-memory and play-object vocabulary.
- Tactile toy, classroom, school-supply, sticker, plush, or craft materiality.
- Youthful saturated palette and rounded/cartoon geometry.
- Playroom, supply-caddy, sticker-binder, reward-chart, or toy-shelf organization rather than fashion styling or clown performance.

If those disappear, the result becomes generic colorful maximalism, `decora-kei`, `barbiecore`, or a vague retro-childhood moodboard rather than Kidcore.

## Connotation

**Connotation**: Primary mode is nostalgic quotation with contemporary revival energy. Kidcore should feel affectionate, tactile, and optimistic: a backward glance at childhood supplies, cartoons, toys, stickers, and play infrastructure. It is not inherently ironic, uncanny, or luxury-coded. Fatigue risk appears when implementations reduce it to rainbow colors and smiley faces without the object system that makes the memory specific.

## Related / Subsets

Related aesthetics from the dictionary and candidate set:

- `clowncore`: shares stickers, balloons, toys, and party materials, but clowncore requires makeup/persona/performance grammar.
- `decora-kei`: shares bright toy-like accessories; Kidcore centers playroom and school-supply infrastructure rather than fashion accessory stacking.
- `barbiecore`: shares toy/doll nostalgia but Barbiecore is hot-pink glam and brand-doll polish rather than broad childhood object systems.
- `dreamcore-weirdcore`: shares nostalgic artifacts in some variants, but Kidcore should remain clear, tactile, and optimistic rather than liminal or uncanny.
- `maximalism`: shares density and abundance, but Kidcore needs childhood-object specificity.

Deferred routing terms: `toycore` may become a package/collectible-display subset if future evidence grows; `partycore`, `birthdaycore`, and `ballooncore` are useful celebration motif libraries, not standalone slugs in this batch.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Organize around childhood infrastructure: recess supply caddies, sticker books, reward charts, toy bins, pencil cases, classroom cubbies, lunch notes, and arcade-token panels. Make objects legible and sortable. Decoration should look like stickers, tabs, crayon marks, stamps, paper labels, plastic trays, and magnets rather than a generic pile of charms.

This aesthetic works well for onboarding, empty states, learning products, playful dashboards, badge systems, inventory views, and celebratory progress feedback. Keep account, payment, error, and long-read surfaces simpler so the nostalgia does not undermine task clarity.

## CSS Translation

- Color roles: map primary crayon colors into named tokens for tray backgrounds, sticker accents, active tabs, badges, and support surfaces.
- Surface effects: use paper grain, crayon SVG strokes, sticker shadows, plastic gloss, notebook lines, and subtle glitter behind solid readable areas.
- Shape language: build cards and controls from tabs, rounded bins, sticker outlines, block geometry, stars, hearts, and smiley badges.
- Layout: use a compartment grid, sticker binder, supply tray, or cubby system where each panel has a clear object role.
- Motion: tab slide, sticker peel, stamp pop, token spin, and magnet bounce are optional enhancements; honor `prefers-reduced-motion` with static offsets.

## Typography / Fonts

Choose rounded classroom, toy-package, lunchbox, or bubble-letter display type for headings and labels. Pair with a sturdy rounded sans for legibility. Add marker-like handwritten accents only for short notes, sticker labels, or doodles; avoid making all microcopy childish or hard to read.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Kidcore draws on childhood nostalgia. Keep it affectionate and inclusive rather than infantilizing users or implying that adult users should be treated as children. Avoid using copyrighted characters or brand marks directly; translate the source vocabulary into generic toy, sticker, school-supply, and cartoon-inspired forms.

## Anti-Patterns

- Bright rainbow palette without toys, supplies, stickers, or playroom structure.
- Clown props, greasepaint, ruffs, or theatrical self-performance that turn the design into `clowncore`.
- Accessory piles around a fashion/self-styling panel, which drift toward `decora-kei`.
- Dreamlike blur, empty rooms, or VHS unease, which drift toward `dreamcore-weirdcore`.
- Babyish copy or patronizing UX in serious workflows.
