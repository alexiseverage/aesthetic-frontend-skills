# Tactical Sci-Fi Utility UI

## Scope

Tactical Sci-Fi Utility UI is the game and speculative-military HUD overlay system built from reticles, minimaps, ammo/status modules, compasses, brackets, and translucent data frames. Use it for fictional tactical overlays, game-inspired dashboards, or sci-fi mission metaphors. Do not treat it as real military, medical, automotive, or industrial HMI guidance.

The evidence is limited and text/visual-reference-source based. Motion states are noted from asset descriptions, but timing should be treated cautiously unless inspected directly.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Cyan, teal, amber, green phosphor, white, and red alert accents over dark or transparent grounds. Colors should act as status or targeting codes, not lifestyle branding.

**Type**: Technical monospace, condensed sans, all-caps module headers, numeric telemetry, coordinate strings, ammo counts, timers, player stats, and compact labels. Friendly rounded marketing type weakens the tactical signal.

**Texture**: Screen glow, scanlines, wireframe overlays, translucent panels, grid lines, particle fields, and thin luminous strokes. Texture should enhance targeting/readout feeling without obscuring content.

**Shape**: Center reticles, brackets, circles, crosshairs, hex frames, compass arcs, minimaps, weapon/ammo modules, health bars, objective panels, and angular edge ornaments.

**Motion**: Reticle states, aim start/wide changes, data population, scan sweeps, and module in/out transitions are appropriate recommendations. Provide static reduced-motion states.

**Spatial**: Viewport-framing HUD layout: center reticle, corner modules, edge-anchored panels, compass at top/bottom, status clusters at periphery, and transparent central field.

**Cultural markers**: First-person shooter and sci-fi game UI conventions, tactical telemetry, mission/objective language, weapon/ammo readouts, and fictional military interface vocabulary.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: viewport overlay composition; reticle/targeting geometry; compact telemetry typography; luminous data accents; peripheral module layout. Without these, it becomes generic FUI or high-tech decoration.

## Connotation

This is genre quotation of tactical readiness, threat scanning, and mission control. It can evoke violence or surveillance; use it only where that framing is appropriate and clearly fictional.

## Related / Subsets

`high-performance-hmi` is real operator doctrine and should stay more restrained, grayscale-first, and safety oriented. `fictional-user-interface-diegetic` is broader cinematic screen graphics. `j-gov-futurism` is bureaucratic and institutional rather than tactical.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Use for non-critical overlays, visualization demos, fictional scanning states, game-themed cards, or narrative hero sections. Keep real action buttons, errors, legal text, and settings outside fragile HUD microtext.

## CSS Translation

Use SVG reticles, conic/radial gradients, angular border images, translucent panels, grid backgrounds, thin line icons, glow tokens, and status-color classes. Disable scan sweeps and reticle pulsing for reduced motion.

## Typography / Fonts

Use monospaced or condensed technical faces for numbers and labels, with strong tracking control. Pair with a neutral readable sans for body copy. Avoid tiny all-caps labels as the only explanation.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Avoid glamorizing real targeting, surveillance, or weapons systems. Do not borrow real military insignia, classified-looking markings, or operator UI claims. Keep the aesthetic fictional and transparent.

## Anti-Patterns

- Claiming the UI is suitable for real safety-critical use.
- Filling every corner with illegible telemetry.
- Confusing this with cinematic diegetic FUI or real HMI.
- Making animation necessary to understand state.
