# Classic Film Noir

The classic cinematic vocabulary of selective illumination, tense layered space, morally uncertain narration and deliberate reveals, anchored in 1940s cinema and continuing through the 1950s. Localized light, framing and narrative sequence matter more than a mandatory night street, cigarette, gun or trench coat. Bright domestic rooms and daylight scenes are meaningful variants.

## Scope

Use for serial-fiction readers, cinematic editorial pages, archives and chapter-based experiences. Apply tonal hierarchy, foreground/threshold relationships, title treatment and revelation to the interface itself. A regular card with a monochrome movie still is insufficient. This is separately useful from German Expressionist Horror's painted distortion, Techno-Noir's future/neon technology and contemporary Scandi Noir.

The research is mainly a curatedUS classic-cycle sample, with aUS poster for aBritish film as promotional boundary. It is not a complete global history. Film stills, publicity photography, projection geometry and modern video transfers have different evidentiary limits.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Black, pale highlights and modeled midgrays organize the screen compositions. Local face, hand, lamp and window highlights can carry hierarchy. Not every scene is low-key; bright interiors and daylight recur. Color theatrical posters form a separate promotional vocabulary. No universal historical hex values are established.

**Type**: Actual credits show large serif capitals, italic descriptive lines, centered hierarchies and cast shadows; a poster adds condensed display/credit lettering. Environmental signs are source-specific. Exact fonts are unknown; typewriter text is not established as a defining requirement. Keep body text readable rather than extending a title effect everywhere.

**Texture**: Selective fabric, stone, wood, glass and atmospheric light detail against quieter dark fields. Modern transfers and 600 px still reproductions limit original stock/grain claims. Scratches, blur and fake aging are optional quotations, never a substitute for composition or an excuse to degrade text.

**Shape**: Frames within frames, stairs, doors, lamps, columns, repeated slats and cast silhouettes. Some compositions are oblique/occluded, others stable and frontal. Use directional shadow and bounded shapes purposefully; do not tilt every control or require violence and seduction.

**Motion**: Sampled playback supports moving scenery under held credits, bodily blocking, changing views and a visible title dissolve. It does not establish a universal cut rate or UI easing. Contemporary chapter reveals can be brief and user-triggered, with immediate reduced-motion state changes. Avoid constant flicker, camera shake and compulsory autoplay.

**Spatial**: Strong foreground/background relationships, layered gazes, partial obstruction, isolated value islands and framed pictures/thresholds create tension. Functional reading areas must remain stable, evenly readable and semantically ordered. Mobile can preserve light/dark hierarchy while stacking the sequence.

**Cultural markers**: Classic studio/Poverty Row cinema, crime/fatalism, uncertain narration, domestic pressures, working spaces, glamour and institutional power. Women and racialized subjects are not interchangeable stereotypes. Acknowledge credited creators, particular films and the difference between criticism and universal historical fact.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: cinematic tonal hierarchy, deliberate spatial tension and a coherent narrative/reveal sequence. Type, controls, reading surfaces and progress should participate in that composition. Black-and-white coloring or a noir photo alone does not qualify.

## Connotation

**Mode:** contemporary revival.

Can suggest suspense, withheld identity, intimate narration and a cinematic after-hours atmosphere. Use original stories and assets; do not counterfeit a period film, institutional dossier or authentic historical website.

## Related / Subsets

- `german-expressionist-horror`: expressive shadows overlap, but photographed everyday/social settings and the classic noir cycle differ from distorted horror scenography.
- `techno-noir`: futuristic technology and neon are distinct, optional blend material rather than classic-period requirements.
- `scandi-noir`: regional contemporary bleakness/social-crime atmosphere is a different context.
- Daylight, domestic melodrama, prison/rail/city settings and British noir are documented prose variants here, not new independent subset slugs.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

A serial reader can use a title-credit masthead, chapter accordions, a lit reading surface, dark margins, a film-strip progress rail and a bookmark ticket. Make chapter opening, read status and return-to-bookmark real states. Use explicit action labels, visible focus, stable targets and meaningful focus recovery. A single active chapter avoids uncontrolled page growth; native or correctly implemented disclosure semantics preserve keyboard use. Keep suspense in the story, not in ambiguous navigation or hidden errors.

## CSS Translation

Use a restrained near-black/pale/midgray token system, high-contrast reading planes, thin rules and bounded diagonal or slatted shadow in decorative zones. A display serif with a short dark offset shadow can quote observed credits; simpler serif body text and compact utility lettering serve long reading. Grid can layer title, sequence and reader without making content transforms or overlays unreadable. Clip decorative planes rather than focus rings. Reserve original asset dimensions. Proposed brief opacity/reveal feedback must yield to reduced motion; no ambient flashing loop.

## Typography / Fonts

Fit type category/treatment to inspected credits, not an invented single noir font. System Georgia or a licensed cinematic serif can serve display/body roles, with a compact sans/monospace for utility labels when useful. Verify licensing, coverage and loading cost for additions. Do not assert a source font from a screenshot alone. Avoid all-caps paragraphs, illegible condensed labels and title shadows on body copy.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Treat genre criticism, historical production facts, individual film content and modern design inference separately. Do not flatten women into predatory archetypes or racialized people into threatening silhouettes. Tobacco, weapons and institutional coercion are contextual evidence, not required motifs. Credit artists/cinematographers where verified; preserve uncertain still authorship and transfer provenance. A public-domain label on one film/upload does not clear every restoration, poster or soundtrack for reuse.

## Anti-Patterns

- A generic component decorated with a grayscale film photograph.
- Universal black-night/rain/detective rules that erase bright/domestic variants.
- Future neon substituted for the classic cinematic vocabulary without naming the blend.
- Constant flicker, jitter, autoplay, tiny gray text or shadow across reading copy.
- Typewriter text, fake scratches, guns or gender stereotypes used as the whole style.
- Still-derived timing claims or misleading read/bookmark state.
