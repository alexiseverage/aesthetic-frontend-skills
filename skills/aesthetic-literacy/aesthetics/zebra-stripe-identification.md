# Zebra Stripe Identification

## Scope

The visual language of individual zebra identification from coat markings. A new encounter is placed beside comparable flank views and checked with annotations, patterned features and recorded metadata. The pattern belongs to an individual animal and provides visual evidence for a review decision. [Research profile](../../../knowledge/aesthetics/zebra-stripe-identification.md)

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Keep black, cream and warm photographic neutrals legible. Reserve strong annotation colors for a crop, selected correspondence, or review state. Give colors accompanying numbers or labels.

**Type**: Set encounter identifiers, side, rank and record fields in a compact consistent form. Make the identity field easy to distinguish from the image number.

**Texture**: Preserve fine stripe edges, forks and interruptions. Keep photographic exposure and viewpoint visible. For a teaching aid, mark a drawing or procedural flank crop as illustrated.

**Shape**: Crop to a repeatable body region such as the flank or shoulder. Compare branches, joins, gaps and terminal shapes. Boxes, keyed landmarks and curves indicate where to look.

**Motion**: Keep the inspection pair stable. Replace the candidate or show marks on request. Fast tracking can move detection boxes with the animal when working with field video.

**Spatial**: Place same-side encounter and reference views at a comparable scale with the region unobscured. Set the candidate list and review record beside or below the pair.

**Cultural markers**: Include species, original side, encounter time and a stable identifier when useful. Make the availability of an unmatched or defer decision clear. Share generalized locations where required by field policy.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**:

- Show two comparable and clearly labeled flank or shoulder views.
- Separate the observed stripe features, the proposed individual and the review decision.
- Label original side and record identity even when the view has been normalized.
- Keep rankings distinct from the reviewer’s judgment.

## Connotation

**Connotation**: A conservation record or visual matching workbench. The tone is observational, practical and evidence-led.

## Related / Subsets

Specimen drawers share controlled visual comparison and stable labels. Zebra identification has a narrower operation linking a new in-field observation to a previously recorded animal through the continuity of specific coat features. Contact sheets and species keys use related layouts for different questions.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Offer comparable image crops, a toggleable reference key, a ranked candidate list and separate review actions. Include observable reasons for each comparison. A sample interface can provide illustrated identities and encounter notes.

## CSS Translation

Use a stable grid and matched aspect ratios, object-position or SVG view boxes for crop control, and separate overlay elements for landmarks. Keep focus visible on candidate and review buttons. Let short metadata wrap within either panel.

## Typography / Fonts

A plain readable sans for decisions, tabular or monospaced identifiers, and short uppercase labels with enough contrast at small sizes.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Use fictional encounters or media with explicit reuse rights in teaching interfaces. Support uncertain matches and preserve source metadata. Treat habitat and field locations according to the originating project’s publication policy.

## Anti-Patterns

- Decorative stripes in place of comparable documented marks.
- Using mirrored left and right flanks as direct evidence for one another.
- Presenting rank as a confirmed identity.
- Color-only matched or rejected states.
