# Corporate Grunge / Grunge™

Corporate Grunge is the commercial appropriation of 1990s grunge graphic language: distressed print texture, typewriter or stencil lettering, collage damage, and anti-polish cues held inside a still-readable campaign or retail scaffold. It should feel like underground-flyer roughness repurposed to sell something, not like pure subcultural grunge or clean alternative branding.

## Scope

Use Corporate Grunge for youth-culture campaigns, music/skate/snowboard promotions, limited-run brand activations, alternative retail pages, and editorial surfaces where manufactured edge is part of the message. Avoid it for trust-critical services, healthcare, finance, dense commerce flows, or any product where heavy distress would undermine readability and confidence.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: soot black, paper beige, sepia brown, muddy olive, rust, dirty white, washed khaki, photocopy black-and-white contrast, and desaturated red/orange accents; colors should feel industrial and worn rather than fresh or natural.

**Type**: distressed sans or serif display type, typewriter/monospace cues, stencil or label-maker text, photocopied edges, rough spacing, and deconstructed editorial type inspired by grunge graphics. Keep enough hierarchy for commercial messaging.

**Texture**: grain, scratches, torn paper, ink bleed, halftone dots, dirt, concrete/rust wear, scan noise, crumple, paint splatter, collage seams, duct-tape/sticker traces, and layered print damage.

**Shape**: irregular edges, cropped fragments, pasted blocks, rough rules, stamp boxes, sticker labels, photocopied rectangles, and image panels that visibly overlap rather than pristine geometry.

**Motion**: jitter, flicker, frame-stepped degradation, jump cuts, unstable reveals, parallax grime, scanline drift, opacity flashes, and tape-like transitions; smooth luxury motion breaks the aesthetic.

**Spatial**: dense, occupied, anti-polish composition with asymmetry, diagonals, crowded framing, poster-like stacks, visible layering, collision, and rough hierarchy, but still readable enough to operate as advertising.

**Cultural markers**: 1990s alternative music spillover, youth-culture branding, underground-flyer language, photocopy rebellion, skate/surf/teen retail, MTV-era campaign energy, zines, label makers, VHS/glitch traces, and corporate appropriation of anti-establishment grit.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: muddy industrial palette + heavy photocopy/grain distress + rough mechanical or deconstructed type + collage layering + a recognizably commercial scaffold. Too clean becomes generic alternative branding; too chaotic returns to underground grunge source culture.

## Connotation

Corporate Grunge carries built-in irony: it borrows anti-establishment signals and packages them for brands, campaigns, covers, and youth-market products. The tension is the point. Contemporary use should acknowledge that the look is mediated and commercial rather than pretending it is authentic underground culture.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Treat distress as a controlled layer over a usable campaign page. Keep CTAs, navigation, and product/action text legible, then add torn edges, rough overlays, mechanical type, sticker labels, and scan noise around them. Favor short-lived landing pages, posters, and editorial modules over persistent product interfaces.

## CSS Translation

- Color roles: `--bg-soot`, `--surface-dirty-paper`, `--accent-rust`, `--accent-muddy-olive`, `--text-photocopy`, `--distress-wash`.
- Borders/dividers: torn-paper masks, stamp boxes, photocopied rules, duct-tape strips, rough label frames, and imperfect sticker edges.
- Radius language: mostly hard or ragged edges; use irregular `clip-path` masks rather than polished rounding.
- Effects: grain/noise overlays, halftone dots, scanlines, scratches, ink bleed, crumple, photocopy contrast, and multiply-blended grime.
- Layout: asymmetrical poster stacks, collage overlaps, dense campaign framing, and a visible commercial grid beneath the damage.
- Motion: stepped flicker, jitter, scan drift, jump cuts, degradation reveals, and reduced-motion static distressed states.

## Typography / Fonts

Use typewriter, monospaced, stencil, distressed display, label-maker, OCR, and rough editorial lettering. Avoid smooth geometric sans-serifs as the dominant voice unless they are visibly damaged or used as the hidden corporate scaffold beneath the grunge treatment.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

- **Context:** The style is commercialized subcultural grit. It can flatten anti-establishment signals into sellable “edge.”
- **Use with care:** Preserve the appropriation tension instead of presenting the texture as neutral rebellion.
- **Safer translation:** Pair roughness with explicit campaign context, not claims of authentic underground identity.
- **Avoid:** romanticizing poverty/decay, using distress to obscure required information, or turning grunge culture into an anonymous texture pack.

## Anti-Patterns

- Clean “edgy” branding with a single grunge font and no material distress.
- Pure underground zine chaos with no commercial readability or campaign scaffold.
- Luxury polish, smooth motion, glossy gradients, and pristine cards.
- Distress that damages forms, labels, pricing, safety text, or primary actions.
