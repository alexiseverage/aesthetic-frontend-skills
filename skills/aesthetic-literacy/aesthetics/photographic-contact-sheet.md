# Photographic Contact Sheet

## Scope

A process-derived photographic visual system: repeated image windows retain capture/strip order, small frame/roll identifiers, physical print/film traces, and optional editorial marks. It is a working-artifact vernacular, not a unified art movement. Use for photo selection, media review, photographic portfolios and sequence-based editorial interfaces.

One contact-printed photograph, an instant-photo card stack, a slide-mount grid, or any tiled gallery does not by itself establish this identity. Preserve comparison and indexing when adapting it to software.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Dark photographic fields with monochrome frames are common; color photography and cream-paper proof sheets also occur. Editorial marks can be red/orange, yellow, green or blue. Dark ground and red marks are useful branches, not universal requirements.

**Type**: Small film-edge words, mechanical/stamped frame numerals, typed archival forms and handwritten roll notes. No single display font follows from contact printing. Keep essential text readable rather than reproducing tiny original labels literally.

**Texture**: Photographic paper, perforated strip edges, slight misalignment, waxy pencil lines and occasional wear. Clean proofs remain valid. The modern product mat/drop shadow around a reproduction is not historical evidence.

**Shape**: Rectangular frame windows and horizontal/vertical strips; four-frame medium/large-format sheets and dense 35mm arrays both occur. Optional select boxes, circles, arrows or dots interrupt the regular sequence. Empty film areas may remain visible.

**Motion**: The reference artifacts are still. Captured sequence does not imply animation. Use deliberate selection and frame changes; optional brief color changes are contemporary choices and should disappear under reduced motion. Avoid autonomous film advance.

**Spatial**: Order-preserving comparison with stable frame identity, narrow dark or pale paper gutters, incomplete rows and varied density. Metadata remains subordinate to images; editorial selection forms a separate hierarchy. Mobile reflow must preserve order and labels.

**Cultural markers**: Darkroom editing/reference practice, roll/frame identifiers, proof selection and photographer attribution. This is not synonymous with monochrome nostalgia, paparazzi imagery or a particular photographic subject.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: Repeated photographic windows that support comparison; meaningful sequence/index cues; a clear distinction between image content and editorial metadata/state. Material or marking details should reinforce that working system rather than replace it.

## Connotation

**Mode:** contemporary interpretation or nostalgic quotation of photographic editing. Suggests process, considered choice, iteration and access to the work behind a finished image. Worn edges alone convey generic vintage styling. Keep actual selection states useful and restrained.

## Related / Subsets

- **Cyanotype**: may share contact printing; chemical blue image formation is a different identity from multi-frame editing.
- **Color-Infrared Landscape**: specifies material-dependent false-color image formation, not proof-sheet arrangement.
- **Spirit Photography / Séance**: exposure/ghost subject can use proofs but is unnecessary here.
- Dark/light ground, color/monochrome, and 35mm/medium/large-format are documented variants, not separate canonical slugs.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Build a media review/selection tool, ordered portfolio case study, or editorial comparison surface. Use frame identifiers, compact session header and genuine selection/filter controls. Keep selected marks distinct from keyboard focus; pair color with check/text/pressed state. A selected-only filter should retain original IDs and offer a clear route back to all frames.

Place important text on opaque fields. Review enlargements should preserve full image ratio. Reflow to fewer columns on narrow screens without shrinking labels below readable size. Native buttons/checkboxes and in-flow review panels work well; reserve decorative perforations for noninteractive edges.

## CSS Translation

Proposed values, estimated from screen inspection rather than sampled historical colors: background `#151515`, surface `#222222`, paper/text `#F4F0E5`, muted `#BBB7AD`, amber metadata `#CBB66D`, select `#FF8065`. Use ink on paper or paper on ink for essential text; test actual combinations.

Use 0px radii, flat fields, 1px dividers, 4–16px frame gaps and 24–32px section space. Selection can be a 2–3px irregular SVG stroke beside an explicit label/check; do not rotate semantic text or content. Optional 120ms color transitions are proposed and disabled under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`. Noise should be subtle, static, and noninteractive.

## Typography / Fonts

System monospace suits frame/roll metadata, neutral Arial suits controls, and Georgia is an optional contrasting editorial heading. These are contemporary mappings, not historical font identifications. Keep body text near 16px, metadata at least 12px, and modest label tracking. No additional font payload is necessary.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Credit photographers and respect image rights. Do not republish inspected archive images without permission or mislabel generated scenes as authentic historical capture. Avoid invented artist quotations, locations or branded film metadata. Do not infer the meaning of every pencil color from appearance. Mark fictional demo content honestly outside the artifact's photographic narrative.

## Anti-Patterns

- Random masonry grids with no sequence or frame identity.
- Treating all contact prints, slide arrays and instant photographs as synonyms.
- Mandatory red marks/black paper/grunge imposed on every variant.
- Marks that imply selection while controls have no state.
- Tiny edge-code text as primary navigation, color-only status, hover-only review, or compulsory moving filmstrips.
