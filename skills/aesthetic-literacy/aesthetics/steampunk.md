# Steampunk

Steampunk is application-ready because its signal is distributed across the seven-dimension framework rather than resting on palette alone. The most important implementation guardrail is to preserve the combined palette, material, shape, motion, spatial, and cultural-marker system; if only one cue is present, the result will read as generic speculative technology.

## Scope

Use when the product, interface, or visual artifact benefits from this exact machine/body/worldbuilding language. Avoid when a neighboring aesthetic would communicate the intent with fewer cultural or accessibility risks.

Preserve the whole system across interface scale: the aesthetic should survive in a hero, a card, an icon set, and a form state without relying on one decorative cue. If the product only needs a lighter reference, borrow one or two tokens while naming the result as an influence rather than the full aesthetic.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Warm brass/copper and aged ivory contrasted with dark metal, wood or leather; black machinery and red enamel are legitimate variants. Use metal as a differentiated material rather than an all-over copper tint.

**Type**: Victorian-influenced display serifs, instrument numerals, engraved labels and cabinet nameplates. Separate explanatory text from decorative inscriptions; exact source typefaces vary.

**Texture**: Patinated metal, visible fasteners, pipe joints, wood, glass and exposed mechanisms communicate assembly. Decorative gears should connect to an understandable instrument or construction.

**Shape**: Round gauges, interrupted dial rings, pistons, riveted cases and branching clock frameworks. A complete engineered silhouette matters more than scattered gear icons.

**Motion**: Mechanical relationships may inspire direct valve, needle, hinge or winding controls. Still objects establish no universal ticking speed; any transition timing is proposed. Immediate states are valid, and reduced motion preserves every control.

**Spatial**: Compose around a dominant instrument, with nested case, dial, mechanism and service controls. Use complete workshop or cabinet logic rather than a generic dashboard covered with brass.

**Cultural markers**: Alternate Victorian machinery, inventor/maker culture and speculative industrial craft. Real modern technology, combustion hot rods or generic post-apocalyptic salvage do not become Steampunk merely through a filename or bronze finish.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: alternate industrial-era invention + legible mechanical assembly + differentiated handcrafted materials + an engineered instrument or machine silhouette.

## Connotation

Steampunk carries a specific robotics/speculative-technology connotation; use the source-backed cues above as a system rather than as isolated moodboard decoration.

Because the entry is intended for practical frontend use, connotation should be calibrated to the audience and product domain rather than applied as costume. Favor legible homage, contemporary revival, or clearly bounded genre quotation over accidental parody.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Start with workshops, libraries, airships, laboratories, Victorian streets and automatons, boiler torsos, monocular eyes, pistons, clockwork arms, then layer brass, copper, sepia, dark brown, soot black, aged ivory., Victorian serif, engraved labels, cabinet typography, and gears, patina, turned wood, leather, gauges, boiler soot. Use the aesthetic for heroes, feature cards, empty states, branded dashboards, campaign pages, illustration frames, and high-value CTAs where its cultural markers are relevant. Keep primary navigation, forms, prices, error states, and body copy on stable accessible surfaces.

## CSS Translation

- Color roles: translate brass, copper, sepia, dark brown, soot black, aged ivory into named background, surface, accent, and text tokens; reserve common variants (Adjacent aesthetics can borrow this dimension only if the core non-negotiables remain intact.) for depth or emphasis.
- Surface effects: use gears, patina, turned wood, leather, gauges, boiler soot with CSS gradients, borders, masks, shadows, noise, or SVG filters; keep texture behind solid readable panels.
- Shape language: build cards, badges, dividers, icons, and frames from automatons, boiler torsos, monocular eyes, pistons, clockwork arms rather than neutral rectangles alone.
- Layout: organize pages around workshops, libraries, airships, laboratories, Victorian streets with enough whitespace and hierarchy for product tasks.
- Motion: use tick, wind, hinge, crank, piston, pressure release as optional enhancement with `prefers-reduced-motion` fallbacks.

## Typography / Fonts

Use Victorian serif, engraved labels, cabinet typography for identity moments, headings, numerals, labels, or badges. Pair it with a neutral readable sans or serif for body text and controls. Avoid making all microcopy decorative; the aesthetic reads more strongly when focal typography contrasts with quiet support typography.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

The source-backed cultural markers are Verne/Wells, automatons, inventor culture, steam power. Use them as context signals, not as extractive decoration. When an implementation references living cultures, subcultures, fandoms, gambling, youth media, war machinery, or speculative harm, keep claims specific, fictionalized where appropriate, and avoid presenting stereotypes or protected symbols as generic ornament.

## Anti-Patterns

- Using only the palette while dropping the shape, texture, spatial, and cultural-marker system; that becomes generic moodboard styling rather than Steampunk.
- Overloading every component with motifs so navigation, forms, pricing, or body copy become hard to scan.
- Borrowing adjacent-aesthetic cues so heavily that the entry loses its own source-backed center of gravity.
- Animating glow, grain, flash, or mechanical effects without reduced-motion and solid-surface fallbacks for critical content.
