# Shanghai Calendar Posters

A Shanghai-centered commercial print vocabulary joining modeled illustrative scenes, business lettering, ornamental frames, product emblems and date information. Its 1920s–1930s peak draws on earlier calendar/New Year print traditions and wider coastal circulation. Preserve the relationship between picture and useful information, rather than treating a glamorous portrait as the entire aesthetic.

## Scope

Use for cultural calendars, editorial features, hospitality/event planners and illustrated business pages. Dates, choices, summaries and form controls should share the frame and typographic hierarchy. A regular calendar beside an unrelated vintage image is insufficient.

The research includes a Shanghai/Hong Kong collection category and approximate dates; it does not claim every example was printed in Shanghai or made in the 1930s. Some available poster reproductions have no visible date grid. Do not invent missing date material or equate all Chinese advertising with this scoped tradition.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Soft tonal scenes contrast selectively colored frames and labels. Pale green, blue-gray, pink, warm yellow, red and darker multicolor surrounds recur; tan and sparse variants matter. Source fading/photography affect color. Proposed interface tokens need real contrast checks; no universal historical hex palette is established.

**Type**: Chinese business headings, vertical copy/date rails, Latin logos and product labels organize several information levels. Brush-like and print-like characters coexist with varied Latin lettering. Some period headers read right-to-left. Exact fonts are unknown. Use real, checked language and a clear modern reading order; never fake Chinese glyphs as texture.

**Texture**: Modeled faces, textiles, flowers, glass and interiors contrast repeat-pattern borders and dense date marks. FAAM identifies offset prints; individual V&A works are lithographs; HKU shows a modern photographic reproduction. Do not assign one process by appearance alone or require aged paper, blur and distress.

**Shape**: Tall sheets, narrow banners, rectangles, oval portraits, curved business banners, cartouches, seals and grouped product emblems. Elaborate floral/key-pattern frames and restrained thin rules both occur. A particular dress, body, pose or commercial product is optional.

**Motion**: The counted evidence is still print/reproduction. Gestures and leisure scenes do not establish historical animation. Intentional stillness and immediate manual selection suit the informational hierarchy. Any contemporary transition is proposed and needs a reduced-motion equivalent.

**Spatial**: A narrative/portrait field relates to an outer business/date/product structure. Narrow side calendars, lower grids, small scene insets, image-plus-label groupings and generous empty fields are variants. Preserve usable semantic order and clear input surfaces when stacking on mobile.

**Cultural markers**: Shanghai-centered commerce, local/foreign businesses, changing fashion, staged modern lifestyles, traditional narratives and family/leisure scenes. Advertisements express aspirations and commercial framing rather than a neutral record of women's lives. Wider Hong Kong/coastal circulation and individual artist practices deserve explicit context.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: a defensible commercial-calendar reference; a modeled illustrative/narrative counterpart; composed business/information hierarchy; and a deliberate relationship among picture, frame, emblems and date/product modules. A portrait, red border or generic “Asian” ornament alone is insufficient.

## Connotation

**Mode:** contemporary revival.

Can suggest an illustrated commercial calendar, considered hospitality, seasonal leisure and early urban consumer culture. A new planner should use original content and communicate its actual function, without counterfeiting a historic business or pretending to recreate an authentic period website.

## Related / Subsets

- `art-deco` overlaps in geometric framing and modernity; soft narrative illustration and local commercial history extend beyond it.
- `guochao` has contemporary heritage/national-style revival contexts; it is not a Republican-era advertising alias.
- `chinoiserie` is a different, externally constructed decorative history; these Chinese commercial networks require their own account.
- `pochoir-fashion-plates` shares illustrated fashion and caption hierarchy, with different production, page conventions and cultural lineage.
- `taisho-roman` is a comparison for regional modern/traditional mixtures, not a Japanese substitute identity.
- Sparse hanging sheets, ornate oval portraits, narrative tableaux and dense date-frame advertisements remain prose variants here.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Make a calendar or planner the information architecture: branded heading, original illustrated scene, bordered date controls, clearly labeled times, form fields and a framed summary. Let active/closed dates, selected sessions and confirmation belong to the same line weights and cartouche rhythm. Use explicit labels, native semantics, visible focus, readable date numbers and generous targets. Preserve the user's choices when editing. An export should create a real, valid artifact rather than pretend to submit a reservation.

## CSS Translation

Use grid for scene/date/summary relationships, bounded ornament and solid reading surfaces. Fine paired rules, repeated medallion-like marks, rectangular date rails and restrained curved plaques can carry the style into controls. Proposed cream, deep green, vermilion and muted gold roles should separate text from decoration. Keep calendar text in HTML, avoid image-only labels, reserve artwork dimensions and stack the planner in reading order on mobile. Do not invent lunar dates, historical breakpoints or a universal border pattern. Disable nonessential motion under reduced motion.

## Typography / Fonts

A legible serif or appropriate CJK serif can complement business headings, with clear numerals and simpler field labels. Verify actual character coverage/licensing if adding fonts. Use native Chinese text only when its meaning and reading order are known; English-only functional controls can be appropriate in a bilingual revival. Brush-like headings are not a license for pseudo-script. Exact source fonts remain unidentified.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Distinguish artist/studio attribution, print medium and photographic reproduction. Preserve uncertain dates and geographic provenance. Avoid turning Chinese culture into interchangeable ornament, copying a protected poster, exoticizing bodies or treating colonial/commercial aspirations as everyone's lived experience. Historical tobacco, alcohol and pharmaceutical advertisements are evidence, not contemporary endorsement or reliable health claims. Research access is not commercial image-reuse permission.

## Anti-Patterns

- An ordinary component with a period portrait inserted beside it.
- Generic red/gold luxury, pseudo-Chinese glyphs or compulsory dragons/qipao.
- Inferring every object's year/location from collection-level labels or costume.
- Mandatory paper damage, unreadable tiny date grids or decorative form labels.
- Invented lunar conversions, fake reservation confirmation or unsupported historical motion.
