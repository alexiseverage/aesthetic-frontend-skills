# CD-ROM Edutainment Worlds

## Scope

A descriptive branch of 1990s multimedia learning interfaces: illustrated or rendered places, collections and objects organize exploration, supported by persistent navigation and readable detail views. Museum rooms, cabinets, maps, workshops, storybooks, notebooks and learning apparatus are useful branches. The term does not claim a single historical movement or make every educational CD-ROM an example.

Preserve the relationship between subject, place and action. Objects and materials must shape information hierarchy, navigation or tasks. A standard page with a themed illustration alone does not establish this aesthetic. A quiet white reading page can belong inside a more expressive world.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Subject-related color: habitat greens, underwater blues, warm wood/brass, parchment, colorful outlined stories, muted rooms or bright white museums. Children's branches often use clear saturated accents; subdued references and monochrome reading are important variants. Keep text/control contrast independent of texture.

**Type**: Expressive serif, hand-drawn or rendered title; stable serif/sans prose and short labelled controls. Different subjects can have different title treatments while navigation remains consistent. Original fonts are mostly unverified. Use readable contemporary sizes rather than copying tiny period labels.

**Texture**: Painted/outlined illustration, photographic cutouts, early rendered rooms, hatched diagrams, wood, paper, stone, metal, cloth and physical tools. Carry selected materials through the working controls, not just the background. Plain reading surfaces offer necessary contrast. Scan wear and package photography are not interface requirements.

**Shape**: Named object selectors, picture-and-label tabs, plaques, arrow signs, framed paper pages, display cases, maps, cutaways and task apparatus. The form relates to the lesson: a cabinet sorts specimens, a lever changes a variable, a notebook holds discoveries. Avoid unidentified decorative hotspots.

**Motion**: Three sampled programs demonstrate animated introductions, local story/scene changes and spatial library selection. Exact timing/easing and sound were not established. Favor brief user-triggered feedback with stable labels and immediate navigation. Do not force autoplay or simulate slow hardware. Reduced motion removes nonessential transforms/transitions while preserving every task/state.

**Spatial**: Bounded hub or collection with persistent Home/Index/Help; focused articles, diagrams, questions or creative tools open from it. Objects may overlap, but essential text has a clear reading zone. Original fixed desktop screens do not establish responsive rules: move choices into a legible flow on mobile rather than scaling an entire scene down.

**Cultural markers**: Multimedia learning/publishing, museum/collection discovery, storybook exploration, workshop experimentation and the pleasure of finding connected facts. Western commercial software dominates this research sample; this is not a global census. Cultural objects belong to particular authored subjects and histories.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: a meaningful place/collection metaphor; objects or material controls that organize real learning or making; consistent wayfinding and clear readable detail content; a coherent relationship between scene, labels and actions. Bright cartoons, a mascot, pseudo-3D and sound are optional.

## Connotation

**Mode:** nostalgic quotation with contemporary usability.

Convey curiosity, patient discovery and small productive experiments. A cabinet can feel intimate; a museum can feel spacious; a storybook can feel warm. Treat period limitations as historical context rather than desirable friction. Do not presume every audience remembers these programs or shared one childhood.

## Related / Subsets

- **Skeuomorphism:** shared physical affordances; this branch organizes connected learning into places/collections rather than material imitation alone.
- **DVD Menu:** shared disc-era context; pointer-led exploration, questions and creative tools differ from remote-control/safe-area/video-loop navigation.
- **Early Internet:** adjacent era, but offline software screens do not establish browser behavior.
- **Fictional User Interface — Diegetic:** world-integrated controls overlap; readable educational reference and productive exploration distinguish this branch.
- **8-bit Pixel:** limited displays can overlap, but deliberately pixel-built geometry is not compulsory.
- Museum/cabinet, illustrated storybook, workshop/apparatus and notebook branches remain internal variations, not separate canonical slugs.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Build a small working learning cabinet. Labelled object selectors open substantive lessons; persistent home/index/help provide redundant routes. A lesson can contain an annotated diagram, question/check/retry and notebook stamp earned only by the real correct state. An index lists actual available content and current progress. Preserve answers and stamps across navigation within the session, with an explicit reset if offered.

Apply the grammar to heading plaques, tab shapes, answer choices, focus states, feedback slips and navigation. Use semantic buttons, inputs and reading order. Modal help needs a title, Escape, focus containment and return to its opener. Do not require sound, mouse hover, color recognition or a hidden hotspot. Make touch controls at least 44px; narrow layouts rearrange the world rather than shrink it.

## CSS Translation

Proposed application roles: ink `#29261F`, paper `#FFF9DF`, dark wood `#59402C`, brass `#DFC17B`, forest `#264B40`, blue `#294B76`. These are authored values, not sampled historic pixels. Use layered light/dark borders for restrained bevels, small inset shadows, bounded grain, squared paper pages and gently irregular object silhouettes. Keep the reading plane quiet.

Proposed sizes: 16px/1.55 body, 13–14px labels, 28–48px short title, 8/12/20/32px spacing, 44px controls. An optional 180ms small object lift or 240ms reveal is a design proposal; remove it under reduced motion. Avoid continuous scenic motion, fake loading and flashing. Calculate actual foreground/background contrast and test textured states.

## Typography / Fonts

Georgia/system serif works for book-like reading and display; system sans supplies clear short tools. Irregular original SVG lettering may decorate a short mark while the real title remains accessible text. Font categories are proposals, not identified originals. No single “CD-ROM font” is supported.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Link historical screenshots/recordings for analysis; access does not grant asset or character reuse. Create original marks, illustrations and lesson prose. Do not copy branded characters, logos or distinctive scene arrangements. Avoid extracting Indigenous objects from 500 Nations, ancient-culture material from Ancient Lands, Jamaican identity from Orly or monastery motifs from Monks into a generic exotic-world kit. Source current lesson facts independently; period medical/science/history text may be outdated or incomplete. Represent credits and unresolved version dates honestly.

## Anti-Patterns

- Ordinary components with one themed picture as their only aesthetic signal.
- Calling every disc, encyclopedia window or 1990s educational game this aesthetic.
- Tiny fixed-scene controls scaled down to a phone.
- Hidden hotspots, mandatory narration, hover-only choices or color-only answers.
- Copied characters, logos or culturally specific objects used as random ornament.
- Fake loading, unwanted scene animation, arbitrary glitch or unsupported timing claims.
- Empty “learning” panels, predetermined progress or a stamp awarded without doing the task.
