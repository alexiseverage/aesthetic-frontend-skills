# Flash Portal Vernacular

## Scope

Flash Portal Vernacular names the visual and interaction grammar around browser game/animation portals and the authored stages they published, especially during the 2000s. It is a bounded descriptive entry, not a claim that every Flash website looked like Newgrounds. Flash also powered unrelated corporate, experimental and luxury sites. The relevant relationship here is community chrome around a work: categories, ranking, rating, author, sponsor, review and a clear play stage.

Period Newgrounds combines compact information architecture with branded industrial/metallic framing. Miniclip uses friendlier blue rounded modules; GameHouse shows a colorful neighboring Java/Shockwave portal. Current continuations retain some portal structures but modern skins are dated honestly. Independent game menus vary from lime cartoon bubbles to silver selector rings, gold consoles, isometric plates, narrow techno type, stars and plain white geometric interfaces. That variation matters: a black/orange recolor alone is not a source-backed account of Flash culture, and a game screenshot inside a generic card does not translate its interface.

## 7-Dimension Profile


**Palette**: Black/charcoal framing, amber/orange highlights and gray chrome in Newgrounds; blue/navy in Miniclip, red/yellow in GameHouse, purple in Nitrome; authored stages vary radically. High for specific works, moderate as a cross-portal generalization; no universal orange palette.


**Type**: Dense compact sans navigation, bold condensed headings, beveled/extruded wordmarks, pixel and angular techno displays coexist with spare thin geometric and handwritten stage type. Directly visible; exact proprietary font families not verified.


**Texture**: Beveled metal, inset wells, gradients, outlined vector art, pixels and atmospheric textures; some stages deliberately plain. High varied source support, not every effect required at once.


**Shape**: Chamfered tabs, framed stage, narrow rank rows, badges, glossy pills/orbs, irregular illustrated buttons and dimensional slabs. High across actual menus and portal controls.


**Motion**: User-triggered start, loading-to-menu, title sequences, mode selection and sliding/unfolding help/forms observed in independent authored works. High for state relationships, no exact historical timing measurements; failed controls explicit.


**Spatial**: Dense category/rating/creator shell surrounds a comparatively bounded game/animation stage; small utility controls contrast with oversized in-stage actions. Strong portal-to-stage relationship; modern responsive behavior is application inference.


**Cultural markers**: Creator uploads, player judging, awards, sponsor/host credits, remix/parody, chat/forums and browser-play distribution. Primary histories establish publishing context; Flash is a technology and does not imply a single artistic genre.

Use `color: var(--text)` on `background: var(--surface)` for reading areas, and `var(--action-ink)` on `var(--primary)` for filled actions. These solid pairs calculate to **6.92:1** and **11.84:1** respectively. This checks these pairs only, not whole-interface accessibility. Accent/secondary surfaces are decorative until their actual composited text, focus and state contrast is checked. The surround may differ from the reading plane; do not place ordinary text directly on a low-contrast patterned background.

Proposed spacing scale: 4px, 8px, 12px, 16px, 24px, 32px; control/panel/focal radii: 0px, 4px, 12px, modified by the stated custom geometry. Use 1–2px structural rules and a clear 3px focus outline with offset. Actual user-triggered original stage transitions inform the relationship, not exact timing. A proposed short abstract animation is pausable/restartable; under reduced motion default to a still selectable frame and equivalent status. No fake network loader. Any optional motion duration/easing is an implementation choice; honor `prefers-reduced-motion` and retain equivalent selected/loading/success information.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Flash is a technology, not one visual genre. Distinguish original game art, portal chrome and modern Ruffle wrappers; failed source controls do not establish working gameplay. Archive hosting does not license games, characters or sound. A fictional local portal should not impersonate actual creators, uploads, player counts or a live review service.

Include: creator credits; ranked submissions; framed playable stage; vote/review state. Avoid: copied tank/game characters; fake plug-in installer; abusive period comments; autoplay sound. Source publication is not a reuse license. Preserve maker/artist/publisher distinctions and use original assets. Research may inspect people or clothing; the showcase translates their material/form relationships without portraits or generated fictional people.

Flags: the artifact uses its documented local palette inside the surrounding site; no separate automatic dark variant is claimed. Reduced-motion operation and final accessibility are implementation responsibilities. Do not infer trend popularity or fatigue from this corpus; no unsupported current trend ranking is supplied.
