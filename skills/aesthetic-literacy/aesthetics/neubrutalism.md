# Neubrutalism

Neubrutalism is a contemporary web/UI style that reacts against polished sameness with bold type, bright flat color, thick borders, hard shadows, exposed component boundaries, and deliberate visual friction. It is a branded interface grammar of anti-polish, not a license to make hierarchy chaotic.

## Scope

Use Neubrutalism for marketing pages, creator tools, portfolio systems, design products, editorial commerce, and calls to action that need confidence, play, or confrontation. It works best when rawness clarifies the interaction model: obvious buttons, visible cards, blunt headings, and strong boundaries.

The style borrows the name and attitude of brutalism, but its practical center is web-native component language. Treat architectural Brutalism as lineage/metaphor, not as a requirement to mimic concrete buildings.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Bright high-contrast accents framed by black, white, or grayscale structure: yellows, reds, blues, greens, and other primary-like fills. Muted tasteful palettes reduce the rebellious signal.

**Type**: Oversized bold sans headings, unconventional grotesks used as graphic material, compact supporting sans text, tabular labels, and loud button copy. Elegant editorial serif systems usually pull the design away from neubrutalism.

**Texture**: Raw component edges, exposed dividers, flat solid fills, thick outlines, hard unblurred shadows, visible grid seams, optional rough or noisy backgrounds, and low-definition grit kept away from dense text.

**Shape**: Thick-outlined boxes, hard-corner cards, simple blocks, assertive buttons, occasional exaggerated rounding subordinate to border weight, and 45-degree solid shadow offsets.

**Motion**: Snap transitions, emphatic state changes, obvious hover offset, press feedback, short directional shifts, and simple reveal moves. Avoid floaty ambient motion that softens the style's edge.

**Spatial**: Asymmetry, visible structure, compressed rhythm, stacked blocks with strong boundaries, hero zones that break grid expectations, loud promo cards, and limited polite whitespace.

**Cultural markers**: Anti-polish stance, web-native rebellion against cookie-cutter SaaS, thick borders, hard drop-shadow cards, bright block-color buttons, rough individuality, and attention-demanding calls to action.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**:

- Thick visible borders or exposed component boundaries.
- Hard, unblurred shadow or offset feedback language.
- Bold type and high-contrast color used as structure, not decoration alone.
- Intentional rawness that still preserves hierarchy and usability.

## Connotation

**Mode:** contemporary revival.

Neubrutalism reads as loud, direct, playful, rebellious, and internet-native. It signals confidence and individuality, but overuse can feel shouty or careless if every element demands equal attention.

## Related / Subsets

- `brutalism` provides naming lineage and anti-polish attitude, but digital Neubrutalism is brighter, more playful, and more component-forward.
- `early-internet` can share visible construction and anti-slickness, but Early Internet is amateur and publishing-centered while Neubrutalism is deliberate and branded.
- `glassmorphism` is a near-opposite surface logic: thick opaque boundaries and hard shadows versus transparency and blur.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Use the style to make interaction obvious: buttons can jump with offset shadows, cards can advertise their boundaries, and headings can become structural blocks. Maintain a hierarchy of loudness so primary actions, navigation, body copy, and decorative callouts do not all compete at the same volume.

Reserve rough texture for backgrounds, dividers, or hero accents; do not place long body copy on noisy fields without a stable reading plane.

## CSS Translation

- Color roles: `--ink`, `--paper`, `--accent-yellow`, `--accent-blue`, `--accent-red`, `--accent-green`, and `--shadow-hard`.
- Borders/dividers: 3–4px solid outlines, heavy rules, visible card seams, and high-contrast focus rings.
- Radius language: mostly square or simple block corners; occasional exaggerated radius only when border weight remains dominant.
- Effects: unblurred `box-shadow` offsets, pressed-state transforms, flat fills, rough texture overlays, and no glass blur.
- Layout: asymmetrical hero blocks, stacked outlined cards, dense boundary-led grids, and loud CTA zones.
- Motion: snap hover/press offsets, short transitions, and reduced-motion-safe state changes.

## Typography / Fonts

Use bold grotesks, heavy sans-serif display faces, or system sans type at assertive weights. Pair with plain readable body text; do not use elegance, delicacy, or high-contrast serif refinement as the dominant voice.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

The style's deliberate friction can create real accessibility problems when visual noise, color contrast, and hierarchy are not controlled. High contrast is necessary but not sufficient: users still need predictable focus states, readable copy, and a clear action order.

## Anti-Patterns

- Making every card, button, heading, and badge equally loud.
- Confusing “ugly” with “usable” or “raw” with “unstructured.”
- Rough textures behind dense copy or critical controls.
- Soft blur, glass panels, delicate hairlines, or luxury polish as the main surface.
- Importing the name without thick borders, hard shadows, or visible component rawness.
