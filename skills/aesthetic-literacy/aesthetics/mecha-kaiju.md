# Mecha / Kaiju

Japanese giant-machine and giant-creature genre lineages express extraordinary mass through a world that makes scale visible. Mecha emphasizes constructed mechanisms; kaiju emphasizes giant strange creatures. Mechanical monsters connect them without making the terms interchangeable.

## Scope

Use for a deliberate genre quotation built around imposing mass, articulated construction or organic enormity, and a legible relation to cities, terrain or infrastructure. Godzilla (1954) and Mobile Suit Gundam (1979) anchor different histories; a steel-colored dashboard alone does not establish either. The combined entry includes intersections and visual comparison rather than claiming one uniform franchise style.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Dark industrial greys and warning colors are common, but official examples also use white/blue/red/yellow armor, cyan skies, organic browns, black-and-white photography and restrained red titles. Select a coherent branch; gunmetal and neon are not required.

**Type**: Large cinematic titles, vertical or diagonal emphasis, contrasting small credits, technical markings and numerals occur in different branches. An English heavy display face plus clear labels is a practical quotation. Exact fonts remain source-specific; serif and illustrated titles also occur.

**Texture**: Hard armor planes, seams, vents, scuffs and cylindrical mechanisms contrast with rough scales, membranes and organic surfaces. Mechanical and creature materials should remain distinguishable. Damage and soot are options, not requirements.

**Shape**: Heavy silhouettes, strong foreshortening, deep negative spaces, faceted plates and large appendage masses make weight legible. Preserve a clear large form before adding tiny mechanical ornament.

**Motion**: Played contemporary references show thrust, traversal, recoil, impact and debris. Weight depends on scale and staging, not constant shaking. Exact interface timing is a proposal; deliberate stillness suits a poster, scale drawing or inspection view.

**Spatial**: Cities, aircraft, hangars and terrain provide comparison. Tiny known objects beside a dominant form are often more effective than an unsupported large number. Bright constructed cities are as valid as ruins.

**Cultural markers**: Tokusatsu film, manga/anime, model and game traditions carry specific Japanese histories. Distinguish nuclear-anxiety, military-industrial, heroic and playful contexts. Do not treat all Japanese visual culture as robot warfare.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**:

- Make extraordinary mass and its relation to a world legible.
- Distinguish constructed mechanisms from organic creature material when both appear.
- Use an intentional genre/worldbuilding relationship rather than generic sci-fi telemetry.
- Preserve readable task content despite dramatic scale, material and framing.

## Connotation

For contemporary revival or nostalgic quotation, state the intended branch: imposing industrial mass, giant-creature catastrophe, heroic construction or playful spectacle. Avoid pretending fictional threat numbers are real operational data. The nuclear history of early Godzilla deserves context when catastrophe is central.

## Related / Subsets

`anime-mecha-realism` concentrates on believable mechanisms and maintenance; this entry gives greater weight to extraordinary scale and spectacle. `chibi-mecha` changes the proportions toward compact toy-like appeal. `cyberpunk` concerns urban technology and social relations, which neither giant machinery nor kaiju automatically implies. No canonical subsets are defined.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Begin with one dominant original form and a credible comparison object or ground datum. Use panel faces, joints, vents or contrasting organic texture selectively. Forms and numerical readouts need stable solid surfaces. If a scale comparison is interactive, change the actual geometry and derived values together; identify hypothetical dimensions. Keep states visible through text and shape as well as color. An export should preserve the displayed configuration.

## Typography / Fonts

Choose categories by role, not a fictional universal mecha font: heavy grotesk for mass, readable sans for controls and tabular numerals for comparison. Local Arial Black/Arial fallbacks are workable proposed choices. Official examples vary considerably; use Japanese writing only when meaningful, accurate and supported by the content.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Keep Godzilla's Japanese postwar/nuclear context distinct from later franchise spectacle and Gundam's separate anime lineage. Protected characters, logos, film posters and game footage are research evidence, not free production assets. Use original nonhumanoid construction, materials, geometry and typography when people/character imagery is outside scope. Label fictional engineering comparisons honestly.

## Anti-Patterns

- Generic red/black HUD with invented missiles, danger percentages and fake authorization.
- Calling every giant robot a kaiju or assuming all kaiju are mechanisms.
- Mandatory darkness, military fonts, warning stripes or destruction despite bright counterexamples.
- Tiny serial text carrying essential values, unreadable armor texture or continuous quake effects.
- Borrowed recognizable robots/monsters, decorative pseudo-Japanese, or nuclear catastrophe as context-free ornament.
