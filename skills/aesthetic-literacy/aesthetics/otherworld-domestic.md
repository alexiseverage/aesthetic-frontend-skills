# Otherworld Domestic

Otherworld Domestic translates the uncanny doubled home of *Coraline* into a usable design language: recognizable domestic things, visibly handmade materials, exaggerated proportions, and an inviting threshold into a familiar-but-wrong counterpart. The name is an editorial synthesis for this catalog, not a historical movement or an established name used by the book's or film's creators.

## Scope

Use for imaginative homewares, craft studios, literary editorial, exhibition guides, narrative onboarding, and small collections where a room or an object can carry the story. Preserve an ordinary domestic reference and an unsettling alteration: a chair with improbable legs, a reassuring room with excessive depth, a button that becomes an ominous detail, or a familiar arrangement repeated with different materials and light.

The book supplies the alternative home and its coercive hospitality. The film contributes a particular collaborative visual interpretation through miniature sets, costume construction, drawing-like contours, and spatial staging. Later book illustrations are another interpretation, not evidence that the original novel prescribed the film's palette. Keep these branches distinct.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Contrast a restrained everyday field—gray-blue, faded cream, dusty pink, olive or brown—with concentrated otherworld color: violet, cobalt, coral, turquoise, yellow, or orange. Warm light can make the strange room attractive before it becomes threatening. Monochrome ink drawings and pale interiors are important variants; a purple background alone does not establish the aesthetic. Specific hex values are contemporary application choices, not sampled historical standards.

**Type**: Treat display lettering as one crafted focal element: a slightly eccentric book serif, irregular terminals, a lean italic, or a theatrical title. Pair it with clear restrained labels and readable prose. Source art supports narrative hierarchy and occasional expressive lettering, not one mandatory font. Keep buttons, options, form labels and prices stable and legible; avoid crooked body text and perpetual letter jitter.

**Texture**: Material-specific tactility: painted wood, fabric seams, knit stitches, glazed ceramics, patterned wallpaper, worn floorboards, ink hatching and broken drawing edges. Make surfaces explain what an object is made from. Uniform distressed noise, shiny plastic everywhere, or a paper filter over otherwise generic UI loses the physical construction.

**Shape**: Slightly bowed frames, tall doors, nested apertures, thin tapering furniture legs, four-hole buttons, narrow keys, scalloped household objects and organic handles. Familiar functions should remain recognizable beneath the distortion. A single peculiar silhouette is stronger than covering every component with spikes, eyes and teeth.

**Motion**: Purposeful physical action and staged revelation. Let a chosen view expose a different room, a drawer open, or a selected object become easier to inspect. Stop-motion is not synonymous with a crude low-frame-rate effect: LAIKA's production explanation distinguishes animation on ones from twos. Web durations and easing are proposed translations, not recovered film timings. Provide immediate equivalents under reduced motion and keep essential controls still.

**Spatial**: Rooms, thresholds and layered depth organize the composition. Use foreground curtains or framing, an object plane, and a distant opening; contrast a constrained arrangement with a more abundant counterpart. Reserve clean negative space for text and a predictable content grid for tasks. Meaningful repetition of the same room or object is more distinctive than an unrelated fantasy collage.

**Cultural markers**: Domestic doubling, keys and small doors, sewing and clothing construction, miniature scale, tea tables, cupboards, wallpaper, exaggerated hospitality and the uncertainty of a home that seems too accommodating. The underlying works include fear, captivity and courage; treat these as narrative context, not instructions for manipulative interface behavior.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**:

- A recognizable domestic world and a deliberate uncanny alteration or double.
- Visible material/craft identity, rather than mood color alone.
- Spatial framing, scale distortion or nested depth that makes the familiar strange.
- Stable, understandable task controls beneath the theatrical presentation.

## Connotation

**Mode:** contemporary interpretive quotation.

The tone is curious, intimate, seductive and uneasy. A usable branch may remain quietly strange; it need not reproduce the story's strongest horror. The literary and film lineage is documented, while this cross-media frontend category is newly authored. Do not claim widespread community consensus or an independent historical era for the catalog name.

## Related / Subsets

- `storybook-gothic` organizes contemporary folklore branding through emblems, heritage lettering and mythic anchors. Otherworld Domestic organizes a tangible household and its uncanny counterpart.
- `whimsigothic` shares eccentric furnishings and theatrical wit. Domestic doubling, constructed miniature materiality and threshold-based unease distinguish this entry from a decorative salon.
- `storybook-spooky-cute` balances mild suspense with a reassuring child-facing resolution. Otherworld Domestic can carry more sustained ambiguity and should not automatically be presented as suitable for young children.
- `dreamcore-weirdcore` shares familiar-but-wrong spaces but often uses degraded digital imagery and context collapse. This entry foregrounds coherent physical rooms and crafted objects.
- `folk-horror-pastoral` locates unease in land, custom and communal authority. This entry centers the intimate house and its counterfeit welcome.

No independent subsets are defined. Ink illustration, muted everyday rooms and vivid otherworld rooms are useful branches within the same system.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Build a recognizable product surface: an editorial shopfront, object gallery, reading view, materials accordion or invitation form. A reversible atmosphere switch can show the same scene in two treatments while preserving prices, labels, navigation, selected variants and focus. Keep the return route explicit.

Product cards should pair original object imagery with plain names, prices, options and visible add/remove feedback. Put material and care detail in native disclosures. Use layered imagery around a readable hero, not behind dense text. A journal preview can supply narrative pacing; a form should remain an ordinary labeled form with honest validation and submission states.

Do not turn hospitality into a dark pattern: no disguised consent, hidden costs, moving checkout controls, forced exploration, surprise audio or fake escape mechanics. Narrative uncertainty belongs in the illustration and story.

## CSS Translation

- Suggested application roles: `--ink: #172136`, `--paper: #F3E9D2`, `--wall: #1C233D`, `--muted: #655D6B`, `--lilac: #D5BFDC`, `--thread: #E5A18F`. These are proposed UI values, not extracted source colors.
- Use a rectangular functional grid beneath one irregular framed image. A low-radius control system can coexist with an arched or off-axis decorative frame.
- Express depth through separate foreground/frame/object/background planes and restrained offset shadows. Keep perspective transforms away from text and hit targets.
- Use small repeating wallpaper marks, actual seam imagery, a dashed inset stitch or rough silhouette selectively. Do not apply full-screen noise to form copy.
- Suggested interaction values: 160ms color feedback and a 420ms user-triggered atmosphere change. Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, remove transitions and preserve all state changes immediately.
- At narrow widths, stack the scene and content, show complete object silhouettes, keep clear labels, and let prose expand. Avoid shrinking a desktop dollhouse into an unreadable miniature interface.

## Typography / Fonts

A readable book serif such as Georgia with an italic display treatment is a practical system-font translation; an appropriately licensed irregular display serif is another option. Pair with a plain sans for navigation, prices, options and form labels. These are application suggestions, not identifications of the film's title font or of the book's production typefaces.

Use sentence-case headlines with controlled line breaks, modest tracked captions, and comfortable prose leading. Reserve visual eccentricity for one focal title. Preserve selectable HTML text and real semantic headings.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Credit the literary source, Henry Selick and LAIKA's collaborative film production, and the specific artists whose work supports an observation. Dave McKean and Chris Riddell illustrated different book editions; Tadahiro Uesugi explicitly described developing film designs from reading the book rather than copying its illustrations. Do not merge these contributions into a single artist attribution.

Create original room and object artwork for application examples. Reference film stills, characters, logos and book illustrations analytically; their availability online does not establish reuse permission. Decorative buttons, keys and sewing materials should be independently designed rather than replicas of recognizable franchise assets.

## Anti-Patterns

- A purple theme with button stickers and no doubled domestic structure.
- A cozy craft shop with no uncanny proportion, threshold or altered counterpart.
- Generic haunted-house/Halloween decoration, gore, jump scares or automatically moving shadows.
- Calling the film a Tim Burton production or treating every contributor's design as one artist's style.
- Artificial 12fps stutter applied to every interaction because the source is stop-motion.
- Low-contrast labels, text on busy wallpaper, deliberately confusing navigation or deceptive consent.
