# Memphis / Post-Modern

Memphis / Post-Modern is the loud, anti-good-taste design language of the Memphis Group: postmodern furniture, laminate surfaces, clashing geometry, squiggles, dots, and pattern collision used as a critique of modernist seriousness. It is object-led and design-theory-driven, not the same thing as late-2010s Corporate Memphis illustration.

## Scope

Use Memphis for campaign pages, playful product launches, design-history features, museum/exhibition microsites, youth-oriented merchandising, and object-like brand systems that can support theatrical irreverence. Avoid it for serious civic services, healthcare/finance flows, dense dashboards, restrained luxury, or contexts where clashing pattern would undermine comprehension.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Pastel grounds with bright primaries, teal, pink, lemon yellow, cobalt, black-and-white pattern, and deliberately non-harmonious contrast. Color should feel synthetic, laminate-like, and intentionally “wrong,” not tastefully balanced.

**Type**: Bold slab serif, decorative sans, playful oversized labels, or postmodern display type used as graphic object. Neutral corporate sans systems can support body copy but should not drain the theatrical quality.

**Texture**: Laminate, terrazzo-like speckle, squiggle patterns, dots, zigzags, checkerboards, zebra stripes, and synthetic furniture-surface cues. Avoid grunge distress or photoreal luxury materiality.

**Shape**: Triangles, circles, squiggles, oblique cuts, stacked blocks, totemic furniture silhouettes, checkerboards, toy-like object forms, and geometric interruptions. Organic hand-drawn curves without geometric tension are off-model.

**Motion**: Playful but stiff: sliding pattern panels, object-piece assembly, confetti-like entrance accents, and stagey reveals. Ultra-smooth luxury motion makes it feel too tasteful.

**Spatial**: Object-on-stage composition, pattern fields, segmented planes, modular blocks, and room-like graphic collage. Whitespace can exist, but not as quiet minimalist calm.

**Cultural markers**: Ettore Sottsass, Memphis Milano, Italian postmodern design, anti-functional laminate furniture, bold squiggles, Masanori Umeda’s Ginza Robot cabinet/object branch, and 1980s design theory rebellion.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: clashing pastel/primary palette; abstract geometric pattern mixing; squiggle/dot/zigzag surface language; postmodern object-stage composition; and anti-harmonious humor. Flat startup blob people alone are Corporate Memphis, not Memphis.

## Connotation

Memphis connotes postmodern play, graphic irreverence, anti-functional wit, and youthful decorative rebellion against modernist good taste. It is theatrical and self-aware rather than timeless, austere, or frictionless.

## Related / Subsets

- `corporate-memphis` shares simplified geometry and bright color, but it is flat platform illustration with very different politics and reception.
- `pop-art` shares loud commercial color, but Pop Art is representational and media-ironic while Memphis is abstract, object-led, and design-theory-driven.
- `italian-radical-design` supplies the anti-modernist context; Memphis is the more commercially legible, pattern-heavy postmodern branch.
- Subsets include Memphis Milano furniture language, revival surface graphics, and robotics/object treatments anchored by Umeda’s Ginza Robot cabinet.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Use Memphis as a controlled art-direction layer: patterned hero backgrounds, modular product cards, decorative object panels, sticker-like labels, and stage-like layouts. Keep interactive controls, form fields, and explanatory text on stable high-contrast surfaces. Separate Memphis the design movement from Corporate Memphis in copy and metadata.

## CSS Translation

- Color roles: `--memphis-pink`, `--memphis-teal`, `--memphis-lemon`, `--memphis-cobalt`, `--memphis-black`, and `--memphis-paper`.
- Patterns: repeating radial dots, zigzags, checkerboards, squiggle SVG backgrounds, and terrazzo flecks.
- Shapes: absolute-positioned circles/triangles/squiggles, oblique `clip-path` blocks, and hard-edged modular panels.
- Layout: stage-like grids, segmented planes, and object cards with clashing backgrounds.
- Motion: slide, snap, and assemble pieces with reduced-motion still compositions.

## Typography / Fonts

Use bold slab serifs, Cooper-like or decorative display faces, geometric sans headlines, and oversized label treatments. Pair with a readable support sans for body copy; do not let novelty type carry dense instructions.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Avoid conflating Memphis with Corporate Memphis or reducing the movement to “random colorful squiggles.” Preserve its Italian postmodern furniture/object provenance and anti-modernist critique. In accessibility-critical contexts, limit pattern behind text and test contrast aggressively.

## Anti-Patterns

- Calling flat SaaS blob illustration Memphis without postmodern object context.
- Making everything tasteful, muted, and grid-minimal.
- Letting pattern sit behind essential copy or controls.
- Using generic party confetti with no laminate/object-stage logic.
- Treating clashing color as a substitute for deliberate geometric composition.
