# Millefiori Glass

## Scope

Patterned glass cane cross-sections, often flower-, star- or eye-like, fused into glass surfaces or enclosed in clear glass. This is a material/pattern grammar, not a synonym for all Murano glass, lampworked flowers or translucent interface panels. Ribbon canes, inlays and other glass techniques can coexist.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Multicolored opaque cane centers contrast with clear, white, blue, green or dark grounds. Nested contrasting rings are more diagnostic than any fixed rainbow. Colors here are visually estimated; a cream, cobalt, coral, mint application palette is proposed.

**Type**: Mostly absent; occasional inscription and silhouette canes occur in A58835/A55021. Use plain readable supporting UI type. Tiny words embedded in glass are evidence of a variant, not a reason to distort labels.

**Texture**: Smooth fused boundaries, clear domed envelopes, local highlights, optical distortion and occasional bubbles. Ancient weathered examples have matte/pitted and iridescent surfaces; corrosion is condition, not a required decorative filter.

**Shape**: Nested radial rosettes, concentric eyes, star cross-sections and small internally patterned disks; some stretch along vessel necks. Simple modeled lampwork petals alone are insufficient; the single-flower weight is excluded.

**Motion**: No source animation measured. Still glass optics support depth and refraction, not floating or waving UI behavior. Proposed control state changes can be instant; no persistent shimmer.

**Spatial**: Close-packed mosaics, concentric arrangements, garlands and scattered slices all occur. Clear gaps can frame a dense center. Functional labels need stable opaque islands; ornament may inhabit rounded control rims or preview disks.

**Cultural markers**: Italian terminology and Venetian revivals are relevant, but museum evidence spans ancient Roman, French, Italian, American and Chinese production. Do not label every technique or object as authentic Murano work without provenance.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: Internally patterned slice units, nested color relationships, fusion into a larger surface, and believable glass continuity. Clear material is common but opaque mosaic variants also exist.

## Connotation

**Mode:** Jewel-like, collectible and exuberant.

A small surface rewards close looking. Playful color can coexist with a quiet usable order panel.

## Related / Subsets

Glassmorphism concerns translucency and blur in interface layering; it does not require patterned cane slices. Web 2 Gloss shares edge highlights but lacks this internally repeated material structure. Ebru has fluid surface marbling rather than sliced radial cross-sections.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Use actual radio-style pattern selectors, a quantity stepper and cart confirmation. Put patterned cane geometry into the controls and panel rims themselves. Keep all options named, selected state redundant in text/icon, keyboard access and 44 px targets.

## CSS Translation

Construct original SVG concentric scalloped disks with nested rings and limited palette variation; combine with curved highlights and restrained inner shadows on rounded edges. Opaque label surfaces preserve contrast. Mobile choices wrap with full labels. No perpetual shimmer; reduced motion disables optional transitions.

## Typography / Fonts

No defining typeface. A readable rounded sans or modest serif is an application choice; avoid assigning a Venetian font to every example.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Do not imply place-of-manufacture certification or copy maker signatures/brand canes. Source photos remain linked evidence; original abstract slice arrangements are application assets. Ancient weathering is not evidence of original color or universal finish.

## Anti-Patterns

- Flower wallpaper with no cross-section structure.
- Calling any glass flower millefiori.
- Text placed over dense refractive pattern.
- Treating faceted reflections as separate works.
- Claiming all millefiori is Venetian or transparent.
