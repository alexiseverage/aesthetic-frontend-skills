# Peruvian Chicha Poster Graphics

Peruvian Chicha Poster Graphics is a living regional lettering and print tradition linked to chicha music, migrant urban communities and screen-print workshops. Oversized authored names or phrases, bright contrasting color, and ranked poster communication form its distinctive system. A thirty-work corpus covers concert re-editions, community/service commissions and later typographic prints by Monky and Elliot Tupac; it does not exhaust the wider Chicha art umbrella.

## Scope

Use this aesthetic for concert bills, event calendars, cultural publishing, print collections and expressive neighborhood announcements. A dominant original name or phrase should organize the artifact, with dates, venue and actions ranked clearly around it.

Generic fluorescent music graphics, a tropical palette or any Peruvian illustration are insufficient. The broader Chicha art movement includes activist and illustrative work with other formal systems. For a specifically early original poster reconstruction, research period objects rather than treating a contemporary re-edition as an exact dated original.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Bright lime/yellow, pink, orange, blue and red create forceful title/field contrast. Black grounds are common, but blue, turquoise, orange, lime and multicolor fields also occur. Large flat fills and broad gradients are both valid. Fluorescent inks are documented in the tradition; screen images cannot measure fluorescence or identify exact pigments, and early basic-ink examples precede the later fluorescent vocabulary.

**Type**: Author the message silhouette: disproportionate band names, enlarged initials, interlocking swashes, curled terminals, angled or arched baselines, broad faces and compact factual capitals. Script, decorative serif, condensed and geometric block lettering coexist. Dark outlines, offset sides and bright external contours are common, not universal. One mandatory script font would misrepresent the corpus.

**Texture**: Preserve a printed or painted graphic surface. Screen-print layer counts and textured paper may be documented for specific editions; small reproductions do not support precise grain or brush measurements. Paper margins, restrained print irregularity or flat ink-like fields can ground a digital translation. Do not require artificial distress, wall grime or glowing digital bloom.

**Shape**: Large letterforms carry the principal shape vocabulary. Long underlines, linked counters, nested contours, small boxed facts, stars and occasional flower/figure accents support the words. Portraits, service illustrations, ribbons and celebratory objects are contextual variants. A generic flag/emblem without expressive lettering hierarchy does not carry the identity.

**Motion**: Actual historical timing and animation are unknown from the still corpus. Music context does not require animated text. A contemporary event browser may use immediate poster swaps or short optional reveals, with an immediate reduced-motion alternative; these are proposed UI choices.

**Spatial**: Establish emphatic main title/name scale and coherent supporting hierarchy. Concert bills often use compact date and venue boxes in corners or top bands; dense secondary names and offers occupy smaller zones. Phrase-led prints can remove the fact grid while retaining shaped lettering and color relationships. Portrait and landscape formats both work; avoid shrinking a full dense poster until essential facts become unreadable.

**Cultural markers**: Retain the Peruvian Chicha music, migrant and workshop lineage and named maker context. Band/event names and popular phrases are meaningful communication, not generic party decoration. Contemporary community, restaurant and international commissions extend the vocabulary. Do not credit a sole inventor or exact founding year without stronger historical evidence.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**:

- Authored expressive lettering and an emphatic name/message silhouette.
- Strong color relationships integrated with that lettering, rather than palette alone.
- A deliberate hierarchy appropriate to a poster or short typographic message.
- Grounded Peruvian Chicha context and attribution, without generic regional costume.

## Connotation

**Mode:** contemporary revival / living vernacular translation.

The result should feel publicly communicative, confident and culturally situated. Concert promotion is a core application, while later phrase prints show personal and collective expression. Visual fatigue grows when every utility label receives the same giant outlined treatment; keep a dominant message and readable supporting information.

## Related / Subsets

- `mexican-rotulismo` is a regional commercial-lettering sibling. Chicha's concert-print organization and Peruvian workshop/music context differ from Mexican storefront service communication.
- `rave-flyer` shares music promotion and sometimes bright color, but does not imply Chicha's popular lettering construction, production history or community lineage.
- `risograph` is a print-process neighbor. Layered ink or limited color alone does not establish Chicha; screen printing and Risograph are distinct methods.
- `fileteado-porteno` shares regional lettering and some curled motifs. Fileteado's modeled ornamental frame and Porteño lineage differ from Chicha's oversized message hierarchy and Peruvian concert-print system.

Named application variants include concert bills, painted banners, community/service commissions and contemporary phrase-led prints. They remain internal guidance, not separate canonical subset slugs. Wider activist Chicha art is related context; the entry does not claim one template covers all of it.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Build an actual event or print artifact. A fictional concert browser can show one dominant original bill, compact date/venue panels and a readable personal-plan area. Selecting an event should change its poster, schedule, venue and availability together. Saving a night should retain the selected event across browsing, with an explicit removal action.

Use ordinary support text for ticket information, filters and navigation. Preserve semantic headings, accessible event descriptions and keyboard selection. At small widths, stack the bill and details, keeping title and date prominent. Reduce nonessential pictorial accents before sacrificing event facts. Do not copy an existing band's lettering, artist signature or real poster composition as a new asset.

## CSS Translation

- Color roles: `--poster-field`, `--title-face`, `--title-outline`, `--title-offset`, `--date-panel`, `--support-surface`, `--support-ink`. Choose concrete proposed tokens and calculate actual contrast; these role names are not measured inks.
- Display: an original SVG wordmark or suitable licensed display face with authored curves, joins and offset construction. Keep accessible text equivalents; dynamic facts stay in HTML.
- Letter effects: bounded outlines and hard offset shadows; broad gradients may occupy display faces, while body text uses solid tested colors. Avoid low-contrast rainbow body copy.
- Geometry: compact rectangular fact panels and deliberate title silhouettes; small stars or dividers punctuate rather than scatter throughout the app.
- Layout: one dominant poster, supporting event grid/details, readable controls; container adaptation can stack these instead of scaling everything uniformly.
- Surface and motion: optional restrained print irregularity, no mandatory wear/glow. Intentionally still bills and immediate state changes are valid; optional transitions respect reduced motion.

## Typography / Fonts

Choose a lettering category appropriate to the words and format, then compose it rather than applying a generic italic font. Enlarged initials, exaggerated widths, tight joins, outlined counters and long underline strokes can establish a distinct display treatment. Condensed block capitals are useful for date/venue information; neutral sans support copy keeps forms and ticket details clear.

Exact fonts in the historical references remain unidentified. System or licensed contemporary fonts are implementation choices, not historical attribution. Keep extended Latin characters legible. Avoid an obligatory brush-script pairing, all-outlined paragraphs and novelty pseudo-Latin lettering.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

This is a living Peruvian practice, not anonymous neon styling. Preserve maker and community attribution, and avoid flattening migrant or Indigenous histories into a tourist theme. Fictional events must not imply artist/band endorsement. Existing activist messages, protected marks, signatures and source lettering remain analytical references, not assets to reuse.

[UPC's Monky archive](https://cultural.upc.edu.pe/galeria/monky), [Erie Art Museum](https://www.erieartmuseum.org/revolucion-chicha) and [Centre de la Gravure](https://www.centredelagravure.be/en/extra/b53/) ground the context and later extensions. The reviewed corpus is concentrated in two makers and contemporary/re-edition material. Bright color does not excuse flashing, illegible event details or inaccessible controls.

## Anti-Patterns

- Neon gradients without authored popular lettering or poster hierarchy.
- Generic rave/digital collage substituted for the documented Peruvian print vocabulary.
- Assuming every example is black-backed, fluorescent, script-only, gradient-filled and outlined.
- Treating every work by a named Chicha artist as this narrower poster system.
- Copying original band names, signatures or letterforms into supposedly new artwork.
- Shrinking a dense poster into unreadable event facts, or making ticket controls decorative and ambiguous.
