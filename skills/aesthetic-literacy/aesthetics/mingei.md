# Mingei

## Scope

Mingei connects beauty with use, material, process and repeated craft practice through the twentieth-century movement associated with Yanagi Sōetsu, Kawai Kanjirō and Hamada Shōji. Its visual vocabulary includes functional ceramics, constructed textiles, wood/lacquer, metal tools and graphic/applied works. Useful silhouettes, process-made rhythms and decoration following a surface or panel matter more than a compulsory neutral palette.

Distinguish historical objects collected as models from works made by named movement practitioners. Pre-movement Korean, Ryukyuan and Japanese objects retain their own identities; documented British studio-pottery relationships retain their geography. Mingei is not an alias for all handmade or Japanese craft. The [research profile](../../../knowledge/aesthetics/mingei.md) contains thirty complete inspected examples, source attribution, variants and unresolved details.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Material grounds with deliberate painted, dyed, glazed or inlaid marks. Cream, iron brown and rust coexist with indigo, blue-green glaze, vermilion lacquer, ochre, shell white and saturated patterned textiles. Choose a coherent material branch; beige-only and matte-only rules discard established examples. Exact UI colors are proposals, not historical samples.

**Type**: Lettering can be an integrated applied design, as in Serizawa's syllable screen or Kuroda's source-identified inlaid character; many objects have none. Use readable labels and modest hierarchy. Keep optional display lettering deliberate and meaningful, without claiming a movement-wide typeface or using untranslated marks as costume.

**Texture**: Material and construction remain visible: slip trails, pooled glaze, thrown lines, weave, stitches, seams, shell inlay, lacquer gloss, wood grain and metalwork. Irregularity may arise from handling, but uniform repeated modules also belong. Damage, patina and photographic reflections do not automatically establish intentional making effects.

**Shape**: Useful vessel proportions, lips, handles and feet; round and squared bodies; garment panels, screen folds, basket construction, tool grips and low furniture. Let decoration follow the object's construction or useful surface. Avoid reducing everything to wobbly organic blobs or uniformly rounded cards.

**Motion**: The researched corpus is still; no historical animation, timing or easing is established. Intentional stillness with immediate functional feedback is a suitable contemporary translation. Any optional motion is proposed and should preserve reduced-motion parity; do not invent pouring, weaving or breathing as universal Mingei behavior.

**Spatial**: Repeat and variation follow surface, panel and use. Open dish centers, broad textile motifs, dense stitch fields, screen modules and regional narrative maps coexist. Compose practical groupings with visible boundaries and clear reading space; neither extreme emptiness nor uncontrolled clutter is required.

**Cultural markers**: Beauty of use, folk-craft collecting, source-traced regional process, named twentieth-century practitioners and international craft relationships. Anonymous everyday ideals are historical aspirations, not descriptions of every studio work or patron commission. Retain specific maker, place, date and material where known.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**:

- Anchor the design in Mingei's historical craft framework, rather than generic rustic styling.
- Keep useful form and material/process legible in the composition.
- Make repetition or decoration answer to construction and surface.
- Preserve regional and practitioner attribution; collected models and movement-made works remain distinct.

## Connotation

**Mode:** contemporary revival.

The reference can communicate everyday usefulness, rhythmic making and attention to material. It should not imply an authentic historical reconstruction or manufacture provenance that the product cannot substantiate. Mingei's anonymous inexpensive-craft ideals coexist with celebrated named artists, elaborate decoration and commissioned work; flattening these tensions into anonymous beige luxury loses the historical frame.

## Related / Subsets

`arts-and-crafts` shares reform-design and material/use concerns, but its medievalist botanical and book vocabulary is not mandatory here. `shaker-design` shares practical construction while retaining its distinct religious community and storage/order logic. `wabi-sabi-slow-living` emphasizes impermanence and tea-lineage restraint; age, damage and incompletion are not required for Mingei. `scandinavian-modern` has a different regional and institutional functional-design history. `warm-minimalism` is a contemporary lifestyle relation, not the movement's owner.

Historical collected models, named movement practice, graphic/applied craft and documented international studio relations are useful prose branches. Sashiko, bingata, kataezome, Korean or Ainu craft as a whole, and all works by Leach are not aliases or automatic subsets.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Suitable contexts include craft publications, attributed object collections, everyday-use planning and process-aware product interfaces. Use original practical silhouettes, visible material divisions and economical repeat. Give imagery enough space to show construction, with plain readable labels outside patterned zones. Real quantity, arrangement or selection controls should update the object grouping and corresponding text together.

Use high-contrast control surfaces and explicit selected states. Do not hide fields in a textile pattern or turn an art-object inspection tool into a fictitious historical workshop. A current fictional object set should say so where attribution naturally belongs; source references remain research material unless reuse is separately permitted.

## CSS Translation

- Define one material branch's color roles: warm cloth background, light control surface, dark ink text, indigo action, clay secondary and ochre artwork accent. These are application choices, not universal historic colors.
- Keep essential text on quiet surfaces. Use original SVG weave, slip or construction marks within imagery, with restrained seams/rules between practical groups.
- Allow mixed geometry grounded in use: rectangular textile panels and controls alongside round vessels. Avoid arbitrary extreme radii or uneven control hit areas.
- Use a practical grid with `minmax(0, 1fr)`, wrapping labels and stacked controls on narrow screens. Preserve artwork proportions and expose real state in text.
- Prefer zero-duration state changes for a still translation. If animation is added, disable nonessential transforms and retain immediate feedback under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## Typography / Fonts

Use a readable humanist sans or modest serif for labels and long text; system stacks are a valid contemporary proposal. Reserve integrated display treatment for short original words that can be read. Exact historic fonts were not identified. Do not use fake brush calligraphy, random kana, pseudo-Asian glyphs, museum seals or artist signatures as a brand shortcut. Add language-capable licensed fonts only when the content requires them.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

The movement's collecting includes Korean objects under Japanese colonial rule and distinct Ryukyuan and Ainu traditions. Yanagi's documented opposition to oppression does not erase that historical setting or replace communities' own attribution. Do not present every collected object as Japanese manufacture. Ainu contextual text without inspected objects cannot establish reusable motif rules.

Source identity takes precedence over stereotypes: a Korean lacquered-paper tray is not wood merely because it appears in a woodwork gallery, a coverlet is not automatically clothing, and an ornamented studio plate is not necessarily a food-serving vessel. Preserve unresolved materials, inscriptions, ownership histories and symbolic meanings. Use original compositions rather than copying regional patterns or sacred marks as generic decoration.

## Anti-Patterns

- Beige luxury pottery as the entire movement; banning color, gloss, ornament or squared forms.
- Artificial distress, random wobble or repaired damage treated as compulsory authenticity.
- Calling all historic Asian craft Mingei, or backdating the movement to its collected models.
- Assigning anonymity, poverty, commoner status or spiritual authority from an object's appearance.
- Copying a named artist's lettering, regional textile motif or source photograph into an unattributed UI.
- Patterned low-contrast controls, color-only state or invented historical motion.
