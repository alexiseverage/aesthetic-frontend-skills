# Heritage Motorsport Livery

## Scope

A body-aware racing identity language: large color fields/bands, explicit number or identity panels, primary team/sponsor lettering and subordinate technical marks. This entry centers on1960s–1990s circuit/rally systems and identifiable later revivals. Each team/sponsor has its own grammar; Gulf colors are one branch.

Translate it into product cards, merchandise listings, event entries and club content. Preserve recognition hierarchy and panel relationships. Fast-car imagery or diagonal stripes alone are insufficient.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: A few strong body colors with readable numbers/marks; white/blue/red, pale-blue/orange, black/gold, orange, pink, navy/yellow and green/orange systems occur. No compulsory sponsor palette. Keep secondary marks subordinate.

**Type**: Large race numbers and primary wordmarks; smaller suppliers and driver/technical information. Number fields may use plain or outlined numerals. Sans, serif, italic, blackletter and rounded sponsor categories coexist; no single racing font is mandatory.

**Texture**: Smooth painted or vinyl-covered panels, crisp graphic edges and vehicle reflections. Gloss differs by period/process. Dust, wear and mud are condition, not compulsory distress. Source-described restoration materials do not establish every historical finish.

**Shape**: Roundels, rectangles, chevrons, stripes and contour bands tied to body geometry. Solid-color branding, fine perimeter rules, concentric arcs and dashed contours are valid branches. Avoid reducing every livery to three slashes.

**Motion**: Still images establish no animation timing. Prefer still product inspection and immediate state changes. Optional short color transitions must respect reduced motion; engine noise and perpetual speed effects are unnecessary.

**Spatial**: Primary identity repeats in useful zones across nose, door, roof and rear, adapted to curved/segmented surfaces. Reading priority remains clear despite sponsor density. Website copy and controls need their own opaque, quiet area.

**Cultural markers**: Racing teams, competition identifiers, sponsors, supplier badges and craft/restoration practice. Distinguish period schemes from contemporary reprises and photograph dates from model years. Historic tobacco/alcohol marks are context, not required modern endorsements.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: A distinctive identity field tied to panel/surface geometry; a clear primary number or mark; disciplined secondary hierarchy; explicit separation of historical reference and original contemporary interpretation. Palette, checkers and car photography alone do not suffice.

## Connotation

**Mode:** nostalgic quotation or contemporary revival. Recognition, competition and sponsor/team memory matter. Keep the actual project voice clear; racing heritage need not imply aggressive sound, fake engineering claims or invented affiliations.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Build familiar components: a product card with actual variant/quantity selection, event registration card or editorial feature. Let original bands cross the image zone and panel edge; give a large model/entry identifier priority. Keep titles, prices, variant labels and actions semantic and readable.

A bag must retain the selected variant/quantity when the user explores another. Mark selected variants with text plus native radio state. Use44px controls, visible focus, descriptive product alt text and an honest local demo state. Do not simulate telemetry or an entire race-management tool merely to express the aesthetic.

## CSS Translation

Proposed visually estimated roles: paper #F2EEE4, navy #172B3B, red #BE352B, pale blue #AFC8D0, petrol #164B45, gold #C1A45F. These are not official sponsor color specifications. Use navy/paper for reading and vivid accents for bands or identifiers.

Prefer squared panels, fine borders and a few broad original SVG/CSS bands. White number roundels or contrasting rectangles work when they serve identity hierarchy. Responsive columns should stack without losing product visibility. Optional150ms button-color changes are modern choices; disable under reduced motion.

## Typography / Fonts

Use a bold/condensed display category for short model numbers, with clear sans body text. Local Arial Black/Impact plus Arial can provide contrast without font downloads; a licensed grotesk can also work. Preserve distinctions between original custom wordmarks and selectable UI labels. Avoid copying a sponsor's exact lettering or inventing historical font identification.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Credit source makers and keep restoration/period distinctions visible. Existing car/sponsor marks and photographs retain their owners' rights. Create original fictional identities and assets; do not claim team approval or present a generated model as a historical vehicle. Historic tobacco/alcohol sponsorship need not be reproduced to retain the grammar.

## Anti-Patterns

- A universal Gulf palette or generic three-stripe decoration.
- Checkered flags, neon roads and speed blur replacing identity hierarchy.
- Fake lap data, engine sounds or elaborate tool simulation.
- Tracing a famous sponsor logo or claiming an official car replica.
- Bag contents silently changing with the currently selected color.
- Illegible technical copy, color-only swatches or motion-gated controls.
