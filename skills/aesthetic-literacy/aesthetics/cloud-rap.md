# Cloud Rap

Cloud Rap graphics connect particular networked rap releases with altered landscapes, mineral or glowing surfaces, personal photographs, expansive synthetic space and authored identities. Pale cyan/violet atmosphere is a useful branch, not a uniform: monochrome portraits, coarse illustration, commercial polish and severe flat graphics also belong. Keep the rap/community context visible instead of treating every dreamy image as the same aesthetic.

## Scope

The researched center is early-2010s US/Bay Area-linked releases and collaborators, alongside a distinct Swedish Sad Boys/Gravity branch. Lil B, Clams Casino, Keyboard Kid, Main Attrakionz and Friendzone supply important context. The term is contested; artists may accept it, resist its restrictions or move beyond it. A later archive or neighboring release is a bounded reference, not permission to classify an entire current career.

Compared with `vaporwave`, this is grounded in rap release/persona networks and particular collaborations, rather than a generic consumer-nostalgia system. `digicore` overlaps later communities and personal media but belongs to a different period/platform grouping. Preserve an authored release/world, recognizable image hierarchy and room for contradictory moods.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Pale lavender, cyan, magenta and icy white coexist with near-black stone, green natural scenes, warm burgundy flash photography and monochrome. Dark-to-luminous depth is common in the atmospheric branch. Treat these as visually estimated families, not universal source hex values. Keep usable text contrast on separate solid planes.

**Type**: Widely spaced plain uppercase, condensed name bands, blackletter, loose script, bevel/outline/metallic custom marks and mono technical labels. Wordless covers are genuine examples. One focal identity can carry a large field; source-specific logos and scripts are not free genre fonts. Supporting metadata should remain readable and calm.

**Texture**: Veined marble, glowing/cloudy fields, glints, coarse photographic grain, keyed cutouts, digital edge ghosts and hand-drawn or painted imagery. Clean cinematic and studio surfaces provide counterexamples. Clams Casino's own account documents choosing a marble image for a cover; it does not establish rights to reuse it or make stone compulsory. Describe unverified materials/processes as appearance, not provenance.

**Shape**: Floating figures/objects, fantasy environments, cover squares, globe/gem outlines, branded product quotations and small symbolic marks. Ordinary streets, rooms, trees and people can carry the whole image. Build original props and imagery; do not copy performer likenesses, game cards, commercial emblems or foreign scripts to simulate belonging.

**Motion**: Actual inspected videos show keyed layers, gesture, scale changes and cuts, ordinary walking/reframing, and polished tracking/location edits. They do not establish one slow ambient loop, exact easing, frame rate or universal strobe rule. A contemporary cover editor can remain intentionally still, with immediate user-triggered scene/lettering changes and at most brief colour feedback. Reduced motion removes transitions while preserving all controls and exports; sound requires explicit action if offered.

**Spatial**: Large singular material/photo fields, a few centered or banded identity lines, distant atmospheric depth and small human/object scale. Other compositions use dense collage, ticket metadata or triptych narrative. Separate expressive image space from functional reading/control space. Preserve the full authored composition in narrow containers instead of cropping away the main subject.

**Cultural markers**: Rap/Based networks, artist-directed personas, online release circulation, local friendships and particular US and Swedish contexts. Firsthand accounts distinguish groups and individual interests. Do not erase Black rap origins by presenting the whole category as Swedish pastel imagery, or assume sincerity, parody, sadness, sexuality or drug use from a cover's appearance.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**:

- A specific authored rap-release, artist-world or relevant network context beyond generic dreamy decoration.
- Deliberate material, photographic, illustrated or synthetic-image treatment that produces a recognizable personal world.
- A clear relationship between expansive image space and release/identity information, with room for spare and dense variants.

## Connotation

**Mode:** contemporary revival.

Suspended, intimate, expansive, strange or emotionally direct, sometimes theatrical and playful. The studied examples disprove mandatory irony, low fidelity and pastel nostalgia. A new fictional image tool can evoke personal cover-making without pretending to stream, distribute or endorse real music. Preserve human agency alongside digital atmosphere.

## Related / Subsets

- `vaporwave`: shared digital repurposing and occasional material motifs; different musical/community context. Marble or cyan alone identifies neither.
- `pen-and-pixel`: shared rap-release spectacle; a different researched commercial compositing/lettering grammar and production history.
- `early-internet`: shared release circulation and modest authoring; Cloud Rap is a particular later networked music context, not all early websites.
- `digicore`: shared peer collaboration and personal media; overlapping influence does not merge distinct generations or every artist's changing work.
- `hyperpop`: shares authored experimental pop worlds and later overlap; do not replace rap lineage with a platform umbrella.
- `dreamcore-weirdcore`: shared displacement and atmosphere, without automatically supplying this release/community context.

No separate canonical subsets are defined. Based, Sad Boys, Gravity Boys/Drain-related work and specific label catalogs are not interchangeable identities or exhaustive genre labels.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

A fictional cover-postcard studio can pair original atmospheric imagery with an honest editable preview, native scene carousel, title/byline controls, frame/resolution choices and genuine PNG export. A release page, editorial player or catalog is also possible when its offered behavior is real. Do not offer a decorative play/upload/share button with no corresponding action.

Keep image text available as HTML labels/descriptions. Validate and bound user text, fit long unbroken strings and preserve Unicode. Wait for actual image decode before canvas drawing/export, handle failure and retry, prevent stale async work after edits/unmount and release object URLs. Distinguish local file generation from online publication. Empty, loading, error and reset states need understandable recovery.

## CSS Translation

- Proposed dark studio roles: ink `#131327`, panel `#25213e`, violet `#cbc3f2`, paper `#f2effb`, citrus `#e2f7a1`, muted `#bdb4d3`, border `#746989`. These are new application choices, not recovered pigments.
- Use one original image field or quiet synthetic space against solid readable controls. Subtle grain belongs to imagery; decorative glow should not obscure essential text or focus.
- Large custom-treated system sans identity can use a silver gradient/outline with sufficient luminance; pair it with 16px sans body and 12–14px mono metadata. Essential reading never depends on tiny source lettering.
- Fine 1px rules, square image bounds, 4–8px control corners, 4/8/16/24px spacing and roughly44px targets distinguish image versus tool planes.
- Container queries should stack narrow embedded/gallery placements as well as mobile viewports. Preserve full image and meaningful title/credit information.
- Proposed feedback: at most120ms colour changes, no ambient movement. Reduced motion removes transitions. No auto-pan, strobe, automatic audio or invented fake waveform.

## Typography / Fonts

Observed categories vary too much for one mandatory font. Arial/Arial Black can support large plain or outlined identity, Georgia/Times a deliberate serif quotation, and system mono readable file metadata. Identify exact historical fonts only with credible credits; copied artist marks are not substitute typefaces. Test fallback metrics, complete words and Unicode input/export. Downloaded fonts need actual licenses and coverage checks.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Artist and designer testimony takes priority over outsider caricature. Marra credits Lil B's concepts while illustrating 6 Kiss; Laurence describes a later personal-photo direction and moving away from borrowed Japanese iconography. These are particular practices, not blanket permissions. Religious imagery, racial oppression, sexual bodies, commercial marks and substance references have specific meanings and should not become anonymous decorative stock.

Public access, uploader verification and archival hosting do not establish reuse rights. Distinguish source photographer, cover designer, musician, label and filmmaker; unknown credits stay unknown. Release dates and remaster/reissue copyrights may differ from original image creation. New applications should use original fiction and assets, with no copied recordings, lyrics or performer identity.

## Anti-Patterns

- A lavender gradient, cloud photograph or marble slab with no authored release context.
- Treating all Based, Swedish rap, later Drain-related output, Digicore or internet music as interchangeable.
- Required copied kanji, game sprites, branded drinks, artist faces, sadness or substance imagery.
- Mandatory slow motion or glitch inferred from still covers, unreadable low-contrast metadata and uncontrolled flashing.
- Fake radio status, player, share/upload actions or exported files that do not match the current preview.
