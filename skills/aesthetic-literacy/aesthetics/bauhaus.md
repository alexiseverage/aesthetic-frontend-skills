# Bauhaus

The inspected works make the Bauhaus legible as a changing school and workshop practice. Timber frames, glazed ceramic vessels, woven surfaces, polished metal, expressive early print and later geometric sans typography coexist. Primary colors and basic shapes are important teaching/legacy signals, but a primary-color-only rule would exclude many original works. A contemporary interface can demonstrate controlled changes in interval, repeat, material and hierarchy rather than merely display a red circle, blue square and yellow triangle.

## Scope

Separate school-era work (1919–1933), work by former members outside the school, and later museum legacy graphics. The 1968 Bayer and 1973 Allner posters are clearly dated legacy interpretations. The Singer armchair is retained only as a neighboring material/construction comparison. Feininger portfolio cover and title are one publication; the same Dell service photograph under three accessions is one design artifact.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Earthy glazes, black/cream cloth, muted blue/rust fields and bright legacy primaries all occur.

**Type**: Early angular lettering and later bold lowercase/sans systems differ; geometric sans is a useful translation, not every period artifact’s type.

**Texture**: Visible timber, metal, glass, glaze, weave and printed paper express construction. No one smooth industrial finish covers all workshops.

**Shape**: Repeated frames, circles, cylinders and intersecting axes are useful parts of functional relationships.

**Motion**: Asymmetric information groups, repeating fields and unusual photographic viewpoints extend beyond an orthogonal primary-color grid.

**Spatial**: Purposeful immediate state changes are a proposed interface translation. No historical assembly speed or easing is established.

**Cultural markers**: Preserve textile/craft contributors and the changing educational/industrial context; distinguish later revival graphics from original school works.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: Functional relationships; experimental reduction and repeat; visible material/construction logic; situated workshop pedagogy.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Use an exercise or tool that exposes the relationship between a unit and its arrangement. ## Anti-Patterns

- A primary-color toy with no functional relationships.
- Erasing weaving, craft and women workshop contributors.
- Later legacy posters misdated as school-era originals.
- Decorative motion or ahistorical startup minimalism.
