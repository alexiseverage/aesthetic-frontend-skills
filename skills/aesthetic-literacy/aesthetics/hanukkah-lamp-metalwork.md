# Hanukkah Lamp Metalwork

## Scope

Jewish ceremonial lamps made for Hanukkah organize eight festival lights and a distinguishable auxiliary light within a made object. Compare oil troughs, candle sockets, individual cups, pierced backplates, freestanding branches and modern horizontal forms. This object-led visual language suits an exhibition, collection guide, or educational comparison. [Research profile](../../../knowledge/aesthetics/hanukkah-lamp-metalwork.md)

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Use the contrast of polished highlights, dark recesses and warm or cool metal. Patinated copper alloy, gilded silver, clear glass and isolated stone or enamel details vary by object. Museum photography backgrounds are part of the display.

**Type**: Some objects carry inscriptions in dedicated plates, cut through an arch, or built into the support itself. Use readable contemporary typography for labels and interpretation. Preserve attributed inscriptions in their original context.

**Texture**: Relief can be embossed, pierced, traced, cast, polished, or assembled in filigree. Choose a coherent technique for object diagrams and draw fine boundaries between punched voids, ridged edges, and reflective planes.

**Shape**: Maintain an identifiable set of eight festival-light positions and a separately located or distinguished auxiliary light. Recognizable supports include a long tray, a vertical backplate, central branches, a hanging curve, and a horizontal beam. Curved and radiating arrangements also appear.

**Motion**: Use still compositions. The research corpus establishes neither historical movement nor timing. Change the selected form or annotation immediately, with a short optional opacity transition for a change in diagram.

**Spatial**: Set the lights in a clear tier with a visible relationship to a tray, backplate, arms, stand, or wall mount. Structure a comparison so the label, maker and period stay associated with the selected form on every screen size.

**Cultural markers**: The lamp is Judaica. Preserve function, the role of the auxiliary light, the specific regional and maker attributions, and the difference between the working light positions and a motif depicted on a backplate.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**:

- An orderly, recognizable grouping of festival lights with a distinct auxiliary position in each interpretive diagram.
- A clear distinction between the fabricated support and the light positions it organizes.
- Contextual labels that identify the specific ceremonial object and the source of the interpretation.

## Connotation

**Mode:** Contemporary museum interpretation.

Use direct object labels, precise geometry and generous inspection space. Let the objects carry their varied materials and provenance.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Build a collection viewer with three labeled forms and a “Compare form” mode. Selecting a form updates the interpretive diagram, explanatory labels and provenance. Add detail controls for light positions, auxiliary position and fabrication. Keep navigation and reading order consistent when the comparison stacks on mobile.

## CSS Translation

Use a dark bronze charcoal background `#1c211d`, matte display surface `#262e27`, warm paper `#f3ebd9`, light text `#f8efd8`, secondary text `#c8c8ab`, and brass action `#e4c886` with dark ink `#211e15`. These are proposed interface colors. Use thin metal-colored rules, shallow 2–4 px corners, and a 4/8/12/16/24/32 px spacing scale. Confine layered highlights to the diagram and selection border. Keep transitions optional and at or below 140 ms, with immediate changes under reduced motion.

## Typography / Fonts

Use a readable book serif for exhibition headings at 32–48 px with compact leading. Use a system sans at 14–16 px with open leading for explanatory text, form tabs and source labels. Display catalog references in tabular numerals.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Name the role of the auxiliary light and keep inscriptions tied to actual objects. Draw structural diagrams with explicit interpretation labels. A religious symbol or an authentic inscription requires its own context. Museum image and display rights are independent from the artifacts' dates.

## Anti-Patterns

- Staging the ritual arrangement as a progress indicator or an ornament for unrelated content.
- A generic seasonal palette that erases material contrast and sculpture.
- Using the same straight-row silhouette for all regional examples.
- Placing tiny, low-contrast provenance labels over metallic highlights.
