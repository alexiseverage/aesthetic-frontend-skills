# Taishō Roman

## Scope

A retrospective category for lyrical Japanese modern illustration and commercial print culture: book and magazine covers, frontispieces, sheet music, postcards and decorative papers. Expressive title/image relationships, patterned textiles or botanicals, controlled color planes and graceful figurative or ornamental contours form a usable visual system. Takehisa Yumeji, Takabatake Kashō, Fukiya Kōji and Kobayashi Kaichi offer distinct branches rather than one interchangeable artist style.

The Taishō era was1912–1926; roots extend into late Meiji and continuities into early Shōwa. Keep individual work/edition dates explicit. Not every Taishō-era object, all Yumeji work or every Japanese woodblock print belongs. See [MOMAT](https://www.momat.go.jp/en/exhibitions/570), [Nichibunken](https://www.nichibun.ac.jp/en/topics/db/2022/03/08/s001/) and [Musashino](https://www.musashino.or.jp/museum/1002006/1002258/1002259/1002308/1002312.html) for historical context.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Pale paper and dark ink masses anchor selective coral/red, blue/teal, lilac, olive or ochre. Bold flat color is as valid as muted lyricism; near-monochrome Kaichi designs are a counterexample to mandatory saturation. Sepia is not the identity.

**Type**: Expressive Japanese mastheads and framed vertical titles can act as image elements, with dense small contents or credits in a separate zone. Roman marks occur in commercial contexts. Describe the role before choosing a modern font; use actual meaningful language and do not fabricate calligraphy.

**Texture**: Flat printed planes, fine expressive contour, patterned cloth and flowers, and restrained local variation. Woodblock stationery, reproduced magazine illustration and paintings have different surfaces. Paper damage and stains are later condition, not required design ingredients.

**Shape**: Long figures, inclined heads, flowing sleeves, flowers and leaves are common; Kaichi adds hard cards, checkers, stars and theatrical curtains. Western clothes and nonportrait decorative designs prevent a kimono-only formula.

**Motion**: Evidence is still printed/pictorial media. Depicted wind or gesture does not establish timing. Intentional stillness and immediate native form/navigation states are appropriate contemporary proposals; optional transitions must honor reduced motion.

**Spatial**: Image and title blocks form a deliberate editorial composition: slender illustrated panels, rectangular title frames, asymmetric floral masses, quiet grounds and localized dense pattern. Dense contents pages coexist with sparse covers. Give functional copy a clear unpatterned zone.

**Cultural markers**: Japanese modern lyrical publishing, youth/consumer illustration, stationery collecting, literary/music covers and specific mixes of Japanese and Western dress or motifs. This is historically situated urban aspiration, not a timeless national style or proof of universal prosperity.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**:

- A grounded Japanese lyrical publishing reference, with clear period/continuity boundaries.
- Integrated image/title framing and a deliberate hierarchy of contour, pattern and quiet ground.
- Expressive figurative or botanical composition that survives beyond a cream/red palette.

A kimono, one flower species, downcast gaze, aged paper and vertical type are common/variant signals, not compulsory ingredients.

## Connotation

**Mode:** contemporary revival.

Convey intimacy, cultivated curiosity and expressive print craft. Nostalgia should acknowledge selective commercial imagery rather than romanticize every social condition of the era. Different artists and readerships require distinct treatment.

## Related / Subsets

- `art-nouveau`: shared organic contour and integrated lettering; the Japanese publishing context distinguishes this entry.
- `art-deco`: angular theatrical commercial branch, especially Kaichi, without making every lyrical image Deco.
- `shin-hanga`: adjacent period and some shared media, but a separate modern print revival. Medium, maker or date alone does not classify either.
- Yumeji's lyrical covers, Kashō's magazine portraits and Kaichi's graphic stationery are branches here, not separately canonical slugs.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Use an original cover-like illustration beside a legible reading or form panel. Give the masthead, main title and supporting labels distinct scales. Put textile/flower patterns in artwork or bounded ornament; keep field labels and body text clear. Native inputs, validation, clear selected states and focus follow real content changes. A newsletter demo must disclose whether it actually sends or subscribes.

## Typography / Fonts

A readable local serif such as Georgia for display/italic intimacy, with Arial/system sans for small labels, is a modern translation. These are not identified source fonts. For actual Japanese content use a suitable Japanese family, correct text and language tagging; preserve legibility rather than arbitrary vertical Latin labels or pseudo glyphs.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Attribute actual works, makers, publishers and dates. Scan availability does not automatically grant reuse rights; item-specific notices apply. Create separate original application art, without copied signatures, seals or source compositions. Do not flatten regional, gender, class or religious context into generic exotic decoration. Specific flower/card/church symbolism requires specific evidence.

## Anti-Patterns

- Generic beige nostalgia, sepia stains or kimono imagery without editorial composition.
- Calling every Taishō object, all works by one artist or all modern woodblocks this aesthetic.
- Reproducing historical compositions, signatures or meaningless Japanese lettering.
- Treating Western dress, bold color, hard geometry or monochrome as disqualifying.
- Constant petals, parallax or gesture animation inferred from still images.
- Patterned low-contrast form controls or a fake subscription confirmation.
