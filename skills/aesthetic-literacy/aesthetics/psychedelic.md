# Psychedelic

Psychedelic design is the late-1960s counterculture language of expanded perception: vibrating color, flowing hand-lettering, organic ornament, and dense immersive poster space. It works when it feels like a sensory event, not when it is reduced to generic rainbow decoration.

## Scope

Use Psychedelic for music, festivals, artist pages, album launches, immersive brand experiences, experimental portfolios, and full-commitment campaign surfaces. Avoid it for readability-critical tools, corporate/professional services, data visualization, calm transactional flows, or contexts where drug-culture coding, perceptual overload, or illegibility would be unsafe or mismatched.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Perceptually charged figure/ground relations, often saturated blue/magenta, yellow/purple, orange/blue or green/lavender in a few adjacent inks. Pale pink/gray, near-monochrome blue and black/white collage are documented variants. Fluorescent pigment cannot be established from an RGB reproduction; a full neon rainbow is not compulsory.

**Type**: Flowing sculptural hand-lettering, fused counters, warped display faces, Art Nouveau revival curves, and letterforms that become image. Readability may be strained in display zones, but functional copy needs a fallback layer.

**Texture**: Dense drawn line, stipple, halftone imagery, hatching, repeated bands and layered inks. British screenprinting and San Francisco offset lithography both matter; psychedelic does not imply one print process. Paper creases and scan wear are artifact history, not mandatory decoration. Liquid projection belongs to a related audiovisual medium and needs direct playback evidence for motion claims.

**Shape**: Deformed contours, swollen letters, plumes, ripples, whiplash lines, rays, irregular islands and symbolic illustration. Optical mazes, rectangular frames and extruded block letters can participate; reduced rectilinear geometry alone is not sufficient identity.

**Motion**: A still poster can be complete: optical tension does not require actual animation. Pulsing, swirling or liquid morphing are possible contemporary/audiovisual routes, not timing established by the inspected still corpus. If motion is used, ground it in actual playback research and provide reduced-motion alternatives; avoid aggressive flashing.

**Spatial**: Type and image share a charged surface, often through dense pockets, radial relations, framed fields and elaborate borders. Some posters retain substantial open space while distorted lettering and optical color carry the effect. All-over horror-vacui is common rather than compulsory.

**Cultural markers**: San Francisco Fillmore/Matrix/Avalon and British UFO/Marquee/Roundhouse poster contexts; Wes Wilson, Bonnie MacLean, Victor Moscoso, Rick Griffin, Stanley Mouse/Alton Kelley, Michael English/Nigel Waymouth, Martin Sharp and others. Art Nouveau/Secession revival, counterculture, light shows, underground publications and altered-consciousness language are relevant; no single founder or city exhausts the history.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**: A perceptually charged composition of deformed lettering, contour, optical/pattern or illustrative relations with a credible psychedelic/counterculture lineage. Display words often act as images. Generic colorful fantasy is insufficient; no single neon palette, human figure, density level, print process or motion loop is mandatory.

## Connotation

Psychedelic often connotes immersion, expanded consciousness, communal music experience and sensory intensity. Individual works may also be humorous, commercial or political; avoid treating sincerity versus irony as an absolute boundary with neighboring art movements.

## Related / Subsets

- `pop-art` overlaps in popular imagery, print and color; psychedelic poster practice places particular emphasis on perceptual immersion, deformed lettering and counterculture context.
- `rave-flyer` shares music-event density and sensory intensity, with a different historical/technological setting; it is not universally cold-neon or machine-typed.
- `op-art` overlaps in optical vibration and geometry. Counterculture event lettering, symbolic illustration and organic/collage relations give this corpus a different emphasis; Op Art is not universally clinical.
- No canonical subsets are listed; Fillmore Poster Style, British Psychedelia, and Psychedelic Light Show are internal variants here.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Use Psychedelic in controlled immersive zones: heroes, album pages, festival microsites, poster cards, artist portfolios, and experiential takeovers. Keep ticketing, safety, navigation, body copy, and accessibility information in clearer companion surfaces.

## CSS Translation

- Color tokens: `--psych-magenta: #ff00ff; --psych-cyan: #00ffff; --psych-yellow: #ffff00; --psych-purple: #4b0082; --psych-lime: #32cd32; --psych-orange: #ff6600`.
- Texture: original contour bands, stipple, halftone-like or drawn line systems; flat limited inks can be stronger than a generic gradient. Do not label all print texture screenprint grain.
- Shape: blob masks, paisley-like SVGs, radial frames, maximal border-radius, and organic section edges.
- Motion: intentional stillness or immediate manual poster changes are valid. Any researched temporal treatment needs restrained, optional motion and a reduced-motion state.

## Typography / Fonts

Use Monoton, Fugaz One, Lobster, Bungee Shade, licensed psychedelic revival faces, or custom hand-lettered SVG for display. Pair with a highly legible sans for navigation, captions, and transactional text; do not set essential information in unreadable poster lettering.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Psychedelic language carries counterculture, music, altered-consciousness, and drug-culture associations. Avoid using it as neutral decoration in contexts where intoxication cues, overload, or illegibility create accessibility, safety, or cultural mismatch problems.

## Anti-Patterns

- Generic rainbow gradients with no poster, lettering, or counterculture lineage.
- Clean corporate layouts with a few groovy blobs pasted on.
- Hiding essential information in illegible display type.
- Using intense pulsing or swirling motion without alternatives.
- Flattening 1960s counterculture into harmless “trippy” décor for inappropriate institutions.
