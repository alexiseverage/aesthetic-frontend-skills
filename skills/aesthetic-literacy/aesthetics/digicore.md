# Digicore

Digicore's visual language grows around personal releases, online collaboration and internet-rap circulation. Ordinary photographs, small authored drawings, avatars, game imagery, damaged digital surfaces and informal file-like presentation recur, but polished studio images and sparse compositions also belong. This is a contextual design grouping around a contested music scene, not a claim that every participant uses one palette or graphic style.

## Scope

The researched center is early-2020s release artwork and scene editorial graphics, with earlier singles and a later self-identified continuation. Discord and SoundCloud are distribution/community contexts, not proof that every design must imitate either interface. Artists change direction and may reject genre labels; do not classify an entire current discography from an early association.

Compared with the glossy product-world branch of `hyperpop`, this grouping gives more space to personal media, rough modification, private-public identity and ordinary environments. The two overlap substantially. The useful boundary is artist/community context and the selected visual grammar, rather than an absolute slick-versus-rough rule.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Muted blue, green, grey, beige, black and white recur in domestic/suburban photographs. Violet/lime chat graphics, magenta sticker marks, green screen light and pink optical hardware supply vivid alternatives. Limited colour and severe tonal reduction can be as effective as neon. Source colours were visually estimated, not sampled into universal tokens.

**Type**: Small plain serif, handwritten lowercase, outlined informal text, pixel lettering, coded geometric glyphs and occasional large authored marks. Many complete works are wordless; environmental signs or a garment symbol can carry identity. Monospace file labels are a proposed interface translation, not a historically mandatory Digicore typeface.

**Texture**: Grain, explicit JPEG damage, thresholded silhouettes, photocopy-like breaks, soft low-light capture, cutout edges and hand-drawn overlays. Glossy objects and clean studio photographs are real counterexamples. Distinguish intentional damage from an inadequate scan, and do not infer software or physical print technique from finish alone. Keep texture away from essential UI text.

**Shape**: Personal portraits, room/desk fragments, ordinary architecture, naive doodles, avatar/character imagery, coarse contours, small symbols and stickerlike mixed-scale layers. Source characters, app icons and performer identities are authored/owned elements, not an anonymous asset kit. New work should construct its own drawings and media.

**Motion**: All qualifying visual references in this profile are still works. Blurred heads, drawn speed, a music-software screen, static streaming previews and chat-app illustration establish no animation timing or behavior. A release browser can be intentionally still, with immediate selection, filtering, editing and disclosure. Any brief control feedback is a proposed application value; reduced motion removes transitions and retains every function.

**Spatial**: One personal image often occupies a broad quiet field, with very little added type. Other works layer line art, stickers and game/social-interface references over ordinary photographs. A single editorial graphic explicitly quotes a channel sidebar, avatar circles and unread badges. This supports a useful chat/file-browser application, not a universal historical layout rule. Preserve clear information planes and complete image compositions on mobile.

**Cultural markers**: Internet-rap lineage, online friend networks, collaborative releases, alternate personas, self-directed image-making, ordinary life and emotional directness. d0llywood1's firsthand account distinguishes community identity from a platform's Hyperpop umbrella. Respect the agency of Black, queer, trans and international participants; avoid treating youth, trauma, illegibility or chaos as required authenticity.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**:

- A specific personal release, artist or peer-network context, rather than generic glitch decoration.
- Deliberate use of personal/ordinary media, modification, drawing or avatar-like identity to express that context; rough and polished variants remain possible.
- A readable release/identity hierarchy that accommodates intimate, playful and contradictory images without requiring maximal density or irony.

## Connotation

**Mode:** contemporary revival.

Personal, provisional, close to everyday digital life, sometimes playful or abrasive. A local release folder can evoke work sent among friends while offering reliable contemporary controls. Do not fabricate online presence, private conversations, collaborator approval or real-world distribution. Intentional incompleteness is a visual choice; broken functionality is not an aesthetic requirement.

## Related / Subsets

- `hyperpop`: overlaps artists, synthetic materials and experimentation. Digicore's internet-rap/community context and ordinary media/circulation branch warrant separate guidance; the boundary is contested and changes with the artist.
- `myspace-chaos`: shares intimate online identity and mixed media; Digicore can be sparse and organized without losing its context.
- `glitch`: shares damaged digital surfaces. Grain or compression alone does not establish Digicore, and many researched works use neither.
- `8-bit-pixel`: shares game/bitmap imagery, but most researched work is not pixel art. A quoted game scene does not authorize reuse of its sprites.
- `emo`: shares diary-like expression and ordinary photography; this grouping is anchored in later networked rap/pop release culture rather than an interchangeable scene history.
- `early-internet`: shares modest publishing and user-authored media, with a different period and platform ecology.

No canonical subset slugs are defined. Named collectives, platform playlists and alternate artist accounts are not interchangeable labels or permanent genre assignments.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

A fictional netlabel release folder can combine an original personal-media image, a readable release list, filters, credits and a selected-item inspector. Use real local draft creation and a genuine metadata download if those actions are offered. Keep artwork separate from text, label file types, and make any local-only behavior explicit.

Native search/select controls, named keyboard-operable item buttons and a reversible draft form work better than requiring a precise drag gesture. If images can be selected, validate type, byte size and successful decode, cancel stale asynchronous work, describe failures and release temporary URLs. Do not imply an uploaded file reached a server when it remains in browser memory. Empty and reset states need useful recovery actions.

## CSS Translation

- Proposed quiet-folder roles: ink `#202521`, paper `#e4e9df`, muted text `#485447`, olive `#526744`, violet `#6e477e`, pale green `#d1e4a7`, white `#fafbf6`. These are new application values, not recovered source pigments.
- Use one original grainy photograph or small drawing beside a disciplined file/credit structure. Solid paper panels and thin dark rules preserve reading clarity; avoid a noisy overlay over controls.
- A new serif/italic identity can sit beside 12–16 px monospace metadata and 16 px sans body text. Small source lettering is not a reason to shrink essential instructions.
- Square or 2–4 px corners, 1 px rules, dotted separators and a 4/8 px spacing rhythm can suggest a provisional archive. Keep roughly 44 px targets and a visible 3 px focus treatment.
- Use container-aware stacking for narrow gallery/embedded placements as well as small viewports. Retain full artwork and meaningful file labels.
- Proposed feedback: immediate local state changes with at most 120 ms colour transitions. Reduced motion removes transitions; no shake, automatic scrolling, fake waveform, strobe or ambient glitch loop.

## Typography / Fonts

Source variety supports categories rather than a compulsory font list. Times/Georgia can supply an unpolished personal masthead; Arial and system monospace can carry functional metadata. These are application suggestions, not identifications of source lettering. Preserve original identity without copying a band's/artist's mark. Test fallback metrics and language coverage, especially when source media contains Portuguese, Japanese or encoded glyphs.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Artist testimony takes priority over flattening platform labels. Use current names, and describe earlier public stage identities only when necessary for provenance. Do not reproduce private locations, medical details, traumatic accounts or slurs as decoration. A cute image, religious clip art, commercial sign, game sprite or Japanese speech bubble is one work's choice, not a genre-wide cultural uniform. Public reference availability and verified uploader status do not establish permission to reuse every incorporated photograph or character.

Current deliveries of period tracks may have revised artwork; advisory labels, remasters and preview images must remain edition-specific. Visual credits are often unresolved and must not be inferred from music-production credits. The showcase should use original fiction and assets, with no borrowed source imagery, voices, lyrics or recordings.

## Anti-Patterns

- A glitch filter plus neon with no personal release or network context.
- Calling all internet music, all Hyperpop, every artist's later work or an unrelated band named Digicore the same aesthetic.
- Mandatory low resolution, maximal density, youth caricature or a single outsider's label despite artist objections.
- Copying chat screenshots, game sprites, artist portraits, private handles or source graphics as stock decoration.
- Fake uploads, automatic playback, tiny metadata, colour-only file status or pointer-only selection.
