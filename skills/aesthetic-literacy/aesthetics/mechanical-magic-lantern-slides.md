# Mechanical Magic Lantern Slides

## Scope

A hand-operated projection system, with painted glass, opaque masks and movable parts inside wood or metal carriers. Slipping slides change which painted areas the audience sees. Levers pivot a part of the scene. Rackwork can turn layered glass discs to change the geometry inside a fixed aperture. This entry covers those mechanical relationships and their use in contemporary exhibition readers. [Research profile](../../../knowledge/aesthetics/mechanical-magic-lantern-slides.md)

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Use near-black around the aperture. Concentrate gold, cobalt, turquoise, rose and ivory inside the light field. Treat warm wood, graphite and aged brass as separate support colors.

**Type**: Keep descriptive type near the apparatus. Use clear labels for name, mechanism, direction or position, and sources. Occasional engraved or printed words within the glass are content in their own right.

**Texture**: Use broad matte wood grain or darkened frame surfaces around smooth glass, clear paint boundaries and translucent overlaps. Give the control a distinct metal or wooden handle.

**Shape**: Build a rectangular carrier and an unmistakable circular or rectangular aperture. Distinguish the fixed border from the sliding pane or rotating discs. Give a lever a visible axis or range.

**Motion**: Let the user drive motion. Use lateral travel for a slip, opposing rotations for a two-disc study, and angular motion for a pivot. The mask and physical limits stay visible. Historic projection speed varies with the operator and is not established by the recordings.

**Spatial**: Keep the aperture central and separate the apparatus controls from the observed projection. A sight-line register or an overlaid edge can explain the mechanics without hiding the glass.

**Cultural markers**: Record workshop or collection attribution, mechanism, and approximate date. Distinguish historical slides, documented demonstrations and original reconstructions.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**:

- A fixed viewing aperture and at least one deliberately movable image layer.
- A visible or named mechanical control with an immediate visual consequence.
- A clear difference between the carrier material, masked field and transmitted image.

## Connotation

**Connotation**: Hand-operated exhibition, layered projection and an image whose movement is legible through its support.

## Related / Subsets

- Stage Magic Lithography uses stage imagery and billed spectacle. Lantern slides organize the picture around a physical aperture.
- Paper Theatre similarly layers scenery and material supports. A lantern makes focus and transmitted color central.
- Op Art can share overlapping grids and repetitive geometry. Lantern effects arise from deliberate mechanical movement in a confined field.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Give the stage a user-operated position control with arrow-key support. Show two original projections, such as opposed colored discs and a moon passing behind a slotted mask. Make the mechanism and current position discoverable as text. Provide immediate redraw and preserve all states under reduced-motion settings.

## CSS Translation

Start with near-black `#151218`, lit amber `#fbc56c`, wood `#743e2b`, and ivory labels `#f5e6cb`. Use clear frame edges, a hard aperture clip and controlled SVG transforms. Derive the display directly from manual input and avoid automatic animation.

## Typography / Fonts

Use a readable exhibit serif for the title and a straightforward sans for controls and notes. Keep annotations outside the optical field. Reserve small-cap treatment for short terms if it remains legible.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Credit provenance and explain when a sequence is reconstructed. Use independently drawn motifs for applications and keep manipulative historical caricatures in source context.

## Anti-Patterns

- A generic spinning kaleidoscope with no fixed aperture or user control.
- Glass and housing rendered as a single uniformly luminous surface.
- Applying an animated projection to the controls or prose.
- Claiming calibrated historical motion based on a modern edit.
