# Decora Kei

Decora Kei is a Harajuku street-fashion aesthetic translated into interface language through accessory stacking, candy-bright color, toy-plastic tactility, and joyful decorated density around readable content islands. Existing evidence is limited but coherent: subculture writing and academic fashion research support the core claim that decora is identity performance through layered, colorful, DIY self-styling.

## Scope

Use Decora Kei for social profiles, creator and fan communities, sticker-book tools, toy or collectible commerce, playful onboarding, youth-culture editorial, and virtual wardrobe or inventory products. It fits products where expressive self-styling, friendship, collecting, and visible customization are central.

Avoid it for serious enterprise, financial or medical workflows, quiet luxury, text-heavy knowledge work, or any context where dense ornament would trivialize content. The layout can be highly decorated, but it must protect a readable center for actual use.

## 7-Dimension Profile

**Palette**: Candy-bright rainbow stacks: bubblegum pink, aqua, lemon, lilac, mint, cherry red, plastic white, and occasional pastel variants. Use neutral or white utility anchors so joyful density stays readable.

**Type**: Bubbly rounded sans and sticker-caption display type. Headings can use rounded, inflated, or cutout-style letterforms; body, settings, and controls need a cleaner rounded sans. Type should feel attached to a sticker/accessory system, not just generically cute.

**Texture**: Toy-plastic and craft accessory tactility: glossy charms, foam stickers, puffy vinyl, bead shine, felt or fleece softness, scrapbook decals, and translucent acrylic clips. Use small gloss highlights, layered sticker shadows, and material-specific badges rather than one global gradient.

**Shape**: Accessory stacking vocabulary: hearts, stars, flowers, bows, blobs, clips, beads, rounded tabs, sticker frames, charm badges, and over-decorated edge clusters. Corners are soft, but the signature behavior is accumulation and layering around content.

**Motion**: Toy-like microinteractions: charm bounce, sticker pop, confetti bursts, bead wiggle, inventory pickup, and badge-attach motion. Timing should be quick and elastic; reduced-motion fallbacks should keep the static accessory signal.

**Spatial**: Cheerful anti-minimalist crowding with a readable center. Decoration clusters along edges and corners while core cards, forms, and navigation remain discoverable. Strong layouts use layered callouts, sticker piles, friend/profile cards, charm inventories, and dense collectible grids with clear content islands.

**Cultural markers**: Harajuku street-snap energy, hair clips, bead bracelets, kawaii characters, DIY accessory overload, rainbow plastic charms, sticker-book/social-profile layouts, playful self-styling, and community expression.

## Non-Negotiables

**Non-negotiables**:

- Visible accessory stacking, not just bright color.
- Toy, plastic, sticker, bead, or craft-material cues.
- Intentional playful clutter around readable modules.
- Joyful self-styling and community-expression tone.

## Connotation

**Mode:** authentic / nostalgic quotation.

Decora Kei should feel exuberant, sincere, and community-shaped. The frontend translation may be maximal and decorated, but it should not turn into cynical “kawaii overload” parody or generic cute theming.

## Frontend / UI Guidance

Build the usable task flow first, then decorate its edges and identity surfaces. Good targets include profile headers, avatar frames, sticker inventories, friend cards, collectible grids, onboarding rewards, empty states, and customization drawers.

Keep navigation, settings, form labels, and body copy calmer than the surrounding accessory field. Let stacked charms, stickers, and badges carry the aesthetic while interaction targets remain large, clear, and legible.

## CSS Translation

- Color roles: `--bg-candy`, `--surface-plastic-white`, `--accent-bubblegum`, `--accent-aqua`, `--accent-lemon`, `--accent-lilac`, `--accent-mint`, and `--text-ink`.
- Borders/dividers: sticker outlines, double strokes, puffy label borders, charm rings, and cutout frames.
- Radius language: soft 14–28px corners plus irregular sticker silhouettes and badge shapes.
- Effects: layered drop shadows, gloss highlights, acrylic translucency, puffy-vinyl bevels, foam-sticker offsets, and bead-like specular dots.
- Layout: readable content islands surrounded by clustered decoration, edge piles, inventory trays, and modular sticker sheets.
- Motion: springy attach/pop/bounce effects with `prefers-reduced-motion` fallbacks that leave layered static accessories visible.

## Typography / Fonts

Use rounded display faces for headlines, labels, and sticker-like captions, paired with a cleaner rounded sans for body and controls. Mixed playful caption styles can work in small doses, but dense interface text should not become decorative.

Avoid severe grotesks, formal serifs, and display type everywhere. The strongest systems contrast a few bubbly focal moments with readable support typography.

## Cultural / Ethical Notes

Treat Decora Kei as a Harajuku street-fashion lineage and living self-expression practice, not a generic “Japanese cute” costume. Preserve the logic of personal accessory stacking, DIY joy, and community identity rather than flattening it into stereotypes.

Because the current profile has no reviewed image corpus, keep claims tied to the documented subculture and fashion-research synthesis. Do not present unsupported micro-trends or image-specific variants as canonical.

## Anti-Patterns

- Bright rainbow UI with no layered accessories or material toy cues.
- Random kawaii icons scattered over a conventional app shell.
- Unreadable clutter where controls, copy, and hierarchy disappear.
- Cynical or infantilizing parody of Harajuku fashion communities.
- Chrome-heavy Y2K futurism or abstract Memphis geometry as the main signal.
